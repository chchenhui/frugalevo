# Optimization Insights Analysis

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns
- **Hybrid softmin-gradient + deterministic polish is the new best strategy**: The current best (`hybrid_adam_lattice_polish`, Gen 30, combined score **0.84**, min_area 0.03, 0.31s) combines a perturbed order-3 triangular lattice + centroid seed, 500-iteration softmin-annealed Adam ascent (temperature τ = 0.02·e^(−it/150), floored at 4e-4), then a 12-direction local polish targeted at the bottleneck points (those appearing most often in tight triangles ≤1.5× min). This beats the previous best bottleneck-annealing approach (Gen 15: 0.65) by +0.19 while running faster (0.31s vs 1.16s).
- **The hybrid resolves the earlier softmin weakness**: Previous insights noted softmin/Adam (Gen 10, Gen 24: 0.53) "maximizes the wrong objective" by spreading weight over near-minimal triangles. Gen 30 keeps the smooth Adam stage for fast coarse convergence but adds the membership-count bottleneck polish afterward to sharpen the single minimum — lifting min_area from 0.02 to 0.03 and breaking the long-standing 0.02 plateau.
- **Perturbed lattice + centroid multistart remains a reliable seed family**: Gen 30's `_initial` (order-3 lattice with centroid, Gaussian jitter σ=0.02, 5 seeds) gives structured but non-symmetric starting points; this matches earlier findings (Gen 17, Gen 18) that structured seeds with perturbation outperform pure random or pure lattice.
- **Bottleneck membership-count targeting generalizes across methods**: Whether inside annealing (Gen 15, 0.65; Gen 26, 0.60) or inside a deterministic polish (Gen 30, 0.84), selecting the 1–3 points most involved in near-minimum triangles consistently outperforms untargeted moves.

## Ineffective Approaches
- **Pure bottleneck gradient-lift converges to nothing**: Gen 20 (`bottleneck_gradient_lift`) scored 0.00 with min_area 0.00 despite 24.8s of computation — projected gradient moves with backtracking plus annealing kicks on the bottleneck alone, from multiple seeds, found nothing better than the fallback. Expensive multi-seed local gradient work on the bottleneck without a smooth surrogate (softmin) or coarse global stage fails.
- **Coordinated-perturbation/pattern-search machinery without global structure wastes compute**: Gen 21 (0.37 combined, min_area 0.01, 5.91s) spent 6 seeds × multi-round refinement yet landed near-degenerate — confirming that elaborate local refinement cannot compensate for weak initialization/escape mechanisms.
- **Degenerate returns from unverified optimizers persist**: Gen 27 (`bottleneck_correlated_lift`) scored 0.05 with min_area 0.00 in 0.01s — again the silent degenerate/fallback failure mode; Gen 30's explicit `best_val < 1e-6` check and exception-wrapped feasible fallback prevent this.
- **Greedy hill-climbing alone stalls**: Gen 14 (0.25) and Gen 13 (0.00) confirm strictly-greedy or weak-diversification local search cannot handle the rugged n=11 landscape; Gen 29's pattern search (0.55) is similar but better-seeded (boundary-heavy golden-ratio seeds).

## Implementation Insights
- **Softmin gradient with exact analytic gradients + Adam is the efficient global stage**: Gen 30's `_adam` computes all 165 cross products vectorized via precomputed `_I,_J,_K`, forms softmin weights `z = exp(-(a - a.min())/tau)`, and assembles gradients via `np.add.at` — 500 iterations across 5 seeds in well under a second. The temperature floor (`max(0.0004, ...)`) keeps the surrogate from becoming numerically degenerate late in the run, addressing Gen 10's plateau.
- **Bottleneck-aware polish reuses the membership-count trick**: Gen 30's `_polish` computes tight-triangle membership counts (`cnt` via `np.add.at` over `_I/_J/_K[tight]`), then tries 12 directional moves on the top-3 constrained points at geometrically shrinking steps (0.01→0.0003) — combining Gen 15's targeting with Gen 29's multi-direction pattern search. (Note: dead placeholder lines `a = best[[_TRIP...]]` / `areas = _min_area(...)` remain and should be removed.)
- **Cheap barycentric clip keeps every candidate feasible**: Gen 30's `_clip` (clip λ to [1e-9, 1], renormalize, map back via matrix multiply) is called after every Adam step and every polish candidate — same pattern as the previous best, contrasting with Gen 16's 12s Python-loop clamp.
- **Guarded fallback with value checks**: `best_val < 1e-6` triggers a deterministic polished-lattice fallback, avoiding the 0.00-score failures of Gens 13, 20, and 27.

## Performance Analysis
- **Score trajectory confirms hybridization as the breakthrough**: 0.00 (Gen 13/20, degenerate local search) → 0.25–0.44 (Gens 14, 21, 23, greedy/plain annealing) → 0.52–0.60 (Gens 24, 25, 26, softmin or multi-stage anneal) → 0.65 (Gen 15, bottleneck annealing) → **0.84 (Gen 30, softmin-Adam + bottleneck polish)**. Gen 30 is the first to exceed min_area 0.02, reaching 0.03.
- **Fast hybrid beats expensive pure-annealing**: Gen 30 achieves 0.84 in 0.31s, versus Gen 26's 0.60 in 9.11s, Gen 20's 0.00 in 24.8s, and Gen 17's 0.64 in 22.4s — gradient-based coarse optimization plus cheap targeted polish dominates large annealing budgets.
- **min_area drives the score ceiling**: All programs at min_area 0.01–0.02 scored 0.25–0.65; Gen 30's jump to 0.03 lifted combined score to 0.84, indicating the score is highly sensitive to breaking the 0.02 plateau (known optimum ~0.0237).
- **Cheap local refinement within ~0.03 of optimum needs only sub-second compute**: at 0.31s, Gen 30 has runtime headroom — prior data (Gen 17: 22.4s for 0.64) suggests more diversification/multistart within a few seconds could push min_area toward 0.037+ levels, but the proven lever is better global structure, not raw iterations.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

# Actionable Recommendations for Future Mutations

1. **Clean up dead code in `_polish` and make the bottleneck targeting adaptive**: Remove the placeholder lines `a = best[[_TRIP...]]` and `areas = _min_area(best) * np.ones(len(_TRIP))` (pure waste, and the min-area *value* differs from the unnormalized `ar` used for `tight` by a constant factor only — currently harmless but fragile). Then widen the tight-triangle threshold adaptively per step size: use `ar <= ar.min() * (3.0 - 2.4 * step_idx/3)` so coarse steps (0.01) move points involved in near-minimal triangles broadly, while fine steps (0.0003) target only the exactly-binding constraint. This costs nothing (the mask is already vectorized) and mirrors the proven softmin temperature annealing pattern in `_adam`.

2. **Exploit the runtime headroom with a second Adam reheat stage**: At 0.31s vs. a presumably multi-second budget, wrap the pipeline in an outer loop: after `_polish`, re-run `_adam` from the polished configuration with a *flatter* temperature schedule (τ = 0.004·e^(−it/300), 300 iterations) followed by a second `_polish`, repeating 3–5 times or until no improvement. This is the basin-hopping/reheat idea from earlier recommendations but implemented with the now-proven cheap gradient machinery instead of expensive annealing — it directly attacks the 0.02–0.03 plateau by letting the smooth surrogate re-homogenize areas around the bottleneck before re-sharpening.

3. **Add symmetry-breaking seed diversity to the multistart**: Keep the 5 lattice seeds but add (a) a golden-ratio low-discrepancy seed (barycentric coordinates from fractional parts of (k·φ, k·φ²)) and (b) a repulsion seed: run ~50 vectorized iterations of inverse-square repulsion from a jittered layout, projecting back with `_clip`. Gen 17/22 evidence shows lattice symmetry biases the local optimum basin; the current σ=0.02 jitter may be too small to escape it, and with 0.31s runtime there is ample budget for 10–15 total seeds.

4. **Move multiple correlated bottleneck points in `_polish`**: Currently only one point is moved per improvement step (the `break` after first improvement). Extend the candidate set to include simultaneous moves of the top-2 bottleneck points along the same 12 directions (and opposite directions), which escapes pair-traps that single-point moves cannot — the tight bottleneck is typically a small cluster of 3–4 points whose triangles all bind simultaneously. Additionally, when accepting a move, continue scanning the remaining directions at the same step before re-computing bottleneck counts, to amortize the per-iteration cost.

5. **Precompute incremental area updates for polish candidates**: For each of the 12 directional candidates, only the ~2·C(10,2)=90 triangles containing the moved point change area; all others are invariant. Precompute for each point index the array of triple rows containing it (via `np.where` on `_I/_J/_K` membership), maintain the full 165-vector of areas, and update only affected entries when evaluating a candidate — then `min` over the updated vector. This cuts each polish evaluation from ~165-triangle vectorized recompute to ~90 and, combined with recommendation 3's extra seeds, allows a substantially larger multistart budget within the same sub-second runtime.
