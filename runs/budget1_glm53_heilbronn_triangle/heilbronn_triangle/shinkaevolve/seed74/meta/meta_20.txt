# Optimization Insights Analysis

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns
- **Bottleneck-targeted annealing is the current best strategy**: The best program (`random_dir_anneal_bottleneck`, Gen 15, combined score **0.65**, min_area 0.02, 1.16s) moves 1–3 points chosen by counting membership in near-minimal triangles (area ≤ 1.5× min) via a precomputed boolean point-triplet membership matrix, plus occasional all-point jitter (15% probability). This targeted exploration beat untargeted annealing (Gen 16: 0.38; Gen 11: 0.18) and greedy/pattern local search (Gen 22: 0.55, Gen 10: 0.53, Gen 17: 0.64).
- **Diverse structured + jittered + free-form seeding with per-seed annealing and best-of selection**: The best program anneals each of 6 seeds (2 lattice-like, 2 jittered variants with pinned vertices, 1 uniform-random free-form) with per-seed RNGs and keeps the best. Gen 17 (0.64) similarly refined 4 curated seed families; Gen 18 (0.60) used three deterministic layouts plus multi-scale perturbations. Multi-seed diversity is a consistent top-scoring ingredient.
- **Incremental objective evaluation enables more search within budget**: Gen 18 (`fast_barycentric_incremental_sa`, 0.60, 4.86s) recomputes areas only for triplets containing the moved point, and the best program's vectorized `_areas` over precomputed triple indices keeps per-iteration cost low, allowing 2500 iterations × 6 seeds in 1.16s total.
- **Fallback verification against objective value**: The best program computes the fallback's min-area and only uses it if it beats the optimized result — avoiding the silent degenerate-fallback failure of Gen 13 (score 0.00).

## Ineffective Approaches
- **Untargeted/too-cold annealing fails**: Gen 11 (`barycentric_sa_minarea`) scored only 0.18 (min_area 0.01) — plain SA with coarse greedy polish steps (1e-3–3e-3) got stuck; Gen 16 scored 0.38 with acceptance scale T*1e-3 (too cold) and an expensive per-iteration Python-loop `_in_triangle` clamp (11.99s wasted). Bottleneck targeting in the best program (0.65) directly addresses this.
- **Strictly-greedy local search without restarts collapses**: Gen 13 (`seeded_local_search_heilbronn11`) scored **0.00** in 0.01s — strictly improving moves with no restarts left either collinear/coincident points or a degenerate configuration; validation of non-collinearity before return is missing.
- **Softmin/differentiable smoothing maximizes the wrong objective**: Gen 10 (softmin-annealed Adam, 0.53) spreads weight across many near-minimal triangles rather than sharply maximizing the single minimum, capping min_area at 0.02.
- **Lattice-biased seeds limit the ceiling**: Gen 17 (0.64) and Gen 22 (0.55) both note that symmetric/lattice seeds bias the search toward symmetric local optima rather than the true Heilbronn arrangement (~0.0237 known optimum), keeping all programs plateaued at 0.01–0.02.

## Implementation Insights
- **Membership-matrix bottleneck detection is cheap and effective**: The best program precomputes `member` (n × 165 boolean) once, then `cnt = member[:, tight].sum(axis=1)` identifies points appearing in many small-area triangles — a vectorized way to focus moves where the objective is binding, at negligible cost.
- **Incremental projection, not per-iteration full loops**: The best program's `_project` is fully vectorized (clip, sum, rescale only over over-sum rows) and applied once per candidate — vs. Gen 16's Python-loop clamp that made 20k iterations take ~12s.
- **Verified fallback with explicit min-area comparison**: `if best_val < fb_val: best_xy = _to_xy(fallback_b)` — the fallback is only used when better, preventing the Gen 7/13 pattern of silently returning a zero-scoring degenerate configuration.
- **Temperature-scaled move magnitudes**: The best program scales Gaussian perturbations by T (geometric cooling 0.012→1e-4), so moves shrink as annealing progresses — combined with accepting `v >= cur` (not strictly greater), which prevents stagnation that hurt strictly-greedy Gen 13.

## Performance Analysis
- **Score trend across all evaluations**: 0.00 (Gen 13, degenerate local search) → 0.18 (Gen 11, plain SA) → 0.38–0.42 (Gen 16, 12: weak annealing/hill-climbing) → 0.53–0.60 (Gen 10, 18, 22) → **0.65 (Gen 15, current best)**. Bottleneck-targeted annealing at 1.16s beats both more expensive approaches (Gen 17: 0.64 at 22.40s; Gen 16: 0.38 at 11.99s) and cheaper ones (Gen 9: 0.41 at 0.05s).
- **Time does not buy score**: Gen 17 spent 22.40s for 0.64 vs. the best program's 1.16s for 0.65 — targeted moves and vectorized evaluation dominate raw iteration count.
- **Universal plateau at min_area_normalized ≈ 0.02**: Every passing program (Gens 10, 12, 15, 17, 18, 19, 22) lands at 0.01–0.02, well below the known optimum ~0.0237 — the bottleneck is global search structure/initialization, not local refinement or objective computation. Combined-score differences (0.42–0.65) at identical min_area suggest the score also rewards other criteria (speed, robustness), favoring fast, reliable programs.
- **Zero scores stem from correctness, not optimization**: Gen 13's 0.00 despite passing validation confirms prior insight that degenerate/collinear configurations and unchecked fallbacks are the primary total-failure mode — the best program's fallback-value comparison is the proven mitigation.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Implement incremental area updates for moved points**: Currently `score()` recomputes all 165 triangle areas per iteration. Precompute, for each point, the list of triple indices containing it (the `member` matrix already encodes this — convert to index lists via `np.where`). When the annealer moves 1–3 points, recompute only the union of affected triplets (~45–90 instead of 165), and apply the same trick in `bottleneck_points`. Gen 18 showed incremental evaluation enabled 2500+ iterations cheaply; the current 1.16s could drop substantially, freeing budget for 10–15 seeds or 10k iterations.

2. **Add basin-hopping restarts with best-so-far memory within each seed**: After the geometric cooling schedule completes, if the seed's best min_area is below the current global best, reheat (T reset to ~0.006, half of T0) from the best configuration and re-anneal for another ~1500 iterations, up to 2–3 reheat cycles. This directly targets the plateau at 0.02 that all passing programs hit — the first annealing pass likely converges to a shallow local optimum from lattice-ish seeds, and reheating from `best_b` (rather than continuing from `b`) explores around the best basin.

3. **Diversify initialization with repulsion-based and golden-ratio asymmetric seeds**: Keep the 6 existing seeds but add (a) an electrostatic-repulsion seed: start from a jittered layout and run 50 vectorized iterations pushing points apart with 1/d² forces projected back into the simplex, and (b) a golden-ratio low-discrepancy seed (b[k] = fractional parts of (k·φ, k·φ²) scaled into the simplex). Gen 17/22 insight shows lattice symmetry biases local optima; these seeds break symmetry structurally rather than relying on jitter alone.

4. **Coordinate multi-point moves weighted by bottleneck membership**: Replace the uniform pick of 1–3 bottleneck points with probability proportional to `cnt` (the membership counts already computed in `bottleneck_points`), and occasionally move the *top-2 bottleneck points simultaneously along the same direction* (correlated moves). Coordinated moves escape single-point traps that independent perturbations cannot — a key reason the 15% all-point jitter helps, but that jitter is uncorrelated and small. Keep the vectorized `_project` unchanged.

5. **Add adaptive threshold widening in bottleneck detection**: The tight-triangle threshold is fixed at 1.5× min. Widen it adaptively during annealing (e.g., 3.0× at high T, narrowing to 1.2× at low T), so early iterations move points involved in *near*-minimal triangles broadly (more exploration), while late iterations focus only on the exact binding constraints (fine polish). The `tight = a <= thresh` computation is already vectorized; only the multiplier becomes a function of T. This mirrors the proven temperature-scaled move magnitudes and costs nothing extra.
