## Successful Algorithmic Patterns
- **The current best remains the softmin-Adam + bottleneck polish hybrid (score 0.84, min_area 0.03, 0.31s)**: perturbed order-3 lattice + centroid seeds, 500-iteration temperature-annealed softmin Adam (τ = 0.02·e^(−it/150), floored at 4e-4), followed by deterministic 12-direction polish on the top-3 bottleneck points (membership counts in triangles ≤1.5× min). This is the top performer across all generations and remains the best-scoring approach by a wide margin.
- **Multi-seed + temperature-annealed softmax/softmin weighting is the most reliable coarse stage**: Gen 41 (multi-seed softmax-weighted Adam, 500 iters with exponential-decay temperature, 8 diverse seeds) scored **0.80 in 0.62s** — the second-best result, confirming the smooth-surrogate gradient approach generalizes. Gen 37's multi-stage anneal → gradient polish with adaptive line search scored **0.71** (min_area 0.03, 4.96s). All three best programs (0.84, 0.80, 0.71) share: diverse structured seeds + smooth surrogate + deterministic polish.
- **Bottleneck-targeting remains the best polish/refinement mechanism**: Whether in annealing (Gen 32: 0.70, min_area 0.03, 1.65s — weighted/correlated moves on smallest-area triples) or deterministic polish (Gen 30 best, Gen 41), moves aimed at points in tight triangles consistently beat untargeted jitter (Gen 33: 0.39; Gen 38: 0.20 — both min_area 0.01).
- **Cheap coarse stage + cheap polish beats expensive annealing**: Gen 30 (0.84 in 0.31s) and Gen 41 (0.80 in 0.62s) both complete in under a second, while heavy multi-start annealing (Gen 35: 7 seeds × 4000+ iters, 13.25s → 0.50; Gen 28: 15.09s → 0.62; Gen 17: 22.4s → 0.64) spends 20–40× more time for worse results.

## Ineffective Approaches
- **Silent exception-swallowing caused a 0.00 failure**: Gen 31 (`bottleneck_gradient_reheat`) returned combined_score 0.00 / min_area 0.00 in 0.01s despite substantial refinement machinery — the bare `except` fell back to an unrefined perturbed lattice with near-collinear triplets. - **Pure bottleneck-gradient annealing without a smooth surrogate plateaus at 0.02**: Gen 28 (bottleneck-only gradient moves, basin-hopping, 8 seeds, 15.09s) scored only 0.62 with min_area 0.02. Gens 34 (0.60), 35 (0.50), 36 (0.60) all confirm bottleneck-guided annealing alone cannot break the 0.02 area plateau regardless of seed variety (lattice, golden-ratio, repulsion) or iteration budget.
- **Randomized jitter local search gets trapped in poor local optima**: Gen 33 (jitter descent + polish, 0.93s → 0.39, min_area 0.01) and Gen 38 (jitter descent + bottleneck-relief escapes, 0.87s → 0.20, min_area 0.01) show unstructured perturbation descent recovers only ~1% of best-known area despite multistart and escape moves.
- **Elaborate coordinated/pattern-search refinement machinery adds cost without gain**: Gen 35's golden/repulsion seeds plus 4000+ annealing iterations per seed added 13.25s of compute for 0.50 — expensive search machinery contributed little over cheap seeds.

## Implementation Insights
- **Vectorized softmin gradient via precomputed triplets is the key efficiency enabler**: The current best's `_adam` computes all 165 cross products from precomputed `_I,_J,_K`, forms softmax weights `z = exp(-(a - a.min())/tau)`, and accumulates exact analytic gradients with `np.add.at` — 5 seeds × 500 iterations in well under a second. Gen 36's incremental update (recomputing only ~45 triplets containing the moved point) is an alternative efficiency trick worth noting (5.11s, 0.60).
- **Bottleneck membership-count polish is cheap and decisive**: `_polish` counts tight-triangle membership (`tight = ar <= ar.min() * 1.5`) via `np.add.at`, targets top-3 constrained points with 12-direction moves at geometrically shrinking steps (0.01→0.0003). This converts the softmin stage's broad optimum into a sharp minimum, lifting min_area from 0.02→0.03. (Note: two dead placeholder lines `a = best[[_TRIP...]]` / `areas = _min_area(best) * np.ones(...)` remain and should be removed.)
- **Barycentric clip after every candidate keeps feasibility free**: `_clip` (barycentric conversion, clip to [1e-9, 1], renormalize, matrix multiply back) is called after every Adam step and polish candidate — sub-millisecond cost, no penalty landscapes needed.
- **Guarded fallback with explicit value checks**: `best_val < 1e-6` triggers a deterministic polished-lattice fallback, which is exactly what Gens 31 and 27 lacked — their silent fallbacks produced 0.00 scores.

# Actionable Recommendations for Future Mutations

1. **Implement the Adam→polish→reheat outer loop with a flatter second temperature schedule**: The pipeline runs in 0.31s, leaving ample budget for 3–5 outer iterations of `_adam` (τ = 0.004·e^(−it/300), 300 iterations, reset Adam moments each round) followed by `_polish`, keeping the best configuration seen. This uses the now-proven cheap gradient machinery to re-homogenize areas around the sharpened bottleneck before re-polishing — directly attacking the 0.02→0.03 plateau without the expensive annealing that failed in Gens 28/34/35.

2. **Add golden-ratio and repulsion seeds to the multistart, exploiting the runtime headroom**: Gen 41's 0.80 came from a more diverse 8-seed mix (including golden-ratio and repulsion seeds), confirming lattice symmetry biases the basin reached by the σ=0.02 jitter. Add (a) a low-discrepancy seed from fractional parts of (k·φ, k·φ²) mapped to barycentric coordinates, and (b) a repulsion seed from ~50 vectorized inverse-square repulsion iterations projected via `_clip` — at 0.31s current runtime, 12–15 seeds fit comfortably under a second.

3. **Move correlated pairs of bottleneck points simultaneously in `_polish`**: Replace the single-point 12-direction candidates with an extended candidate set that also tries simultaneous moves of the top-2 bottleneck points along the same and opposite directions (12 + 24 extra candidates). The tight bottleneck is typically a cluster of 3–4 points whose triangles bind simultaneously, so pair moves escape pair-traps that single-point moves provably cannot (Gens 33/38 show untargeted moves are the failure mode, but the current single-point `break` limits even the targeted version).

4. **Make the tight-triangle threshold adaptive per polish step and remove the dead placeholder lines**: Delete `a = best[[_TRIP...]]` and `areas = _min_area(best) * np.ones(...)` from `_polish`, then set the mask to `ar <= ar.min() * (3.0 - 2.4 * step_idx/3)` so coarse steps (0.01) move broadly-constrained points while fine steps (0.0003) target only the exactly-binding constraint. This mirrors the proven temperature-annealing pattern of `_adam` within the already-vectorized mask computation and costs nothing.

5. **Use incremental area updates in `_polish` via precomputed per-point triplet indices**: Precompute for each point index the rows of `_TRIP` containing it (`np.where` on `_I/_J/_K` membership, ~90 affected triplets per point vs. 165 total), maintain the full area vector, and update only affected entries when evaluating each directional candidate — then take the min over the updated vector (Gen 36 validated incremental updates as a viable efficiency trick). This cuts polish evaluation cost ~2×, freeing budget for recommendations 2 and 3's larger multistart and richer candidate sets within the same sub-second runtime.
