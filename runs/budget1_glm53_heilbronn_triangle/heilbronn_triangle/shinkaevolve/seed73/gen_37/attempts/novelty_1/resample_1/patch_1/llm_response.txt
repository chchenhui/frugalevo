<DESCRIPTION>
The key structural weakness of single-point perturbation is that degenerate (near-collinear or "hinge") configurations often involve several points from two nearly-minimal triangles simultaneously; moving one point at a time cannot escape such a saddle because improving one triangle worsens the other. Following the recommendation, I add a group move in the soft phase: with ~10% probability, perturb all vertices belonging to the two smallest triangles at once with independent per-vertex noise. When the two triangles share a vertex, this acts as a "hinge" move that can flip the degenerate layout single-point moves cannot resolve. I also track triangle indices alongside areas so the two smallest triangles are easy to identify, and reuse them for a targeted hard-phase kick if the polish stalls. This raises the achievable minimum area (primary objective) with negligible runtime cost since it reuses the already-computed areas within the same iteration budget.</DESCRIPTION>
