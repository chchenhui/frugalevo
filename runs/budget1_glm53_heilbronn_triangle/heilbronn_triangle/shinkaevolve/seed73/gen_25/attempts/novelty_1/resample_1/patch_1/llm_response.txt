<DESCRIPTION>
The current single-start winner is selected by the hard-min value after a fixed refine budget, then returned. The hard-min metric is notoriously plateau-prone: the winning start may just be lucky, and the runner-ups (often within a few percent) are discarded. I propose keeping the top-3 candidates from the multi-start loop and running an additional greedy, strictly-improving hard-min polish (with step-halving) on each of them before selecting the final winner. This is deterministic, cheap (~60 sweeps × 3 candidates), and directly attacks the hard-min objective with coordinate moves after the soft/hard search phases. I also tighten the hard-refine rounds slightly for better convergence per candidate. This follows the proven clip-then-renormalize projection (via _project_inside) and keeps the module-level caching for fast eval_time.</DESCRIPTION>

def _greedy_polish(pts, triples, step=0.004):
    """Strictly-improving deterministic coordinate polish on the hard-min
    objective, with step-halving when no single move helps."""
    pts = pts.copy()
    best, _ = _min_triangle_area(pts, triples)
    while step >= 1e-7:
        improved = False
        for i in range(len(pts)):
            for d in range(2):
                for s in (+1, -1):
                    cand = pts.copy()
                    cand[i, d] += s * step
                    cand = _project_inside(cand)
                    val, _ = _min_triangle_area(cand, triples)
                    if val > best + 1e-15:
                        pts, best = cand, val
                        improved = True
        if not improved:
            step *= 0.5
    return pts, best
