# Global Insights Analysis

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- **Compounding pair-exchange + directed polish + hard refine rounds is the new best recipe**: The current best (Gen 55, "compounding_pair_exchange", 0.84, 0.03 area) differs from the 0.80–0.82 tier by wrapping the escape machinery in *compounding rounds* (up to 4 iterations of pair-exchange → directed_polish → hard_refine), always tracking the global best with stall detection. This iterative cycling of escapes beats single-pass pipelines.
- **Two-stage seed screening with soft-min surrogate + promotion**: Gen 55 screens ~8–9 seeds with a log-sum-exp soft-min over the 20% smallest of the 165 triangles, then promotes only the top 3 to the expensive soft search — matching Gen 50 (0.80) and confirming the prior insight that seed screening concentrates compute on promising basins. Seed count saturates around 5–9 structured variants (Gen 57's 6 seeds → 0.74; Gen 55's promotion of 3 → 0.84).
- **Pair-exchange remains the top differentiator**: Across all rounds, pair-exchange programs (Gen 55: 0.84; Gen 54: 0.82; Gen 53: 0.81; Gen 50: 0.80) consistently beat group/hinge-move-only pipelines (Gens 52, 57: 0.74–0.76). Gen 55's version moves the two points of the minimal triangle in coordinated opposite directions with strict `v > val + 1e-15` acceptance.
- **Import-time/module-level precomputation decouples runtime from score**: Gens 52 and 54 report eval_time 0.00 at 0.76 and 0.82 respectively by running optimization at module import; Gen 55's 7.51s shows explicit runtime doesn't hurt either — the runtime budget is effectively unbounded, so search effort should be maximized, not economized.

## Ineffective Approaches

- **Pure subgradient ascent collapses to the bottom**: Gen 46 (0.39, area 0.01) used 12 seeds with annealing + directed subgradient ascent + greedy polish — the worst correct score of the round. The hard-min objective with fixed decaying steps gets trapped in bad local maxima; gradients of a min-function are too sparse to guide the search.
- **Barycentric clipping bugs cause total failure**: Gen 56 scored 0.00 because its `_project` routine allowed points outside the triangle (validation tolerance 1e-06). The correct programs (including the current best) use vectorized barycentric clip with negative-coordinate clamping + renormalization — geometry invariants must be validated against all three edge half-planes.
- **Pure greedy hard-min acceptance limits basin diversity**: Gen 53's feedback notes its hard-min acceptance (`val >= cur_val`) is "too greedy" and the fixed RNG seed limits diversity — it hit 0.81 vs Gen 55's 0.84 which uses screening + compounding rounds instead of relying on a single hard anneal pass.
- **More polish machinery doesn't break the 0.03 ceiling**: Gens 41–57 (17 programs across rounds) all cluster at 0.02–0.03 normalized area against the ~0.049 known optimum. Feedback across Gens 46, 52, 54, 55, 57 consistently says the search lands in a suboptimal local basin — the ceiling is structural to the local-search framework and to lattice-near initialization, not a tuning issue.

## Implementation Insights

- **What makes the current best (Gen 55, 0.84) effective**: (1) cheap soft-min screening of all seeds with 15 quick perturbations each, promoting top 3; (2) soft coarse search with annealed steps (0.05 → 1e-3 over 9000 iters, permissive `>= cur_val - 1e-12` acceptance); (3) 9000-iteration soft phase followed by 5000-iteration `_hard_refine` with mixed 1–3 point group moves; (4) the compounding Phase 3 loop with `stall >= 2` early exit and strict improvement tracking.
- **Strict improvement acceptance + step-halving remains standard**: Gen 55's `_directed_polish` and `_pair_exchange` both use `v > val + 1e-15` with `step *= 0.5` halving below 1e-7 — deterministic convergence per phase, contrasting with Metropolis/annealing acceptance that regressed in earlier generations (Gen 43: 0.44).
- **Vectorized doubled-area over precomputed triplet indices is universal**: Every passing program (Gens 50–57) computes all 165 triangle areas via precomputed `itertools.combinations` index arrays with NumPy cross products; combined with `_clip_to_triangle` barycentric projection, this is the validated correctness backbone.
- **Unreachable/dead code is harmless but wasteful**: Gen 54 (0.82) kept ~half the file as dead code after `return _POINTS.copy()` — harmless to score but signals the module-import precompute pattern carries legacy weight.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Add a triple-move escape for shared-vertex degeneracies inside the compounding loop.** After `_pair_exchange`, detect when the minimal and second-smallest triangles share vertices (overlap in the argmin triplet indices); when they do, move the shared vertex toward the centroid of 6–8 hexagonal-neighborhood candidate positions around its current location and accept with `v > cur_val + 1e-15`, then re-run `_hard_refine(0.0005, 1000)`. Pair-exchange can't fix cases where one binding vertex constrains multiple small triangles, and stacking escape types is the proven pattern behind the 0.76 → 0.84 progression.

2. **Increase compounding rounds from 4 to 8–10 with escalating perturbation scale.** Gen 55 exits after 2 stalls or 4 rounds; since runtime is decoupled from score (7.51s at 0.84 vs 0.00s at 0.82), raise the round cap and restart the loop after a stall with a larger `_hard_refine` step (e.g., cycle step0 through 0.005 → 0.002 → 0.0005) so each fresh round explores at a different scale before converging. Keep global-best tracking and strict `1e-15` acceptance exactly as-is.

3. **Add qualitatively different seeds to break the lattice-basin ceiling.** The 0.03 area ceiling across ~17 programs is attributed to lattice-near initialization; add 2–3 seeds with nested equilateral rings (rotated 30° relative to the outer triangle) and a boundary-heavy seed with 6–7 points near the edges. Screen them with the existing cheap 15-perturbation `_soft_obj` pass and promote top 3 of the enlarged pool (~11 seeds stays tractable, per Gen 45/55 screening evidence).

4. **Anneal the soft-min temperature within Phase 1 and sharpen across Phase 2.** Replace fixed `k=300` in `_soft_obj` with a schedule `k = 100 + 400*(it/iters)` over the 9000 soft iterations, keeping `frac=0.20` and the permissive `>= cur_val - 1e-12` acceptance unchanged. This smooths the soft→hard transition (which currently jumps from `k=300` soft-min straight to hard min in `_hard_refine`) and follows the multi-scale sharpening pattern that distinguishes the 0.8+ tier.

5. **Apply the compounding escape loop per promoted seed rather than only to the global best.** Currently only the best of the 3 promoted seeds gets Phase 3 escapes; run 2 compounding rounds (pair-exchange → directed polish → hard refine) on each of the 3 finalists first, then take the best of the three through the full 4-round loop. Gen 57 (6 seeds → 0.74) shows seed count alone isn't enough — the 0.84 win came from compounding, and combining it with per-seed escapes protects against discarding a seed that's in a better basin but slightly behind after soft search.
