# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- **Three-phase soft-min → hard-min → deterministic greedy polish is the new best recipe**: The current best program (Gen 16, "Multi-Phase Stochastic Search", combined score 0.75, min_area_normalized 0.03) used structured seeds (triangular lattice, boundary-heavy, uniform interior) plus a soft-min log-sum-exp surrogate over the smallest 20% of the 165 triangle areas with large random steps, then hard-min refinement with shrinking steps, then a coordinate-wise greedy polish. This is the highest score across all 11 generations and confirms the previous insight that two-stage soft-then-hard optimization works — Gen 3 (0.64) and Gen 21 (0.73) also used this structure.
- **Soft-min surrogate over the smallest 15–20% of triangles is the key escape mechanism**: Programs using log-sum-exp smoothing consistently outperform pure hard-min local search: Gen 16 (0.75), Gen 21 (0.73), Gen 3 (0.64), versus hard-min-only or degenerate softmin programs scoring 0.13–0.53 (Gens 15, 17, 18, 20).
- **Long optimization budgets with shrinking step schedules remain essential**: Gen 16 ran 12k soft + 10k hard iterations plus 60 greedy-polish sweeps, taking 8.43s — the longest eval time among correct programs and the highest score. This extends the previous finding that iteration count correlates with score (40k → 0.66 in Gen 6; 22k+polish → 0.75 in Gen 16).
- **Multi-start with diverse structured seeds outperforms single seeds**: Gen 16's three seed types (lattice, boundary-heavy, Dirichlet interior) echo Gen 3's 13 starts (0.64) and Gen 9's hexagonal + 10 random (0.51); every single-seed program (Gens 1, 4, 5, 11, 12, 17) scored ≤0.29.

## Ineffective Approaches

- **Seeds containing collinear/near-collinear points doom the search to zero min-area**: Gen 11 (min_area 0.00), Gen 12 (0.00), and Gen 17 (0.01, "three points on y=0") all started from hand-designed symmetric seeds with collinear points. Single-point local moves cannot fix degeneracy since all collinear points must move simultaneously — hard-min objective plateaus at zero.
- **Barycentric clamping that folds points onto boundaries creates coincident points**: Gen 15's `_bary_fold` projection produced coincident points and zero-area triangles, capping it at 0.13 despite 4.15s of runtime. The best program's clip-then-renormalize avoids this by spreading weight back into the triangle.
- **Broken seeds/layouts are fatal correctness failures**: Gen 14's triangular lattice produced only 10 points (1+2+3+4 rows) → "Invalid shapes: points = (10, 2)" → score 0.00; Gen 13 crashed on an inhomogeneous array construction from its layout generators → 0.00. Sound optimizer machinery is worthless if any start yields the wrong shape.
- **Flawed soft-min implementations neutralize the smoothing benefit**: Gen 17's softmin multiplied its correction term by 0.0, making it a near-useless objective; combined with a degenerate symmetric seed it reached only 0.21 despite otherwise solid structure.

## Implementation Insights

- **Gen 16's winning details**: vectorized doubled-area computation over precomputed C(11,3)=165 triplet indices; clip-to-[0,1]-then-renormalize barycentric projection (never folds points onto each other); non-worsening acceptance (`val >= cur_val - 1e-12`) in stochastic phases but strictly improving acceptance (`> cur_val + 1e-15`) in the greedy polish; deterministic per-seed RNGs (12345 + 1000*seed_id); and a fallback validity guard on the output.
- **Soft-min tuning matters**: Gen 16 used k=300 with frac=0.20 (smallest ~33 triangles); Gen 21 used frac=0.15 and scored 0.73. Both partition areas rather than exponentiating the full vector — `np.partition` on the near-minimal subset is the efficient, numerically stable pattern.
- **Greedy polish converges by halving step size on failure**: Gen 16's Phase 3 sweeps all points/coordinates at step 0.005 and halves the step when no improvement is found (floor 1e-7), adding deterministic refinement that the stochastic phases can't achieve alone. Gen 12's coordinate-wise hill climb failed only because its seed was degenerate — the polish mechanism itself is sound.
- **Guaranteeing exactly-n points in every seed is critical**: Gen 14's bug (lattice rows summing to 10, with a `len(pts) < n` trim branch that never fired) shows seeds must be shape-asserted before annealing.

## Performance Analysis

- **Gen 16 (0.75) is the new best, surpassing the previous best Gen 6 (0.66)**: the gain came from replacing single-layout annealed local search with three diverse structured seeds, a properly implemented soft-min coarse phase, and a deterministic greedy polish — not merely more iterations (Gen 16's 22k stochastic iterations is fewer than Gen 6's 40k).
- **Correct programs cluster: min_area 0.01–0.03 vs known optimum ~0.040**: Gen 16 and Gen 21 both reached 0.03 (scores 0.75/0.73); Gens 18 (0.02/0.53), 20 (0.01/0.23), 17 (0.01/0.21), 15 (0.00/0.13) trail. The soft-min + diverse-seeds combination is what pushes past the 0.02 plateau that single-point local search previously saturated at.
- **Correctness failures cost everything**: Gens 13 and 14 scored 0.00 from array-shape bugs despite sound vectorized machinery — validating output shape (11,2) is worth more than any algorithmic tuning.
- **Runtime does not penalize up to ~8.4s**: Gen 16's 8.43s eval scored highest; Gen 21's 3.74s scored 0.73; Gen 9's near-zero time scored only 0.51. Within the observed range, longer budgets with soft-min phases win, though the previous insight about diminishing brute-force returns stands — the marginal gains came from structure (seeds, phases), not raw iteration count alone.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

# Recommendations for Future Program Mutations

1. **Add biased point selection targeting the smallest triangles during stochastic phases.** Keep Gen 16's three-phase structure intact, but with probability ~0.6–0.7 select the perturbed point from the argmin triplet (or among points participating in the smallest ~10 triangles, computable via a quick count over `_all_areas`), and uniform-random otherwise. This directs soft/hard-min effort at the binding constraints, which uniform random moves waste; prior evidence shows targeted moves were only invalid due to syntax errors, never conceptually.

2. **Introduce coordinated group moves (2–3 points simultaneously) in Phase 1.** All top programs saturate at min_area 0.01–0.03 below the known ~0.037 optimum, suggesting single-point moves can't escape certain configurations. With ~20% probability during the soft-min phase, perturb the 2–3 vertices of the current smallest-area triangle with correlated Gaussian deltas scaled to the current step — the soft-min objective makes such coordinated moves acceptable where hard-min would reject them.

3. **Expand multi-start to 6–10 seeds with more diverse structured layouts, splitting the phase budget.** Gen 16's three seeds are its edge over Gen 21 (0.75 vs 0.73), and Gen 3's 13 starts scored 0.64 with far weaker machinery. Add a hexagonal-perturbation seed, a "rings" seed (points on concentric scaled triangles), and 2–3 additional Dirichlet samples with different fixed seeds, giving each start ~6k soft + 5k hard iterations within the same ~8.4s runtime envelope.

4. **Tune the soft-min parameters and add a mid-k transition phase.** Gen 16 used k=300/frac=0.20 (0.75) vs Gen 21's frac=0.15 (0.73); try frac=0.20 with k annealed from ~150 to ~600 across Phase 1 so the surrogate progressively sharpens toward hard-min, avoiding the abrupt Phase 1→2 objective switch. Also try frac=0.25 to lift more near-minimal triangles simultaneously.

5. **Run the Phase 3 greedy polish on the top-2 or top-3 seed results rather than only the best.** The polish's strictly-improving acceptance plus step-halving is deterministic and cheap (~60 sweeps), so polishing the two runners-up (within ~5% of best_val) costs little and protects against the winner merely being lucky under the hard-min metric. Keep the clip-then-renormalize projection and the output validity guard unchanged — they are proven correctness-critical.
