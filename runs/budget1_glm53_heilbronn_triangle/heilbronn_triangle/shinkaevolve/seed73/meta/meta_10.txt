# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns
- **Long-budget annealed hill-climbing with shrinking steps is the current best strategy**: The current best program (Generation 6, "Annealed Local Search", score 0.66, min_area 0.02) ran 40k iterations of single-point perturbation with shrinking Gaussian steps plus a 10k-iteration fine polish (step 0.002→1e-5). This beat shorter-budget approaches (Gen 4's 20k iterations/1.5s cap → 0.29; Gen 5's 6000 iterations → 0.10; Gen 10's 6000+2000 iterations → 0.49).
- **Multi-start with structured seeds helps escape local optima**: Gen 3 ("Pattern Search", score 0.64) used 13 starts (triangular lattice + 12 random barycentric samples), and Gen 9 (score 0.51) used a hexagonal lattice seed + 10 random starts — both outperformed single-hardcoded-layout approaches with similar or less total work.
- **Soft-then-hard two-stage optimization works**: Gen 3's log-sum-exp soft-min over the smallest 15% of triangles, followed by hard minimum refinement, achieved 0.64 — the second-best score — showing smoothing the objective helps navigate the rugged landscape.
- **Vectorized minimum-area computation over all C(11,3)=165 triplets** is universal among all scoring programs and enables high iteration counts within reasonable eval time.

## Ineffective Approaches
- **Short, poorly-tuned annealing schedules collapse to near-zero area**: Gen 1 (greedy, 400 iterations, score 0.00), Gen 4 (temperature `step*0.05`, cooling factor 0.6 every 2000 iters, score 0.29), and Gen 5 (large 0.03 step scale, score 0.10) all failed to escape poor local optima. - **Single hardcoded starting layout limits exploration**: Every program relying on one hand-designed symmetric seed (Gen 1, 4, 5) scored ≤0.29, versus multi-start programs scoring 0.51–0.66.

## Implementation Insights
- **Barycentric clipping with renormalization is the proven feasibility mechanism**: The current best program's `_clip_to_triangle` computes barycentric weights, clips to [0,1], and renormalizes — keeping all points strictly feasible throughout. Gen 10's "folding" variant (clamp + renormalize) was also praised as "a clean constraint-handling choice."
