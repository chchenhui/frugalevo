# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- **Three-phase soft-min → hard-min → greedy polish remains the dominant recipe**: The current best program (Gen 32, "coordinated_group_moves_phase1", combined score 0.80, min_area_normalized 0.03, 3.23s) confirms and extends the previous best recipe from Gen 16 (0.75) and Gen 26 (0.80). It uses a soft-min log-sum-exp coarse phase (15k iters), a hard-min annealed phase with shrinking steps (30k iters, step 0.05→1e-4), then a fine polish (10k iters, step 0.002→1e-5). Gen 26 ("heilbronn11_multistart_refined") also scored 0.80 with the same soft-then-hard structure over diverse seeds.
- **Coordinated group moves are the key escape mechanism**: Gen 32's distinctive addition — simultaneously translating/expanding all three vertices of the current smallest triangle (with 20% probability) during the soft-min phase — lets the soft objective tolerate temporary hard-min worsenings and escape "pinning" configurations that single-point perturbations cannot fix. This mirrors earlier findings that collinear/degenerate traps require simultaneous multi-point moves.
- **Biasing perturbation toward binding constraints works**: In Gen 32's hard phase, 50% of moves select a vertex from one of the 8 smallest triangles rather than a uniformly random point, directly targeting the binding constraints of the min-area objective.
- **Soft-min surrogate over the near-minimal subset stays consistent**: Gen 32 selects areas within `min + 0.05 * triangle_area` and applies log-sum-exp with k=60, matching the established pattern of smoothing over the smallest ~15-20% of the 165 triangles.

## Ineffective Approaches

- **Silent exception swallowing produced a valid-but-scoreless program**: Gen 7 (0.00) had a `np.triu_indices(n, 3)` unpacking bug caught by a broad try/except, silently falling back to a degenerate spiral layout with zero minimum area. Correctness guards must not be exception-based fallbacks.
- **Sophisticated gradient machinery (Adam, analytic gradients) underperformed simple stochastic search**: Gen 19's Adam soft-min ascent with greedy polish scored only 0.39 (min_area 0.01) in 4.41s; Gen 24's similar Adam approach scored 0.30 in 3.86s. The clamping/projection flow collapsed points together rather than spreading them.
- **Under-consuming the time budget correlates with weak scores**: Gen 22 finished in 0.17s (score 0.24) and Gen 23 in essentially zero time (0.34), both well under the budget; premature breaks and accepting unoptimized seeds wasted available optimization time.
- **Broken seed generation remains fatal**: Gen 27 (0.00, crash "index 10 out of bounds") generated a ring seed with only 10 points — identical failure mode to earlier Gen 14. Every seed must be shape-asserted to yield exactly 11 rows.

## Implementation Insights

- **Gen 32's winning details**: module-level precomputed `_TRIPLETS` (165×3 index array) with fully vectorized doubled-area cross products; clip-to-[0,1]-then-renormalize barycentric projection (`w /= w.sum()`) that spreads weight instead of folding points onto boundaries; non-worsening acceptance (`val >= cur_val - 1e-12`) in stochastic phases with strictly-improving best-tracking (`> best_val + 1e-15`); shared-translation group moves plus outward centroid expansion with per-vertex noise.
- **Runtime efficiency enables longer schedules**: Gen 32 runs 55k total iterations in 3.23s thanks to the vectorized area computation — less total time than Gen 16's 8.43s but with more iterations and equal-or-better score, showing vectorization, not wall-clock time, is the lever for budget scaling.
- **Annealing acceptance in the soft phase only**: Gen 32 uses Metropolis-style acceptance (`rng.random() < exp((s_new - soft_cur) * 40)`) only during the soft phase, then switches to non-worsening acceptance for the hard and polish phases — a clean separation matching Gen 16's pattern.
- **Validity guard on output**: Gen 32's final fallback check (`isfinite`) guarantees a valid (11,2) result even if optimization fails — cheap insurance against score-zero correctness failures.

## Performance Analysis

- **Correct programs cluster tightly at 0.01–0.03 normalized area**: Gen 32 and Gen 26 top the cluster at 0.03/0.80; Gen 25 (pattern search) reached 0.02/0.64; gradient-based and pure hill-climbing approaches (Gens 19, 22, 24, 28, 23) plateau at 0.01 with scores 0.22–0.39. Known optimum remains ~0.040, so all current programs are still local-optimum-limited.
- **Group-move coordination separates the top tier**: Gen 32 (0.80) vs Gen 28 (0.22) — both multi-seed annealed local searches, but only the coordinated multi-vertex moves reach 0.03; single-point hill climbing saturates at 0.01 regardless of seed diversity (Gen 28 used 9 diverse seeds and still failed).
- **Runtime within budget is not penalized**: Gen 32's 3.23s scored highest, Gen 26's near-zero time also scored 0.80 — indicating score depends on search structure (phases, group moves, seed diversity), not raw wall-clock time. Under-consuming the budget (0.17s, Gen 22) is however a symptom of broken search loops, not a strategy.
- **Crash-prone implementations cost everything**: Gen 27 (index bug) and Gen 7 (silent exception) both scored 0.00 despite sound core machinery, reconfirming that output-shape validation and per-seed shape assertions are worth more than any algorithmic tuning.

# Recommendations for Future Program Mutations

1. **Add a multi-start layer on top of Gen 32's exact machinery.** Gen 32 beats Gen 28 (9 seeds, single-point moves) purely via group moves, but Gen 26 matched 0.80 with multi-start — combining both is untested. Run Gen 32's three-phase pipeline (perhaps with 4k soft + 8k hard + 3k polish iterations each) on 4–5 seeds: the current heuristic layout, a Dirichlet(2.0) sample, a concentric-rings layout, and a perturbed-hexagonal grid, keeping the best. Runtime headroom exists (3.23s vs ~8.4s budget) thanks to the vectorized `_all_areas`.

2. **Scale group-move coordination up to two-triangle moves in the soft phase.** Gen 32's single-triangle group move (20% probability) is its key differentiator; extend it to perturb all vertices of the two smallest triangles simultaneously (~10% probability) with per-vertex independent noise. When the two smallest triangles share a vertex (check `_TRIPLETS` overlap), this creates a "hinge" move that can flip a degenerate configuration single-triangle moves cannot resolve.

3. **Anneal the soft-min sharpness k from ~30 to ~120 across Phase 1.** Gen 32 uses fixed k=60; annealing k progressively sharpens the surrogate toward hard-min, smoothing the Phase 1→2 objective transition that Gen 32 handles with an abrupt switch. Keep frac at 0.05 (`min + 0.05 * tri_area`) since that matched the winning pattern, and keep the Metropolis acceptance (`exp((s_new - soft_cur) * 40)`) unchanged.

4. **Add a targeted polish phase that cycles through the k smallest triangles per sweep.** Gen 32's polish (10k iters, single random point) wastes moves on non-binding points. Replace it with an ordered sweep: for each of the ~10 smallest triangles (sorted by `_all_areas`), try single-vertex moves along the outward-normal directions of its two adjacent edges plus a small random component, accepting strictly-improving moves with step-halving on failure. Deterministic direction sampling is cheap and targets exactly the binding constraints.

5. **Add a final coordinate-descent "pair-exchange" perturbation as a last-resort escape.** All top programs plateau at 0.03 vs. the known ~0.037–0.040 optimum, suggesting trapped basins. After the polish phase, spend ~2k iterations trying a structured move: pick a vertex from the smallest triangle and swap it with a nearby non-binding vertex (one whose 20 smallest incident triangle areas are all large), then re-run a short hard-min polish (~3k iters, step 0.01→1e-4) from the swapped configuration, keeping the result only if `best_val` improves. This exploits the vectorized area machinery to precompute per-vertex binding scores cheaply.
