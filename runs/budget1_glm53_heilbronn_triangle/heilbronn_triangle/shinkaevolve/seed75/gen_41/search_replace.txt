<DESCRIPTION>
The current program spends 20s in a best-of-K random perturbation loop that converges to mediocre local optima (~0.03 normalized). Two targeted improvements: (1) cut the time budget to 10s (eval_time is secondary but the 20s budget mainly buys redundant restarts, and 0.71 < the 0.76 of the faster program, so time isn't buying quality); (2) append a deterministic geometric polish stage that directly attacks the argmin (worst) triplet: move each of its three points along the unit perpendicular direction away from the opposite side — the exact direction gradient of the triangle's area — with a shrinking step size. This "worst-triangle expansion" polish is the classical refinement for Heilbronn-style max-min problems and squeezes out extra area after the stochastic search stalls, since increasing the smallest triangle's area only ever requires moving its own three points.

    def _perp_dir(pa, pb, pc):
        """Unit vector from c perpendicular to line ab, on c's side (increases area)."""
        ex, ey = pb[0] - pa[0], pb[1] - pa[1]
        L = np.hypot(ex, ey) + 1e-12
        ux, uy = ex / L, ey / L
        dx, dy = pc[0] - pa[0], pc[1] - pa[1]
        if -uy * dx + ux * dy >= 0:
            return np.array([-uy, ux])
        return np.array([uy, -ux])

    def _argmin_polish(pts, triples, iters=600):
        """Deterministic polish: expand the argmin (worst) triangle geometrically."""
        step = 0.01
        cur_val, widx = _min_area(pts, triples)
        for _ in range(iters):
