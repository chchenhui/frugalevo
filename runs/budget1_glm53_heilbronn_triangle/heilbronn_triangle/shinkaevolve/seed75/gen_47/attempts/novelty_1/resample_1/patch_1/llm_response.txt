<DESCRIPTION>
The current `softmin_obj` applies the temperature-annealed log-sum-exp over all 165 triplet areas. This dilutes the gradient signal across ~145 non-binding triples whose areas are far above the minimum; their exponentials are near-zero anyway, but at moderate temperatures (like the first annealing stage, temp = 1e-2 * area_T) they still pull the soft-min estimate upward and bias the Powell search toward increasing many irrelevant areas rather than pushing up the truly binding worst triples. Switching to a top-k (k=20) softmin — take the k smallest areas and apply the log-sum-exp only over those — concentrates the smooth surrogate exactly on the binding constraints. This is the smooth analogue of the worst-triplet targeting that made the discrete `_polish`/`_stochastic_refine` pipelines effective in earlier generations, but fused into the Powell pipeline so the continuous optimizer benefits. The `a.min()` shift for numerical stability is retained, and `np.partition` makes the selection O(C). The k=20 choice keeps some robustness (the binding set can change between iterations) while excluding the irrelevant bulk. Expected effect: sharper descent on the true min area at each annealing stage, raising min_area_normalized and hence combined_score, with negligible extra runtime cost.

    def softmin_obj(theta, temp, k=20):
        pts = unpack(theta)
        p = pts[triples]
        cross = ((p[:, 1, 0] - p[:, 0, 0]) * (p[:, 2, 1] - p[:, 0, 1])
                 - (p[:, 1, 1] - p[:, 0, 1]) * (p[:, 2, 0] - p[:, 0, 0]))
        a = 0.5 * np.abs(cross)
        # top-k softmin: focus the smooth surrogate on the k binding
        # (smallest-area) triples instead of diluting across all 165.
        ak = np.partition(a, k)[:k]
        amin = ak.min()
        # smooth lower bound on min(a): -temp*log(sum(exp(-a/temp)))
        return temp * np.log(np.sum(np.exp(-(ak - amin) / temp))) - amin
