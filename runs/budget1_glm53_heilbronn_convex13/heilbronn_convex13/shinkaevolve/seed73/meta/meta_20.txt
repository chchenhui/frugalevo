# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- **Hybrid soft-min gradient + hard-objective polish is now the best approach (0.87)**: Gen 20's "vectorized_softmin_gradient_and_polish" (0.87, min_area 0.03, 1.65s) and Gen 18's "fast_basin_hopping_heilbronn13" (0.80, min_area 0.02, 17.66s) both outperform the previous best (Gen 6's pure hard-min coordinate descent, 0.72). This revises the earlier insight that soft-min gradients are strictly inferior: when a *surrogate* softmin stage is followed by exact hill-climbing on the true ratio and a *smoothed blended score* (0.6·min + 0.4·mean of smallest 5 areas) is used for intermediate selection, softmin becomes a useful accelerator rather than the final word.
- **The decisive pattern: optimize the hard objective for the final answer, smooth only as a guide**: All top performers (0.80–0.87) select and polish candidates using true_score = min_area/hull_area, while earlier soft-min-only programs (Gen 14's 0.18, Gen 21's 0.40) plateaued because they relied on the softmin path entirely.
- **Symmetry can seed, but must not constrain**: Gen 20's 4-base-point 3-fold rotation expansion achieved the best score, but Gen 21's fixed two-ring hexagonal initialization (0.01 min area, 0.40) and Gen 14's constrained symmetric parametrization (0.01, 0.18) show structural priors must be combined with broad multi-start (Gen 20 uses 31 seeds including 24 random) and symmetry-breaking refinement.
- **Diverse multi-start initialization with candidate funneling**: Gen 18 (13-gon, symmetric, jittered grid, random seeds) and Gen 20 (31 seeds) both use broad initialization; Gen 20 funnels only the top 4 candidates into the expensive exact hill-climb, giving speed (1.65s vs Gen 18's 17.66s) at a higher score.

## Ineffective Approaches

- **Pure softmin gradient without hard-objective polish**: Gen 14 (0.18) used softmin gradient ascent + coordinate polish on the surrogate with fast-decaying steps (0.999^iters) and got stuck at min_area 0.01. Gen 21 (0.40) shows the same failure mode even with a 16-direction pattern search when the initialization is structurally trapped.
- **Over-constrained structural priors**: Gen 21's two concentric hex rings (0.01, 0.40) and Gen 13's 3-fold symmetric hill climb (0.02, 0.70) both limited exploration; feedback consistently notes the symmetry assumption "over-constrains the configuration."
- **Interface/robustness bugs still waste sound algorithms**: Gen 15 (0.00, returns (13,2) array when harness expects 3 unpackable values) and Gen 19 (0.00, stray `========` merge-conflict marker causing a syntax error) never executed. This echoes the earlier triu_indices unpacking crash (Gens 2, 4, 5).
- **Degenerate output from clip-and-normalize**: Gen 17 reported all-zero metrics (0.00) despite ~20s of elaborate optimization — likely duplicate/collapsed points, confirming that a final duplicate-point safety pass (as in Gen 20) is essential.

## Implementation Insights

- **The current best program's funnel architecture**: Gen 20 expands 31 four-point bases via 120° rotations + center, runs vectorized LogSumExp softmin gradient ascent (annealed sharpness 30→600, cosine LR decay, bincount-based exact analytic gradients) on each, ranks by true_score, and applies coordinate hill-climbing + deterministic basin-hopping only to the top 4. This staging gives 0.87 at only 1.65s.
- **Blended intermediate score smooths the rugged objective**: Gen 20 and Gen 13 both use 0.6·min + 0.4·mean(smallest 5) for hill-climb guidance while using the exact ratio for final selection — this reliably outperforms pure min-based climbing of low-scoring programs.
- **Vectorized bincount gradients keep per-iteration cost low**: Gen 12 (0.63s) and Gen 20 (1.65s) both use bincount over all 286 triples' exact analytic gradients, demonstrating O(n) assembly per iteration is cheap; contrast with Gen 18's 17.66s slow Python hull-chain loop — the hull computation is now the main runtime bottleneck, not the triple evaluation.
- **Defensive output validation matters**: Gen 20's duplicate-point safety pass (nudging coincident points apart) and fallback to a regular 13-gon ensure a valid return; Gen 17's lack of such checks produced a 0.00 score.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Scale up the candidate funnel: increase seeds and polish slots within the time budget.** Gen 20 runs 1.65s while Gen 18 spent 17.66s, so there is ~4× headroom. Raise random seeds from 24 to 60–80, keep the fast grad_ascent at ~300 iters, and widen the exact-polish funnel from top 4 to top 10–12 candidates. Evidence: the funnel design (cheap softmin on all seeds, exact polish on few) is the demonstrated win; broad multi-start is what broke the 0.02 plateau to reach 0.03.

2. **Replace the Python monotone-chain `_hull_area` with `scipy.spatial.ConvexHull` or a vectorized version.** The global insight explicitly identifies hull computation as the main runtime bottleneck (Gen 18's 17.66s came from its slow hull loop). Since `score`/`true_score` call `_hull_area` hundreds of times per hill-climb sweep, vectorizing it directly buys more polish sweeps and more seeds at fixed runtime.

3. **Generalize seed expansion beyond 3-fold symmetry: add 4-fold and 5-fold rotation expansions plus fully asymmetric seeds.** Gen 20's 3-fold expansion won, but Gen 13/21 show fixed symmetry over-constrains. Add expand4 (4 base points × 3 rotations + center is current; also try 3 base points × 4 rotations + center, and 2×6+center variants) and pass a small set of fully random 13-point seeds directly through grad_ascent + hill_climb, ranked by true_score. This preserves the successful symmetry-seeding while breaking its structural trap.

4. **Add a hull-expansion move and joint hull/area scoring in the polish stage.** Since score = min_area/hull_area, occasionally try scaling all non-hull-interior points outward by 1.01× and accept only if true_score improves, exactly as recommended before but now inside the proven hill_climb loop. Also consider a second blended-score variant (0.8·min + 0.2·mean-smallest-3) for hill-climb guidance to check whether the 0.6/0.4 blend is optimal, keeping exact ratio for final selection.

5. **Strengthen basin hopping with adaptive amplitudes and re-seeding from near-miss candidates.** Currently 3 fixed perturbations around the best; instead run 8–10 perturbations with amplitudes 0.01–0.08 drawn deterministically, and also perturb the 2nd and 3rd-ranked candidates, re-running grad_ascent + hill_climb on each. Evidence: Gen 18's basin-hopping variant reached 0.80 and Gen 20's limited basin-hopping stage exists but is underused; deterministic multi-perturbation around multiple elites is a cheap, proven-safe extension.
