# Updated Global Insights

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- **Candidate funnel (cheap-for-all, expensive-for-top-4) remains the top architecture**: The current best program (Gen 20, 0.87, 1.65s) runs vectorized softmin gradient ascent on all 31 seeds but reserves exact hill-climbing and basin-hopping only for the top 4 by true_score. This generation (Gens 22–31) repeatedly confirms this: Gen 31's four-stage funnel reached 0.84, and Gen 27's wide funnel reached 0.81 in just 5.70s — the two best scores of the new batch. Programs that polish everything (Gen 22, 40.59s for 0.58; Gen 23, 30.61s for 0.70) pay 10–25× runtime for worse results.
- **Final selection must use the hard objective (min/hull), with blended scores only as search guidance**: Gen 20 (0.87) and Gen 30 (0.73) both use 0.6·min + 0.4·mean(smallest 5) to guide hill-climbing while ranking by the exact ratio. Gen 26's funnel with exact coordinate-descent polish scored 0.62; pure softmin-as-final-answer approaches (Gen 25, 0.15; Gen 29, 0.19) collapse.
- **Multi-start breadth beats symmetry-only seeding**: The best program's 31 seeds include 24 random 4-point bases, breaking out of the symmetric-seed trap. Gens 27, 31 (60–72 diverse seeds: rings, double rings, lattices, random) scored 0.81 and 0.84. Symmetric-only seeds (Gen 28, five symmetric seeds, 0.67; Gen 22, 0.58; Gen 30, 3-fold constrained, 0.73) systematically score lower.
- **Fastest runtimes come from the same top designs**: Gen 28 hit 0.67 in 2.80s and Gen 27 hit 0.81 in 5.70s — well-directed vectorized refinement is both faster and better than brute-force descent (Gen 22's 40.59s, Gen 23's 30.61s).

## Ineffective Approaches

- **Over-constrained 3-fold symmetry with fixed center point caps the ceiling**: Gen 30's 4-base-point 120°-rotation expansion with mandatory center point scored 0.73 with explicit feedback that the symmetry prior "likely caps the achievable minimum triangle area at 0.02." The best program uses 3-fold expansion only as *seeds* (then breaks symmetry via gradient ascent), not as a hard constraint.
- **Pure coordinate descent is too weak for n=13**: Gen 23's 16-direction multi-start coordinate descent (30.61s) scored only 0.70; Gen 22's hard-min descent (40.59s) scored 0.58. Feedback for both: convergence to poor local optima.
- **Softmin-only or gradient-dominant pipelines produce degenerate near-collinear triples**: Gen 25 (softmin + hill climb + basin hopping) scored 0.15 with min_area 0.00; Gen 29 (softmin gradient + polish) scored 0.19 with min_area 0.01. When the softmin surrogate drives the final configuration, near-collinear degeneracy follows.
- **Artifact hygiene failures still zero out otherwise-sophisticated programs**: Gen 24 scored 0.00 purely from a stray `=======` delimiter causing a syntax error — echoing Gen 19's identical failure. Also, dead code wastes compute: Gen 30's grad_ascent had placeholder zeroed analytic gradients falling back to finite differences.

## Implementation Insights

- **Bincount-based analytic gradients of the LogSumExp softmin are the key cheap-refinement engine**: The best program assembles per-point gradients in O(n) per iteration via `np.bincount` over all 286 triples (sharpness annealed 30→600, cosine LR decay), enabling 300-iteration refinement of 31 seeds in ~1.6s total. Gen 28's feedback explicitly flags the pure-Python 286-triple gradient loop as a bottleneck (2.80s for far fewer iterations).
- **Blended intermediate score (0.6·min + 0.4·mean of 5 smallest) smooths the rugged objective**: Used by Gen 20, Gen 30, and Gen 13 for hill-climb guidance, while true min/hull selects the winner. This generation's lower scorers (Gen 25, 0.15; Gen 29, 0.19) climbed on the pure min and stalled.
- **Defensive output validation is mandatory**: The best program's duplicate-point nudge and regular-13-gon fallback prevent 0.00 failures; Gen 24 shows a single stray delimiter line destroys everything regardless of algorithm quality. Programs must ship as one syntactically valid block.
- **Deterministic basin hopping around the best candidate adds marginal gains cheaply**: Gen 20's 3 deterministic sinusoidal perturbations (amp 0.03–0.09) followed by re-descent; Gen 31's amplitude-ladder basin hopping contributed to its 0.84. Gen 30's multi-amplitude Gaussian kicks around top-3 elites achieved 0.73 even with its flaws.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Scale the funnel to 60–80 seeds and top-10 polish slots.** Gen 20 finishes in 1.65s while the runtime budget is far larger (Gens 22–23 spent 30–40s), so there is ~5–10× headroom. Raise random 4-point base seeds from 24 to ~60 and widen the exact hill-climb polish from top 4 to top 10 candidates, keeping grad_ascent at 300 iterations — broad multi-start breadth is exactly what broke the 0.02 plateau (Gen 27's 60–72 seeds hit 0.81, Gen 31's four-stage funnel hit 0.84).

2. **Vectorize `_hull_area` with `scipy.spatial.ConvexHull` (or a shapely-equivalent vectorized monotone chain).** Hill-climbing calls `score`/`true_score` hundreds of times per sweep, and each call runs a Python-loop hull computation. Replacing it makes each polish sweep several times cheaper, which directly converts into more seeds or more hill-climb steps at the same runtime.

3. **Diversify seed expansion beyond 3-fold symmetry.** Keep the proven `expand` (3-fold + center) but add variants: 2 base points × 6-fold rotation + center, 3 base points × 4-fold rotation + center, and a few fully asymmetric random 13-point seeds passed straight through grad_ascent + hill_climb. Evidence: hard 3-fold constraints cap the score (Gen 30, 0.73), while using symmetry only as seeds breaks local-optimum traps.

4. **Strengthen basin hopping into an elite-ladder restart scheme.** Replace the current 3 fixed sinusoidal perturbations with 8–10 deterministic perturbations at amplitudes 0.01–0.08 applied to the top 3 ranked candidates, re-running grad_ascent + hill_climb on each. Gen 20's basin-hopping stage contributed to its 0.87 despite being underused; Gen 30's multi-amplitude kicks around top-3 elites achieved 0.73 even with weaker components.

5. **Add a hull-expansion move and test alternative blended scores in the polish stage.** Inside hill_climb, occasionally try scaling all points outward by 1.01× (reducing the denominator of score = min_area/hull_area), accepting only if true_score improves. Also test a sharper blend (0.8·min + 0.2·mean of 3 smallest) as hill-climb guidance while keeping the exact min/hull ratio for final selection — the 0.6/0.4 blend is proven but untested against alternatives.
