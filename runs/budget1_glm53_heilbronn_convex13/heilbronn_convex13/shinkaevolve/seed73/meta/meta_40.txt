# Updated Global Insights

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- **Wide multi-start + funnel (softmin-gradient for all, exact polish for top-K) remains the winning architecture — now with TWO co-best programs at 0.87**: Gen 20 (1.65s) and the new Gen 39 `heilbronn13_wide_funnel` (6.02s). Gen 39's key upgrade over prior funnels: it scaled random 4-point base seeds to 70 (from 24), expanded polish width to top-10 (from top-4), and used a two-tier blend (0.6·min + 0.4·soft-min for guidance, exact min/hull for selection). Combined with ~6s runtime, this shows breadth of seeding and polish width can scale without prohibitive cost.
- **The four-stage funnel with large seed diversity is confirmed as the second-best design**: Gen 32's staged funnel (60 diverse seeds → stochastic hill climb on top-14 → basin hopping on top-3 → hull-expansion polish) scored 0.84 at 14.45s. Its feedback attributes success to "diversity of geometric seeds (especially symmetric ring configurations)."
- **Symmetry-reduced seeds (not constraints) are cheap and effective**: Gen 36's `sym3_pattern_hybrid` (4 base coords refined via pattern search, then 26-coordinate symmetry-breaking polish with incremental triangle recomputation) hit 0.81 in just **1.22s** — the best runtime-per-score in the entire generation. Gen 39 uses the same expand-then-break-symmetry pattern.
- **Deterministic basin hopping after polishing adds cheap gains**: Gen 39's 6 sinusoidal perturbations (amp 0.025·k) around the best followed by re-ascent + short hill-climb contributed to its 0.87; Gen 32's basin hopping on top-3 elites supported its 0.84.

## Ineffective Approaches

- **Hard 3-fold symmetry with mandatory center point caps the ceiling**: Gen 33's pure symmetric optimizer scored only 0.74 (min_area 0.02) with explicit feedback that symmetry "forces degenerate small triangles that hill climbing cannot escape." Gen 40's similar symmetric pattern search scored 0.73 (0.02) in 18.11s — worse and 15× slower than Gen 36's symmetry-breaking variant (0.81, 1.22s). Symmetry must be a seed, not a constraint.
- **Optimizing a surrogate that diverges from the true objective produces zero**: Gen 34's funnel (19.51s) scored 0.00 because `_min_triple_area` only checked consecutive triples (i, i+1, k), and a unit-hull rescale broke the unit-square constraint — a correct-looking pipeline destroyed by objective mismatch.

- **Differential evolution underperforms the funnel**: Gen 41's DE over softmin surrogate scored only 0.57 (6.73s) — below every funnel variant — with feedback recommending "more aggressive restarts/diversification rather than more local refinement."

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Break the 0.03 plateau with a hull-reduction move in hill_climb.** All three 0.87/0.84 programs are stuck at min_area 0.03, well below the known n=13 optimum ~0.083, suggesting the true objective's denominator (hull area) is underexploited. Add a move that scales all points outward by 1.01× (or pulls the hull's extreme points inward) inside the existing `hill_climb` inner loop, accepting only when `true_score` improves — since score = min_area/hull_area, shrinking the hull while holding min area can jump the ratio past the softmin-guided local optimum. Gen 20's recommendation 5 was never validated; this is the cheapest untested frontier.

2. **Adopt Gen 36's incremental triangle recomputation inside `hill_climb`.** Currently each candidate evaluation recomputes all 286 triangle areas via `_tri_areas` plus a Python-loop hull — hundreds of times per sweep. When moving point `i`, only triangles containing `i` change (~66 of 286); maintain a cached area array and recompute only `idx` rows where `I==i | J==i | K==i`, and use `scipy.spatial.ConvexHull` (or vectorized monotone chain) for the denominator. Gen 36 proved this style of optimization achieves 0.81 in 1.22s; the savings here convert directly into widening polish from top-10 to top-20 or doubling hill-climb steps within the 6s runtime.

3. **Broaden the elite-ladder basin hopping into a multi-amplitude restart scheme.** Gen 39's 6 fixed sinusoidal perturbations (amp 0.025·k) around only the single best contributed to 0.87 but is underused. Extend to 8–10 deterministic perturbations at amplitudes 0.01–0.08 applied to the top 3 ranked candidates (post hill_climb), re-running `grad_ascent(200)` + short `hill_climb` on each. Gen 30's multi-amplitude kicks around top-3 elites and Gen 32's basin hopping on top-3 both support diversification around multiple elites rather than one.

4. **Diversify seed symmetry beyond 3-fold: add 4-fold and 6-fold expansions plus asymmetric full-13 seeds.** Keep the proven 3-fold `expand` but add variants: 2 base points × 6-fold rotation + center (13 pts), 3 base points × 4-fold rotation + center, and pass a handful of fully random 13-point configurations straight through `grad_ascent` + `hill_climb`. Gen 32's success was attributed to "diversity of geometric seeds (especially symmetric ring configurations)" and Gen 33/40 prove symmetry must seed, not constrain — new symmetry families may land in different basins that escape the 0.03 plateau.

5. **Screen candidates with the exact objective before polishing, and add a second-round funnel.** Instead of ranking the ~80 grad_ascent outputs purely by softmin-derived `true_score` and polishing top-10, run a cheap 3-step mini hill_climb on all candidates to re-rank, then full-polish top-10 from the re-ranked list — the softmin surrogate and exact objective disagree near plateaus (Gen 34's lesson), so re-ranking after a taste of exact descent avoids wasting polish slots on softmin-optimal but exact-poor candidates. Gen 32's staged funnel (60 seeds → climb top-14 → basin hop top-3) validated staged narrowing; a two-pass ranking is its cheap generalization.
