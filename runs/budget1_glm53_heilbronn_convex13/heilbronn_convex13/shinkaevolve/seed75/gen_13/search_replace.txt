<DESCRIPTION>
The key weakness of the current code is that its hill climber moves one uniformly random point at a time, wasting most of its budget on points that have no effect on the smallest triangle. Expert knowledge of the Heilbronn problem says the objective is controlled by the "bottleneck" — the few smallest-area triangles — and improving them requires moving the nearly-collinear triples themselves (often all three vertices, or the middle point perpendicular to the line through the other two). I propose: (1) target the perturbation at the vertex(s) of the current smallest triangle (with occasional perpendicular escape moves that directly reduce near-collinearity), (2) accept simulated-annealing style downhill moves so the search can escape local optima, (3) extend the time budget to 3.0s (eval_time is a secondary metric and the previous 3s program scored best), and (4) add a ring+center seed (12-gon + center), which is a strong structural prior for n=13. These changes are deterministic (fixed seed) and preserve the existing score/geometry machinery.
