# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- **Diverse multi-seed initialization + adaptive hill climbing remains the winning formula (current best, score 0.71)**: The current best program (Gen 14, "add_diverse_seeds_and_restarts") extends the Gen 1 approach (0.67) with 8 diverse seeds (regular 13-gon, stretched ellipse, perturbed polygon, random, two-cluster arcs, superellipse, Archimedean spiral, two-ring 7+6) plus a second restart phase from perturbed best with sigma=0.08. This achieved the highest combined score of 0.71.
- **Second-phase restart from perturbed best**: The dedicated restart loop perturbing the incumbent best with fresh, smaller sigma (0.08 vs 0.15) is the key addition that lifted the score from 0.67 → 0.71, providing escape from local optima that the single-pass hill climb stalls in.
- **Vectorized minimum-triangle computation**: All correct programs (Gen 13–19) use precomputed C(13,3)=286 index combinations with vectorized cross products, enabling enough iterations within the 2–3s budget. Monotone-chain hull normalization keeps the objective well-defined.
- **Strict-improvement acceptance (csc > sc + 1e-12)**: The current best's tolerance-guarded acceptance prevents the degenerate drift seen in earlier failures (Gen 11's `>=` acceptance); this pattern is shared across all scoring programs.

## Ineffective Approaches

- **Sophisticated gradient machinery without correctness**: Gen 2 (score 0.0) had a two-stage targeted-gradient + softmin polish but failed validation (likely constraint/format violations), and Gen 20 (score 0.0) crashed with a Qhull "Initial simplex is flat" error because its flawed gradient (omitted hull-area normalization in the chain rule) drove three points to near-coincidence (~1e-8 spread). Gradient-based ascent on the minimum triangle collapses without anti-degeneracy safeguards.
- **Simple arithmetic/initialization bugs**: Gen 22 (score 0.0) indexed point 13 in a 12-point initialization, crashing with "index 12 out of bounds." Point-count and shape bugs cannot be rescued by any optimizer.
- **Bottleneck-targeted moves alone don't beat plain random perturbation**: Despite geometric sophistication, Gen 13's bottleneck-targeted annealing (0.57), Gen 15's bottleneck-pool hill climb (0.60), Gen 16's subgradient+perturbation (0.57), Gen 18's directed SA moves (0.58), and Gen 19's hybrid bottleneck hill climb (0.63) all scored below the current best's simple random-vertex perturbation (0.71). All stalled at min_area_normalized 0.02.
- **Low-dimensional parameterizations**: Gen 17's ellipse-angle Adam parameterization (13 angles + 2 axes, soft-min objective, score 0.57) restricted the search space and got stuck at 0.02, well below the known optimum (~0.075).

## Implementation Insights

- **The current best's robustness comes from construction, not projection**: All 8 seeds are valid by construction and the pipeline contains no rescaling or projection that could push points out of bounds — the key contrast with failed Gen 5/9. A finite-value fallback (regular 13-gon) is the only safeguard needed.
- **Adaptive sigma schedule (grow 1.3x / shrink 0.6x) with per-batch evaluation**: The current best evaluates 200 candidate moves per sigma adjustment, balancing exploration/exploitation; this schedule is shared by the top-scoring programs (Gen 14: 0.71, Gen 1: 0.67) but absent from lower scorers.
- **Multi-seed diversity must be broad but simple**: The 8 seeds span boundary shapes (polygon, ellipse, superellipse), interior fills (spiral), and mixed structures (two-ring, two-cluster arcs) — yet Gen 12's overly structured concentric layout scored only 0.30, showing diversity of *basins* matters more than structural symmetry.
- **Time budget exactness**: The current best finished in 3.02s, matching the budget precisely; scores did not improve with more iterations (Gen 8's 3s budget matched Gen 1's 2s at 0.57-0.67 range), confirming the bottleneck is move quality, not iteration count.

## Performance Analysis

- **Score plateau at 0.02 min_area_normalized is universal**: All seven correct programs in this batch (Gen 13–19) reached exactly 0.02, yet combined scores range from 0.57–0.71 — confirming the combined score is sensitive to factors beyond min_area (e.g., final hull area). The current best's 0.71 comes from a better final configuration geometry, not a better minimum-area value.
- **Simple random perturbation beats targeted moves**: The current best (0.71) uses uniformly random vertex selection, while all bottleneck-focused variants (Gen 13, 15, 16, 18, 19: 0.57–0.63) score lower. Bottleneck targeting concentrates search but may over-commit to a single weak triangle's local gradient.
- **Correctness still dominates**: 3 of 10 programs in this batch scored 0.0 from crashes or constraint violations (Gen 2, 20, 22); the marginal gain from Gen 1 (0.67) to Gen 14 (0.71) came from restart diversification, not from optimizer sophistication.
- **Restart-phase design drives the incremental gains**: Gen 19's perturbation restarts pushed 0.60→0.63, and the current best's cleaner two-phase structure (full seed sweep, then iterated restarts around the incumbent) reached 0.71 — the only program in the batch to break the 0.60s cluster.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Add a phase-3 restart with structured basin-hopping around the incumbent.** The Gen 1 → Gen 14 gain (0.67 → 0.71) came from the perturbed-best restart phase. Add a third phase that perturbs only 3–4 randomly chosen vertices at larger sigma (0.12–0.15) while keeping the rest fixed, then re-runs the adaptive hill climb — this explores basin transitions the full 26-coordinate perturbation (sigma 0.06) smooths out. Keep strict-improvement acceptance (`csc > sc + 1e-12`) throughout.

2. **Implement the incremental triangle evaluation speedup (recommendation 5 from previous round, still untried).** When vertex `i` moves, only the 66 of 286 triangles containing `i` change area; compute their areas in closed form and track a precomputed min over the remaining 220 (with an argmin index for the current bottleneck triangle). Similarly, skip the Python-loop monotone-chain hull unless `i` was a hull vertex. This 4x+ per-iteration speedup converts directly into more restarts/seeds within the same 3.0s budget — the proven lever for score gains.

3. **Add 50% bottleneck-vertex-targeted moves to the hill climb.** With probability 0.5, perturb a vertex of the current minimum-area triangle (found via `argmin` over the 286 cross products) instead of a uniformly random vertex, using the same adaptive sigma schedule. Gen 19's restarts with targeting pushed 0.60 → 0.63; combined with the current best's multi-seed + restart structure and strict acceptance, this should strictly dominate both. Avoid full bottleneck-only targeting (Gen 13–18 all underperformed random).

4. **Add coordinated 3-vertex moves of the minimum triangle.** When targeting the bottleneck, with ~20% probability perturb all 3 vertices of the minimum triangle simultaneously with correlated small sigma (drawn from the adaptive schedule). This gives access to basin transitions single-coordinate moves cannot reach, and strict-improvement acceptance prevents the degenerate drift that killed Gen 5/11. Cap coordinated-move frequency so exploration isn't over-committed to one weak triangle.

5. **Add 2–3 additional basin-diverse seeds built valid-by-construction.** Following the pattern that diversity of basins (not symmetry) matters, add: (a) a two-cluster arcs seed with different cluster ratio (9+4), (b) a logarithmic spiral with reversed chirality, and (c) an irregular randomized convex polygon (perturbed 13-gon re-projected to the boundary *at construction time*, never post-hoc). Truncate the seed loop time per seed (e.g., ~0.35s each) so all seeds get hill-climbed before the restart phases begin — restarts around the incumbent proved more valuable than deep single-seed climbs.
