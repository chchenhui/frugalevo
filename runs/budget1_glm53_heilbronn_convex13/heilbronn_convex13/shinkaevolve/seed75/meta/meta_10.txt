# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns
- **Boundary-biased multi-seed initialization + adaptive hill climbing (current best, score 0.67)**: Generation 1's program (and the current best, which is essentially the same approach) uses regular 13-gon, ellipse, perturbed polygon, and random seeds, then adaptive single-point perturbation with sigma annealing (grow 1.3x on improvement, shrink 0.6x otherwise) over a 2-second budget. This achieved the highest combined score of 0.67 among all evaluated programs.
- **Vectorized minimum-triangle computation**: Precomputing all C(13,3)=286 triangle index combinations and computing areas via vectorized cross products is a shared pattern across all passing programs (Gen 1, 6, 8, 12), enabling many optimizer iterations within the time budget.
- **Bottleneck-vertex-targeted annealing**: Generation 8 (score 0.57) identified and perturbed the vertex belonging to the current minimum-area triangle, helping escape local optima better than pure random perturbation restarts.
- **Time-budget-aware search**: All scoring programs (0.57–0.67) explicitly enforced a 2–3 second budget, trading search depth for guaranteed completion.

## Ineffective Approaches
- **Constraint violations via rescaling/scaling**: Generation 5 (score 0.0) rescaled points by hull area (division by sqrt(hull area)) and used y-scaling 0.87, pushing points outside the unit square; Generation 9 (score 0.0) placed points on a unit circle outside the required domain. Post-hoc rescaling and unconstrained domains cause validation failure regardless of optimizer quality.
- **Broadcasting/unpacking bugs in seed generation**: Generation 4 (score 0.0) crashed on `pts[-1] + np.arange(...)` (shape (2,) vs (3,)); Generation 11 (score 0.0) crashed on `ii, jj, kk = np.array(np.triu_indices(n,1)).tolist()` — triu_indices returns a 2-tuple. try/except wrappers cannot rescue errors occurring during candidate generation.
- **Incorrect gradient math**: Generation 7 (score 0.0) had a wrongly-signed/mixed softmax-weighted gradient, ascending on the wrong quantity; correctness must precede sophistication.
- **Conservative annealing schedules**: Generation 8's T0=0.01 with fast cooling and Gen 6's subgradient on only the single smallest triangle both stalled at min_area_normalized 0.02 — the plateau suggests moves were too small/local.

## Implementation Insights
- **The current best program's effectiveness** comes from simplicity and robustness: no rescaling (points stay in the valid region by construction), finite-value safety fallback, adaptive sigma that expands exploration on improvement and contracts on stagnation, and a cheap monotone-chain hull area for normalization. Nothing in its pipeline can push points out of bounds — a key contrast with the failing Gen 5/9.
- **Degenerate drift from lateral moves**: Gen 11's `new_obj >= best_obj` and Gen 5's `s >= cur_s` acceptance of equal scores permitted degenerate drift; the current best requires strict improvement (`csc > sc + 1e-12`), a safer pattern.
- **Per-iteration full objective recomputation is costly**: Gen 11's 4000 iterations with full re-evaluation per move risked timeout; vectorized NumPy over all 286 triangles (used in all passing programs) is the efficient pattern.
- **Normalization choice matters**: All scoring programs normalized by convex hull area via monotone chain or shoelace, keeping the objective well-defined even for non-convex arrangements.

## Performance Analysis
- **Score plateau at 0.02 min_area_normalized**: All correct programs (Gen 1: 0.67, Gen 6: 0.57, Gen 8: 0.57, Gen 12: 0.30) reach only 0.01–0.02 normalized minimum area — far below the known optimum — indicating that local search variants (hill climbing, annealing, subgradient) all converge to similar weak local optima. The 0.67 vs 0.57 gap between Gen 1 and Gen 6/8 suggests combined score is sensitive to factors beyond min_area (e.g., hull area of the final configuration).
- **Correctness dominates score**: 5 of 10 programs scored 0.0 due to crashes or constraint violations; no optimizer sophistication compensates for a validation failure. The best program (0.67) is among the simplest.
- **Gen 12's structured concentric layout (center + triangle + 9-gon) scored only 0.30**, below the simpler polygon seeds of Gen 1 — structured symmetry alone without strong search is insufficient.
- **Time budgets cluster around 2–3s**: Eval times of passing programs were 2.01–3.01s; scores did not improve with the longer 3s budget of Gen 8, suggesting the bottleneck is search strategy quality, not iteration count.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Add bottleneck-vertex-targeted perturbation to the current hill climb (hybrid of Gen 1 + Gen 8).** Keep the current best program's adaptive-sigma hill climbing intact, but with probability ~50% per move, perturb the vertex participating in the current minimum-area triangle (identifiable via `argmin` over the 286-vectorized cross products) instead of a uniformly random vertex. Gen 8 showed this helps escape local optima, and combining it with the current best's multi-seed initialization and strict-improvement acceptance (`csc > sc + 1e-12`) should strictly dominate either approach alone.

2. **Increase seed diversity with structured asymmetric layouts while preserving validity-by-construction.** The current 4 seeds (13-gon, ellipse, perturbed polygon, random) plateau at 0.02 normalized min-area; add 2–3 more seeds such as a two-cluster configuration (points split between two arcs of the boundary), a Reuleaux-like rounded-square boundary, and a spiral filling the unit square. All seeds must be built directly in the valid domain (no post-hoc rescaling — the failure mode that zeroed Gen 5 and Gen 9). More diverse basins address the plateau that all local-search variants hit.

3. **Add occasional coordinated multi-point moves and lateral-move restarts with strict-improvement acceptance.** The single-point Gaussian perturbations converge to weak local optima; add moves that perturb 2–3 vertices simultaneously (especially the 3 vertices of the minimum triangle together) with sigma drawn from the same adaptive schedule. Accept only strict improvements to avoid Gen 5/11's degenerate drift. This gives the search access to basin transitions that single-coordinate moves cannot reach.

4. **Optimize the objective directly for the combined score, not just min-area ratio.** The 0.67 vs 0.57 score gap at similar min_area (0.01–0.02) suggests the final combined score depends on more than min_area_normalized (likely hull area of the final configuration). Add a secondary criterion: among configurations with comparable min-area ratios (within ~1e-3), prefer the one with larger absolute hull area (e.g., re-center and prefer boundary-hugging configurations). This can be implemented as a lexicographic tie-break in the `best_score` comparison without touching the time budget or validation.

5. **Speed up the inner loop with incremental triangle evaluation to multiply iterations within the 2s budget.** Currently each candidate recomputes all 286 triangles and the Python-loop monotone-chain hull; instead, precompute the hull area only when the perturbed point is a hull vertex, and use an incremental update: when vertex `i` moves by delta `d`, only triangles containing `i` (C(12,2)=66 of 286) change area, and their change is computable in closed form. This 4x+ per-iteration speedup enables either more seeds, more hill-climb iterations, or a full 4-second-per-seed restart strategy — addressing the insight that Gen 8's longer 3s budget didn't help because iteration cost, not time, limited search depth.
