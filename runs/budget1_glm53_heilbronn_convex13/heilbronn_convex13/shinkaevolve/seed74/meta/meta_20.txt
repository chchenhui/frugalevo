# Analysis of Heilbronn 13-Point Program Evaluations

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- **Targeted geometric moves are the best-performing pattern**: The current best program (Gen 21, `targeted_anneal_polish13`, score 0.77) and its sibling (Gen 17, `targeted_argmin_anneal13`, score 0.76) both perturb vertices of the smallest triangles by pushing them perpendicular away from the opposite edge. This dominates non-targeted approaches (Gen 15 generic incremental annealing: 0.48; Gen 18 compass search: 0.24; Gen 14 plain annealing: 0.18).
- **Smooth top-K surrogate (mean of 12 smallest areas) for annealing, true minimum for polish**: The two-stage split — annealing on the smooth score to escape poor basins, then strict greedy polish on the true minimum — is shared by the top scorers (0.77, 0.76, and Gen 22's soft-score polish at 0.67). Accepting Metropolis moves on the surrogate (`s2 >= smooth or rng.random() < exp((s2-smooth)/T)`) provides plateau traversal the pure greedy climber (Gen 12's 0.64, Gen 13's 0.55) lacks.
- **Multi-start over diverse structured seeds + reheat cycles from global best**: The best program seeds with plain ring, alternating ring, 3-fold symmetric triangle, and perturbed grid, then runs 2 reheat cycles restarting from the best. Gen 16/19/22 with 5 seeds (hexagons, 13-gon, triangular lattice) scored 0.57–0.67, confirming seed diversity correlates with score.
- **Vectorized full rescoring of all 286 triples stays within budget**: The best program recomputes `_areas(pts)` per move (286 cross products, vectorized) across 9000+3000 iterations × 4 seeds + reheats, finishing in 5.15s — full rescoring is fast enough; incremental updates (Gen 15) saved time but not score.

## Ineffective Approaches

- **Plain/uniform annealing without targeted moves**: Gen 14 (perturbed grid + Gaussian annealing, ~4000 iterations) scored only 0.18 with min_area_normalized 0.01. Too few iterations and no geometric structure — feedback explicitly cites getting stuck in poor local optima.
- **Compass search on softmin surrogate (Gen 18, 0.24)**: Deterministic coordinate-wise local search with temperature continuation got trapped far worse than stochastic annealing — the smooth LogSumExp surrogate without targeted geometry does not find good basins.
- **Softmin gradient-based optimization (Gen 10, 0.0 — incorrect)**: Despite elaborate annealing/polish stages, the gradient ascent formulation plus dead/placeholder gradient code failed validation entirely, echoing previous Gen 8's softmin sign error. Gradient methods remain 0-for-2 on this problem.
- **Bottom-k vertex targeting risks thrashing**: Gen 19 (k=8 worst triangles) and Gen 16 (bottom-k) both score ~0.57-0.58; feedback notes improving one bad triangle creates another, and hull-area denominator sensitivity adds instability. Small k (< 12) is worse than the best program's K=12.

## Implementation Insights

- **The best program's core loop structure**: pick a random triangle among the K=12 smallest, pick a random vertex of it, and 55% of the time use the `_targeted_new` perpendicular push (plus 35% step-scaled noise for exploration) vs 45% pure Gaussian moves. This balances directed geometric improvement with diversity — a key differentiator from Gen 19's purely targeted k=8 moves.
- **Step/temperature schedules matter**: step0=0.05 decaying ×0.88 every 600 iterations (floor 0.0015), temp0=0.004 with geometric decay; polish uses step 0.01 → floor 0.0005 at ×0.9 per 300 iterations. Reheat cycles use halved temperatures. This fine-grained decay ladder outperforms Gen 14's coarse schedule.
- **Cheap degenerate-move rejection** (`dists.min() < 1e-6` check, masking the moved point with 10.0) prevents duplicate points cheaply — a small but consistent robustness detail across the top scorers.
- **Hull normalization to unit area via monotone chain + shoelace** is validated and universal (all passing programs use it); centering before scaling in the best program is safe and simple. Fixed seed 42 keeps results deterministic.

## Performance Analysis

- **Score trajectory**: Gen 10 gradient = 0.0 (incorrect); plain annealing Gen 14 = 0.18 → Gen 18 compass = 0.24 → Gen 15 incremental anneal = 0.48 → Gen 13 greedy = 0.55 → Gen 19/16 targeted = 0.57-0.58 → Gen 22 = 0.67 → Gen 17 = 0.76 → **Gen 21 = 0.77 (current best, 5.15s)**. Adding targeted geometric moves + smooth surrogate + polish/reheat produced an ~4× score gain over generic annealing.
- **min_area_normalized has plateaued at 0.02** across ALL top programs (Gens 13, 16, 17, 19, 21, 22). Score differences (0.55 → 0.77) come from other combined-score components (likely consistency/validation margin/runtime tradeoffs), not from geometric improvement — indicating local-search refinements are exhausted at this quality level.
- **Runtime vs score**: Best program uses the full ~5s budget (5.15s); Gen 17 at 5.12s scores 0.76, while faster programs (Gen 13, 1.34s = 0.55; Gen 15, 1.07s = 0.48) score lower. There is a positive runtime-score correlation up to ~5s, but runtime alone doesn't explain 0.76 vs 0.77 — surrogate choice (mean-of-12-smallest vs mean-of-12 in both) and restart bookkeeping differ slightly.
- **Feedback across all top programs converges**: longer annealing, more restarts, and known-good Heilbronn constructions/symmetry are repeatedly suggested — the local optimum barrier at 0.02 normalized area is the dominant remaining limitation, and pure local search variants have been thoroughly explored.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Add known-good Heilbronn constructions as seeds.** Replace or augment `_init_grid` with seeds specifically designed for the n=13 Heilbronn problem: points on a parabola `(t, t²)` for evenly spaced t (then scaled), a "no-three-in-line" 13-point lattice configuration, and points on an ellipse with golden-angle spacing. Insights show seed diversity correlates with score and seeding is nearly free, so adding 2–3 more structured basins before the reheat phase directly raises the starting quality of the anneal.

2. **Increase polish iterations and add a second strict polish after reheats.** The current polish uses 3000 iterations with k=6 triangles; extend to 5000 iterations per polish and vary K between the anneal (K=12) and polish (try K=8 then K=4 in a two-tier polish) so late-stage refinement pressure focuses on the truly smallest triangles without thrashing (Gen 19 showed k=8 in the *anneal* is bad, but staged K reduction in greedy polish only tightens the final squeeze). Runtime headroom exists since the 5.15s budget correlation with score is positive.

3. **Add a coordinate-axis fallback move when the targeted push is rejected.** In `_anneal` and `_polish`, when a proposed move fails the acceptance test, try the same step along ±x and ±y separately (reusing the vectorized `_areas`) before rejecting — 4 cheap extra candidates per failed move. This exploits the cheap full rescoring already in place and gives directional diversity that pure Gaussian rejection lacks, per the earlier coordinate-search insight but within the successful stochastic framework.

4. **Tune the accepted-move fraction with adaptive step control.** Track the acceptance rate over the last 200 Metropolis proposals; if it drops below ~15%, halve the step; if above ~50%, increase step by 1.5× (capped at 0.05). This replaces the fixed ×0.88/600-iteration ladder with feedback-driven cooling that keeps the anneal in the productive acceptance band longer, a refinement of the schedule structure that already differentiates the best program from Gen 14's coarse decay.

5. **Add more reheat cycles with a "best-of-cycles" pool.** Instead of 2 reheat cycles always restarting from the single global best, keep the top-3 distinct configurations found across seeds (measured by pairwise point distance > 0.1 to ensure diversity) and run one reheat from each, plus one from the global best. The insights note that local search has plateaued at min_area_normalized 0.02, so escaping requires exploring multiple good basins — a pool-based restart scheme is the cheapest structural change toward that.
