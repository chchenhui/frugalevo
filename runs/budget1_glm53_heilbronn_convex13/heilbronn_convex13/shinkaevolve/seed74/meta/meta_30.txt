# Analysis of Heilbronn 13-Point Program Evaluations (Updated)

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- **Worst-triangle-targeted moves remain the winning core, and the current best (Gen 30, `axis_fallback_moves`, 0.77) adds a cheap directional-diversity fallback**: when a random targeted move is rejected, the annealer tries the same step along ±x/±y axes before giving up. This small extension over plain targeted moves lifted Gen 30 to 0.77 (6.40s), matching the previous best (Gen 21's 0.77) and beating all sibling targeted variants (Gen 29 adaptive-step 0.68, Gen 26 smooth-score 0.69, Gen 31 staged polish 0.72, Gen 32 push moves 0.63).
- **Multi-seed + distinct-basin pool + reheat cycles is consistently validated**: Gen 30 uses 5 structured seeds (hex rings, staggered hexagons, 13-gon, jittered grid, boundary-heavy), anneals each with 2 reheat cycles, pools distinct basins, then reheats from every pool member plus global best before polish. This "pool + restart" bookkeeping (also in Gen 25, 0.57→ improved to 0.77 in Gen 30 with axis fallback) outperforms flat multi-start.
- **Smooth bottom-K surrogate for annealing + strict true-minimum polish remains a proven two-stage split** (Gen 23, 0.57; Gen 26, 0.69; Gen 28, 0.55): Metropolis acceptance on the mean of k smallest areas traverses plateaus, then greedy polish on the true min area squeezes the final configuration. Gen 30 combines this with staged polish focus (k=6→smaller triangles during polish).
- **Vectorized full rescoring of all 286 triples via precomputed index arrays is fully affordable**: Gen 30 finishes its full anneal + polish + pool-restart pipeline in 6.40s with best-in-class score; Gen 25 achieves the same architecture at 2.35s (0.57) showing the architecture scales with budget.

## Ineffective Approaches

- **Programs with sophisticated machinery but broken output produce 0.0**: Gen 7 (`normalized_objective_optimization`) and Gen 11 (`targeted_vertex_relaxation`) both scored 0.0 and failed validation — feedback points to hull-area routine degeneracies (collinear/duplicate points) and output-format/correctness issues, not optimization quality. Python-loop hull construction and per-move full recomputation made Gen 11 also computationally wasteful. Correctness checks on the final output are mandatory regardless of algorithm quality.
- **Pure local refinement cannot break the 0.02 min_area_normalized barrier**: All 10 passing programs in this batch (Gens 23, 25, 26, 28, 29, 30, 31, 32 plus predecessors) plateau at exactly 0.02. Feedback repeatedly states the search is "trapped near local optima" and that "better global structure rather than more local refinement" is needed. Score differences (0.55→0.77) come from secondary combined-score components, not geometric improvement.
- **Aggressive runtimes don't guarantee higher scores**: Gen 32 (10.98s, 0.63) and Gen 23/28 (~9.9s, 0.57/0.55) spend nearly 2× the time of Gen 30 (6.40s, 0.77) and Gen 25 (2.35s, 0.57) for worse results — the extra iterations go into already-explored basins.
- **Geometric "push-away-from-edge" variants alone underperform axis-fallback**: Gen 32's perpendicular pushes with strict staged polish (K=8→K=4) scored only 0.63; feedback notes the strict greedy polish may "over-constrain" after annealing.

- **The current best program's key implementation details** (Gen 30, 0.77): (a) targeted vertex selection from the k=8 smallest triangles via `np.argsort(areas)[:k_bot]` + `np.unique(_IDX[order].ravel())`; (b) the axis-fallback loop (`for d in range(2): for sgn in (1,-1)`) trying 4 directional variants after random-move rejection; (c) geometric-decay step/temp schedules (`temp = t0*(t1/t0)**frac`, `step = step0*(step1/step0)**frac + 0.0005`); (d) best-ever fallback copy (`best_pts`) so annealing exploration never loses the incumbent.
- **Cheap degenerate-move rejection is universal among passing programs**: `np.min(distances) < 1e-6` duplicate check with move masking (Gen 30 keeps this from Gen 21/25 lineage); the failing programs (Gen 7, 11) likely lacked robust degenerate handling in their hull routines, which return 0.0 for <3 hull points.
- **Pool distinctness via sorted-coordinate mean difference** (`np.mean(np.abs(np.sort(pts)-np.sort(qp))) < 0.1`) is a simple, effective basin-diversity mechanism; however, pool dedup keyed on `qv == val` only guards exact ties, and the pool cap of 3 limits diversity — Gen 29's feedback explicitly asks for "more diverse seeds."
- **Hull normalization via Andrew's monotone chain + shoelace, with early degeneracy guards (`len(P) < 3 → 0.0`), is validated across all passing programs**; fixed deterministic RNG seeds (42, 100+si, etc.) ensure reproducibility without score cost.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Add Heilbronn-specific structured seeds (parabola, no-three-in-line lattice, golden-angle ellipse).** The current 5 seeds are all ring/grid-based; augment `_seeds()` with points on a parabola `(t, t²)` rescaled to [0,1]², a 13-point no-three-in-line lattice subset, and golden-angle ellipse spacing. Seeding is nearly free (Gen 30 completes in 6.40s) and pool diversity is the weakest link — the pool cap of 3 with a 0.1 dedup threshold currently admits near-duplicate basins from similar geometric seeds. Also raise `max_pool` from 3 to 5 and add a parabola seed to fill the new slots.

2. **Try multi-vertex coordinated moves on the worst triangle.** The 0.02 min_area_normalized plateau across 8+ programs suggests single-vertex moves cannot fix a worst triangle whose all three vertices need simultaneous displacement. Extend `_anneal` with a secondary move type: with probability ~25%, pick the single worst triangle and move 2–3 of its vertices simultaneously (each with its own Gaussian step), using the same axis-fallback rejection structure. This is the cheapest "global/structural" change consistent with the validated vectorized rescoring pipeline.

3. **Soften the polish acceptance to avoid over-constraining.** Gen 32's strict greedy polish (0.63) underperformed, and feedback warned strict polish can over-constrain after annealing. In `_polish`, allow sideways/thermally-tolerated moves (accept if `s > best - 1e-4` with a small Metropolis term at low temperature) for the first half of iterations, then switch to strict greedy. This keeps the staged-K squeeze (k=6→smaller) from Gen 30 but avoids greedy tunnel-vision in the final steps.

4. **Budget-rebalance: reduce per-seed anneal iterations, add a third reheat round with pool-aware restarts.** Gen 30 spends 2200+2×500 per seed; since runtime past ~6s doesn't pay (Gen 32 at 10.98s scored worse), cut per-seed anneal to 1500 iterations and spend the savings on a final "round-robin reheat": iterate 3 passes over the full pool where each reheat starts from a pool member perturbed toward another member's configuration (crossover-style interpolation `0.7*p_i + 0.3*p_j`). This basin-mixing is a structural escape attempt that plain restarts lack.

5. **Add an adaptive acceptance-rate step controller in `_anneal` and use it in the axis-fallback too.** Replace the fixed geometric step decay with feedback control: track the acceptance rate (including axis-fallback acceptances) over the last 200 proposals; below 15% halve the step, above 50% multiply by 1.5 (capped at 0.05). Gen 29's adaptive step scored 0.68 in isolation, so combining it with Gen 30's axis-fallback (the 0.77 differentiator) tests whether the two gains compose — keeping the step in the productive band should let the fallback directions fire more often.
