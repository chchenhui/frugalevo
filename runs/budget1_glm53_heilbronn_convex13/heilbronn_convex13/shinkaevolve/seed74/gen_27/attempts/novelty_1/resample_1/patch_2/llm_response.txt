The previous attempt failed, likely because the fixed cooling ladder (step *= 0.88 on a fixed 600-iteration schedule) either cooled too fast (locking the anneal into a poor local basin early) or wasted iterations at overly large steps late in the run. This edit replaces the fixed decay ladder with feedback-driven adaptive step control: we track the Metropolis acceptance rate over a sliding window of the last 200 proposals. If the acceptance rate falls below 15%, the step size is halved (the proposals are too aggressive for the current energy landscape); if it rises above 50%, the step is increased by 1.5× (capped at 0.05, the original maximum step), since the search is under-exploring. This keeps the anneal inside the productive acceptance band for a much larger fraction of the run, which is precisely the regime where min-triangle-area improvements are found. The change is self-contained, deterministic (no randomness introduced beyond the existing seeded RNG), and preserves the existing iteration budget and temperature schedule, so runtime and determinism guarantees are unaffected.
