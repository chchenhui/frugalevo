# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- **Generation 39’s active-constraint barrier flow is the new best-quality result:** it achieved **0.83 combined score** with normalized minimum area **0.03** in **7.56 s**, exceeding Generation 36’s prior leader of **0.82** at the same minimum area. Its successful combination is an 80-layout soft-min determinant-gradient population flow, lower-tail ranking, annealed stochastic acceptance, weak-basin restarts, and a final 700-step active-set perturbation polish.

- **Soft-min continuation remains the strongest core optimization mechanism.** Like Generation 36 (**0.82, 5.94 s**) and Generation 40 (**0.81, 3.23 s**), Generation 39 assigns exponentially concentrated weights to low-area triples rather than optimizing only the current single worst triple. Its temperature schedule, from `0.030` toward `0.001`, initially balances a broad lower tail and later focuses on active constraints. This continues to outperform the evolutionary-only methods: Generation 43 reached **0.79** in **22.86 s**, Generation 42 reached **0.74** in **21.37 s**, and Generation 41 reached **0.67** in **27.24 s**.

- **The current best’s explicit lower-tail barrier score appears to improve basin selection.** Generation 39 ranks layouts as `minimum + 0.020 * mean(lowest 12 areas)`, then permits annealed moves based on this ranking even when the strict minimum does not immediately improve. This is consistent with the previous evidence that low-tail objectives help cross the common `0.02` plateau, while still retaining exact-maximin improvements unconditionally.

- **Directed flow plus controlled diversity is more effective than diversity operators alone.** Generation 39 combines structured triangular seeds, broad Dirichlet layouts, side-biased restart layouts, noise, sparse large perturbations, and periodic replacement of only the ten weakest basins. This preserves strong independent layouts while injecting exploration. The result improves slightly on Generation 36’s mixed structured/random seeded flow and substantially surpasses the more elaborate evolutionary diversity systems in Generations 41–43.

## Ineffective Approaches

- **Long evolutionary schedules and complex crossover pipelines remained inefficient relative to soft-min flow.** Generation 41’s diversity-aware evolutionary archive required **27.24 s** for only **0.67** and minimum area **0.02**. Generation 43 improved to **0.79 / 0.03**, but still took **22.86 s**—about three times Generation 39’s runtime for a lower score. Population evolution, mutation, crossover, and elite selection did not deliver as much useful movement per full all-triple evaluation as analytic gradient flow.

- **Exhaustive or enumerative local polishing did not justify its cost.** Generation 42 used coordinate-wise maximin refinement by enumerating feasible small-LP vertices after a 3,600-generation search, yet reached only **0.74** in **21.37 s**. Prior related LP-polished programs likewise stayed around **0.74** while requiring roughly 17–19 seconds. Generation 39 instead uses a relatively inexpensive stochastic active-set polish after its flow phase and reaches **0.83**, showing that active refinement is most effective when it follows a strong soft-min basin search rather than expensive enumerative coordinate optimization.

- **Active-triple targeting is not sufficient as the main search signal.** Earlier results showed active-constraint repair methods could reach **0.69–0.73** or sometimes remain at **0.02** minimum area. Generation 39 succeeds because active-set logic is only its final phase: the first 2,900 iterations use all 165 triples through the soft-min gradient. This supports the earlier finding that active constraints are useful for late refinement but less reliable as a replacement for a smooth global lower-tail objective.

## Implementation Insights

- **Generation 39 evaluates all `C(11,3) = 165` determinant constraints in batched NumPy form for all 80 layouts.** It computes signed determinants and absolute areas together, then uses the sign of each determinant to convert the derivative of absolute area into an analytic ascent direction. This avoids generic numerical optimization overhead and gives every point updates informed by all relevant triples, not only a sampled or currently worst constraint.

- **The gradient accumulation is tailored to the point-triple incidence structure.** The program precomputes flattened per-layout indices (`ia`, `ib`, `ic`) and uses `np.add.at` to accumulate exact determinant derivatives into each point. Fixed triangle corners are then zeroed from the gradient, while movable coordinates are projected back into the simplex. This preserves validity throughout optimization and retains the successful fixed-corner/simplex formulation shared by Generations 36, 40, and the stronger evolutionary programs.

- **Per-layout gradient normalization stabilizes a shared update schedule across heterogeneous basins.** Generation 39 normalizes each layout’s full movable-point gradient by its RMS-like magnitude before applying a decaying flow step. Layouts with many nearly tied low-area triples therefore do not take disproportionate steps merely because more constraints are simultaneously active. This is a concrete continuation of Generation 36’s stable normalized soft-min flow design.

- **The final polish is computationally focused rather than globally exhaustive.** For 700 iterations, Generation 39 selects the 14 lowest-area triples early and 8 later, identifies their movable incident points, and perturbs mostly those points in batches of 56 candidates. Because it accepts only strict maximin improvements, this phase cannot regress the saved incumbent. This targeted post-flow step is a plausible contributor to its **0.01 score improvement** over Generation 36, while avoiding the high runtime of Generation 42’s enumerative LP polish.

## Performance Analysis

- **Generation 39 is the current quality leader, but not the runtime leader.** Its **0.83** score is the best observed, improving by **0.01** over Generation 36 (**0.82, 5.94 s**) and by **0.02** over Generation 40 (**0.81, 3.23 s**). However, it takes about **27% longer than Generation 36** and more than **twice as long as Generation 40**. The additional 80-layout population size, 2,900 flow iterations, periodic restarts, and 700-step active polish appear to trade runtime for the small final quality gain.

- **The meaningful performance threshold remains reaching normalized minimum area `0.03`.** Generation 39, 36, 40, 43, and 42 all reached `0.03`, with combined scores ranging from **0.74 to 0.83**. In contrast, Generation 41 reached only `0.02` and scored **0.67**, consistent with earlier `0.02` methods scoring markedly below the best soft-min approaches. Reaching `0.03` is necessary for strong scores, but the soft-min population methods distinguish themselves within that group.

- **Among the `0.03` programs, analytic soft-min flow dominates score/runtime efficiency.** Generation 39 achieves **0.83 in 7.56 s**; Generation 36 achieves **0.82 in 5.94 s**; and Generation 40 achieves **0.81 in 3.23 s**. The evolutionary alternatives require much more time for lower scores: Generation 43, **0.79 in 22.86 s**, and Generation 42, **0.74 in 21.37 s**. The evaluation data consistently favors batched all-triple gradient information over extended genetic search and expensive local combinatorial refinement.

- **The current best validates a refined version of the prior winning pattern rather than a fundamentally different strategy.** Generation 39 retains the previously successful fixed corners, simplex projection, structured-plus-random initialization, soft-min annealing, and weak-layout rejuvenation from Generations 36 and 40. Its measured improvement to **0.83** coincides with stronger active-constraint final polishing, side-biased restart diversity, lower-tail acceptance, and sparse basin-changing perturbations—incremental additions that preserved the core soft-min flow advantage.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Replace the single monotone temperature decay with a three-stage soft-min schedule.** Retain the current early decay from `0.030`, but hold temperature around `0.003–0.004` for roughly iterations `1300–2200`, then descend toward `0.0005–0.0010` in the final flow segment. This extends the successful soft-min continuation mechanism while keeping several near-active triples influential longer, which may avoid locking into a `0.03` basin too early.

2. **Add an elite-only projected multi-scale deterministic step test late in the flow.** After approximately iteration 2200, for the top 8–12 layouts, evaluate projected moves along the normalized soft-min gradient at scales such as `0.4x`, `0.8x`, `1.2x`, and `1.6x` of `flow_step`, optionally without noise, and retain the candidate with the best lower-tail ranking subject to no maximin loss. The existing annealed update is effective globally, but this inexpensive batched line-search variant could make late movement more reliable around the best basins.

3. **Make restart selection explicitly geometry-diverse rather than restarting only the ten lowest minima.** Preserve the strongest layouts, but choose restart sources from several mutually distant elite layouts using RMS distance over movable barycentric coordinates, then blend each random restart with one selected source rather than always with the single global incumbent. Generation 39 already benefits from controlled basin rejuvenation; distributing restarts across distinct good configurations should reduce collapse around one local arrangement and broaden the chance of finding a better active-constraint pattern.

4. **Upgrade the final polish from isotropic perturbations to active-set gradient-guided candidate batches.** For the selected 8–14 lowest triples, compute an analytic gradient using equal or inverse-area active weights, generate candidates at positive gradient scales plus small orthogonal/random perturbations, and continue accepting only strict true-minimum improvements. This preserves the successful inexpensive active-set finishing phase while directing more of its 56 trials toward repairs that simultaneously raise the current limiting triangle family.

5. **Introduce a small “constraint-pattern shake” for stagnated promising layouts during the middle phase.** If an elite layout has not improved its strict minimum for, for example, 180–250 iterations, perturb only movable points with the highest incidence among its lowest 16–24 triples, using a moderate correlated displacement and then resume soft-min flow. Unlike broad random restarts or evolutionary mutation, this targets the known active-constraint plateau mechanism while retaining the high-quality basin that the all-triple flow has already discovered.
