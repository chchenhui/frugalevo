# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- **The current best, Generation 22’s Annealed Evolutionary Heilbronn Triangle Search, establishes the strongest observed combination of max-min quality and total score.** It reached `min_area_normalized = 0.03` and a combined score of **0.81** in **14.52 s**, surpassing the prior reported leader, Generation 3 (**0.03**, **0.69**, 11.28 s). Its key successful pattern is a population search that keeps the enclosing vertices fixed, evolves only eight barycentric points, preserves elites, and gradually transitions from a tail-aware objective to true max-min optimization.

- **Tail-guided fitness shaping is supported by both the current best and several earlier successful evolutionary searches.** Generation 22 uses an annealed score based on the minimum area plus a low-area-tail reward, then reduces that tail emphasis late in the run. This improved materially over simpler elite evolutionary methods: Generation 20 reached **0.67 / 0.02**, Generation 28 reached **0.59 / 0.02**, and Generation 30 reached **0.66 / 0.02**. The result indicates that preserving information about several nearly active triples, rather than optimizing only the single current minimum from the outset, helped Generation 22 escape the common 0.02 plateau.

- **Elite preservation plus sparse, annealed pointwise mutation remains the most consistently effective search mechanism.** In the current-best code, the top 16 of 128 layouts are copied exactly each generation; all candidate parents are selected from those elites; and only selected movable points receive Gaussian noise. This continues the strong pattern identified in the earlier Generation 3 elite search and outperformed the evaluated DE-heavy alternatives. Generation 25’s related evolutionary active-set approach also achieved **0.73 / 0.03** in only **6.53 s**, reinforcing the value of evolutionary retention and targeted refinement.

- **Hybrid global movement early and local constraint-oriented refinement late can improve quality when embedded in an elite evolutionary loop.** Generation 22 applies differential-style correlated moves only during the early 58% of generations, while its Gaussian mutation shrinks toward local polishing. This is materially more successful than standalone DE approaches: Generations 21 and 26 both achieved only **0.02** normalized area with scores **0.46** and **0.49**, respectively. The evidence suggests DE-like moves were useful in Generation 22 as a temporary diversification mechanism, not as the dominant selection dynamic.

## Ineffective Approaches

- **Differential-evolution-centered searches remained below the best evolutionary approaches despite vectorization, refreshes, and polishing.** Generation 21 used staged soft-min DE, simplex folding, incumbent refreshes, and multiscale polishing but achieved only **0.46 / 0.02** in **12.47 s**. Generation 26 similarly combined current-to-pbest DE, elite blending, refreshes, and coordinate-direction polish, reaching **0.49 / 0.02** in **4.79 s**. These results extend the previous observation that DE variants repeatedly settle at the 0.02 minimum-area level.

- **Explicit side-point parameterizations can be geometrically fragile under an all-triples minimum-area objective.** Generation 23 fixed three side points in addition to the enclosing corners and received **0.00** for both normalized area and combined score despite passing validation. The evaluator found no measurable positive objective performance, consistent with a layout containing an effectively degenerate or nearly collinear triple. This matches the earlier evidence that boundary-constrained constructions can create fatal low-area triples even when all points remain feasible.

- **Additional complexity alone did not improve results.** Generation 20 combined elite recombination, an eight-smallest-area surrogate, exact min-area selection, and batched single-point local polish, yet reached **0.67 / 0.02** in **12.45 s**. Generation 28 used elite crossover, annealed mutation, and a temporary tail bonus but reached only **0.59 / 0.02** in **7.92 s**. By contrast, Generation 22’s particular schedule of tail annealing, early correlated moves, and exact elite retention was sufficient to reach 0.03.

- **Population resizing must preserve indexing consistency; otherwise, algorithmic quality is irrelevant.** Generation 27 failed before validation and scored **0.00** because Phase 2 replaced a size-192 population with a size-128 array but continued indexing with rankings computed for the original population. Indices such as 155 were then out of bounds. This is a complete execution failure rather than an optimization-quality limitation.

## Implementation Insights

- **Generation 22’s implementation efficiently evaluates the full geometric objective while using the low-area tail without full sorting.** It precomputes all `C(11,3) = 165` triples, evaluates determinant magnitudes in batch for the whole population, and uses `np.partition(areas, 7, axis=1)[:, :8]` to obtain the eight smallest constraints. Its quality function is explicitly `smallest_area + tail_weight * mean(eight_smallest_areas)`, while the final incumbent is tracked using the exact minimum-area objective. This separates smoother search ranking from the benchmark-relevant selection criterion.

- **The current-best mutation schedule preserves structures late rather than continually disrupting them.** Its Gaussian scale decays from approximately `0.07065` to `0.00065`, and the probability of mutating an individual movable point drops from `0.38` to `0.13`. Mutation is masked per point using an array shaped `(population_size, n - 3, 1)`, so a changed point receives a correlated two-coordinate displacement while unaffected points retain their position. This is consistent with the prior successful sparse-mutation pattern from Generation 3.

- **Exact elite copying and independent incumbent tracking protect high-quality layouts from both changing surrogate scores and stochastic operators.** Generation 22 ranks the population by its annealed tail-aware quality, copies the top 16 configurations, restores them unchanged into `candidates[:elite_count]`, and separately updates `best` only when an elite has a higher exact minimum area. This is particularly important because the tail-weighted score is deliberately not identical to the final objective during most of the run.

- **Barycentric simplex projection remains a central correctness and robustness mechanism.** The program reconstructs the three weights `(1-u-v, u, v)`, clamps each to at least `1e-10`, renormalizes them, and returns the last two coordinates. This guarantees all evolved points remain within the enclosing triangle after Gaussian or differential mutations. Every strong valid program in the results—including Generations 20, 22, 25, 26, 28, and 30—uses the fixed-corner/simplex-constrained representation, whereas Generation 23 demonstrates that feasibility alone does not prevent poor geometric spacing.

## Performance Analysis

- **Generation 22 is now the clear quality leader in the supplied results.** Its **0.81** combined score is higher than Generation 25’s **0.73**, Generation 20’s **0.67**, Generation 30’s **0.66**, Generation 28’s **0.59**, and the prior global-best Generation 3’s **0.69**. It also shares the top reported normalized minimum area, **0.03**, with Generation 25 and the earlier Generation 3, while materially exceeding both in combined score.

- **The key observed quality threshold is still normalized minimum area 0.03 versus the much more common 0.02 plateau.** Generation 22 reached **0.03**, whereas Generations 20, 21, 26, 28, and 30 all stopped at **0.02** despite being valid and deterministic. The 50% increase from 0.02 to 0.03 is the most consequential performance separation in these reports and aligns with the current best’s more carefully staged evolutionary search.

- **Generation 25 is the strongest efficiency competitor but not the overall leader.** It attained **0.73 / 0.03** in **6.53 s**, less than half of Generation 22’s **14.52 s** runtime. However, the current best gained **0.08 combined-score points** for that additional search budget. This shows that runtime alone does not determine outcome: Generation 21 used nearly the same time as Generation 22 (**12.47 s** versus **14.52 s**) but achieved only **0.46 / 0.02**.

- **The new results reinforce that search dynamics matter more than raw generation count, vectorization, or local polish.** Generation 20 used exact polishing and ran **12.45 s** for **0.67**, while Generation 28 ran 6,500 generations for **0.59**. DE variants with vectorized all-triple evaluation were faster in some cases—Generation 26 took **4.79 s**—but did not convert that efficiency into higher minimum area. The strongest performance is associated with the specific elite-retention, annealed tail-to-maximin, sparse-mutation strategy embodied by Generation 22 rather than with a particular runtime or operator in isolation.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Add a dedicated late active-set offspring class that targets points in the limiting triples.** During the final 25–35% of generations, compute the 8–12 smallest-area triples for selected elite parents, identify movable points appearing most often, and mutate only those points with a smaller sigma (for example, `0.25–0.5 * sigma`). Keep the existing unbiased sparse Gaussian offspring for most candidates, but reserve perhaps 20–30% for this targeted class so the algorithm directly attacks the constraints preventing improvement beyond the 0.03 threshold.

2. **Replace part of the early random-parent differential moves with elite-relative differential proposals.** Instead of drawing all three DE donors from the full population, use an elite base parent plus two distinct donors, e.g. `elite_base + F * (donor_b - donor_c)`, or use a current-to-elite move such as `parent + a*(elite_other-parent) + F*(b-c)`. Generation 22 indicates correlated motion is useful only as early diversification, while DE-centered searches failed; anchoring those moves in the strong elite pool should retain useful large-scale coordinated relocation without repeatedly exploring poor basins.

3. **Use a staged population schedule with a broad discovery phase followed by the proven 128/16 refinement regime.** Start with 192–256 configurations for an initial 20–30% of the compute budget, using a slightly higher point mutation rate and stronger tail weight, then retain the best diverse 128 layouts and run the current exact-elite annealed search unchanged or nearly unchanged. This builds on the best program’s reliable survivor mechanism while increasing the chance of locating multiple distinct 0.03-quality geometric families before late local convergence.

4. **Introduce diversity-aware elite selection rather than selecting all 16 survivors solely by tail-guided score.** Keep the top 8–12 layouts exactly by quality, but fill the remaining elite slots from high-scoring candidates that are sufficiently distant from already retained layouts in barycentric-coordinate space, optionally after allowing point-index alignment through sorted coordinate signatures. The current best’s elite copying is essential, but avoiding 16 near-identical elites can preserve alternate arrangements whose active triples differ and may provide better recombination or escape paths.

5. **Add conservative pointwise elite recombination before sparse mutation, especially in the middle search phase.** For roughly 15–25% of non-elite candidates, construct each of the eight movable points by choosing that point from one of two high-quality elite parents, or by using a convex blend of their barycentric coordinates, then apply simplex projection and ordinary annealed mutation. This preserves the successful elite-only ancestry pattern while allowing complementary point placements from separate high-quality layouts to combine, unlike the less effective full-layout crossover strategies seen in weaker evolutionary variants.
