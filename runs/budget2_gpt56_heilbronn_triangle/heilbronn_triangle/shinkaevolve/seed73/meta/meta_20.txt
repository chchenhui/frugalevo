# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- **The current best remains the elite-preserving evolutionary search (Generation 3), and the new results reinforce that its design is stronger than the newer DE variants.** It is still the only reported valid program with `min_area_normalized = 0.03`, reaching combined score **0.69** in **11.28 s**. Its effective combination is: a 128-layout population, exact preservation of the top 16 layouts, parent selection exclusively from those elites, and 6,500 generations of progressively smaller sparse Gaussian mutations. Generations 14 and 18 used related evolutionary schedules but reached only **0.60** at 12.46 s and **0.60** at 8.54 s, respectively, both with minimum area 0.02.

- **True max-min scoring with a small low-area-tail tie-break is the most successful observed fitness shaping.** Generation 3 scores each layout as its minimum doubled determinant plus `0.035 * mean(eight smallest areas)`. This preserves the actual bottleneck as the dominant term while helping distinguish layouts with identical current minima. Generation 14 similarly included a low-tail bonus and obtained a viable **0.60**, while Generation 17’s island search with tail-weighted early fitness reached **0.67** in only **4.23 s**. These results support tail information as useful for maintaining progress when several near-degenerate triples compete to become active.

- **Barycentric/simplex coordinates with fixed enclosing vertices continue to be the most reliable feasible search representation.** Generation 3 fixes reference-simplex points `(0,0)`, `(1,0)`, and `(0,1)` and mutates only eight points. Generations 10–12, 14–15, 17–18 also passed validation using the same broad pattern. The new best among those additions, Generation 17, achieved **0.67**, confirming that fixed corners plus simplex-constrained movable points remain a robust foundation.

- **Multiple boundary hypotheses can improve efficiency without exceeding the best elite method.** Generation 17 explored islands with 0, 3, and 6 constrained boundary points, obtaining **0.67** in **4.23 s**—nearly Generation 3’s 0.69 score in substantially less time. Its active-set island structure was therefore more effective than the newly evaluated conventional DE searches, though it still did not reproduce the best program’s 0.03 minimum area.

## Ineffective Approaches

- **The newer differential-evolution variants consistently plateaued at normalized minimum area 0.02.** Generations 11, 12, and 15 all used fixed corners, vectorized all-triple scoring, barycentric feasibility, and DE-style population operations, but scored only **0.53**, **0.50**, and **0.57**, respectively. Generation 10’s batched soft-min/Adam method also reached only **0.63** with 0.02. Thus, vectorization and feasibility alone did not overcome the apparent 0.02 local-optimum barrier; none matched Generation 3’s 0.03 bottleneck.

- **More search effort or added global/local complexity did not guarantee a better layout.** Generation 14 ran 6,500 generations and took **12.46 s**, longer than the current best’s 11.28 s, yet scored **0.60**. Generation 15 used current-to-pbest DE, incumbent refreshes, and coordinate-direction polish over 3,600 generations, taking **8.49 s** for **0.57**. These outcomes contrast with Generation 3’s comparatively simple elite-parent Gaussian mutation loop and show that the specific survivor/parent dynamics matter more than adding DE crossover, coordinate polling, or population refresh mechanisms.

- **Boundary-aware constructions can still fail geometrically if they create near-collinear triples.** Generation 13 combined fixed vertices, explicit side points, interior barycentric points, soft-min DE, and stochastic hill refinement, but scored **0.00** after validation because the resulting minimum area was effectively zero. Explicitly placing points on sides appears risky for this objective: the evaluator’s all-triples criterion can be destroyed by one collinear or nearly collinear set even when every point is inside the container.

- **Execution defects remain complete failures regardless of the underlying optimization design.** Generation 16 scored **0.00** because an array-valued `evaluate(...)` result was assigned to scalar entries during incumbent injection, causing “setting an array element with a sequence.” Generation 19 likewise scored **0.00** from an unclosed parenthesis syntax error. Unlike valid but moderate methods, these strategies produced no evaluable configuration at all.

## Implementation Insights

- **Generation 3’s exact elite handling is a concrete differentiator.** At each generation it ranks all 128 members, copies the top 16 configurations, samples every parent from that elite group, and then restores those 16 elite layouts unchanged via `candidates[:elite_count] = elites`. The globally best layout is also copied separately before later mutations. This guarantees that neither stochastic mutation nor a changing tail-aware ranking can erase high-quality designs. The strong **0.69 / 0.03** result is the clearest evidence for this implementation pattern.

- **Its mutation schedule is both sparse and annealed, preserving useful geometric structure late in the search.** In Generation 3, Gaussian standard deviation declines from about `0.1062` to `0.0012`, while each movable point’s mutation probability declines from `0.32` to `0.14`. Importantly, noise is applied per point rather than indiscriminately to all coordinates of every layout. This makes early rearrangements broad enough to search layout families while late updates can alter only a subset of points and retain the geometry that supports the best minimum-area constraints.

- **The best program evaluates the actual geometric objective efficiently and consistently.** It precomputes all 165 triples, gathers `configurations[:, triples]`, calculates absolute two-dimensional determinants in batch, and directly uses the smallest area in the fitness. The `np.partition(..., 7, axis=1)[:, :8]` operation obtains the eight low-area constraints without fully sorting all 165 areas. This supports 128 populations over 6,500 generations within the measured **11.28 s**.

- **Robust simplex projection is a recurring correctness requirement.** Generation 3 reconstructs all three barycentric weights, clamps them to at least `1e-10`, renormalizes them, and returns the two free weights. This creates positive interior/boundary slack and ensures `u >= 0`, `v >= 0`, and `u + v <= 1` even after mutations. The successful newer programs similarly use barycentric decoding or simplex projection; conversely, the broader prior evaluation showed that internal feasibility logic without numerically safe output conversion could still cause zero-score boundary failures.

## Performance Analysis

- **Generation 3 remains the quality leader after the newly evaluated programs.** Its combined score **0.69** and normalized minimum area **0.03** exceed Generation 17’s **0.67 / 0.02**, Generation 10’s **0.63 / 0.02**, Generation 14 and 18’s **0.60 / 0.02**, and Generation 15’s **0.57 / 0.02**. The central quality separation remains the actual bottleneck: 0.03 is 50% larger than the 0.02 reported by every other nontrivial valid program in the supplied results.

- **Generation 17 is the closest new competitor in combined score, but not in minimum-area quality.** Its active-set island search scored **0.67** in **4.23 s**, roughly 2.7 times faster than Generation 3. However, it still evaluated at 0.02 normalized minimum area. This suggests its score is competitive through other combined-score components or a favorable feasible arrangement, but it did not achieve the same maximin-area threshold as the current best.

- **Runtime has only a weak relationship with result quality.** Generation 12 finished in **3.79 s** for **0.50**, Generation 17 used **4.23 s** for **0.67**, Generation 10 used **4.64 s** for **0.63**, and Generation 14 consumed **12.46 s** for only **0.60**. Generation 3’s substantial 11.28-second budget was productive, but the comparison with Generation 14 shows that its score cannot be explained by generation count or runtime alone.

- **The new DE-family results strengthen the observed evolutionary-versus-DE distinction.** Generation 11’s transformed-barycentric DE plus Gaussian refinement scored **0.53** in 5.80 s; Generation 12’s DE plus coordinate polling scored **0.50** in 3.79 s; and Generation 15’s current-to-pbest DE plus multiscale polish scored **0.57** in 8.49 s. All were correct and feasible, yet all remained below the elite evolutionary methods—especially Generation 3—indicating that, in these evaluated implementations, sustained elite preservation and annealed sparse local mutation were more effective than differential recombination and subsequent coordinate polishing.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Use a two-phase or three-phase elite-evolution schedule while retaining the exact 128/16 survivor mechanism.** Start with a larger population (for example, 192–256 layouts) and higher point-mutation probability for broad basin discovery, then seed a 128-layout refinement phase from the strongest unique layouts and run the current low-sigma sparse-mutation schedule. This extends Generation 3’s proven elite-only parent selection and exact elite copying, while separating diversity creation from final max-min polishing.

2. **Anneal the tail bonus toward the true max-min objective rather than keeping `0.035` fixed.** For example, use `min_area + w(generation) * mean(lowest_8)` with `w` beginning near `0.06–0.08`, decreasing to `0.01–0.03` during the final 30–40% of generations. The low-tail term was valuable in Generation 3 and the fast Generation 17 search, but reducing it late should make final survivor selection more directly optimize the 0.03 bottleneck rather than merely improving already non-active low-area triples.

3. **Add active-constraint-aware local offspring alongside the existing unbiased sparse mutations.** For a fraction of non-elite candidates late in the run, identify the 4–10 smallest-area triples, collect the movable points occurring in those triples, and increase mutation probability only for those points; use a smaller sigma than the ordinary exploratory offspring and retain simplex projection. This focuses search effort on the small set of triangles limiting the minimum determinant, without replacing the successful global elite-parent Gaussian mechanism.

4. **Introduce a small number of boundary-structure islands, but keep all movable points numerically inside the simplex rather than explicitly collinear on sides.** Inspired by Generation 17’s successful island diversity, maintain separate subpopulations initialized with different soft priors—fully interior, near-edge, and mixed—but represent all points through the same positive barycentric projection used by Generation 3. Periodically migrate only the best few layouts into the main elite population; this explores multiple geometric families while avoiding Generation 13’s failure mode from exact side-point collinearities.

5. **Add conservative elite recombination and diversity filtering before Gaussian mutation.** For perhaps 20–30% of non-elite offspring, construct each movable point from either a random elite parent’s barycentric pair or a convex combination of two elite parents, then apply the ordinary sparse annealed perturbation; reject duplicate or near-duplicate offspring in favor of another elite-parent draw. Generation 3 already demonstrates that elite-only ancestry is beneficial, and recombination may combine complementary high-quality spacing patterns while diversity filtering prevents the population from collapsing prematurely around one 0.02-area basin.
