# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- **The current best, Generation 36’s Deterministic Population Soft-Min Heilbronn Optimizer, provides the strongest observed quality/runtime combination:** `min_area_normalized = 0.03`, combined score **0.82**, in **5.94 s**. Its main differentiator is population-wide *annealed soft-min gradient ascent* over all 165 triples rather than evolutionary proposals alone. Early temperatures (`0.018` down to `0.00065`) distribute gradient weight across the lower-area tail, then progressively concentrate on the true limiting triangles. This preserves the previously successful tail-to-maximin continuation pattern while replacing expensive generational crossover with directed updates.

- **Soft-min continuation is now better supported than purely evolutionary tail ranking.** Generation 40, a closely related 72-member soft-min population optimizer with dense/random mixed initialization, adaptive gradient updates, annealed kicks, and elite rebuilding, reached **0.81** at **3.23 s**. This nearly matches Generation 36’s best score while taking roughly **46% less time**. Together, these results show that smoothing the all-triples maximin objective during early search is highly effective at escaping the common `0.02` minimum-area plateau.

- **Population diversity mechanisms remain valuable when paired with directed local motion.** Generation 36 seeds half its 72-member population from perturbed near-lattice layouts and half from unrelated random simplex layouts, then periodically replaces 14 weak layouts with noisy copies from ten elite basins. This combination produced **0.82**, exceeding the previous evolutionary leader Generation 22 (**0.81**, `0.03`, **14.52 s**) and the new hybrid evolutionary alternatives, such as Generation 37 (**0.73**, `0.03`, **10.56 s**) and Generation 31 (**0.72**, `0.03`, **6.11 s**).

- **Fixed enclosing corners plus simplex-constrained movable points remain a consistent foundation for strong valid results.** Generation 36 fixes the three reference-simplex vertices and updates only the other eight points under projection. This matches the strong prior designs, including Generation 22 and Generation 25, and avoids feasibility or affine-drift issues while still allowing use of the full triangle domain.

## Ineffective Approaches

- **More elaborate evolutionary-plus-LP polishing did not outperform the soft-min optimizer and imposed substantial runtime cost.** Generation 24 achieved **0.74** with `0.03` minimum area but required **16.83 s**, while Generation 38 reached the same **0.74 / 0.03** in **18.78 s**. Both used exhaustive coordinate-wise LP refinement through feasible 3-plane intersection enumeration. Their valid results confirm that exact local refinement works, but the evaluated score data shows it did not surpass Generation 36’s **0.82** despite taking about three times as long.

- **Boundary-heavy or constrained-island search variants were not reliably beneficial.** Generation 33’s near-edge/boundary-island initialization achieved only **0.65** and fell to `0.02` minimum area in **6.84 s**. Generation 34’s separate islands with 0, 3, or 6 constrained boundary points likewise reached just **0.47 / 0.02** in **4.84 s**. These results reinforce the earlier finding that explicit boundary structure can create or preserve low-area triples under an all-triples objective.

- **Active-triangle targeting alone was insufficient to cross the `0.02` plateau consistently.** Generation 32 used differential evolution, sparse mutation, immigrants, elitism, and active-constraint batched refinement, but reached **0.69 / 0.03** in **7.64 s**; Generation 35 used vectorized scoring, active weakest-triangle repairs, crossover, and local polish yet reached only **0.62 / 0.02** in **2.61 s**. Generation 37 ran **10.56 s** for **0.73**. In contrast, Generation 36’s 3,200 directed gradient iterations reached **0.82**, indicating that its smoother population objective and adaptive update dynamics mattered more than a large generation budget or more proposal types.

## Implementation Insights

- **Generation 36 efficiently evaluates the full constraint set in batch and derives a differentiable search signal from it.** It precomputes all `C(11,3) = 165` triples, computes signed determinants for all 72 layouts simultaneously, then applies weights
  `exp(-(area - minimum_area) / temperature)`.
  This is important because the gradient receives contributions from multiple nearly limiting triangles instead of only one unstable argmin constraint. The annealed weighting directly operationalizes the successful lower-tail principle previously seen in Generation 22’s tail-aware fitness ranking.

- **Its gradient implementation is tailored to the actual geometry rather than relying on generic optimization infrastructure.** For each movable point, Generation 36 accumulates exact determinant derivatives according to whether that point is the first, second, or third member of each triple. It then normalizes each layout’s gradient by its RMS magnitude. Per-layout normalization prevents configurations with many near-tied constraints from taking disproportionately scaled steps, helping a shared step schedule work across diverse population members.

- **Adaptive momentum and second-moment scaling make the soft-min updates more stable across continuation stages.** The program uses `velocity = 0.86 * velocity + 0.14 * grad` and a slowly updated squared-gradient accumulator with coefficient `0.985`, followed by coordinatewise scaling. Combined with a learning rate declining from approximately `0.021` to `0.001`, this gives broad early movement and restrained late movement without requiring expensive coordinate LP enumeration. This is consistent with its better score than the LP-polished evolutionary programs despite much lower runtime.

- **Diversity preservation is explicit and implementation-level, not merely a consequence of random mutation.** Generation 36 combines a perturbed structured lattice seed with random simplex layouts, applies stochastic kicks only during the first 2,100 of 3,200 iterations, and periodically rejuvenates weak layouts from multiple elite sources. This is more robust than allowing all members to follow the same deterministic gradient basin. Generation 40’s related elite-recombination/restart implementation reaching **0.81** further supports that elite basin rebuilding is a productive coding pattern.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Test a two-phase temperature schedule with a longer intermediate soft-min plateau.** Keep the current broad early temperature, but insert a middle phase—for example, hold `temperature` near `0.0025–0.004` for steps 1,400–2,400 before descending to `0.0004–0.0007`. This should preserve gradient contributions from several near-limiting triangles longer, reducing premature commitment to one active triangle while retaining the successful late maximin-like refinement.

2. **Add a late active-set correction pass to a small subset of top layouts rather than using LP polishing globally.** In the final 15–20% of iterations, identify the 8–16 smallest triangles for the best 8–12 population members, accumulate gradients only from those triples, and apply a smaller projected update to points occurring most frequently in that active set. This extends the current all-triple soft-min method with focused limiting-constraint repair, while avoiding the high runtime and weaker score outcomes of exhaustive coordinate-wise LP refinement.

3. **Make elite rejuvenation diversity-aware using geometric distance, not only current minimum area.** Continue retaining the strongest few layouts by minimum area, but select the ten restart sources greedily so each selected source is sufficiently distant in its movable-point barycentric coordinates from already chosen sources. Generation 36 and 40 show that rebuilding weak members from elite basins is valuable; ensuring those basins are genuinely distinct should reduce population collapse and increase the chance that one basin breaks beyond the current `0.03` threshold.

4. **Use a small number of deterministic candidate step scales and accept only improving projected updates for elite members late in the run.** For the top approximately 12 layouts after step 2,300, evaluate projected moves along the current momentum/soft-min direction at scales such as `0.5x`, `1.0x`, and `1.5x` of the nominal rate, then keep the candidate with the best true minimum area or lower-tail score. Batched area evaluation is already efficient, and this adds a lightweight line-search-like mechanism that can prevent late annealed updates from degrading a promising maximin configuration.

5. **Seed a minority of the initial population from transformed elite-quality structured layouts rather than only lattice perturbations and uniform random layouts.** Build several asymmetric templates by taking the near-lattice base, applying independent interior contractions/expansions to selected movable points, and optionally using pointwise blends of two differently perturbed lattice layouts before projection; reserve roughly 20–30% of the 72 slots for these seeds. This retains the successful dense-layout prior while broadening the structured basin coverage, complementing the already effective random half-population and later elite-copy diversity mechanism.
