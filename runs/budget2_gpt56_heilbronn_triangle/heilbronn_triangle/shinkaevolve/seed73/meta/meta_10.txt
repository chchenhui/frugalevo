# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- **Elite-preserving evolutionary search produced the best observed result.** The current best, **Elite Evolutionary Heilbronn Triangle Search** (Generation 3), reached `min_area_normalized = 0.03` and combined score **0.69**, exceeding every other valid program. Its combination of a population of 128, retention of the top 16 configurations each generation, and 6,500 generations successfully maintained strong layouts while continuing exploration through mutations.

- **Barycentric/simplex parameterization with fixed container corners is consistently reliable.** The best program fixes `(0,0)`, `(1,0)`, and `(0,1)` in reference-simplex coordinates, optimizing only the other eight points. This strategy passed validation in Generations 1, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, and 9. It also gives the search access to the full enclosing triangle and avoids wasting optimization effort moving the boundary vertices.

- **Ranking by the true minimum area plus a small low-tail reward appears beneficial.** Generation 3 scores configurations as the smallest determinant area plus `0.035` times the mean of the eight smallest areas. This achieved 0.03, while closely related evolutionary approaches with tail-aware scoring but different search details—Generation 8 at **0.65** and Generation 9 at **0.67**—reached only 0.02 normalized minimum area. The tail term likely helps distinguish configurations with the same immediate bottleneck by favoring layouts that improve several near-active triangle constraints.

- **Annealed, sparse mutations preserve late-stage structure.** In the best program, mutation standard deviation decays from roughly `0.106` to `0.0012`, and the probability of changing each free point falls from 0.32 to 0.14. This supports broad early exploration and controlled late refinement. The successful evolutionary programs all use some form of decaying Gaussian perturbation, whereas the faster differential-evolution variants generally remained at 0.02.

## Ineffective Approaches

- **Returning degenerate or non-optimized configurations entirely fails the objective.** The Generation 0 **Trivial Zero-Point Triangle Arrangement** passes shape/type validation but places all points at `(0,0)`, yielding normalized minimum area **0.00** and combined score **0.00**. Validation alone is therefore not evidence of useful packing quality.

- **Feasibility enforcement that is not robust through final Cartesian conversion causes total failure.** Generation 2’s simulated annealing implementation and Generation 7’s differential-evolution implementation both received combined score **0.00** despite intended simplex projection. Their final outputs included points outside the equilateral triangle beyond the evaluator tolerance. In Generation 2, `(0.5000003635, 0.8660260335)` exceeded the boundary tolerance; in Generation 7, `(0.8843096036, 0.4224299544)` was outside. A correct internal representation was insufficient when final numerical boundary safety was not guaranteed.

- **Differential-evolution hybrids did not match the elite evolutionary result.** Generations 4, 5, and 6 all passed validation but achieved only normalized minimum area **0.02**, with combined scores **0.49**, **0.56**, and **0.62**, respectively. Their jitter injections, differential mutations, simulated-annealing-style acceptance, and polishing avoided stagnation, but did not produce the stronger 0.03 bottleneck attained by Generation 3.

- **More complex global-plus-local machinery did not necessarily improve solution quality.** Generation 6’s Hybrid Differential Evolution Heilbronn Search took **7.61 s** and scored 0.62, while Generation 3 took **11.28 s** but achieved the only 0.03 result. Generation 4 was much faster at **3.37 s** but scored only 0.49. The evaluated results show that runtime-saving search simplifications corresponded to weaker discovered arrangements in this set.

## Implementation Insights

- **The current best’s vectorized all-triple evaluation directly targets the real maximin constraint.** It enumerates all `C(11, 3) = 165` triples once, gathers them in batch with `configurations[:, triples]`, and computes absolute 2D determinants. This makes it practical to evaluate a full 128-member population for 6,500 generations while preserving an objective tightly aligned with normalized triangle area.

- **Simplex projection is implemented at the mutation boundary, not merely assumed.** Generation 3 converts each `(u,v)` pair to three barycentric weights `(1-u-v, u, v)`, clamps every weight to at least `1e-10`, renormalizes, and returns the final two weights. This gives positive slack from the boundary and likely contributes to its validation success, unlike Generations 2 and 7, whose overall coordinate pipeline still allowed final boundary violations.

- **Exact elite copying protects the best designs from destructive mutation.** The current best selects its top 16 scored members, samples parents only from those elites, then explicitly restores `candidates[:elite_count] = elites`. This combines exploitation with mutation-based diversification without losing current high-quality layouts. The best-so-far configuration is also stored independently, so later generations cannot erase it.

- **The best program’s search budget is substantial but productive.** Its 128-member population and 6,500-generation loop lead to the longest reported valid runtime, **11.28 s**, but that budget is associated with the highest measured score, **0.69**. Similar 6,500-generation evolutionary searches were also relatively expensive—Generation 8 took **9.05 s** and Generation 9 took **6.18 s**—but their altered evolutionary mechanisms did not match Generation 3’s 0.03 minimum area.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Run a staged elite-evolution schedule rather than one fixed 6,500-generation schedule.** Keep the current 128/16 elitist loop and barycentric projection, but use an exploratory first phase with larger mutations and perhaps 160–256 population members, followed by a refinement phase seeded from the best 16–32 layouts with much smaller `sigma` and lower mutation probability. This directly extends the current program’s successful annealed sparse-mutation behavior while allocating more diversity early and more focused constraint polishing once a 0.03-quality basin is found.

2. **Make the low-tail objective progressively more maximin-focused during the run.** Retain the current `minimum_area + tail_weight * mean(eight_smallest)` ranking, but anneal the tail coefficient—for example, start around `0.05–0.08` and decay toward `0.01–0.035` in the final third of generations. The existing 0.035 tail reward was associated with the only observed 0.03 minimum-area result, while a schedule may use the tail more strongly to escape clusters of near-degenerate triples and then prioritize the true bottleneck during final refinement.

3. **Add targeted mutations for points participating in the currently smallest triangles.** For each candidate, identify the few triples with the lowest determinant areas, mark their involved free-point indices, and apply an additional small barycentric Gaussian perturbation to those points after ordinary mutation; retain projection and exact elite preservation. Since the objective is controlled by a small active set among 165 triples, this focuses late-stage search on relieving the actual geometric bottlenecks instead of perturbing all eight movable points uniformly.

4. **Introduce occasional elite-parent recombination before mutation.** For a controlled fraction of non-elite offspring, create each free point by choosing its barycentric coordinates from one of two high-ranking elite parents, or use a convex interpolation with a random mixing coefficient, then apply the existing annealed noise and simplex projection. The current method already benefits from selecting parents exclusively from the top 16; recombination can combine complementary spacing patterns from different strong layouts without abandoning the proven elitist evolutionary backbone.

5. **Use deterministic multi-restart evolution with a final shared refinement population.** Execute several shorter searches with distinct fixed RNG seeds—each preserving the current corner fixing, projection, vectorized triple scoring, and elites—then combine the best configurations from all runs into one final 1,500–3,000 generation refinement run. The current approach is deterministic and quality-sensitive to search dynamics; multiple diverse basins followed by the same successful sparse-mutation elite refinement offer a practical route to exceed the single-seed 0.03 result while maintaining guaranteed valid simplex coordinates.
