# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- **The current best program, Generation 26 “Deterministic Simulated Annealing Triangle Search,” is tied for the highest observed score tier at `0.83` with normalized minimum area `0.03`.** Its strongest pattern is the combination of eight deterministic annealing restarts, a cooled lower-tail log-sum-exp objective, 90% low-tail-triple-biased point selection, and final deterministic active-set line-search polishing. This confirms the prior insight from Generation 19: treating many nearly limiting triples, rather than optimizing only the current minimum triangle, is central to reaching the top tier.

- **Fixed enclosing vertices plus eight simplex-feasible movable points remains the most reliable structural formulation.** Generation 26, Generation 19 (`0.83`), Generation 13 (`0.83`), and Generation 17 (`0.82`) all use this form. Barycentric/simplex coordinates make every proposal feasible after projection, while retaining the outer triangle’s geometry and reducing the effective search dimension.

- **Targeting the lower tail is consistently more successful than generic global search.** Generation 26 samples a triple whose determinant is within `current_score + 2.8 * scale` in 90% of moves, then mutates a movable point in that triple. Generation 23’s active-constraint-biased annealing also reached `0.79`, and Generation 27’s lower-tail soft-min plus elite-basin refinement reached `0.77`. These results exceed the differential-evolution-heavy methods, which typically stayed at `0.54–0.69`.

- **Deterministic local refinement after stochastic exploration materially improves robustness.** Generation 26’s active-set polish tests coordinate, simplex-diagonal, active-triple partner-direction, and boundary-tangential directions at multiple line-search lengths. This is more geometry-aware than the simpler final refinement in several `0.75–0.77` programs, and aligns with the top result. Sign-fixed SLSQP polishing can also help, as in Generation 23 (`0.79`) and Generation 20 (`0.77`), but it was not necessary for Generation 26’s top-tier outcome.

## Ineffective Approaches

- **Differential-evolution-centered global optimization remained weaker on this nonsmooth maximin problem.** Generation 29’s DE plus repeated sign-fixed SLSQP process achieved only `0.54` in `16.93 s`; Generation 21’s DE elite portfolio achieved `0.62` in `8.71 s`; and prior Generation 11 DE reached `0.61`. Their valid configurations show that feasibility and optimization effort were not the issue; rather, population search and orientation-cell-restricted polishing did not resolve the tightly coupled set of low-area constraints as effectively as active-tail annealing.

- **Large runtime budgets alone did not produce top quality.** Generation 25 used 14 annealing restarts plus coordinate-wise LP refinement but scored `0.76` in `10.30 s`; Generation 30 used roughly 150,000 annealing iterations plus SLSQP and scored `0.75` in `9.92 s`; Generation 24 ran extensive restarts and polishing for `0.68` in `7.46 s`. This supports the earlier evidence that proposal guidance and handling of changing active triples matter more than raw restart count or optimizer complexity.

- **Orientation-fixed epigraph polishing appears locally useful but globally limiting when it dominates the pipeline.** Generation 21 (`0.62`) and Generation 29 (`0.54`) both used sign-fixed epigraph SLSQP after DE. The discovered orientation pattern constrains polishing to one determinant-sign cell, which can improve a found basin but cannot repair a globally weaker combinatorial arrangement. Generation 26 avoids dependence on such a fixed-cell optimizer and instead searches and polishes directly against absolute determinants.

- **Incremental affected-triple updates improve speed but do not by themselves improve packing quality.** Generation 20’s incremental multi-island annealing was fast at `3.34 s` and reached `0.77`, but it did not match the `0.83` current-best tier. The prior weak Generation 14 result reinforces that efficient area bookkeeping alone is not sufficient without effective lower-tail objective shaping and active-set local moves.

## Implementation Insights

- **Generation 26’s `tail_energy` is a concrete solution to the discontinuity of the raw minimum-area objective.** It computes the minimum determinant minus a scaled log-mean-exp of offsets above that minimum. Early in a restart, the larger scale (`0.0100`) differentiates states with equal minima by considering the full low-area tail; near convergence, cooling toward `0.00065` makes the surrogate approach the true minimum. This directly operationalizes the successful lower-tail principle identified previously in Generation 19 and Generation 13.

- **The current best couples active-triple selection with controlled residual exploration.** In 90% of iterations it selects among triples below the scale-dependent cutoff, then mutates one involved non-anchor point; the remaining 10% randomly chooses a movable point. This avoids spending most proposals on irrelevant points while preventing the search from becoming completely trapped by the current active set. The implementation is especially well matched to the fixed 165-triple constraint system.

- **The active-set polishing is broader than a single-coordinate hill climb.** For every small step scale, Generation 26 gathers all triples close to the current minimum, extracts all movable points participating in them, and tests multiple directions. In addition to axial and simplex-diagonal directions, it creates directions toward and away from the midpoint of the two partner points in each active triple. Boundary-specific tangential directions address a practical defect of simplex projection: a projected radial move near an edge can lose its intended direction.

- **Feasibility and reproducibility are handled simply and reliably.** The `normalize_simplex` function clamps negative coordinates and renormalizes pairs with sum greater than one; all eight free points therefore remain inside the simplex. The fixed NumPy seed and fixed restart/iteration schedules make the configuration deterministic. This common implementation foundation is shared by the strongest annealed programs and avoids the failure risk that would accompany unconstrained Cartesian perturbations.

## Performance Analysis

- **Generation 26 matches the best reported combined score, `0.83`, and exceeds every newly listed Generation 20–30 program.** The nearest new result is Generation 23 at `0.79`; Generation 20 and Generation 27 reached `0.77`; Generation 25 reached `0.76`; Generation 30 reached `0.75`; and the DE-heavy Generation 29 reached `0.54`. Although several programs report normalized minimum area rounded to `0.03`, the combined-score differences indicate that the evaluator captures meaningful unrounded improvements in the limiting area or related quality metric.

- **Relative to the earlier tied leader Generation 19, Generation 26 reaches the same `0.83` score but is slower: `16.99 s` versus `13.27 s`.** Both use the decisive active-tail annealing pattern and deterministic active-set refinement. Generation 26’s eight `30,000`-iteration restarts and extensive multi-scale directional polishing provide top-tier quality, but its evaluation time is roughly 28% higher than Generation 19’s reported runtime.

- **Generation 26’s quality advantage is not explained by normalized-area rounding alone.** Generation 20, 23, 25, 27, and 30 all report `0.03` normalized minimum area, yet their combined scores range from `0.75` to `0.79`, below Generation 26’s `0.83`. The strongest distinction is therefore the detailed handling of low-tail determinants: smooth surrogate ranking during annealing, sampling from multiple dangerous triples, and refinement across the whole active set.

- **Runtime and score remain weakly correlated.** Generation 20 obtains a respectable `0.77` in only `3.34 s`, demonstrating strong score efficiency from incremental multi-island search, while Generation 29 spends `16.93 s` for only `0.54`. Conversely, the current best spends `16.99 s` but reaches the top score tier. Across these evaluations, the quality of active-constraint handling is a clearer predictor of score than whether the algorithm uses more global-optimizer stages, more restarts, or more wall-clock time.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Maintain a diversity-filtered elite archive across annealing restarts, then polish every elite candidate.** Instead of retaining only `best` by raw minimum determinant, store perhaps 6–10 configurations ranked lexicographically by `(min_area, tail_energy at 0.001–0.003, mean of lowest 12–24 areas)` and reject near-duplicates by sorted pairwise-distance or coordinate distance. Apply the existing active-set directional polish to each archive member, because a state with a slightly weaker immediate minimum but healthier low tail may polish into a superior active-constraint arrangement.

2. **Introduce active-set-driven paired-point moves during annealing.** For roughly 10–20% of proposals, select two movable points with high participation among triples below `current_score + 2.8 * scale`, and perturb them jointly using shared, opposite, or partner-centroid-relative directions before simplex projection. The current single-point lower-tail targeting is highly successful, but the limiting triple network can require coordinated displacement of two points to relieve multiple nearly tight constraints simultaneously.

3. **Use a composite, phase-dependent lower-tail merit rather than log-sum-exp alone.** In the first half of each restart, score candidates with a weighted combination of the current `tail_energy`, the mean of the lowest 20–32 determinants, and perhaps a modest quantile such as the 10th percentile; gradually shift weight toward the exact minimum and a narrower lowest-8-to-16 tail late in the run. This preserves Generation 26’s proven smooth lower-tail guidance while distinguishing broad, globally promising basins from states that only improve one temporary minimum.

4. **Add deterministic multi-point active-set polishing after the existing one-point line search.** From the current active triples, form small groups of two or three frequently participating movable points and test synchronized moves along directions such as each point’s active-partner midpoint vector, common simplex-edge tangents, and equal-and-opposite pair directions, with the same multi-length line search. Restrict this expensive stage to the final best/archive candidates and small step scales, extending the successful geometry-aware polish without replacing its reliable coordinate and partner-direction moves.

5. **Diversify deterministic initialization using geometric templates and boundary-biased Dirichlet starts.** Keep several existing `Dirichlet(1.15)` restarts, but reserve others for reproducible constructions with staggered edge-near points, one or two central points, and controlled jitter; also test Dirichlet concentrations below one to increase edge/corner coverage. Fixed enclosing vertices and simplex feasibility remain unchanged, while more varied initial combinatorial arrangements can expose strong basins that interior-biased random samples may miss.
