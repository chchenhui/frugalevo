# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- **Anchored boundary vertices plus feasible barycentric optimization is consistently effective.** The current best, **Deterministic Simulated Annealing Triangle Packing** (Generation 8), fixes the three simplex corners and searches only over eight movable points, reaching `min_area_normalized: 0.03` and `combined_score: 0.74`. The same structural choice was successful in Generations 6 and 7, which also reached `0.03` / `0.74`. In contrast, the zero-initialized baseline had no geometric structure and scored `0.00`.

- **Direct maximin optimization over all 165 triples is necessary for the measured objective.** Generation 8 explicitly maintains the determinant area of every \(\binom{11}{3}=165\) triple and ranks states primarily by the true minimum. The other strongest results likewise directly target the minimum area: Generation 6’s bottleneck-focused annealing and Generation 7’s differential-evolution/SLSQP epigraph formulation both attained normalized area `0.03`.

- **Tie-aware low-tail ranking helps stochastic search on the nonsmooth maximin landscape.** The current best uses `minimum + 0.055 * low.mean()` over the 16 lowest-area constraints for acceptance/ranking, while retaining the actual minimum as the primary comparison for the global best. This mirrors the successful low-tail approaches in Generation 1 (`0.69`), Generation 5 (`0.70`), and Generation 9 (`0.69`), where tail statistics helped distinguish configurations tied on the bottleneck triangle.

- **Guided active-constraint moves combined with random/global proposals are a productive hybrid.** In Generation 8, 68% of proposals target points participating in one of the currently lowest-area triangles, while remaining proposals preserve broader exploration. Directional Dirichlet-blend proposals further diversify the local Gaussian perturbations. This bottleneck targeting aligns with Generation 6’s minimum-triangle-focused moves, which also reached the top observed `0.74` score.

## Ineffective Approaches

- **Returning a shape-valid configuration without optimization completely fails the actual geometric objective.** The **Zero-Point Heilbronn Baseline** passed validation solely because it returned a valid `(11, 2)` numeric array, but all points coincided, yielding minimum area `0.00` and combined score `0.00`. Validation correctness therefore does not imply meaningful objective performance.

- **More elaborate global optimization did not automatically improve solution quality.** **Deterministic Differential Evolution Triangle Packing** (Generation 2) used seeded differential evolution, 18,000 local perturbations, simplex projection, and low-tail merit scoring, yet reached only `min_area_normalized: 0.02`, `combined_score: 0.42`, with `8.72 s` runtime. Its sophistication did not overcome the difficult combinatorial maximin landscape.

- **Computationally expensive coordinate-feasibility methods had weak score efficiency.** **Deterministic Maximin Coordinate Triangle Search** (Generation 3) performed repeated half-plane clipping and binary-search feasibility checks across 18 restarts. It achieved only `0.02` minimum area and `0.50` combined score while requiring `51.73 s`, far below the current best’s `0.74` at `14.13 s`.

- **Annealing variants without the strongest active-set/incremental structure plateaued below the leaders.** Generations 1, 5, and 9 reached `0.03` minimum area but scores only `0.69`, `0.70`, and `0.69`, respectively. Their results indicate that generic multistart annealing and even SLSQP polishing can produce feasible moderate-quality solutions, but did not match the stronger search behavior of Generations 6 and 8.

## Implementation Insights

- **The current best avoids unnecessary full objective recomputation on each proposal.** Generation 8 precomputes `affected[point_index]`, the 45 triples containing each moved point, and updates only those determinant values after a move. Since a single-point change affects \( \binom{10}{2}=45 \) rather than all 165 triples, this supports its large search budget: 18 restarts × 26,000 annealing iterations, followed by 32,000 polishing proposals.

- **Its state-management design supports cheap accept/reject transitions.** Before proposing a move, the program saves only the selected point’s old position and its affected area entries. Rejected proposals restore exactly those values instead of rebuilding all areas. This is materially more efficient than the repeated full-triple scoring noted as a runtime contributor for Generation 4 (`9.89 s`) and Generation 6 (`14.40 s`).

- **Simplex projection ensures feasibility throughout the stochastic process.** The `simplex_project` routine clips barycentric weights to a tiny positive threshold and renormalizes them. This keeps every movable point in the enclosing triangle and avoids boundary leakage or invalid samples. The same feasible barycentric parameterization was a reliable common feature of the correct high-performing programs.

- **The current best combines multiple optimization phases without losing deterministic reproducibility.** A fixed RNG seed, broad Dirichlet initialization, temperature-annealed local perturbations, occasional directional blend moves, active-triangle targeting, and true-objective greedy polishing form a coherent search pipeline. The deterministic behavior and valid output were confirmed by validation, while the staged search attained the best observed score tier (`0.74`).

## Performance Analysis

- **The best observed performance tier is `min_area_normalized: 0.03` with `combined_score: 0.74`.** The current Generation 8 program reaches this tier at `14.13 s`; Generation 6 also reached `0.03` / `0.74` at `14.40 s`; and Generation 7 reached the same score at `9.33 s`. Thus, the score ceiling in these evaluations is shared by three distinct but related anchored maximin methods.

- **A normalized minimum area of `0.03` is necessary but not sufficient for the top combined score.** Generations 1, 5, and 9 all reported the same rounded minimum area `0.03`, but their combined scores were lower: `0.69`, `0.70`, and `0.69`. This suggests that differences below reporting precision, additional score components, or solution characteristics captured by the evaluator separate the `0.74` configurations from other `0.03` results.

- **The `0.02` minimum-area group was substantially weaker regardless of optimizer family.** Generation 2 differential evolution scored `0.42`; Generation 3 clipping-based coordinate maximin scored `0.50`; and Generation 4 multistart annealing scored `0.66`. Even though Generation 4’s score was respectable relative to the other `0.02` methods, all remained below the `0.69–0.74` group that attained `0.03`.

- **Runtime and quality are not monotonically related.** Generation 3 was by far the slowest at `51.73 s` but scored just `0.50`, whereas Generation 5 achieved `0.70` in `4.23 s`. The current best’s `14.13 s` runtime reflects a deliberately large multistart annealing budget, but the results show that search efficiency and objective-landscape handling matter more than raw computation time.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Add an elite-seed refinement stage using several of the best restart endpoints rather than polishing only `best_points`.** Retain (for example) the top 4–8 configurations ranked lexicographically by `(minimum area, low-tail merit)`, then run shorter greedy/annealed polishing runs from each and return the strongest result. This directly extends the successful multistart strategy while reducing the risk that the single nominal best state lies in a poorer local basin for final active-constraint resolution.

2. **Introduce pair- or small-block moves for points shared by multiple active low-area triangles.** Build per-point activity counts from the lowest 10–20 triples, and occasionally move two highly involved movable points together with correlated barycentric perturbations; update the union of their affected triple sets. The current single-point active-set moves are efficient, but bottlenecks can be structurally coupled, where improving one triangle requires coordinated displacement of two nearby interior or boundary-adjacent points.

3. **Use adaptive low-tail merit weights across annealing phases instead of the fixed `0.055 * low.mean()` term.** Early in a restart, rank with a broader tail such as the mean of the lowest 24–32 areas to promote robust global spacing; late in the restart, narrow to the lowest 5–12 constraints and increase emphasis on the true minimum. This preserves the proven tie-aware ranking mechanism while making its smoothing strength match the transition from basin exploration to bottleneck equalization.

4. **Add deterministic local directional searches around active vertices after stochastic annealing.** For a selected point appearing in restrictive triples, evaluate a small stencil of feasible barycentric directions—coordinate-like directions plus directions toward/away from the centroids of its active triangle partners—and accept the best lexicographic improvement before returning to random proposals. Such a derivative-free active-constraint refinement can resolve the nonsmooth determinant landscape more reliably than Gaussian perturbations alone while retaining simplex feasibility.

5. **Diversify initialization with structured boundary/interior templates in addition to Dirichlet samples.** Keep the three fixed corners, but devote a subset of restarts to seeded layouts with several points near distinct edges and the rest in staggered interior positions, followed by small random barycentric jitter; retain other restarts with the existing Dirichlet initialization. Boundary-adjacent candidates are already encouraged by the successful `Dirichlet(0.92)` initialization, and explicit template diversity may reach different high-quality combinatorial arrangements without abandoning the proven anchored-simplex formulation.
