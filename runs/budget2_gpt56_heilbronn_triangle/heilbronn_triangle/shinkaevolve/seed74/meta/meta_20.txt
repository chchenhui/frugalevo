# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- **The current best, Generation 19’s Deterministic Annealed Heilbronn Maximin Search, establishes the strongest observed score tier: `0.83` combined score with normalized minimum area `0.03` in `13.27 s`.** Its most important feature is not merely simulated annealing, but the combination of (1) a lower-tail log-sum-exp surrogate, (2) 90% proposal selection from triples within `current_score + 2.8 * scale`, and (3) final active-set directional refinement. This addresses the changing set of bottleneck triples more effectively than optimizing only the single current minimum.

- **Anchoring the three enclosing-triangle vertices and optimizing only eight simplex-feasible points remains a consistently successful structural decision.** Generation 19, Generation 13 (`0.83`, `19.03 s`), Generation 17 (`0.82`, `12.33 s`), and Generation 10 (`0.76`, `18.95 s`) all use this pattern and reach normalized area `0.03`. This reinforces the earlier conclusion that barycentric/simplex coordinates with fixed outer anchors provide a reliable valid search space for the 11-point problem.

- **Lower-tail and active-constraint methods correlate with the highest scores, especially when paired with local polishing.** Generation 19’s smooth tail energy and active-set directional moves achieved `0.83`; Generation 13’s lower-tail log-sum-exp annealing plus worst-triangle coordinate polishing also achieved `0.83`; and Generation 17’s elite-basin, lower-tail annealing reached `0.82`. These results improve on the earlier `0.69–0.75` group, showing that resolving multiple nearly tight determinants is more valuable than focusing on a single bottleneck alone.

- **Moderate restart counts can be competitive when proposal guidance is strong.** Generation 19 uses eight restarts of 30,000 iterations and outperforms Generation 10’s 14-restart approach (`0.76`, `18.95 s`) and Generation 17’s repeated 18,000-step search (`0.82`, `12.33 s`). Its active-triple selection and temperature/scale schedules appear to make each restart more productive than generic multi-start search alone.

## Ineffective Approaches

- **Multi-stage global optimization does not ensure high packing quality.** Generation 14’s Hybrid Heilbronn Triangle Annealing Optimizer used differential evolution, incremental simulated annealing, greedy refinement, and optional SLSQP, yet achieved only normalized area `0.01` and combined score `0.36` in `7.28 s`. Its efficient incremental updates reduced computation cost, but the search configuration found a valid arrangement without reaching a competitive maximin basin.

- **Differential-evolution-heavy designs were generally weaker than active-tail annealing on this objective.** Generation 11’s Deterministic Differential Evolution Triangle Packing reached only normalized area `0.02` and score `0.61` in `5.57 s`, while Generation 18’s hybrid DE plus active local refinement improved to `0.69` in `3.50 s` but remained well below the `0.82–0.83` annealing leaders. This extends the earlier evidence that population-based global search alone has difficulty resolving the nonsmooth collection of 165 determinant constraints.

- **Expensive search budgets without sufficiently effective constraint handling have poor score efficiency.** Generation 10 spent `18.95 s` on 14 deterministic annealing restarts but scored `0.76`, below Generation 19’s `0.83` in `13.27 s` and Generation 17’s `0.82` in `12.33 s`. The difference is consistent with Generation 19’s more explicit lower-tail sampling and directional active-set polish being more effective than simply increasing restart breadth.

- **Smooth local optimization can be limited by orientation-cell assumptions.** Generation 12’s differential-evolution plus signed-determinant epigraph SLSQP method reached `0.75` in `15.22 s`. Its feedback specifically notes possible limitations from fixed anchors and local orientation-cell refinement; this is consistent with the result trailing the stronger stochastic lower-tail methods despite analytic-Jacobian polishing.

## Implementation Insights

- **Generation 19 uses an objective surrogate specifically designed for the maximin objective’s nonsmoothness.** Its `tail_energy` subtracts a scaled log-mean-exp term from the minimum area. At larger scale it distinguishes configurations with the same worst determinant by accounting for nearby low-area triples; as scale cools toward `0.00065`, it increasingly emphasizes the actual minimum. This is a concrete refinement of the prior successful low-tail merit pattern and aligns with the equal-best result of Generation 13.

- **The current best’s proposal mechanism concentrates computation where it matters while preserving exploration.** In 90% of iterations, it samples a triple from the lower tail and selects one movable point from that triple; 10% of iterations choose a random movable point. This avoids the failure mode of reacting to only one arbitrarily selected tied constraint, while retaining enough nonlocal movement to escape local structures.

- **Its final polishing phase uses the whole active constraint set rather than just one triangle.** For each scale from `0.012` down to `0.0003`, Generation 19 identifies triples within `max(3 * step, 2e-5)` of the current minimum. It then tests axial, diagonal, and partner-centroid directions for all movable points in that active set. Candidate states are ranked first by true minimum area and then by the mean of the 16 lowest areas. This directly implements the active-set principle highlighted in earlier insights and likely explains the improvement from the prior `0.74–0.76` tier to `0.83`.

- **Feasibility and determinism are maintained with low implementation risk.** All moves use a simple simplex projection—clamping coordinates nonnegative and renormalizing if their sum exceeds one—while a fixed NumPy RNG seed makes the result reproducible. As with the previous successful barycentric programs, this guarantees in-triangle points throughout search and avoids invalid-output failure modes.

## Performance Analysis

- **Generation 19 and Generation 13 are tied for the best observed combined score, `0.83`, both with normalized minimum area reported as `0.03`.** Generation 19 is materially faster: `13.27 s` versus Generation 13’s `19.03 s`, a reduction of roughly 30% at the same reported quality tier. Generation 19 therefore improves the score-efficiency frontier among the evaluated correct programs.

- **The new top tier is now clearly above the previous `0.74–0.76` leaders.** Earlier best programs cited in the global insights achieved `0.74`; among the newly listed variants, Generation 10 scored `0.76` and Generation 12/16 scored `0.75`. Generation 19’s `0.83` and Generation 17’s `0.82` show that the evaluator distinguishes meaningful quality differences even though all these methods round to normalized minimum area `0.03`.

- **Runtime is not predictive of score.** Generation 13 needed `19.03 s` for `0.83`, Generation 19 needed `13.27 s` for the same score, Generation 17 obtained `0.82` in `12.33 s`, and Generation 15 obtained only `0.69` despite a relatively quick `4.18 s`. Conversely, Generation 10’s `18.95 s` produced just `0.76`. The relevant differentiator is therefore active-tail search quality, not raw iteration count or number of optimization stages.

- **Incremental computation is beneficial for speed but insufficient by itself for objective quality.** Generation 14 explicitly updated only affected triangle areas yet scored `0.36`, whereas Generation 19 recomputes all 165 areas for candidate states but reaches `0.83`. The comparison shows that, for this small fixed problem size, the quality of the tail objective, active-constraint selection, and polishing directions dominated the benefit of more aggressive incremental bookkeeping.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Retain an elite archive across restarts and apply the active-set polish to several distinct finalists, not only the single highest raw minimum.** Store the top 4–8 restart states using a lexicographic key such as `(minimum area, tail_energy at 0.001–0.003, mean lowest 16 areas)`, while rejecting near-duplicate configurations. Run the existing multiscale directional refinement on every archived seed and return the best polished result; this builds on the evidence that lower-tail quality identifies promising basins even when their immediate minimum is slightly behind.

2. **Add occasional coordinated two-point proposals driven by active-triple participation.** During annealing, count how often each movable point occurs among triples below `current_score + 2.8 * scale`; with perhaps 10–20% probability, select two highly active points and perturb them jointly, including opposite displacements, shared directional displacements, or moves relative to their active-partner centroids. The current single-point proposals successfully target bottlenecks, but coupled active constraints can require moving two points together to improve several nearly tight determinants without sacrificing another one.

3. **Use a phase-dependent tail surrogate rather than a single log-sum-exp scale schedule.** Early iterations should use a broader low-tail merit—for example, combine the current log-sum-exp energy with the mean of the lowest 24–32 determinants—to favor globally well-separated configurations; in the final third, narrow this toward the lowest 8–16 determinants and increase the relative importance of the exact minimum. This directly extends Generation 19’s successful tie-aware objective while making the surrogate progressively more focused as the search transitions from basin discovery to active-constraint equalization.

4. **Expand final active-set refinement with line-search and boundary-aware directions.** For every useful direction already generated by axial, diagonal, and partner-centroid stencils, test several lengths such as `step`, `0.5*step`, `1.5*step`, and `2*step`, and additionally include tangent directions along the three simplex edges when a point is near a boundary. The current polish has demonstrated value, and a small deterministic line search can capture improvements that a fixed displacement misses, especially because simplex projection can distort directions near `u=0`, `v=0`, or `u+v=1`.

5. **Diversify restart initialization with deterministic geometric templates alongside Dirichlet samples.** Preserve several current random barycentric restarts, but seed others from structured layouts containing staggered edge-near points, roughly central points, and small reproducible jitter; use multiple boundary strengths such as Dirichlet concentrations below 1.0 as well as the current `1.15`. Anchored simplex search is consistently effective, and template diversity can expose alternative combinatorial arrangements of near-tight triples that ordinary interior-biased samples may rarely reach.
