Its low score relative to the current best’s `0.83` indicates that rapid population-based global search did not discover comparably strong order types for the nonsmooth minimum-triangle constraint system.

## Implementation Insights

- **The current best’s `tail_energy` is a particularly effective objective construction.** It evaluates  
  `min(area) - scale * log(mean(exp(-(area-min)/scale)))`, with scale cooling from approximately `0.0100` to `0.00065`. Early in a restart, it rewards improving the whole dangerous lower tail; late in the restart, it increasingly approximates the exact minimum. This is a concrete reason it outperforms raw-minimum or less structured global methods.

- **Its proposal mechanism is tightly matched to the 165-triple constraint structure.** At each iteration, 90% of moves choose a triple with area below `current_score + 2.8 * scale`, then mutate a movable point participating in that triple. The remaining 10% of random point choices preserves exploration. This balance is more targeted than generic random coordinate perturbation while avoiding fixation on one arbitrarily selected smallest triple.

- **The deterministic polishing stage is broader than coordinate-only local search.** For active lower-tail triples, the current best tests axial simplex directions, diagonal simplex directions, directions toward and away from the midpoint of active-triple partner points, multiple line-search lengths, and edge-tangential moves. This geometric breadth distinguishes it from simpler coordinate refinement and aligns with its top-tier `0.83` result.

- **Incremental determinant updates are a demonstrated runtime optimization, though not automatically a quality improvement.** Generation 37 achieved `0.80` in `11.41 s` using incremental affected-triple updates, compared with the current best’s full area recomputation at `16.99 s`. The current best’s stronger `0.83` score indicates that its proposal and polishing logic remain more important than update efficiency, but the comparison isolates full recomputation as a meaningful source of runtime cost.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Replace full 165-triple recomputation during annealing with exact affected-triple updates.** Precompute, for each movable point, the indices of triples containing it; after a one-point proposal, recompute only those roughly 45 affected determinants and update a cached area vector. Generation 37 shows this can materially reduce runtime, allowing either more diverse restarts, a longer final cooling phase, or additional elite polishing while preserving the current best’s proven tail-energy and dangerous-triple logic.

2. **Maintain a small diversity-filtered archive scored by both minimum area and lower-tail health, then actively polish all archive members.** Rather than retaining only `best_score`, retain approximately 6–10 distinct candidates using a lexicographic key such as `(minimum area, tail_energy at 0.0015, mean of lowest 16 areas)` and reject geometrically near-identical states. A candidate that is marginally weaker in raw minimum during annealing but has a more balanced active tail may enter a better order type after the current deterministic directional polish.

3. **Add low-frequency coordinated two-point proposals targeted by active-triple participation.** For 10–15% of annealing moves, count movable-point occurrences in triples below `current_score + 2.8 * scale`, select two highly involved points, and test shared, opposite, or partner-centroid-relative Gaussian displacements before simplex projection. This extends the successful dangerous-tail selection mechanism to escape configurations where shifting one point merely transfers the limiting constraint to another nearby triple.

4. **Use a phase-dependent composite tail objective, while retaining the existing cooled log-mean-exp as its core.** Early and mid-restart, combine the current `tail_energy` with a small reward for the mean of the lowest 16–24 determinants, then gradually remove that auxiliary term during the final third so acceptance is dominated by the narrow tail and exact minimum. This should favor basins with many jointly healthy near-active triples—the apparent differentiator of the `0.83` solutions—without sacrificing the current program’s effective late-stage maximin behavior.

5. **Extend deterministic polishing with restricted paired active-set line searches after single-point convergence.** Once the existing per-point directions stall at each small step scale, identify pairs of movable points that co-occur most often in the active triples and test synchronized moves along simplex axes, edge tangents, equal-and-opposite directions, and each point’s active-partner-midpoint direction. Limit this expensive stage to the final archive candidates and fine scales such as `0.003` through `0.0003`, preserving the reliable current polish while adding the coordinated adjustment capability likely needed to balance a tight constraint network.
