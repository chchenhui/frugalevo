<DESCRIPTION>
The previous annealer selects movable points uniformly. In a maximin triangle-area problem, most points are irrelevant to the current objective at any given step: the objective is controlled by a small set of nearly minimum-area triples. Selecting a point from one of these active constraints directs mutations toward the geometry actually limiting the score, while retaining occasional uniform moves for global exploration.

The final stage is also changed from fixed-scale random greedy perturbations into a deterministic active-constraint polishing schedule. Its decreasing radius permits meaningful corrections first and fine adjustment later, and it explicitly identifies near-worst triples before choosing a participating non-vertex point. This should substantially improve the final minimum area without increasing the number of expensive objective evaluations.
</DESCRIPTION>
