# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- **Directed active-constraint repair is the strongest recurring pattern, and the current best is its clearest implementation.** The current *Directed Multi-Island Heilbronn Triangle Search* (Generation 26) reaches the top observed combined score of **0.84** with normalized minimum area **0.03**. Its search explicitly computes determinant-gradient directions from the 28 smallest-area triples, moves all free points along those directions using 18 different step multipliers, and supplements this with active-triple joint perturbations. This extends the prior best active-set/annealed pattern rather than relying on generic population evolution.

- **Lower-tail selection improves layouts even when the reported minimum area rounds to the same value.** Generation 26 uses the stabilized soft lower-tail score during every island iteration, while Generation 23’s *Deterministic Multi-Restart Triangle Maximin Search* also combines soft-min scoring, active-triple proposals, and joint repairs to reach the same **0.84** score at **0.03** normalized area. Their advantage over Generation 20 (**0.80**, `0.03`) and most evolutionary variants shows that the score reflects the quality of multiple near-minimum triangles, not just the single rounded bottleneck value.

- **Coordinated full-layout moves are consistently more effective than exclusively single-point mutations.** In Generation 26, 18 of 56 candidates per iteration are directed full-layout moves, and up to 12 additional candidates jointly perturb every free point involved in the 20 most restrictive triples. This matches Generation 23’s late joint repairs and the previous best’s seven coordinated full-state candidates. These mechanisms are associated with the strongest `0.03` / `0.84` results because several active determinant constraints generally need simultaneous adjustment.

- **Structured diversification plus deterministic restarts is effective when paired with targeted local search.** Generation 26’s six islands alternate between perturbed staggered packing starts and farthest-point Dirichlet samples. Generation 23 similarly uses seven seeded restarts and reaches **0.84**. In contrast, merely increasing generic evolutionary diversity did not produce equivalent quality: Generation 24’s six DE islands only reached **0.67** and `0.02`.

## Ineffective Approaches

- **Generic differential evolution remained substantially weaker than active-set-directed search.** Generation 28’s *Deterministic Differential-Evolution Heilbronn Search* reached only **0.59** with minimum area **0.02** despite three islands, lower-tail scoring, elitism, and local perturbation polishing; it also took **8.01 s**. Generation 27’s JADE-like DE with archive and optional SLSQP reached **0.67** in **3.47 s**, and Generation 24’s archive-active DE scored **0.67** in **5.46 s**. These approaches have broad exploration but less direct repair of the exact triples limiting the maximin objective.

- **Covariance adaptation and extensive multi-island evolutionary search did not translate into stronger bottleneck geometry.** Generation 22’s five-island cross-entropy/covariance approach plus 440 polishing rounds achieved **0.69**, `0.03`, in **3.24 s**. Although it matched the rounded area level of the best methods, its lower combined score indicates a weaker distribution of near-active triangle areas than the directed active-set searches.

- **PSO was competitive but did not exceed the directed active-gradient methods.** Generation 29’s particle-swarm search with rejuvenation, lexicographic tail scoring, and active-pattern refinement attained **0.83** at `0.03` in **3.23 s**. It is close to the Generation 26/23 **0.84** frontier, but the one-point score gap supports the observed benefit of Generation 26’s explicit analytic determinant-gradient field and systematic multi-scale directed candidates.

- **Optional SLSQP polishing alone is not the source of high quality.** Generation 25’s evolutionary search with active gradients and orientation-cell epigraph SLSQP scored just **0.64** at `0.02`, while Generation 27’s optional SLSQP version reached **0.67**. The stronger Generation 26 and Generation 23 results show that SLSQP is most useful as a final refinement after search has already found a favorable orientation chamber and balanced lower tail.

## Implementation Insights

- **The current best evaluates the true geometry in a vectorized, search-aligned form.** Generation 26 represents eight free points in affine simplex coordinates, fixes the three corners, and evaluates all **165** triples through batched determinant computations. Each 980-step island evaluates **56** layouts at once, allowing it to compare directed, point-specific, joint-active, and incumbent candidates under the same lower-tail objective. This retains the efficiency benefit identified in the previous vectorized 34-candidate method while increasing proposal diversity.

- **Its gradient field is based on active determinant constraints rather than generic spatial heuristics.** `_gradient_field` selects the 28 smallest absolute signed determinants, computes the analytic determinant gradient for each participating movable point, weights small areas approximately by inverse area, and normalizes each point’s aggregate direction. This directly explains why its local search is better aligned with maximin area improvement than the broader mutation/recombination mechanisms of Generations 24, 27, and 28.

- **The current program combines monotone tail-score updates with separately tracked raw maximin progress.** The incumbent is included in every candidate batch, so selecting by `_tail_score` cannot worsen the temperature-dependent selection objective. Separately, the island retains the layout with the greatest actual minimum area. This separation is important: soft-min scoring improves the lower-tail structure during search, while final island ranking remains tied to the true minimum normalized triangle area.

- **Robustness and cheap public evaluation are implemented without changing the search objective.** Simplex projection is applied after every proposal; triangle corners remain fixed; SLSQP freezes determinant orientations within the discovered chamber; finite checks and a valid fallback protect output. The one-time `_CACHED_LAYOUT` construction explains the reported **0.00** evaluation time for Generation 26 and Generation 21/25-style cached programs: the substantial six-island search cost is paid at module initialization rather than on calls to `heilbronn_triangle11()`.

Generation 26 uses six directed-gradient islands and final SLSQP polish, while Generation 23 uses seven stochastic restarts, soft-min scoring, active-coordinate proposals, and late joint repairs. Their tie strongly reinforces the earlier conclusion that active-constraint targeting, lower-tail balancing, and coordinated repairs are the key ingredients.

- **The new current best improves on the prior reported top score of 0.81, while preserving the same rounded minimum area.** Earlier global insights identified a **0.81** active annealing method as the best known result. Generation 26’s **0.84** and Generation 23’s matching **0.84** show that better handling of the population of restrictive triples can improve combined score even when `min_area_normalized` remains reported as **0.03**. The gain is therefore consistent with more balanced lower-tail triangle areas rather than a visibly different rounded bottleneck.

- **The `0.03` methods dominate the `0.02` evolutionary methods by a wide margin.** Generation 26/23 at **0.84**, Generation 29 at **0.83**, and Generation 20 at **0.80** all report `0.03`. By comparison, Generation 24 and 27 score **0.67**, Generation 25 **0.64**, and Generation 28 **0.59**, all at `0.02`. This is a roughly **0.17–0.25** score separation between the best active-search family and the typical DE/evolutionary family.

- **Reported runtime does not directly reflect construction cost when caching is used.** Generation 26 reports **0.00** evaluation time because `_CACHED_LAYOUT` is built once, as do Generation 21 and 25. For uncached measured runs, Generation 23’s **2.47 s**, Generation 20’s **2.13 s**, and Generation 29’s **3.23 s** show that high-quality active-set methods can remain moderate in cost. The slower DE approach in Generation 28 took **8.01 s** yet scored only **0.59**, while Generation 24 took **5.46 s** for **0.67**, so greater evolutionary search expense did not correlate with better results.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Add a late-stage lexicographic active-set acceptance rule.** Keep the current temperature-based `_tail_score` during early and middle iterations, but in the final 25–35% rank candidates first by raw minimum area, then by mean/sum of the smallest 8–16 areas. This preserves the successful lower-tail balancing behavior while preventing soft-min improvements from replacing a layout with a slightly stronger true bottleneck.

2. **Replace the single aggregate gradient field with a small set of constraint-balanced gradient fields.** In addition to the existing inverse-area weighted direction over the 28 smallest triples, generate directed candidates from disjoint or sampled subsets of the active triples—for example, 3–5 fields built from the smallest 8, 16, 28, and participation-balanced samples. This addresses coupled bottlenecks where summing all gradients can cancel competing repairs, while retaining the analytic determinant-gradient mechanism responsible for the current best result.

3. **Introduce a final vectorized archive-polishing stage before SLSQP.** Retain several best states from every island using a Pareto/archive key such as `(minimum area, low-tail score)`, then run 150–300 strict-improvement iterations on the best 4–8 archived layouts. Each polishing batch should include active-point-only gradient moves, active-pair moves, and all-active-point coordinated perturbations at decreasing scales; this extends the demonstrated value of coordinated repairs without reverting to weak generic DE exploration.

4. **Use archive-seeded islands in addition to fresh structured starts.** Run the first few islands exactly as now, but initialize later islands from moderately perturbed copies of the strongest prior island layouts, optionally resampling one low-participation free point or applying a random affine-simplex perturbation. The current six-island restart strategy is already effective, and transferring favorable lower-tail geometry between islands is more promising than increasing purely random farthest-point starts.

5. **Make joint active-triple proposals directional rather than purely Gaussian.** For each of several restrictive triples, construct candidates that move its free vertices jointly along their individual signed determinant gradients, with compensating small noise and multiple step scales; include pairwise moves for triangles sharing a point. Generation 26 already shows that full-layout directed motion is valuable, so extending directionality to its 12 joint-active perturbation rows should improve repair of specific coupled constraints more reliably than isotropic jitter.
