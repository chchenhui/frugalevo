# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- **Active-constraint, multi-restart soft-min search is the strongest observed pattern.** The current best, **Deterministic Multi-Restart Triangle Point Optimizer** (Generation 10), reached `min_area_normalized = 0.03` and the top combined score of **0.81**. Its seven seeded restarts, temperature-annealed soft-min objective, and selection of points participating in currently small-area triples combine global diversification with targeted repair of the true bottlenecks.

- **Coordinated updates improved over strictly one-point local search.** Generation 10 explicitly mixes one-point proposals, global replacement moves, and coordinated multi-point perturbations. Its score of **0.81** exceeds the related **Deterministic Soft-Min Triangle Point Search** (Generation 2), which scored **0.74** at the same `0.03` normalized minimum area. The current implementation’s reserved batch of seven joint perturbations is a concrete improvement: it can alter several nearly active determinant constraints together instead of being trapped by coordinate-wise moves.

- **Lower-tail objectives consistently outperform raw-min-only evolutionary/global formulations.** Generation 10 (`0.81`) and Generation 2 (`0.74`) use a soft-min objective; Generation 5’s annealed objective also combines the minimum with the mean of the 12 smallest areas and reached **0.71**. This is stronger than the raw-maximin differential-evolution/SLSQP programs: Generation 4 scored **0.63** and Generation 6 **0.54**, both with only `0.02` normalized minimum area.

- **Deterministic feasibility-preserving barycentric/simplex parameterizations are reliable foundations.** The current best keeps three corners fixed and represents the eight free points with two barycentric coordinates, using `project_simplex` after every move. This guarantees points remain inside the triangle and helped all strong implementations pass validation. Generation 8’s affine-simplex evolutionary search plus local polishing similarly reached `0.03` and **0.72**.

## Ineffective Approaches

- **Complex island differential evolution was ineffective despite robust engineering.** **Deterministic Island Differential Evolution** (Generation 9) used three DE islands, structured/random initialization, restart injection, local polishing, simplex projection, and validation checks, yet produced `min_area_normalized = 0.00` and score **0.01**. The result shows that adding evolutionary machinery does not help if its selection dynamics fail to preserve or improve nondegenerate near-optimal configurations.

- **Raw minimum-area optimization with generic global optimizers underperformed the tailored lower-tail searches.** The SciPy differential-evolution plus SLSQP hybrid in Generation 4 attained only **0.63**, while Generation 6’s differential evolution plus epigraph polishing scored **0.54**; both remained at `0.02` normalized area and took **12.41 s** and **14.00 s**, respectively. Their generic global/local optimization did not translate into a stronger eleven-point configuration than active-constraint stochastic search.

- **More search budget alone did not imply better layouts.** Generation 3 used 28 annealing restarts × 6,500 steps, but scored only **0.64** with `0.02` minimum area and took **5.40 s**. By contrast, the current best’s seven restarts × 1,850 iterations scored **0.81** in **4.38 s**, indicating that proposal quality and objective shaping mattered more than a larger number of perturbation steps.

- **Validity without geometric separation is not meaningful optimization.** Generation 0’s all-zero output passed shape/numeric validation but gave zero area and score **0.00**. This establishes that the benchmark’s performance is governed entirely by avoiding near-collinear and coincident triples, not merely by returning feasible coordinates.

## Implementation Insights

- **The current best evaluates all 165 constraints in vectorized batches, enabling richer proposal sets efficiently.** `areas_for_batch` builds batched 11-point states and computes all triangle determinants simultaneously. Each iteration evaluates 34 candidates, making it practical to compare local, global, incumbent, and joint-motion candidates under the same objective. This supports its **0.81** score without exceeding the 4–6 second runtime range of other stochastic methods.

- **Its soft-min is carefully stabilized around the current minimum.** The score
  `lo - temperature * log(sum(exp(-(areas - lo)/temperature)))`
  avoids numerical issues by subtracting `lo` and rewards improvement across multiple near-minimum triples. The temperature decreases from roughly `0.0055` to `0.00055`, so early optimization considers a broader lower tail while late optimization increasingly emphasizes the real limiting triangle.

- **The active-triple weighting is directly aligned with the maximin objective.** At each step, the program identifies triples within a decaying cutoff above the current minimum, counts each free point’s participation, and preferentially moves the most implicated point. This is more targeted than Generation 3’s generic random perturbation annealing and is consistent with the higher `0.03`/`0.81` result.

- **Feasibility and robustness are handled without distorting the main search.** The simplex projector enforces positive barycentric components with `eps = 0.0015` and rescales excessive coordinate sums. Fixed corners, deterministic RNG seeding, finite-result checks, and a hard-coded fallback all ensure correctness. These safeguards were also present in several weaker programs, but Generation 10 couples them with an effective objective and proposal policy.

## Performance Analysis

- **Generation 10 is the best measured result: score `0.81`, normalized minimum area `0.03`, runtime `4.38 s`.** It improves on the closest soft-min predecessor, Generation 2, by **0.07 combined-score points** (`0.81` vs. `0.74`) while also being slightly faster (`4.38 s` vs. `4.06 s` is close, with the score difference substantially favoring Generation 10). Both reach `0.03`, so the combined-score gain likely reflects a better overall distribution of triangle areas or benchmark-specific quality terms beyond the rounded minimum.

- **The strongest group all reaches a reported minimum area of `0.03`, but their scores separate based on layout quality and search refinement.** Generation 10 leads at **0.81**, followed by Generation 2 at **0.74**, Generation 8 at **0.72**, and Generation 5 at **0.71**. The commonality is deterministic constrained search with a lower-tail-aware or lexicographically maximin objective; the current best distinguishes itself through active-point selection plus coordinated jitter.

- **Programs capped at `0.02` were consistently behind.** Generation 1 scored **0.59**, Generation 3 **0.64**, Generation 4 **0.63**, and Generation 6 **0.54**. Thus, increasing the rounded normalized minimum from `0.02` to `0.03` correlates with a substantial combined-score increase, especially when paired with improvements to several small triangles rather than a single limiting constraint.

- **Runtime does not correlate positively with score in this evaluation.** The slowest approaches—Generation 4 at **12.41 s** and Generation 6 at **14.00 s**—scored only **0.63** and **0.54**. The current best outperformed them by **0.18** and **0.27** score points respectively while taking roughly one-third of their time. Conversely, Generation 8’s cached retrieval reported `0.00 s` and scored **0.72**, showing that caching can eliminate evaluation-time cost after offline search, though its achieved layout still trailed Generation 10’s **0.81**.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Replace the single-temperature soft-min with a lexicographic lower-tail acceptance score.** Rank candidates first by minimum normalized area, then break near-ties using a soft-min or mean of the smallest 8–16 areas, e.g. `score = min_area + λ * mean(sorted_areas[:12])` with `λ` scaled below the meaningful minimum-area resolution. This retains Generation 10’s successful lower-tail shaping while preventing a candidate with a slightly worse true minimum from being accepted solely because it improves many nonbinding constraints.

2. **Use constraint-gradient-informed joint proposals for active triples instead of purely Gaussian coordinated jitter.** For the worst few triples, compute each determinant’s signed gradient with respect to the involved free-point Cartesian/barycentric coordinates, aggregate gradients per point, and generate several projected ascent steps plus small noise. The current active-point counting identifies *which* points matter; adding directional information can make the seven coordinated candidates deliberately increase the limiting determinants rather than relying on random perturbations.

3. **Introduce an explicit active-set repair phase in the final annealing quarter.** When temperature and sigma are low, identify the 3–10 smallest triples and generate candidate batches that move all participating free points together, while holding uninvolved points fixed and using multiple step sizes/directions. This is a focused extension of the current joint-motion mechanism and should be especially effective because high-quality layouts are limited by several coupled near-active determinant constraints.

4. **Archive and recombine strong restart states rather than treating restarts as fully independent.** Maintain a small elite archive keyed by minimum area plus lower-tail score, then initialize later restarts from both fresh repulsive samples and “crossover” states assembled from elite layouts after point matching in barycentric space. Multi-restart diversification is already a key strength, and elite recombination could transfer useful local geometric structure between the seven successful search trajectories without adopting the ineffective full island-DE selection dynamics.

5. **Add deterministic post-search coordinate/block polishing around the best archived state.** After the seven main restarts, run a short final phase with a much smaller proposal scale, cyclically test each active point, active point pair, and all-point joint perturbations, accepting only lexicographic lower-tail improvements. This spends a bounded additional batch budget on the single strongest configuration, building on the observed advantage of coordinated updates while avoiding the large runtime costs and weak results of generic DE/SLSQP polishing.
