# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- **The current best combines active-constraint selection, annealed soft-min scoring, and coordinated moves effectively.** The current program reaches the top observed result: `min_area_normalized = 0.03`, combined score **0.81**, in **4.38 s**. Its seven deterministic restarts, 1,850 iterations each, and 34-candidate vectorized batches maintain broad exploration while repeatedly targeting points that occur in triples below a decaying area cutoff. This strengthens the prior observation that tailored lower-tail optimization is more effective than raw minimum-area search alone.

- **Soft-min/lower-tail objectives consistently correlate with the strongest scores.** The current best uses a numerically stable temperature-annealed soft-min over all 165 triangle areas and leads at **0.81**. Related approaches also perform well: *Annealed and Polished Heilbronn Search* (Generation 11) reaches **0.74** and *Active-Constraint Annealing Triangle Search* (Generation 12) reaches **0.77**, both with reported normalized minimum area `0.03`. These results reinforce that rewarding improvement across several nearly limiting triples avoids sacrificing the broader lower tail merely to improve one raw minimum.

- **Active-triple-biased repair is more successful than generic evolutionary refinement.** Generation 12’s critical-triangle perturbation policy, including coordinated two-point moves, achieved **0.77**, substantially above the faster but less targeted DE variants: Generation 13 scored **0.41**, Generation 17 scored **0.55**, and Generation 19 scored **0.53**. The current best improves further by counting each free point’s participation across all active triples and moving the most implicated point.

- **Joint perturbations appear to provide a concrete improvement beyond one-point local moves.** The current best reserves seven candidates per iteration for small simultaneous motion of all eight free points. This design matches the geometric fact that several tight determinants often need to change together. It exceeds Generation 11’s annealing plus optional SLSQP polish (**0.74**) and Generation 12’s active-constraint annealing (**0.77**) while remaining in a comparable runtime range.

## Ineffective Approaches

- **Generic differential evolution plus constrained polishing was costly without matching quality.** *Differential Evolution Heilbronn Triangle Search* (Generation 7) scored **0.63** at `0.02` normalized area in **19.49 s**, while *Hybrid Differential Evolution Heilbronn Search* (Generation 15) scored **0.56** in **22.81 s**. Both use global DE followed by SLSQP-style polishing, but neither reached the current best’s `0.03` area or its **0.81** score. Their generic population search and local solver phases did not improve active bottleneck geometry as efficiently as the tailored annealed search.

- **Fast DE implementations often produced merely feasible rather than competitive packings.** Generation 13’s batched differential evolution reached only **0.41** and `0.01` normalized minimum area despite vectorization, lower-tail shaping, and hill refinement. Generation 17 and Generation 19 improved this to `0.02`, but their **0.55** and **0.53** scores remain far below the active annealing methods. The evaluation feedback consistently describes these layouts as valid but not strong maximin configurations.

- **Complex evolutionary/gradient active-set machinery was not inherently reliable.** *Deterministic Evolutionary Maximin Triangle Search* (Generation 16) scored only **0.04** with reported normalized minimum area `0.00`, despite elitism, differential-style recombination, Gaussian mutation, and tight-triple gradient polishing. This is consistent with the earlier failed island-DE result: elaborate population mechanics can still lose nondegeneracy if selection and local updates do not reliably preserve good lower-tail configurations.

- **More optimizer budget did not compensate for weaker proposal design.** The DE/SLSQP programs spend roughly 19–23 seconds yet trail the current best by **0.18–0.25** score points. Generation 12’s 34-restart annealing is also slower than the best at **9.03 s**, but scores **0.77**, indicating that active proposals help; the current best’s better score/runtime balance comes from vectorized candidate batches and a more selective mix of targeted, global, incumbent, and joint candidates.

## Implementation Insights

- **The current best’s fully vectorized 34-candidate evaluation is central to its efficiency.** `areas_for_batch` evaluates all **165 triples** for every candidate state at once. Each iteration compares local perturbations, early global replacements, seven coordinated full-state perturbations, and the incumbent without repeated scalar constraint evaluation. This supports a rich proposal distribution while keeping total evaluation time at **4.38 s**.

- **Its stabilized soft-min is aligned with the measured objective without becoming numerically fragile.** The score is computed as  
  `lo - temperature * log(sum(exp(-(areas - lo)/temperature)))`.  
  Subtracting the per-candidate minimum `lo` stabilizes the exponential calculation. Cooling from `0.0055` to `0.00055` shifts the search from broad lower-tail balancing toward the true maximin constraint late in optimization. This is a more directly targeted mechanism than the weaker raw-min DE formulations.

- **The active-point mechanism is implemented from actual constraint participation, not only nearest-point geometry.** The code identifies triples satisfying `area <= current_min + cutoff`, then counts how often each free point appears in those triples. The point with greatest active-triple participation is preferentially changed. This directly targets the source of weak areas and is more focused than Generation 13’s general DE/hill-refinement process or Generation 14’s evolutionary-plus-gradient approach, which scored **0.60**.

- **Feasibility is enforced cheaply and consistently through simplex coordinates.** All free points are represented by two barycentric variables, projected by `project_simplex` after every local, global, and joint proposal, then converted to Cartesian coordinates only for determinant evaluation/output. Fixed corners, deterministic RNG state, finite-result checks, and a valid fallback mirror the robustness patterns of other correct programs, but here they are paired with the highest-quality search logic.

## Performance Analysis

- **The current best is the leading measured program by a meaningful margin.** At **0.81**, it exceeds Generation 12’s active-constraint annealing (**0.77**) by **0.04**, Generation 11’s annealed/SLSQP approach (**0.74**) by **0.07**, and Generation 18’s JADE DE plus optional SLSQP (**0.70**) by **0.11**. All four report rounded normalized minimum area `0.03`, so the score gap indicates that the current program likely produces a stronger distribution among the many small triangle areas, not just a similar single minimum.

- **The `0.03` normalized-area group is clearly stronger than the `0.02` group.** Generation 7 (**0.63**), Generation 14 (**0.60**), Generation 15 (**0.56**), Generation 17 (**0.55**), and Generation 19 (**0.53**) all remain at `0.02` and are substantially behind the current best. The transition from `0.02` to `0.03` is associated with score gains of roughly **0.18–0.28** relative to the typical DE-based methods.

- **The best score is not a consequence of the longest runtime.** The current program’s **4.38 s** runtime is less than half Generation 12’s **9.03 s**, roughly one-quarter Generation 7’s **19.49 s**, and below Generation 15’s **22.81 s**. It is slower than the lightweight DE variants—Generation 13 at **0.99 s**, Generation 17 at **1.46 s**, and Generation 18 at **1.65 s**—but those faster methods give up substantial quality, scoring **0.41**, **0.55**, and **0.70** respectively.

- **Active stochastic search dominates the evaluated quality frontier, while DE variants occupy a speed-oriented but lower-quality region.** Generation 18 is the strongest DE-family result at **0.70**, using JADE-like p-best mutation, archive differences, adaptive parameters, lower-tail scoring, and SLSQP refinement. Nevertheless, it remains below the current best’s **0.81**, supporting the observed pattern that deterministic multi-restart annealing with explicit active-constraint repair and joint candidate moves is the most successful evaluated design.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Replace purely random joint jitter with active-triple gradient proposals.** For the smallest 3–10 triangles, compute signed determinant gradients with respect to each involved free point, aggregate area-increasing directions per point, map them into barycentric coordinates, and add several projected ascent candidates to each 34-state batch. Keep Gaussian joint perturbations as diversity candidates, but reserve 4–8 proposals for multiple gradient step sizes; this directly extends the current best’s active-participation logic from identifying *which* points to move to estimating *how* they should move.

2. **Use a lexicographic acceptance score late in the search instead of only annealed soft-min.** During the final third or quarter of each restart, rank candidates primarily by raw minimum normalized area, then by a lower-tail statistic such as the mean of the smallest 8–16 areas or a very-low-temperature soft-min. This preserves the current program’s strong lower-tail balancing early while preventing a slightly worse true bottleneck from being accepted solely because it improves many less-critical triangles.

3. **Add a bounded final active-set block-polishing phase for the best archive state.** Save a small archive of states ranked by `(minimum area, tail score)` across all restarts, then spend a fixed number of vectorized batches polishing the best few states after the main 1,850-step loops. Generate candidates that move only points occurring in the current smallest triples—single-point, active-pair, and all-active-point moves—with decreasing scales and strict lexicographic improvement; this exploits the demonstrated advantage of coordinated moves without paying for generic SLSQP or DE refinement.

4. **Make active-constraint selection more balanced by sampling from participation weights rather than always taking the maximum.** The present argmax-plus-tiny-noise rule can repeatedly focus on one highly implicated point even when several coupled points need repair; sample the moving point from a tempered distribution proportional to `weights + baseline`, or cycle through the top 2–4 weighted points within a short block. This retains explicit active-triple targeting while improving coverage of coupled constraints, especially when multiple near-minimal triangles share different vertices.

5. **Introduce archive-seeded restarts with structure-preserving perturbations.** Keep the existing fresh repulsive simplex starts for diversity, but initialize several later restarts from archived high-quality states perturbed at moderate barycentric scale, optionally replacing one or two low-contribution points with fresh simplex samples. The current multi-restart annealing is already highly effective, and this modification transfers strong lower-tail geometry between successful trajectories without reverting to the weaker full-population differential-evolution machinery.
