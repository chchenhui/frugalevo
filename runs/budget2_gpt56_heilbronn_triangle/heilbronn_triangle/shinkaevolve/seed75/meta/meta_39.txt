## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- **Active-constraint-directed hybrid local search remains the strongest family.** The current best program, **Generation 34 `balanced_active_gradients`**, reached **0.84 combined score** and **0.03 normalized minimum area**. Its most important mechanism is constructing analytic determinant-gradient fields from several lower-tail sizes—**6, 12, 24, and 40** restrictive triples—rather than one aggregate field. - **Combining smooth lower-tail selection early with strict maximin selection late is repeatedly successful.** Generation 34 uses a temperature-controlled soft-tail objective through 72% of each restart, then switches to a lexicographic selection keyed first by true minimum area and second by the mean of the 12 smallest areas. Generation 31’s `lexicographic_slsqp_polish` also reached **0.76** at `0.03`, supporting the value of late lexicographic treatment, though its less directed search was weaker.

- **Coordinated full-layout moves complement targeted single-point repair.** In Generation 34, each iteration includes eight joint random moves across all eight free points and four directed full-layout gradient-field moves, in addition to focused mutations of the point most involved in active triples. This is consistent with the earlier `0.84` active-search frontier: near-optimal layouts have multiple simultaneously restrictive determinants, so single-point changes alone are often insufficient.

- **Orientation-cell SLSQP polish is beneficial after a strong discrete/local search stage.** Generation 34 freezes determinant signs for all **165** triples and solves a maximin epigraph problem from the best state plus four finalists. The same search-plus-polish pattern appears in Generation 35 (`active_polish11`, **0.84**) and Generation 38 (`add_oriented_maximin_polish`, **0.84**), confirming that sign-frozen polishing is useful when it starts from a favorable orientation chamber rather than serving as the primary optimizer.

- **Generic differential evolution remained materially weaker despite multi-stage refinement.** Generation 32’s `archive_restart_maximin` reached only **0.59** and `0.02` in **5.43 s**, while Generation 37’s `global_epigraph_search` reached **0.63** at `0.02`. ## Implementation Insights

- **Generation 34 evaluates the true maximin geometry efficiently and consistently.** It fixes the three simplex corners, stores eight free points in simplex coordinates, and evaluates every one of the **165 point triples** in batched NumPy form. Absolute determinants in this coordinate system equal normalized areas directly, avoiding repeated Cartesian conversion during optimization and ensuring that every candidate is judged by the actual validation-aligned objective.

- **Feasibility is maintained structurally rather than repaired only at the end.** Its `project` routine clips coordinates to nonnegative values and rescales points whose simplex-coordinate sum exceeds one. Every proposed candidate—single-point mutation, random joint move, directed gradient move, and SLSQP output—is projected before use. This contributes to the program’s correct validation result and avoids wasting evaluations on outside-triangle layouts.

- **The active-gradient implementation specifically addresses gradient cancellation.** For each lower-tail field size, Generation 34 computes signed determinant derivatives for every movable point in restrictive triples, applies orientation-aware signs, and divides contributions by per-point active-triple participation. The participation balancing prevents frequently occurring points from dominating the field merely because they appear in more constraints. Normalizing each field by its maximum point-vector norm makes the directed proposals comparable across the 6-, 12-, 24-, and 40-triple tails.

- **Caching separates construction cost from repeated API-call cost, but the actual construction is expensive.** The function caches the final Cartesian layout in a function attribute and returns a copy on later calls. However, the reported **17.90 s** evaluation time reflects a substantial first construction: **seven restarts × 1,850 iterations × 38 candidates**, followed by up to five SLSQP calls with `maxiter=900`. This explains why the same quality level can be achieved more cheaply by related variants such as Generation 36.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Adopt the faster active-search schedule used by the other `0.84` variants before adding more search complexity.** Reduce the current seven × 1,850-restart budget to a smaller portfolio such as 3–4 restarts of 700–1,000 iterations, while retaining the multi-scale fields `(6, 12, 24, 40)`, joint moves, and orientation-cell polish. Generations 36 and 38 demonstrate that the same quality frontier is reachable in roughly 2.6–4.7 seconds, so the saved budget can instead be spent on better finalist diversification or a targeted final repair phase.

2. **Maintain a lexicographic lower-tail archive throughout every restart, not only each restart’s best raw minimum.** Store candidates using a key such as `(minimum area, mean smallest 12, mean smallest 24)` and retain 6–12 nondominated states globally; include states that lose a tiny amount of minimum area but substantially improve the next restrictive determinants. Polish the strongest 4–6 archive members with short strict lexicographic active-set search before SLSQP, since the score differences among layouts with rounded minimum `0.03` are strongly associated with lower-tail distribution quality.

3. **Add directed repair candidates for individual bottleneck triples and overlapping bottleneck pairs.** In addition to the four aggregate balanced fields, select several of the smallest triples and generate moves that shift all of their movable vertices along their own signed determinant gradients, using step multipliers such as `0.4, 0.8, 1.4` times the current gradient step. For pairs of active triples sharing a free point, construct a joint field from only that pair; this should resolve coupled local constraints more precisely than the current aggregate tails while preserving the proven orientation-aware derivative logic.

4. **Use disagreement-aware gradient fields to explicitly exploit cancellation structure.** Compute the per-triple signed gradient contribution vectors already implicit in the current field construction, then cluster or split active triples based on the direction of their contribution at the most-participating points. Generate one candidate for each coherent gradient cluster—for example, positive versus negative projection onto the dominant active-point direction—rather than only prefix tails of sizes 6, 12, 24, and 40; this is a direct extension of the successful multi-field mechanism for competing bottlenecks.

5. **Strengthen orientation-cell polish with a lower-tail secondary optimization after maximin SLSQP.** First run the existing sign-frozen epigraph solve to maximize `t`; then, with constraints `signed_det >= t_best - epsilon` for a very small epsilon, run a second SLSQP objective maximizing the sum or smooth minimum of the 12–24 smallest signed determinants in that fixed chamber. This preserves the exact maximin result while directly targeting the low-tail balance that separates the `0.84` active-search family from weaker layouts sharing the same rounded minimum area.
