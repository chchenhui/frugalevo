# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- **Lexicographic top-K local search is the current best refinement objective**: Gen 31 ("Lexicographic Top-K Balancing with Randomized Multistart Placement", 26.25) improves moves/swaps by targeting the K=3 hottest GPUs and accepting only changes that lexicographically reduce the sorted top-K KVPR vector — a strictly richer objective than minimizing only the single max KVPR. This beat pure max-KVPR local search variants (Gen 23: 24.39, Gen 29: 22.71).
- **Randomized multistart with ordering diversification now ties for the top**: Gen 31 and Gen 27 ("Las Vegas Multistart Greedy Placement with Local Search", both 26.25) both use ~30 restarts over varied orderings (deterministic density-sorted, fully shuffled, and jittered by req_rate/slo). This shows multistart + local search has now caught up to and surpassed the binary-search-on-λ family (Gen 25: 25.96, Gen 28: 25.82, Gen 23: 24.39), overturning the earlier belief that threshold search had a stable ~3-point advantage.
- **Random tie-breaking among near-equal candidate GPUs is a cheap but effective diversifier**: Gen 31's greedy placement collects all GPUs within 1e-12 of the best resulting KVPR and picks randomly, which combined with jittered/shuffled orderings escapes poor greedy basins without extra restart cost.
- **Multiple orderings under a λ-threshold remain a strong fallback pattern**: Gen 25 (25.96, first-fit) and Gen 28 (25.82, four orderings) confirm the binary-search family is robust, but its ceiling (~26) is now matched by multistart approaches.

## Ineffective Approaches

- **Trivial bugs still zero out entire programs**: Gen 26 ("Three-Stage Greedy...") crashed every test with `'<' not supported between 'float' and 'dict'` due to a swapped tuple assignment (`best_val, best_pl = pl, v` instead of `= v, pl`) — its sophisticated three-stage heuristic was never exercised, scoring 0.00. Gen 21 also scored 0.00, likely from a missing `random` import, echoing Gen 11's identical failure.
- **Hard failures / infeasible fallback paths kill scoring**: Gen 18 (0.00) and Gen 22 (0.00) both failed outright — Gen 22 raised ValueError on memory-infeasible placements instead of degrading gracefully; Gen 18's restart loop combined with binary search (25 restarts × 60 iterations) was slow and its fallback path apparently crashed. Layered, guarded fallbacks remain mandatory.
- **More restarts/iterations alone don't help if the core is broken**: Gen 18's 25 Las Vegas restarts produced 0.00 while Gen 27's 30 restarts (with correct guards and fallback) scored 26.25 — the restart count is irrelevant compared to robustness.
- **Discarding local search costs ~0.4 points vs. the best**: Gen 28 (binary search, no local search, 25.82) trails Gen 31 (26.25) by ~0.4, showing refinement on top of a good construction now yields a measurable (not negligible) gain at this score level.

## Implementation Insights

- **What makes Gen 31 effective**: (1) `ratio()` guard returning `float('inf')` when free memory ≤ 1e-9 — the validated crash-prevention pattern present in all top scorers; (2) `1e-12` epsilon tolerances in greedy candidate comparison enabling tie-collection for random tie-breaking; (3) a 3000-iteration guard on the local-search loop; (4) lexicographic top-K comparison (`ntop < top` on sorted descending top-3 vectors) that also improves the second- and third-hottest GPUs, indirectly enabling further max-reductions in later iterations; (5) both moves and pairwise swaps (consistent with prior findings that move-only refinement underperforms).
- **Fallback design in the best program**: Gen 31 retries the deterministic density-sorted ordering if all randomized restarts fail, and only then raises — a two-layer safety net that preserved the 100% success rate.
- **Score distribution update**: Multistart + top-K lexicographic local search (Gen 31, Gen 27: 26.25) > binary-search λ + local search (Gen 25: 25.96) > multi-ordering binary search (Gen 28: 25.82) > single-ordering binary search + local search (Gen 23: 24.39) > worst-pair rebalancing binary search (Gen 29: 22.71) > crashed programs (Gen 18, 21, 22: 0.00).
- **Runtime remains a non-factor**: All scoring programs run in 0.00–0.02s; combined score = max_kvpr + 1 at 100% success, so placement quality alone differentiates.

## Performance Analysis

- **The binary-search family plateaued at ~25.7–26.0** (Gen 16: 25.71 → Gen 25: 25.96), while multistart + lexicographic local search pushed to 26.25 — the first family to break past the threshold-search ceiling, revising the previous generation's conclusion that binary search dominated.
- **Local search quality now differentiates within ~0.9 points**: Gen 25 (moves+swaps on hottest GPU, 25.96) vs Gen 29 (worst-pair targeting, 22.71) — notably, Gen 29's more aggressive two-hottest-GPU rebalancing with 200 iterations *underperformed* simpler hottest-GPU targeting by ~3 points, suggesting overly complex refinement objectives can hurt.
- **Crashes are fully preventable and entirely determine scoring eligibility**: three of ten programs this round (Gen 18, 21, 22) scored 0.00 from import errors, swapped assignments, or unguarded infeasibility — all bugs unrelated to algorithmic quality.
- **Density ordering (req_rate/slo descending) remains universal** across every scoring program, either as the base ordering (binary search family) or as one restart among many (Gen 31, Gen 27), confirming it as the strongest single-ordering heuristic.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Try K > 3 in the lexicographic top-K objective.** Gen 31 uses K=3; the top-K vector objective generalizes trivially — try K=4, K=5, and K=full (all GPUs, which becomes full lexicographic balancing of the entire sorted KVPR vector). Keep the same move/swap acceptance test (`ntop < top`) and 3000-iteration guard. Since GPU counts in tests may be small, full-vector lexicographic comparison costs nothing extra and could unlock improvements the K=3 window ignores.

2. **Add a hybrid λ-binary-search construction as additional restarts.** Instead of only greedy lowest-resulting-KVPR placement, add restarts that place models via first-fit under a binary-searched KVPR threshold λ (the Gen 25/28 pattern), then run the same lexicographic top-K local search on each result. This merges the two strongest families — threshold construction finds different basins than greedy — and takes the best of ~30 total restarts at negligible runtime (0.02s currently).

3. **Diversify the restart orderings with structured keys.** Beyond density-shuffle and ±20% jitter, add restarts sorted by `model_size` descending, by `(req_rate/slo + model_size)` composite descending, and by density with only within-tie-group shuffling (preserving global density order). Gen 25/28 showed multiple fixed orderings are robust; combined with random tie-breaking, each ordering becomes a family of candidate basins rather than a single one.

4. **Use a smarter first-improvement strategy in local search instead of first-accept.** Currently the first move/swap that lexicographically improves top-K is taken and the scan restarts. Instead, at each iteration evaluate all feasible moves/swaps from the K hottest GPUs and take the one with the best new top-K vector (best-improvement). This costs one extra pass per iteration but converges to better local optima; guard with the existing 3000-iteration cap. If runtime is a concern, apply best-improvement only to moves and first-accept to swaps.

5. **Add a perturbation/restart loop around the local search (basin hopping).** After local search converges, take the best placement and apply a random kick — swap 2–3 random model pairs regardless of objective — then re-run local search, accepting only if the final max KVPR strictly decreases; repeat ~10 kicks per restart within the 30-restart budget. This escapes the local optima that plain multistart (which only diversifies construction, not refinement) cannot, while reusing the exact crash-proof guards of Gen 31.
