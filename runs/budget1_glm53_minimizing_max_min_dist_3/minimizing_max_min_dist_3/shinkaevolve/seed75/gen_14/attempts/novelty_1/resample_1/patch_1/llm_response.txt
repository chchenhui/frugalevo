<DESCRIPTION>
The current SLSQP formulation uses a hard min-max constraint (t² ≤ d² for all pairs), which is highly non-smooth and frequently stalls at poor local optima (score 0.07). Replace it with a smooth annealed objective: minimize `τ·logsumexp(-d²/τ) + τ·logsumexp(d²/τ)` over the raw coordinates with L-BFGS-B. The first term is a smooth approximation of `-min(d²)` (push smallest distances up) and the second a smooth approximation of `max(d²)` (acts as a soft scale normalization, keeping the diameter in check). Annealing τ from coarse to fine gradually sharpens both terms toward the true min/max objective, which is far more robust than the cold-started hard constraint solve. Keep the original SLSQP as a cheap final polish on the annealed point.

Additionally, add structured seeds known to be near-optimal geometries for 14 points: the heptagonal antiprism (two staggered 7-rings at several heights/radii — this family is a strong candidate for the 14-point optimum, analogous to how anti-prisms are optimal/competitive in related Tammes-type problems), plus a hexagonal-close-packed-like layered seed. Annealing from these structured starts plus the existing icosahedron+poles seeds gives much better chances of reaching the reference squared ratio ≈ 0.24.
