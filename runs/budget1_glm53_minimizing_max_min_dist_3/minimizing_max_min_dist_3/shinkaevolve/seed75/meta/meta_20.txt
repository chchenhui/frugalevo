# Analysis of Program Evaluation Results

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- **The current best program (Gen 8, `stronger_multistart_optimization`) set a new top score of 0.99 with ratio 0.24** by combining three basin strategies: the icosahedron+2-poles seed, a random unit-sphere start, and jittered restarts around the incumbent best (0.08 × randn), refined via annealed soft-min/soft-max L-BFGS-B over tau 0.1→0.005 (10 trials). This is the only program to break past the long-standing ratio-0.22 plateau.
- **A final coordinate-wise greedy hill-climb directly on the true (hard) min/max ratio is the key differentiator**: the best program's polish moves individual coordinates (±step, halving on plateau) against the exact ratio, closing the soft/hard objective gap that other annealed programs (Gens 16, 17, 19, 20, 21 — all stuck at 0.22) could not.
- **Structured geometric seeds consistently outperform random-only starts**: D6 hexagonal-ring families (Gens 15, 16, 20, 21, ratios 0.21–0.22), icosahedron+poles (Gen 22, ratio 0.20), and twisted heptagons (Gen 17, ratio 0.22) all reach the 0.20–0.22 plateau, confirming the earlier insight that structural priors are essential.
- **Diverse multi-family seed libraries with annealed L-BFGS-B annealing are robust but plateau**: Gen 19's library (icosahedron+poles, staggered hex rings, twisted heptagons, 12-ring+poles) achieves 0.90 in 0.86s — fast and reliable, but the same ratio ceiling.

## Ineffective Approaches

- **Off-by-one point counts are fatal**: Gen 11 (`family_grid_hard_polish`) crashed with "index 12 is out of bounds" because its ring-plus-poles candidate families with k ∈ {10,11,12} + 2 poles produced only 12–13 points against an n=14 pairwise-distance index — scoring 0.00. Candidate-generation code must guarantee exactly 14 points in every family.
- **Misaligned smooth objectives can destroy quality despite heavy compute**: Gen 14 (`softmin_annealing_with_antiprism_seeds`) ran 30 seeds with annealed tau 0.2→0.0008 yet scored only 0.19 (ratio 0.05, 46.52s) — the soft-min/soft-max diameter term dominated and the SLSQP min-max polish failed from infeasible starts. Gen 3 previously showed SLSQP underperformance (0.07) as well.
- **Soft surrogates alone cap at ratio 0.22**: Gens 16, 17, 19, 20, and 21 all converged to 0.22 with annealed logsumexp objectives but lacked a hard-ratio polish, confirming the soft/hard objective gap is the binding limitation.
- **Over-constrained parametric priors limit the optimum**: Gen 15's D6 analytic seed with closed-form grid search was extremely fast (0.71s) but trapped at 0.21 — the coarse analytic parameterization itself constrains the reachable configuration.

## Implementation Insights

- **Exact-ratio polishing after smooth annealing is the decisive technique**: the best program's structure is (1) diverse seeds → (2) annealed soft-min/soft-max L-BFGS-B refinement → (3) coordinate-wise greedy hill-climb on the true ratio → (4) centroid-centering and scaling so dmax = 1. Steps 1–2 get near the basin; step 3 closes the gap from 0.22 to 0.24.
- **Cheap jittered exploitation of the incumbent**: trials 2–9 restart from `normalize(best_pts + 0.08*randn)` — perturbing the best-so-far rather than fresh random starts, a low-cost multi-basin strategy the feedback explicitly credits.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

# Recommendations for Future Program Mutations

1. **Expand the greedy hill-climb polish to move pairs and triples of points simultaneously, not just single coordinates.** The coordinate-wise polish (±step on one coordinate at a time) is what broke the 0.22→0.24 plateau, but single-coordinate moves may miss improvements that require coordinated displacement of the two closest points (moving them apart requires both to shift). Add candidate moves that perturb the pair of points realizing the current min-distance along their separating direction, plus the pair realizing the max-distance inward — these are the only distances that directly matter to the ratio.

2. **Add a D6-symmetric seed (two staggered hexagonal rings + 2 poles) as a third initial basin alongside the icosahedron and random-sphere starts.** Gens 15, 16, 20, and 21 all independently converged near this structure at ratio 0.21–0.22, and the feedback notes top programs land *near* but miss this symmetric optimum. Since the current pipeline already handles multiple trials, adding `P0 = normalize(d6_rings_seed)` as trial 1 or 2 costs nothing and starts the annealing already inside the most promising basin — critical because the annealed L-BFGS-B alone is a local method. Verify the seed produces exactly 14 points (Gen 11's off-by-one crash shows the hazard).

3. **Insert a short force-based repulsion relaxation between seeding and the annealed L-BFGS-B stage.** Run ~100 iterations where each point moves away from its nearest neighbor (as in the existing scipy-unavailable fallback), applied to each structured seed before the tau 0.1→0.005 chain. This pre-conditioning spreads points apart before smoothing kicks in, addressing the case where structured seeds sit in cramped configurations that the soft-min objective — which is dominated by the largest distances at high tau — may polish in the wrong direction (the failure mode that capped Gen 14 at ratio 0.05).

4. **Make the hill-climb step adaptive per point: use larger steps (0.05–0.1) for points involved in the min-distance pair, smaller steps for others.** The current uniform 0.01 initial step with halving spends many sweeps on coordinates far from the binding constraints, and the 31.95s runtime is dominated by 14×3×2 evaluations per sweep. Directing effort at the closest-pair and farthest-pair points should reach the same or better ratio in significantly less time, improving the combined score's runtime component while preserving the exact-ratio convergence that makes Gen 8 the best.

5. **Extend the annealing floor below 0.005 (e.g., tau 0.002, 0.001) with clipped exponentials for numerical safety.** Add `np.exp(np.clip(±dv/tau, -50, 50))` guards in the soft-min/soft-max objective so lower taus don't overflow, then append the two extra tau values to the schedule. This sharpens the surrogate toward the true discrete metric before the hard polish begins, reducing the soft/hard gap the greedy stage must close — potentially letting the hill-climb finish at a higher ratio than 0.24 from a better starting configuration, while the clipping guard prevents the NaN/inf failures that crash-scored earlier generations.
