- **The current best program (Gen 47, `clipped_anneal_hard_polish`, 1.00, ratio 0.24, 14.9s) achieves the top score at a new efficiency frontier**: seed library (dual hexagonal rings with poles, icosahedron+poles, twisted 7-gons, random) → clipped soft-min/soft-max annealed L-BFGS-B → **hard polish on all near-binding pairs** → perturb-reanneal phases. It matches the 1.00 scores of Gens 43 (10.28s) and 46 (43.25s) while being 2.5–3× faster than the older 35–40s winners (Gens 33/34/36/39/40), showing the winning recipe has been compressed without loss.
- **Near-binding multi-pair polish is the critical refinement**: rather than only moving the single argmin/argmax pair, the best program collects all pairs within `tol=5%` of the extremes and tries single-pair moves, then combined min-separate + max-contract moves on up to 3 pairs each. Gen 40 previously validated multi-pair binding moves; Gen 47's implementation with the combined-move fallback is the most complete form.
- **Structured D6 two-hexagonal-ring seeds with poles remain decisive**: every 0.24/1.00 program in this batch (Gens 43, 46, 47, 45 at 0.99) uses them; Gen 44 explicitly confirms "the structured D6 seed family... dominating the random seeds." Repulsion-relaxation-only pipelines (Gens 13, 44, 48) stall at 0.22/0.93.
- **Phase 3's anneal↔polish alternation matters**: the best program re-anneals the polished configuration at very low temperatures (taus 0.003→0.0003, ftol 1e-14) because "pair moves alone are not a local optimum of the smooth objective" — a closed feedback loop between exact-ratio polish and surrogate relaxation.

## Ineffective Approaches

- **Epigraph SLSQP formulation failed completely (Gen 18, score 0.0)**: despite multi-start structured initialization, the 43-variable epigraph formulation with ftol=1e-14 was numerically fragile and produced an invalid output. This is the second total failure after Gen 12's Adam ascent — non-L-BFGS-B optimizers are 0/2 on this problem.
- **Repulsion relaxation without soft-ratio annealing caps at 0.22 (Gens 13, 44, 48, all 0.93)**: even with adaptive targets, basin-hopping, and D6 seeds, pure repulsion never reaches 0.24. Gen 48's *addition* of a soft-min/soft-max polish stage still yielded 0.22 — the polish must be an *exact hard-ratio* pair-move stage, not just a smoother pass.
- **Wrong seed geometry or incomplete refinement settles at 0.21–0.22**: Gen 50 (0.87, ratio 0.21) used heptagonal antiprisms and lean seeds, and its feedback notes the optimizer "settles into a poor local optimum" despite multi-restart annealing — confirming that seed family breadth (specifically D6 rings) plus exact-ratio polish, not generic restarts, drive the 0.24 jump.
- **Excessive runtime buys nothing**: Gen 45 spent 37.23s for 0.99 and Gen 46 spent 43.25s for 1.00, while Gen 43 hit 1.00 in 10.28s and Gen 47 in 14.9s — exhaustive multi-restart designs (Gen 46's "30 escalating perturbation trials") are wasteful relative to the leaner Phase 2 (6 trials) + Phase 3 (3 trials) structure of the best program.

## Implementation Insights

- **The best program's `hard_polish` is the highest-value component**: greedy moves on every near-binding min pair (separate along connecting direction) and near-binding max pair (contract along connecting direction), with combined min+max moves as fallback and step decay (×0.6, floor 1e-8) when no move improves. Each candidate is re-normalized (`norm_cfg`) and evaluated on the *exact* ratio — this closes the soft-surrogate/exact-metric gap.
- **Clipped soft-min/soft-max surrogate with normalization inside the loss**: exponentials clipped to ±50, distances normalized by Dmax *inside* the loss (scale invariance), and recentring inside both `loss` and `norm_cfg`. This contrasts with Gen 18's epigraph constraints and Gen 12's Adam ascent — both of which failed validation.
- **Guard discipline everywhere**: `np.isfinite(q).all()` after each L-BFGS-B run, try/except around anneal steps, and a final fallback (shape check, finite check, random config if dmax ≤ 0) — present in the best program and all passing programs, absent in the failed Gen 18.
- **Rationalized compute budget**: 14 structured/random seeds in Phase 1 with a 5-rung tau ladder (0.05→0.001), 6 escalating perturbation trials (scale 0.02+0.02·trial) in Phase 2, and only 3 tiny-kick trials (scale 0.003–0.007) in Phase 3 — this is the allocation that hits 1.00 in 14.9s, vs. Gen 38's 97s and Gen 46's 43s for identical scores.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

# Recommendations for Future Mutations

1. **Push the ratio beyond 0.24 with a basin-probe variant of Phase 3**: The current 1.00 tier is saturated at ratio 0.24, so the only remaining upside is a higher ratio. Add a final stage that runs 5–8 trials perturbing *only* the points involved in near-binding min/max pairs (±0.01–0.02 along random unit directions), each followed by the existing low-temperature anneal + hard polish, keeping the best exact ratio. This targets the binding constraints the deterministic axial pair moves cannot rearrange, and is guarded so it cannot degrade the incumbent.

2. **Add tangential/anisotropic pair-move candidates in `hard_polish`**: Currently pair moves are purely axial (along the connecting direction), which can be blocked by third neighbors. For each near-binding pair, additionally generate 2 candidates with a tangential component (e.g., 0.3·step perpendicular to u within the tangent plane, keeping the axial component). This lets binding pairs slide around obstacles at the same step size, closing the soft/exact gap faster and in fewer iterations — a direct extension of the program's highest-value component.

3. **Trim the seed sweep and reinvest in perturb-reanneal restarts**: Phase 1's 14-seed library is the largest fixed cost, and the global analysis shows D6 rings dominate (all 1.00 programs use them; heptagonal/lean seeds settled at 0.21). Cut to 4 D6 ring seeds + 2 icosahedron+poles + 1 twisted-heptagon + 2 random (9 seeds, saving ~5s) and add 2–3 extra Phase 2 perturb-reanneal trials. Gen 43 proved 1.00 is reachable in 10.28s; this pushes toward that efficiency tier without touching the proven polish.

4. **Increase the `tol` tolerance band in the polish's near-binding collection as a staged schedule**: Start `hard_polish` with `tol=0.10` to improve many near-binding constraints early (Gen 40's multi-pair insight at broader scope), then decay to `tol=0.02` for precision. The current fixed `tol=0.05` may miss pairs that become binding after each move; a staged tolerance lets the polish escape earlier local optima cheaply while still converging to exact-ratio precision at the end.

5. **Add a symmetry-aware final refinement exploiting the D6 seed family**: Since the winning configurations descend from D6 two-ring-with-poles geometry, add a final stage that projects the incumbent onto a slightly perturbed D6 parameterization (fit ring radius r, height h, pole offset p, and twist angle by a small 4-parameter grid/L-BFGS search around the current geometry), then re-anneal at taus ≤0.001 and hard-polish. This confines the search to the empirically winning symmetry family and could find the exact optimal D6 configuration the unconstrained 42-dim search only approximates.
