# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns
- **Direct constrained optimization with SLSQP (maximize dmin subject to dmax ≤ 1)** is the dominant winning pattern: the current best program (Gen 2, ratio 0.23, score 0.98) and Gen 7's `icosa_multistart_slsqp_packing` (ratio 0.23, score 0.98, 4.40s) both use this formulation. Aligning the optimization objective exactly with the min/max ratio metric (via the diameter-1 constraint) is what makes dmin/dmax directly the optimized quantity.
- **Multi-start with structured seeds**: The best program uses a deterministic Fibonacci sphere start plus 9 random normalized starts; Gen 7 uses icosahedron + perturbed poles + Fibonacci + seeded random starts. This robustness against local optima is why both reach 0.98 while single-start heuristics plateau near 0.85–0.88.
- **Smooth surrogate objectives with annealed sharpness** are the strongest alternative: Gen 8's `softmin_ratio_optimizer` (softmin/softmax, β=20, L-BFGS-B, 60 restarts) reached 0.93; Gen 10's two-stage sphere→free-space annealed-beta log-sum-exp refinement reached 0.88 in only 0.50s.
- **Icosahedron-based initialization with symmetry-breaking perturbations** gives fast, strong baselines (Gen 3: 0.86 in 0.05s; Gen 6: 0.85 in 0.94s), useful for cheap high-quality starts.

## Ineffective Approaches
- **Pure force-relaxation on the sphere fails badly**: Gen 4's `sphere_repulsion_solver` scored 0.04 (ratio 0.01, 3.41s) and Gen 9's `tammes_sphere_relax_pipeline` scored 0.11 (ratio 0.03), despite multi-seeding, kicks, and polishing. Force heuristics without direct objective alignment converge to poor local configurations for N=14.
- **Hard-constrained unit-sphere placement is limiting**: multiple sphere-projected methods (Gen 4, 9, 10's first stage) score below 0.9, while the top programs allow free-space configurations with only the diameter bound — off-sphere layouts evidently enable higher ratios.
- **Unchecked seed construction causes runtime crashes**: Gen 5's `softmin_gradient_optimization_14pts` scored 0.00 due to an "index 8 is out of bounds" error — a cuboctahedron-like seed filter produced 6 points instead of 12, then 8 after stacking, breaking the optimizer's n=14 assumption. Seed generators must guarantee exactly n points.
- **Random unoptimized placement** (Gen 0 baseline, ratio 0.00, score 0.02) confirms the task requires explicit geometric optimization.

## Implementation Insights
- The best program's core mechanics: pairwise distance vectorization via precomputed index arrays (`P[ii] - P[jj]`), a scalar SLSQP objective `-dists.min()`, an inequality constraint `1.0 - dists.max()`, `maxiter=300`, `ftol=1e-12`, and best-of-10 selection on the true dmin/dmax ratio. Each element (vectorized distances, tight tolerance, ratio-based selection across restarts) contributes to robustness.
- **Selecting across restarts by the true ratio, not the surrogate objective**, ensures multi-start gains actually materialize in the final output (seen in the best program and Gen 7).
- **Ratio-invariance normalization** (e.g., Gen 6 normalizing diameter each iteration, Gen 10's scale-normalized ratio) makes objectives well-conditioned; the diameter≤1 constraint achieves the same effect in the winning programs.
- **Repulsion-based methods need sharp short-range potentials and structured starts** to be competitive at all: Gen 6's inverse-12th-power near-pair repulsion with diameter normalization (0.85) far outperforms Gen 4's inverse-cube forces (0.04).

## Performance Analysis
- Score distribution shows a clear tiering: constrained-SLSQP methods at 0.98, smooth-surrogate methods at 0.88–0.93, icosahedron-repulsion at 0.85–0.86, sphere force-relaxation at 0.04–0.11, random at 0.02, and crashed programs at 0.00.
- The current best (0.23 ratio, 0.98 score, 7.36s) matches Gen 7 (0.23, 0.98, 4.40s) exactly on quality, suggesting 0.23 is at or near the practical optimum for 14 points in 3D; Gen 7 achieves it ~40% faster via additional structured starts.
- Runtime-quality tradeoff: Gen 10's annealed-beta two-stage method (0.88, 0.50s) is ~15× faster than the best program but slightly lower quality; Gen 3 (0.86, 0.05s) shows icosahedron seeds get close nearly instantly.
- Consistent correlation: runtime spent on restarts + structured initialization correlates with score up to ~0.98, but only when the objective directly targets dmin/dmax — heuristic methods with comparable runtime (Gen 4: 3.41s) still fail.

3. **Guarantee seed validity with exact-count assertions**: Before running any optimizer, assert that every seed generator produces exactly `n=14` points (e.g., `assert P0.shape == (14, 3)`), and validate the SLSQP result shape before reshaping. This is a cheap safety net that preserves all runtime investment.

4. **Reduce runtime via parallelizable restart budget with adaptive maxiter**: Split the 10 restarts into a first pass with `maxiter=150` to rank candidates, then re-run only the top 2–3 starts with `maxiter=600, ftol=1e-14` for full convergence.
