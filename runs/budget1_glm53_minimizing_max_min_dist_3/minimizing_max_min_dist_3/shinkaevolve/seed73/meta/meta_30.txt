# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns
- **Repulsion → SLSQP two-stage hybrid is now firmly validated as the dominant pattern**: The current best program (Gen 23, `repulsion_slsqp_hybrid`, ratio 0.24, score 1.00) uses diameter-normalized 1/r¹² repulsion followed by SLSQP maximizing dmin subject to dmax ≤ 1, and now reaches the maximum combined score. Gens 21, 25, and 28 replicate the same pattern at 0.24/1.00, 0.24/1.00, and 0.24/1.00, confirming robustness rather than a single lucky run.
- **Polishing both the repulsion output AND the raw seed is a cheap, effective trick**: Gen 23 runs `_slsqp_stage` on both `pre` (repulsion result) and `init` (raw seed) per seed. SLSQP polishing directly from structured seeds (e.g., icosahedron+poles) sometimes outperforms polishing after repulsion, and this dual-candidate approach adds negligible cost while widening the candidate pool.
- **Icosahedron + 2 perturbed poles remains the winning seed family**: Gens 21, 23, 24, 25, 28 all include icosahedron+poles structured seeds; the N=14 = 12 icosahedron vertices + 2 poles geometry matches the suspected optimal antipodal arrangement. Gen 25's feedback explicitly credits this seed structure for reaching the reference optimum.
- **Fallback acceptance checks in SLSQP polish preserve quality**: Gen 23's `_slsqp_stage` rejects non-finite outputs or outputs that don't improve the true ratio, returning the input unchanged. This robustness check prevents SLSQP failures from destroying a good repulsion candidate and is credited in its feedback for the final ratio.

## Ineffective Approaches
- **Soft-min/soft-max annealed objectives remain catastrophic or mediocre**: Gen 20 (`scipy_smooth_annealing_optimization`) collapsed to ratio 0.09/score 0.39 — the soft landscape guided optimization into clustered, degenerate configurations. Gen 31's p-norm annealed variant (0.17/0.72) and Gen 27's beta=20 softmin/softmax (0.22/0.92) both undershoot the 0.24 target; smoothed objectives consistently mis-rank candidates vs. the true min/max ratio.
- **Antipodal symmetry projections do not add gains on top of the core hybrid**: Gen 24 (`antipodal_symmetry_polish`, 0.24/0.98) and Gen 28 (`antipodal_symmetrization_polish`, 0.24/1.00) apply antipodal symmetrization passes but achieve the same 0.24 ratio as plain repulsion+SLSQP (Gen 23, 0.24/1.00) — the extra machinery adds complexity and runtime without improving the ratio, suggesting the optimum is already (near-)antipodally symmetric via the seeds.
- **Bottleneck-pair perturbation polish yields no measurable benefit**: Gen 26's bottleneck escape (`smooth_softmin_and_bottleneck_polish`) scored 0.93 with ratio 0.22 — actually *below* Gen 23's plain hybrid — indicating the perturbation plus softmin surrogate combination hurt more than it helped, and Gen 25's bottleneck stage reached 1.00 only because the underlying cascade already converged.
- **Longer runtimes do not buy quality**: Gen 26 (42s, 0.93) and Gen 21 (41s, 0.99) spend more compute than Gen 24 (13.35s, 0.98) or Gen 31 (0.24s, 0.72) with worse or comparable results — extra repulsion iterations and restart loops dominate cost without improving the ratio past 0.24.

## Implementation Insights
- **Dual-candidate polishing loop (Gen 23's key structure)**: `for cand in (pre, init): polished = _slsqp_stage(cand)` — every seed spawns two SLSQP starts, doubling the local-search basin per seed at minimal cost while keeping the global best across all candidates by true ratio comparison.
- **Diameter normalization inside repulsion and pre-SLSQP scaling**: Gen 23 divides by dmax every repulsion iteration and rescales points to dmax ≤ 1 before SLSQP, keeping configurations in the feasible region of the `dmax ≤ 1` constraint so SLSQP starts near-feasible — this aligns repulsion output with the constrained objective.
- **1/r¹² force exponent is validated across five generations**: Gens 21, 23, 25, 26, 28 all use inverse 12th-power (or similar short-range) forces with per-iteration normalization; prior inverse-cube attempts scored 0.04. The steep force profile aggressively separates the closest pairs, which is exactly what the dmin objective needs.
- **Cheap multi-seed ranking: polish everything, compare true ratio**: Gen 23 uses 12 diverse seeds (3 icosahedron+poles variants, 3 Fibonacci offsets, 5 random sphere + 5 random ball starts) and compares all polished results by the true dmin/dmax ratio. Gen 22's variant (rank 22 candidates cheaply, polish top-5, early-exit at target) reached 0.98 in 19.11s — a valid runtime-saving refinement, though it slightly undershoots the full-scan Gen 23's 1.00.

## Performance Analysis
- **The 0.24 ratio / 1.00 score tier is now well-populated**: Gens 23, 25, 28 all hit 0.24/1.00, while Gens 21 (0.99), 22 (0.98), 24 (0.98) sit just below — the score is highly sensitive near the reference ratio, so exact convergence (achieved by Gen 23's full multi-seed scan with dual-candidate polish) is required for the maximum.
- **Runtime does not separate the top performers**: Within the 0.24-ratio cluster, runtimes range from 13.35s (Gen 24) to 41.44s (Gen 21); Gen 23 took 37.67s. The spread comes from repulsion iteration counts and restart passes, not from algorithmic quality — all converge to the same ratio, so runtime optimization (e.g., Gen 22's cheap-ranking + early-exit at 19.11s for 0.98) is the remaining trade-off axis.
- **Soft-surrogate methods form a distinct lower tier**: Gen 20 (0.39), Gen 31 (0.72), Gen 27 (0.92), Gen 26 (0.93) vs. constrained-SLSQP hybrids at 0.98–1.00 — a persistent ~2-tier score structure confirming the previous generation's finding that only exact constrained optimization on dmin/dmax reaches the practical optimum.
- **Fastest programs are all soft-objective variants and all fail**: Gen 31 (0.24s, 0.72) and Gen 20 (5.14s, 0.39) show that the speed of smooth annealed objectives comes entirely from converging to wrong optima; the ~13–38s cost of the repulsion+SLSQP hybrid is the price of the correct answer at N=14.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Early-exit at target ratio after each seed to cut runtime without losing the 1.00 score**: Insert a check after each seed's dual-candidate polish — if `best_ratio >= 0.24` (or a slightly conservative 0.2395), break out of the seed loop immediately. Since the icosahedron+poles seeds are the first three and reliably reach the optimum (Gens 21/23/25/28 evidence), this could cut the 37.67s runtime to well under Gen 24's 13.35s while preserving the score. Gen 22's early-exit variant (19.11s, 0.98) validated the concept; placing ico+poles seeds first makes it strictly stronger.

2. **Add escalating SLSQP tolerance passes with a tiny perturbation restart between passes**: Replace the single `_slsqp_stage` call (maxiter 500, ftol 1e-12) with a cascade of passes `[(250, 1e-12), (400, 1e-14), (400, 1e-16)]`, and between passes apply `pts += 1e-6 * rng.standard_normal(pts.shape)` to escape stalled SLSQP active sets. The fallback acceptance check (keep the better of input/output by true ratio) already in `_slsqp_stage` makes each extra pass risk-free, and tighter ftol could push a 0.239 plateau to exact convergence.

3. **Add rotated icosahedron+poles seed variants to exploit the best seed family further**: In `_seeds`, add 2–3 variants of the ico+poles seed with a random rotation applied (generate a random orthogonal matrix via QR of `rng.standard_normal((3,3))` and multiply), plus one variant with slightly larger pole perturbation (0.1 instead of 0.05). Structured seeds demonstrably reach the optimum more reliably per restart than random starts, and rotations explore equivalent basins of the rotation-invariant objective at near-zero cost.

4. **Apply shape/finite validation before SLSQP, not only after**: Add a guard at the top of `_slsqp_stage` checking `np.all(np.isfinite(pts))` and `pts.shape == (N, D)`, returning a sanitized copy (or the rescaled input) on failure. Currently a malformed repulsion output would flow into `minimize` and could crash the whole run, wasting the ~13s+ of prior work — the existing post-check proves the pattern; this closes the remaining failure window and is zero-cost.

5. **Try a bottleneck-pair-targeted micro-polish on only the top-2 candidates**: After the seed loop, take the two best polished configurations, identify the min-distance pair, and displace each of the two points by 1e-4 along the normalized direction away from its partner, then run one final SLSQP pass (maxiter 200, ftol 1e-14). Unlike Gen 26's failed global bottleneck perturbation with softmin surrogates, this targets the exact non-smooth bottleneck with the true constrained objective and only touches converged candidates, so the existing fallback check guarantees no regression.
