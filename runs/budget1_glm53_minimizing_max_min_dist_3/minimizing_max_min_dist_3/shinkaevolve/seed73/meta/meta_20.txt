# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns
- **Two-stage repulsion → SLSQP polish is now the proven dominant pattern**: The current best (Gen 18, ratio 0.24, score 0.98) uses fast inverse-12th-power repulsion across 12 seeded inits, then polishes the top 5 with three escalating SLSQP passes (maxiter 250→400, ftol 1e-12→1e-16). This breaks the previous 0.23 ceiling — the ratio improved from 0.23 to 0.24.
- **Multiple SLSQP restart passes with escalating tolerances**: Gen 18's `two_phase_slsqp_repolish` re-polishes each candidate up to 3 times with tighter ftol, unlike Gen 14 (single polish, 0.22) and Gen 16 (single polish, 0.23). This repetition is the specific change that pushed ratio to 0.24 and score to 0.98.
- **Structured icosahedron+poles seeds remain critical**: Every top program (Gen 16: 0.96, Gen 1: 0.95, Gen 18: 0.98, Gen 12: 0.93) uses icosahedron-based seeds; 14 = 12 vertices + 2 poles matches the problem geometry well.
- **Selecting best across all restarts by the true dmin/dmax ratio** (Gen 18 keeps repulsion candidates as fallbacks and compares true ratios at every stage) ensures multi-start gains materialize.

## Ineffective Approaches
- **Softmin/softmax (log-sum-exp) surrogate methods plateau at 0.21–0.22**: Gens 11, 13, 15, 17, 19 all score 0.86–0.89 with ratio 0.21, consistently below the 0.2404 target. Despite fancy beta-annealing (20→400), free-space refinement, and perturbation polish, the smoothed objective landscape mis-ranks configurations versus the true min/max ratio.
- **Sphere projection constraints limit achievable ratio**: All sphere-projected softmin methods (Gen 11, 12, 17, 19) stay ≤0.93; only free-space layouts with a diameter≤1 constraint (Gen 18) reach 0.24.
- **Expensive random-perturbation hill climbing wastes time**: Gen 1 (166s, ratio 0.23) shows single-coordinate perturbation climbing is ~12× slower than repulsion+SLSQP for equal-or-worse quality (Gen 18: 13.25s, 0.24).
- **Repulsion alone is insufficient**: repulsion candidates in Gen 18 serve only as a "safety net"; SLSQP polish is what converts them to 0.24.

## Implementation Insights
- **Escalating tolerance schedule in SLSQP**: Gen 18's pass schedule [(250, 1e-12), (400, 1e-14), (400, 1e-16)] with early exit when objective change < 1e-15 gives fine convergence without wasted iterations — a concrete upgrade over Gen 14's single-pass polish (0.22).
- **Diameter-normalized repulsion each iteration** (`pts /= dmax` inside the loop, Gen 18) keeps the objective well-conditioned and aligned with the ratio metric; combined with 1/r¹² short-range forces it matches Gen 6/16's successful repulsion.
- **Polishing only the top 5 of 12 candidates** balances runtime (13.25s vs Gen 16's 18.66s for 0.96) against quality.
- **Inverse-power force choice matters**: 1/r¹² (Gens 14, 16, 18) works; prior inverse-cube force relaxation failed badly (0.04).

## Performance Analysis
- **Score tiering updated by this generation**: constrained-SLSQP hybrid methods at 0.96–0.98 (Gen 16: 0.23/0.96, Gen 18: 0.24/0.98), softmin-surrogate methods stuck at 0.86–0.93, repulsion-only at 0.85–0.86.
- **Gen 18 broke the 0.23 ratio plateau**: prior best programs (Gen 2, Gen 7, Gen 16) all hit exactly 0.23; the three-pass repolish pushed to 0.24, indicating the "practical optimum" was not yet reached and fine-grained SLSQP convergence still yields gains.
- **Fast-but-low-quality cluster**: Gens 11/13/15/17/19 run in 0.58–2.32s but score ≤0.93; Gen 18 shows paying 13.25s runtime for direct constrained optimization is worth it at this problem size.
- **Runtime-quality correlation holds only with the right objective**: Gen 1 spent 166s on random perturbation for 0.95; Gen 18 spent 13.25s for 0.98 — objective alignment, not raw compute, drives score.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Add a fourth SLSQP pass with even tighter tolerances plus a midpoint perturbation restart**: The 0.23→0.24 gain came specifically from escalating passes [(250,1e-12), (400,1e-14), (400,1e-16)]. Extend with a [(500, 1e-16)] fourth pass, and between passes 3 and 4, apply a tiny symmetric perturbation (x += 1e-6·N) to escape stalled SLSQP active sets before final convergence.

2. **Increase polished candidates from top-5 to top-7 and repulsion inits from 12 to 16**: With runtime at 13.25s, budget remains for broader multi-start. Add more structured seeds — perturbed icosahedron rotations (random orthogonal matrix applied to the seed-0 ico+poles config) and a Fibonacci-sphere variant — then polish top 7. Gen 7/16 evidence shows structured seeds reach the optimum more reliably per restart than random starts.

3. **Add a mirror/antipodal-symmetry refinement pass**: The icosahedron+poles optimum is likely symmetric. After the final SLSQP pass, symmetrize the best solution by averaging each point with its antipodal partner (or detecting near-symmetry via distance-matrix matching), then run one short SLSQP pass (maxiter 200, ftol 1e-16) from the symmetrized configuration to polish a potentially better symmetric arrangement.

4. **Add shape assertions and fallback validation before polishing**: Before SLSQP, assert each repulsion output has shape (14,3) and finite values, and assert `res.x.size == 42` before reshaping. This is a zero-cost safety net ensuring a single malformed candidate cannot zero out the 13s of runtime (the Gen 5 crash pattern).

5. **Try a local coordinate exchange polish on the top-2 solutions**: After SLSQP, for the top-2 candidates, run a short swap/rotation micro-search: attempt rotating the point with smallest pairwise distance slightly along the direction away from its nearest neighbor (gradient of dmin), then re-run one SLSQP pass. This targets the exact bottleneck (the min-distance pair) that non-smooth SLSQP can stall on, and is cheap since it only touches top candidates.
