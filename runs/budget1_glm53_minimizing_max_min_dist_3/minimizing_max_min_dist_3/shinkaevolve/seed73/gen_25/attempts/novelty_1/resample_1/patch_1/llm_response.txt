<DESCRIPTION>
The SLSQP polish maximizes the non-smooth dmin and often stalls because its gradient only sees the single bottleneck pair. Following the recommendation, I add a cheap micro-search stage applied to the top-2 candidates: identify the point involved in the smallest pairwise distance, nudge it a small step along the direction away from its nearest neighbor (i.e., along the gradient of that pair's distance), then re-run one short SLSQP pass. This directly attacks the binding constraint without disturbing the overall configuration. Since dmin/dmax for 14 points in 3D is bounded by a kissing-number style argument, small targeted escapes from stalled minima are exactly where gains remain.
</DESCRIPTION>

def _bottleneck_polish(pts, tries=6, step=0.05):
    """Micro-search: push the point in the closest pair away from its
    nearest neighbor, then re-polish with a short SLSQP pass."""
    best, best_r = pts, _ratio(pts)
    for _ in range(tries):
        ds = _pair_dists(best)
        k = int(np.argmin(ds))
        i, j = PAIRS_I[k], PAIRS_J[k]
        direction = best[i] - best[j]
        nrm = np.linalg.norm(direction)
        if nrm <= 0:
            break
        direction = direction / nrm
        for s in (step, step / 2, step * 2):
            cand = best.copy()
            cand[i] = cand[i] + s * direction
            cand[j] = cand[j] - s * direction
            cand = _slsqp_stage(cand)
            r = _ratio(cand)
            if r > best_r + 1e-12:
                best, best_r = cand, r
                break
        else:
            break
    return best
