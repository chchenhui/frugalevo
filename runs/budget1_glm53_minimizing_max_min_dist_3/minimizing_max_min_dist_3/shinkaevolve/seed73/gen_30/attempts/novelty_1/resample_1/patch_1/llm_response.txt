<DESCRIPTION>
The current SLSQP stage maximizes the raw `min()` of pairwise distances, which is non-smooth and stalls early. Replacing the objective with a smooth log-sum-exp soft-min (with a sharpness parameter escalated over a few internal restarts) gives SLSQP usable gradient information everywhere, which typically pushes dmin noticeably higher under the same diameter constraint. I also add the recommended zero-cost safety checks (shape/finiteness assertions) so a malformed candidate cannot crash the whole run, and increase the number of internal restarts. Since the metric is squared-ratio, even a 1% gain in dmin/dmax is a ~2% score gain.

    def obj_soft(flat, beta):
        # smooth soft-min: -1/beta * log(sum(exp(-beta*d)))
        ds = pdists(flat)
        a = -beta * ds
        amax = a.max()
        return (amax + np.log(np.sum(np.exp(a - amax)))) / beta
