# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- **Structured geometric seeds remain the decisive success factor**: The current best program (Gen 30, `analytic_lse_grad_fine_anneal`, score 1.00, ratio 0.24, 3.55s) uses icosahedron+poles/axis variants, cube+face-pushes, five staggered hexagonal-ring configurations, and 16 random starts. Gen 23 (Adam+SLSQP, 1.00, 1.60s) with 27 diverse seeds and Gen 28 (0.96, ratio 0.23) with ~35 polyhedron-based seeds confirm that seed diversity — especially staggered-ring and icosahedron-based families — reliably reaches the 0.24 basin.
- **Smooth log-sum-exp surrogate with analytic gradients, annealed over sharpness, then polished on the exact objective**: The best program anneals p = 2→8→32→128 via L-BFGS-B with hand-coded analytic gradients, then runs up to 6 jittered-retry polishes directly on the exact (dmin/dmax)² objective. This mirrors Gen 24 (1.00, p=4→16→48), Gen 27 (0.99, softmin/softmax annealing + exact polish), and Gen 22's smooth→SLSQP→Nelder-Mead chain (0.78).
- **Jittered restarts during the exact polish escape kink stalls**: Gen 30's feedback credits the jitter-retry polish loop and per-stage recenter/rescale as "key robustness details that prevented optimizer stalls" — the raw min/max objective is non-differentiable, and the retry loop (jitter σ=0.01) recovers convergence when L-BFGS-B stalls at a kink.
- **Hybrid smooth-gradient + SLSQP pipelines reach 1.00 fastest**: Gen 23 (Adam on LSE surrogate → SLSQP with hard constraints) hit score 1.00 in 1.60s — the fastest top-scoring program in this batch — confirming the two-stage polish pattern.

## Ineffective Approaches

- **Fancy-indexing/vectorized constraint Jacobian in SLSQP caused a hard crash**: Gen 21 (`ratio_grad_slsqp_polish`, score 0.00) failed with "shape mismatch: (91,3) could not be broadcast to (91,91)" from a buggy line `J[:m, 3*i_arr] = -2*diff`; the per-pair loop fill it was replacing was correct. Vectorizing constraint Jacobians without shape testing is a fatal hazard.
- **Simple syntax errors destroy otherwise sound pipelines**: Gen 26 (score 0.00) never ran due to an extra `)` in its icosahedron seed line — the same class of preventable failure as prior shape-truncation bugs. Two of nine programs scored 0.00 on trivial bugs, not algorithms.
- **Centrally symmetric (antipodal) construction drastically underperformed**: Gen 25 (`antipodal_7code_on_sphere`) scored 0.06 with ratio 0.01 despite mathematically elegant Tammes-7 reduction; feedback points to a final normalization/centering bug (dividing by pre-centering dmax). - **Annealed soft-min without sufficient start diversity lands in local optima**: Gen 22 reached only ratio 0.19 (score 0.78) despite the full smooth→SLSQP→Nelder-Mead chain, indicating the annealed soft-min landscape alone still traps optimizers; more/better structured starts (as in Gen 23/30) close the gap.

## Implementation Insights

- **The best program's core mechanics**: exact golden-ratio icosahedron, hard `assert s.shape == (n, 3)` on every seed (directly preventing the Gen 9/16/20 truncation failure mode), analytic gradients for both the LSE surrogate and the exact ratio, per-stage recenter/rescale (dmax=1) for conditioning, selection by *true* ratio `_ratio_sq` (not surrogate value), and a graceful scipy-missing fallback with gradient descent.
- **Analytic gradients of the LSE ratio use quotient-rule weights**: `g = -(w_min*smax - smin*w_max)/smax²` over pairwise weights, then chained via `np.add.at` on pair-difference vectors — this is the shared mechanics behind Gen 23/24/27/30 all reaching ≥0.99, and the reason Gen 30 runs in 3.55s vs the earlier 71.56s numerical-gradient version.
- **Staggered hexagonal-ring seeds are a newly confirmed strong family**: Gen 27 credits staggered rings, Gen 28 uses swept-parameter staggered heptagons, and Gen 30 sweeps five (h, r, offset, pole-height) ring parameterizations — these seeds matter because n=14's optimal geometry plausibly resembles staggered ring layers plus poles.
- **Guard clauses cost nothing and prevent zeros**: Gen 30's seed-shape assert and finite-check fallbacks (`best_pts` None/degenerate handling) directly answer the repeated 0.00 failures of Gen 16/20/21/26.

## Performance Analysis

- **Score plateau at ratio 0.24 confirms a shared attractor basin**: Gen 23, Gen 24, Gen 30 (all 1.00) and Gen 27 (0.99), Gen 28/Gen 8 (0.96) reach ratio 0.23–0.24 via four distinct pipelines (Adam+SLSQP, L-BFGS-B+exact polish, fine LSE anneal, simulated-annealing-style). Independent methods converging to the same ratio strongly indicates a common geometric basin reached from structured starts.
- **Runtime at top score now spans 1.60s–42.35s**: Gen 23 (1.60s) and Gen 27 (1.31s, 0.99) lead; Gen 30 (3.55s) is competitive; Gen 24 (42.35s) is an outlier at equal score — over-wide seed/trial counts without parallel benefit. The efficiency lever remains analytic gradients + tight iteration budgets (Gen 30's maxiter 300/400 with 25 seeds).
- **Correctness bugs, not algorithms, dominate the failure modes**: Gen 21 and Gen 26 scored 0.00 (indexing/syntax), Gen 25 scored 0.06 (normalization bug) — collectively three of nine programs lost nearly all score to implementation defects despite sound mathematical designs. Gen 22 (0.78) was the only genuine algorithmic shortfall.
- **Fastest path to 1.00**: diverse structured seeds (≥25) → analytic-gradient surrogate annealing (Adam or L-BFGS-B) → exact-objective or SLSQP polish with jittered retries — Gen 23 and Gen 30 jointly demonstrate this recipe at 1.60s and 3.55s respectively.

# Recommendations for Future Mutations

1. **Add cuboctahedron+poles and Fibonacci-spiral seed families to escape the shared 0.24 basin.** All top-scoring programs (Gen 23/24/27/30) converge to ratio 0.23–0.24 from the same icosahedron/ring families, and the known optimum is ~0.62, indicating a common local attractor. Append cuboctahedron vertices + 2 poles, a 14-point Fibonacci spiral (z = 1−2k/13, φ = k·π(3−√5)), and swept-pole icosahedron variants (poles along x, y, z with pz ∈ {1.0, 1.2, 1.5}) as additional starts — the existing true-ratio selection already guarantees these can only help.

2. **Run the full seed list at a cheap coarse annealing schedule, then re-run only the top-3 basins with the fine schedule.** Gen 30 spends fine annealing (4 stages × 300 iters) on all 25 seeds including 16 random starts that likely land in the same basin. First pass: p = 4 only, maxiter 100, on all seeds; select top 3 by `_ratio_sq`; second pass: the full p = 2→8→32→128 schedule plus exact-objective jitter polish on only those 3. This should cut eval time from 3.55s toward Gen 23's 1.60s at equal score.

3. **Apply Coulomb/repulsion preprocessing only to random restarts before annealing.** Random Gaussian starts cluster and waste trials; per Gen 13/17 insights, repulsion helps unstructured but not structured starts. Run ~150 iterations of projected gradient descent minimizing Σ 1/‖pᵢ−pⱼ‖² with per-step recenter/rescale to dmax=1 on the 16 random starts, then feed these quasi-uniform configurations into the annealing schedule — a diverse set of new basins at low cost.

4. **Add a constrained SLSQP polish for the top-3 candidates using the analytic ratio gradient.** Gen 23 reached 1.00 in 1.60s with Adam→SLSQP on hard constraints; Gen 30 uses only unconstrained L-BFGS-B polish. Take the top 3 post-annealing candidates, apply σ=0.05–0.3 symmetry-breaking perturbations, and run SLSQP maximizing dmin² subject to d²ᵢⱼ ≤ 1 constraints — with the constraint Jacobian built per-pair in a loop (Gen 21's vectorized fill crashed on a shape mismatch, so avoid fancy indexing there).

5. **Add per-seed parallel trial pruning and a degenerate-basin fallback triggered on repeated results.** After coarse annealing, if ≥3 seeds yield identical `_ratio_sq` within 1e-9, they are in the same basin — skip the fine stage for duplicates and reallocate their budget to seeds from underrepresented families (rings, cuboctahedron, Fibonacci). This directly attacks the observed plateau where four independent pipelines all land on 0.24, freeing compute to explore structurally different geometries that might break past it.

**Implementation guardrail for all mutations**: keep Gen 30's `assert s.shape == (n, 3)` on every seed and the finite-check/None fallbacks — three of nine failures in this batch were shape/syntax bugs (Gen 21/25/26), not algorithmic. Test any vectorized gradient or Jacobian against `np.allclose` with a finite-difference check before trusting it.
