<DESCRIPTION>
Every prior program converges to a squared ratio near 0.23–0.24 from essentially the same icosahedron+poles-like attractor, while the known optimum for this problem is much higher (~0.62 squared). The optimizer itself is reasonable (true-ratio selection guard, dmax normalization, annealed local search), so the bottleneck is the seed pool: all structured starts are highly correlated (two heptagon layers, icosahedron slices, random Gaussians). I add genuinely distinct 14-point geometries with known good spacing properties as additional starts: (a) a Fibonacci spherical spiral (near-uniform 14 points on S²), (b) cuboctahedron vertices (12) plus 2 poles with swept pole radii, (c) icosahedron plus poles at multiple pole radii and a rotated axis (a family of starts sweeping 0.5–3.0), (d) rhombic dodecahedron vertices (14 points — the optimal kissing-like configuration), and (e) a Johnson/antiprism cap variant. The existing best-of-seeds selection guard plus final polish ensures we keep the best basin found, so extra seeds can only help or tie, at modest extra runtime (each seed gets fewer iterations to stay within timeout).
