The current program regressed from 2.25 to 2.13. The regression came from the new "corner" moves: pushing two-wall-touching circles diagonally into corners is wrong for this hex layout — most wall-touching circles here touch exactly one wall, and the tol-based classification misfires, systematically dragging circles out of the LP-optimal lattice. Two fixes: (1) revert the directed move to the proven "push away from the wall you touch" rule (this was the 0.86-scoring version), and (2) add a cheap final greedy sweep after the hill-climb: for each circle, try small ±deltas in x and y, re-solve the LP, and keep any improvement. This directly exploits the fact that the LP gives exact optimal radii for given centers, so any center move that helps is measurable; the greedy pass polishes irregular placements the random hill-climb misses (the headroom identified in the analysis). Also raise iteration count slightly since each LP solve is fast (~ms).

    # ---- Final greedy "growth pass": single-circle coordinate tweaks ----
    # Try small shifts for each circle, one coordinate at a time, and keep
    # any shift that strictly increases the LP-optimal sum. Repeat with
    # shrinking step sizes until no improvement is found.
    step = 0.01
    improved = True
    while improved and step > 1e-4:
        improved = False
        for i in range(n):
            for axis in (0, 1):
                for direction in (+1.0, -1.0):
                    cand = best_centers.copy()
                    cand[i, axis] = np.clip(
                        cand[i, axis] + direction * step, 0.001, 0.999
                    )
                    if cand[i, axis] == best_centers[i, axis]:
