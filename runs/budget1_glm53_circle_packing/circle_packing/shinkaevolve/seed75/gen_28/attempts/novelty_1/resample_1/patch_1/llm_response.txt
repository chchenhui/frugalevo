<DESCRIPTION>
The best prior variant (2.39) used a uniform hexagonal seed, which forces all 26 circles into near-equal-sized lattice roles. Expert knowledge from circle packing says the optimal n=26 configuration gains most of its advantage from a few LARGE corner circles (three-sided wall slack) plus smaller filler circles — corner circles can have radius ~0.09-0.12 while interior lattice circles stay near 0.07-0.08. A symmetric lattice seed cannot discover this because the hill-climb's wall-push moves actively push circles AWAY from walls, preventing corner anchoring.

Changes:
1. Modify the seed: pull the 4 lattice circles nearest each corner diagonally INTO the corners (asymmetric anchoring), giving the LP water-filling solver immediate three-sided slack there so it assigns them large radii.
2. Add directed corner moves in the hill-climb: circles touching two walls get pulled further into the corner (instead of being pushed away), while single-wall circles are still pushed away from the wall.
3. Keep the final greedy growth pass unchanged — it now operates on a much better basin.
