# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- **The fixed-point hex seed + warm-started Gauss-Seidel solver + greedy shift search is now confirmed as the top recipe, and the current best (Gen 24, "warm_started_shift_search") wins on speed**: same 2.48/0.94 score as Gen 17 and Gen 20, but at 0.27s vs 2.94s (Gen 17) and 9.86s (Gen 20). Warm-starting `solve_radii` from the previous radii during shift evaluation (`init=best_r`) cut runtime ~10x with zero score loss.
- **Warm-started Gauss-Seidel beats lossy proportional shrinking**: Gen 24's `solve_radii` uses per-circle maximization `min(walls_i, min_j(d_ij - r_j))` — feasibility by construction, matching the monotone-repair pattern shared by all top performers (Gens 8, 17, 20).
- **4-5-4-5-4-4 row family remains the best seed**: Gens 24 (2.48), 17 (2.48), 20 (2.46) all use staggered row layouts; Gen 20's feedback explicitly credits the staggered 5,4,5,4,5,3 family. All three cluster at 2.46–2.48.
- **Fast approximate radius solving during search + exact/heavier solve at the end is a validated pattern**: Gen 30 (2.44, 1.91s) used cheap Gauss-Seidel water-filling for hill-climb evaluation and a scipy LP only on final centers — near-best score at moderate cost.

## Ineffective Approaches

- **LP-per-iteration hill-climbing is expensive and does not beat cheap local search**: Gens 27 (2.33, 5.33s), 23 (2.33, 7.90s), 28 (2.40, 9.31s), 25 (2.38, 2.95s) all re-solve scipy `linprog` every step — hundreds of LP solves — and all score *below* Gen 24's 0.27s Gauss-Seidel pipeline. Optimal radii per fixed centers does not compensate for plateaued center search.
- **Relaxation/spreading dynamics without strong structure collapse**: Gen 26's over-inflate-and-relax multi-seed scored only 1.73/0.66 — the worst correct program — showing pairwise-repulsion dynamics actively destroy good hex seeds.
- **Per-iteration LP with no robustness can zero out entirely**: Gen 21 (contact_relax_lp_packer) failed with all metrics 0.00 — likely a runtime exception in `construct_packing` (scipy import or LP infeasibility leaving None). This is the second total failure in the lineage (after Gen 11's syntax error), confirming fragile heavy machinery is a hazard.
- **Local hill-climbing around symmetric seeds plateaus at 2.33–2.44 regardless of move-set flavor**: Gens 22 (2.39), 23 (2.33), 27 (2.33), 28 (2.40), 25 (2.38), 30 (2.44) — corner-anchoring, wall-pushes, growth holes, and jitter variants all fail to break past the lattice basin.

## Implementation Insights

- **Warm-starting is the single biggest efficiency lever found**: Gen 24's `solve_radii(cand, iterations=60, init=best_r)` makes each shift candidate evaluation nearly free, enabling 4 delta scales × 2 sweeps × 26 circles × 4 directions in 0.27s total. Gen 28's 600 LP re-solves cost 9.31s for a worse result.
- **Coarse-to-fine delta schedule (0.01→0.0005) with strict-improvement acceptance guarantees monotone progress**: Gen 24 only accepts shifts with `new_s > best_s + 1e-9`, making the input packing a lower bound — the same design as Gen 17, now at 10x speed via warm starts.
- **Fixed-point seed sizing (`2r + 5·dy = 1`, `r = sqrt(dy² + dx²)/2`) is cheap and reliable**: Gen 24 solves it in 100 trivial iterations, matching Gen 8's pattern, avoiding scipy dependency in the seed entirely.
- **O(n²) Python Gauss-Seidel loops are fast enough at n=26**: Gen 20's ~10s runtime was driven by its heavy multi-start loop, not the solver — the solver itself is sub-second.

## Performance Analysis

- **Score ceiling at ~2.46–2.48 for all lattice-seed pipelines is now overwhelming**: Gens 17, 20, 24 all hit 2.46–2.48 (score 0.94) via different search machinery (multi-start grow/relax, pressure perturbation, warm-started shift search) — strong evidence the lattice basin, not search intensity, is the binding constraint toward 2.635 (~6% gap remaining).
- **Speed ranking at equal score**: Gen 24 (0.27s) ≫ Gen 17 (2.94s) > Gen 20 (9.86s) at identical 2.48/0.94 — warm-started local search strictly dominates multi-start pressure-relax on the runtime axis.
- **Radius engine hierarchy confirmed**: warm-started Gauss-Seidel (2.48, 0.27s) > single Gauss-Seidel + LP final (2.44, 1.91s) > LP-per-step (2.33–2.40, 3–9.3s) > proportional-shrinking relaxation (1.73, Gen 26). LP is never the bottleneck when used once at the end, but per-step use is pure cost.
- **Batch trajectory**: Gen 21 (0.00, failure) → Gen 26 (0.66) → Gens 23/27 (0.88–0.89, LP-per-step) → Gens 25/22 (0.90–0.91) → Gen 28 (0.91) → Gen 30 (0.93) → Gen 20 (0.94, 9.86s) → **Gen 24 (0.94, 0.27s, current best)** — the winner is the one that made the proven pipeline cheap rather than adding more search machinery.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Add diagonal (45°) and corner-direction moves to `_local_shift_search`**: The current move set only tries ±delta along x and y axes, which cannot slide circles along the diagonal toward/away from corners where three-sided wall slack lives. Add diagonal candidates (±delta/√2, ±delta/√2) for each circle (especially the 12 perimeter circles) at each delta scale; warm-starting keeps each extra evaluation near-free, and the 0.27s budget has ~10x headroom.

2. **Seed a second full run with a corner-anchored variant and keep the best**: Build an alternative seed where the 4 corner circles start at (0.09, 0.09)-style positions with the same hex interior, run the identical pipeline (solve → shift search → final solve) on both, and return the better result. This directly targets the identified corner-radius headroom while the strict-improvement acceptance guarantees the 2.48 baseline cannot be lost; total cost should stay under ~1s with the warm-started solver.

3. **Run the shift search twice with a re-enlarge pass in between**: After the first `_local_shift_search`, re-run `solve_radii` from scratch with full iterations (200), then re-enter `_local_shift_search` at the finest deltas only (0.001, 0.0005). The insight summary notes slack remains in tiny-delta moves after re-solving; iterating shift→resolve→shift is a cheap fixed-point refinement that monotonically improves on the current best.

4. **Vectorize the Gauss-Seidel solver with NumPy to enable multi-seed search at scale**: The O(n²) Python loop over 26 circles × 60 iterations per candidate dominates evaluation cost. Restructure to a vectorized Jacobi/weighted iteration (radii = min(walls, min_j(d_ij − r_j)) with over-relaxation, e.g. ω=1.5), which converges nearly as tightly per the monotone-repair pattern, then spend the 5–10x speedup running 5–8 hex-seed variants (different row_counts: [4,5,4,5,4,4], [5,4,5,4,5,3], [4,4,5,4,5,4]) in the proven multi-start style of Gen 20 but within Gen 24's runtime.

5. **Sweep the stagger offset dx around the fixed point**: The seed hard-codes dx = s/2 = 0.1 from the equal-distance fixed point, but near-optimal packings often benefit from slightly asymmetric staggering where row-pair contacts alternate between tight and loose. Parameterize dx ∈ {0.08, 0.09, 0.10, 0.11, 0.12} per adjacent row pair (allowing alternating offsets like 0.09/0.11), triage each with a cheap solve + one shift sweep, then fully refine the top 2 — a guaranteed-not-worse generalization of the confirmed staggered-row family.
