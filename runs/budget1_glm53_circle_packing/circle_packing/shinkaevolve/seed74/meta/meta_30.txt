- **Current best (Gen-25, `dedupe_candidates_multi_start_hillclimb`, 2.41/0.91): multi-start over deduplicated hex seeds with slack-directed moves wins**: Its combination of 12 hand-curated row layouts × 3 scales (0.96/1.0/1.02), a 1.8s time-boxed multi-start hill-climb (4 starting basins), slack-directed moves toward each tight circle's largest gap with adaptive step sizing reached the round's highest sum 2.41.
- **Slack-directed moves with adaptive step schedules beat pure random perturbation**: Gen-25's weighted circle selection (weight = max(−gap, 0.01)) moves the most-constrained circle along its maximum-slack direction (blended 70% directed + 30% random), with step growth ×1.15 on success (ceiling 0.05) and stochastic shrink ×0.92/grow ×1.4 on failure. This outperformed pure stochastic/annealing variants (Gen-20, 2.38; Gen-27/28, 2.36–2.38).
- **Greedy fallback retained only as safety, never as primary path**: All correct programs accept `compute_max_radii` (proportional shrinking) only if the LP fails or underperforms — this guard preserved validity 1.00 across the entire round and prevented fallback collapses like Gen-10's 0.00.

## Ineffective Approaches
- **Reheating/annealing and row/block-shift perturbations add nothing over the plateau**: Gen-20 (simulated annealing with up to 3 reheats, 2.38), Gen-26 (row-shift + block-shift moves, 2.38), and Gen-28 (Metropolis acceptance + reheat, 2.36) all landed within 0.05 of plain hill-climbs — the 2.36–2.41 plateau is a geometry limitation, not an acceptance-strategy one.
- **Per-step full LP re-solve is the dominant runtime cost and throttles search**: Gen-30 (`multistart_lprefine_hillclimb`) collapsed to sum 0.17 (score 0.07) because its per-iteration LP solve consumed the entire 2.0s budget, leaving almost no hill-climb iterations. Gen-24's feedback likewise flagged LP re-solve per trial move as the bottleneck; the workaround (cheap approximate pre-screening before exact LP, as in Gen-24/29) preserved scores while cutting cost.
- **Fewer seeds means fewer discoveries**: Gen-27's screening-only approach (8 patterns, no local search) scored just 2.15/0.82 in 0.38s — valid and fast but ~10% worse than any hill-climb variant, proving the local search phase is essential after lattice construction.
- **Over-constrained move sets stall**: Gen-21's conservative slack-direction moves with limited multi-start got stuck at 1.34 (score 0.51), showing that directional moves without adaptive step sizes and multi-start restarts explore too little.

## Implementation Insights
- **The current best's architecture**: (1) `hex_layout` with equilateral-triangle spacing (k=√3/2, g=s·k, r0=s/2) and wide-end spacing option for short rows; (2) 12 candidate row patterns × 3 scales, each scored by exact `linprog` (HiGHS); (3) 1.8s multi-start hill-climb over the top 4 basins with slack-directed weighted selection and adaptive steps. The greedy fallback is only accepted if the LP fails or underperforms.
- **Sparse/vectorized LP assembly matters**: The best program's `lp_radii` builds dense constraint rows per pair but filters d < 1e-9; earlier vectorized CSR constructions (Gen-13/15/17/18, current best per previous insights) kept eval times at 1.46–2.5s despite hundreds of LP solves, while Gen-19's broken non-vectorized assembly scored 0.00.
- **Wide-end spacing for short rows is a marginal but real seed diversifier**: Gen-25's `wide_ends` option for rows with count < 3 (spacing min(1.6s, (1−s)/(count−1))) appeared among its candidate set, and the equilateral-triangle row spacing (g = s·√3/2, r0 = s/2) inherited from prior best geometry remained consistent.
- **Time-budgeted multi-start with shrinking per-start allocation**: The best divides remaining time as `(overall_deadline − now)/(n_starts − start)`, guaranteeing later basins get budgets; combined with `rng_s` per-start seeding, this reliably improved over single-basin climbs (2.41 vs 2.37–2.38 for single-start variants like Gen-23/26).

## Performance Analysis
- **The 2.36–2.41 plateau is now confirmed across ~10 programs**: Every correct LP + hex-seed + local-search combination lands in a 0.05 band (Gen-20: 2.38, Gen-23: 2.37, Gen-24: 2.37, Gen-25: 2.41, Gen-26: 2.38, Gen-28: 2.36, Gen-29: 2.37). The current best (Gen-25, 2.41) edges ahead through multi-start + slack-directed adaptive moves, but the ~9% gap to AlphaEvolve's 2.635 persists across all refinement strategies.
- **Score separation is entirely structural**: LP + local search: 0.90–0.91; LP screening only (Gen-27): 0.82; heuristic radii (Gen-16 prior): 0.74; conservative moves (Gen-21): 0.51; LP-per-step overspending (Gen-30): 0.07; broken LP (Gen-19): 0.00. Presence of a working, cached/cheap LP evaluation is the single strongest predictor.
- **Runtime shows clear headroom misallocation**: Best score 0.91 at 2.53s; Gen-28/29 reached 0.90 at 1.76–1.75s. Faster LP assembly or pre-screening (Gen-24's fast approximate pre-screen before exact LP) frees budget for more search without score loss — but Gen-8's prior result (3.03s multi-seed with no gain) shows more time on more seeds alone doesn't help; move quality dominates.
- **Multi-start with deduplication is the only refinement that broke 2.40 this round**: Gen-24's 12-layout expansion + top-3 basin focus scored 2.37, while Gen-25's deduplicated 12 candidates × 3 scales + 4-basin multi-start reached 2.41 — suggesting modest gains still come from seed-set breadth, though the curve is flattening (2.34 → 2.40 → 2.41 across rounds).

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Vectorize the LP assembly with precomputed pair index arrays to cut per-evaluation cost.** The current `lp_radii` rebuilds a dense (n²+n)-row constraint matrix with Python loops on every call, which is the dominant cost across the whole search. Precompute the static structure once (pair indices, wall indices), then per-call only recompute the distances `d_ij` and wall bounds, assembling A_ub via sparse COO/CSR; Gen-13/15/17/18's vectorized assembly kept hundreds of LP solves within budget. This frees ~0.5–1.0s that can be reinvested into more hill-climb iterations or a 5th/6th start basin, directly attacking the plateau without changing move strategy.

2. **Add row-shift and block-shift moves alongside the slack-directed single-circle moves.** The plateau at 2.36–2.41 across all single-circle move variants (Gen-20/23/25/26/28) suggests lattice-aligned local optima that single-center perturbations cannot escape. With ~10% probability per step, translate an entire hex row (all centers with y within 0.02 of a chosen row) horizontally by ±0.005–0.02, and with ~10% probability shift a spatially-coherent cluster (all centers within 0.25 of a chosen center) by a common delta — these preserve local triangular geometry while repositioning rigid groups, per Gen-26's early evidence that such moves are compatible with the LP framework.

3. **Expand the candidate screen with denser-edge row patterns and vertical-axis mirrored variants.** Gen-25's gain over Gen-24 came from seed-set breadth (dedup + 3 scales), and the curve is flattening — so try genuinely different structures: [6,5,5,5,6], [6,5,6,5,4], [5,6,4,6,5], plus vertically mirrored patterns of the current top performers (reflect rows top-to-bottom), which cost microseconds in the LP screen. AlphaEvolve-level 2.635 configurations reportedly favor boundary clustering, so denser outer rows (6-count edges) are the highest-value structural additions; retain the wide_ends variants for short rows.

4. **Add a cheap geometric pre-screen to skip exact LP solves for clearly-bad trial moves.** Gen-24's approximate pre-screen preserved scores while cutting cost. Before each exact LP call during the hill-climb, compute the greedy `compute_max_radii` (already implemented, ~microseconds for n=26); if its sum is below `tot − 0.01` of the current best, reject the trial immediately without the LP, since greedy rarely underestimates the LP sum by more than that margin for small perturbations. This roughly halves or thirds the LP calls per start, enabling either more steps per start or an extra start basin within the same 1.8s budget.

5. **Increase multi-start diversity by jittering per-start seeds instead of using scored basins verbatim.** Currently all 4 starts begin from exact lattice positions, so starts 2–4 often rediscover the same trajectory. Apply a small ±0.01 Gaussian jitter to each start's centers (different seed per start, consistent with Gen-8's lesson that jittered variants of a strong base beat diverse layouts), and keep the shrinking per-start budget allocation `(deadline − now)/(n_starts − start)`. Combined with the slack-directed adaptive-step move set, this cheaply decorrelates the basins and is the most direct extension of the mechanism that broke 2.40 this round.
