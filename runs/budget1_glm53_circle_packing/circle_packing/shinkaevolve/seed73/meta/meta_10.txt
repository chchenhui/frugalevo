# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns
- **Multi-candidate lattice seeding + stretch factors (current best, 0.92)**: The best program (Gen 7, "hex_refine_crossover", sum_radii=2.42) evaluates 4 row-count layouts × 3 stretch factors ([1.0, 1.03, 1.06]) and keeps the best refined result. This enumeration over initial layouts is what pushed it past single-layout programs (Gen 3–6, all 2.36–2.37, score 0.90).
- **Gauss-Seidel feasible radius growth**: Growing radii from 0.25·r0 capped by wall distance and (neighbor distance − neighbor radius) is the shared core of all top performers (Gen 3: 2.36, Gen 4: 2.37, Gen 7: 2.42). It guarantees validity (1.00 everywhere) while letting circles expand into unused space.
- **Accept-only-improvement refinement with early exploration**: Gen 7's `refine_layout` keeps only configurations with higher total radius, but accepts non-improving moves in the first half of iterations — this balance of exploitation/exploration yielded the best deterministic nudging result.
- **Greedy expansion sweeps exploit corner/boundary space**: Gen 4 (hex_rows_greedy_expansion, 2.37) explicitly noted greedy expansion captures corner space left by pure hex lattices, adding ~0.01–0.06 over uniform-radius baselines.

## Ineffective Approaches
- **Unconditional `visualize()` execution**: Gen 2 (hexagonal_lattice_packing_26) scored 0.00 across all metrics purely because the unconditional matplotlib call failed at evaluation time, despite a mathematically sound lattice (r ≈ 0.2735, sum ≈ 7.11 would have been a strong layout). Visualization must be guarded.
- **Pairwise proportional shrinking as the radius solver**: Gen 0 (concentric-ring, 0.96 sum, score 0.36) and Gen 5 (relaxation, 1.34, score 0.51) both used proportional shrink-to-non-overlap; it consistently under-allocates versus growth-based methods.
- **Fixed uniform-radius lattices without refinement**: Gen 10's rigid 5-row staggered layout with a conservative 0.98 slack factor reached only 1.70 (score 0.65); pure construction without growth/refinement wastes space.
- **Ad-hoc corner fillers and crude truncation**: Gen 9 (hex_lattice_plus_corner_filler) placed corner circles at arbitrary c=0.10 and truncated the core, dropping to 2.10 (score 0.80) — ~20% below the best.
- **Uniform-radius relaxation targeting**: Gen 5 relaxed centers assuming uniform r0, then recomputed radii afterward — misaligned objectives cost it dearly (1.34 vs 2.37 for growth-based peers).

## Implementation Insights
- **The current best combines the two proven winners**: lattice enumeration (multiple count_sets) + deterministic center nudging + Gauss-Seidel growth (`grow_radii`). Its `refine_layout` pushes each circle away from its nearest neighbor (step decaying from 0.02) while pulling it toward the nearest wall, using `np.clip(0.005, 0.995)` bounds.
- **Diversifying initial layouts beats deepening local search**: Gen 6 (hex_layout_refinement_loop, single layout + nudging, 2.36) vs Gen 7 (multiple layouts + same nudging, 2.42) — the +0.06 gain came from layout diversity, not stronger refinement.
- **Wall-pull term matters**: the 0.5·step pull-toward-wall in the best program's refinement captures boundary free space that pure inter-circle repulsion (Gen 5, Gen 8) misses.
- **Speed is not a bottleneck**: best program runs in 0.87s; Gen 8's 150 annealed steps took 5.55s for the same 2.37 score — expensive relaxation loops buy nothing here.

## Performance Analysis
- **Score progression across generations**: 0.36 (Gen 0 rings) → 0.90 (Gen 3–4 hex + greedy growth) → 0.92 (Gen 7 multi-layout + refinement). All validity=1.00, so all gains came from sum_radii (0.96 → 2.36 → 2.42).
- **Best vs. rest**: Gen 7's 2.42 exceeds the hex-lattice plateau (2.36–2.37 shared by Gens 3, 4, 6, 8), confirming the multi-candidate stretch enumeration is the differentiator. It remains ~8% below the AlphaEvolve benchmark of 2.635 (target_ratio 0.92).
- **Correlation**: programs using growth-based radius computation consistently land at 2.10–2.42; programs using proportional shrinking land at 0.96–1.70; programs mixing relaxation with uniform-radius assumptions (Gen 5) degrade even below pure hex (1.34).
- **A single execution bug zeroes everything**: Gen 2's 0.00 despite strong math shows evaluation robustness (guarding side effects) is a precondition for any algorithmic quality.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Add randomized multi-start perturbations to the layout pool**: Keep the 4×3 lattice enumeration but add ~10–20 perturbed variants (each candidate with small Gaussian jitter of ±0.01–0.02 on centers, seeded deterministically) before refinement. Evidence shows layout diversity (not deeper local search) drove Gen 7's +0.06 gain over Gen 6, so cheaply broadening the candidate set is the highest-leverage extension.

2. **Insert greedy insertion of extra circles after growth**: After `grow_radii` converges, identify the largest void (e.g., grid-sample the square and find the point maximizing distance to all circle boundaries and walls), insert a new circle of that maximal radius if it exceeds ~0.05, and re-run `refine_layout` on 27 centers — then drop the smallest circle if n must stay 26. Gen 4 showed greedy boundary capture adds +0.01–0.06; void-filling is its natural generalization for interior space.

3. **Make the wall-pull and neighbor-push weights adaptive**: Replace the fixed `0.5 * step` wall-pull with a weight proportional to the circle's current radius deficit versus its neighbors (small circles get pulled to walls harder), and push away from the *closest overlapping-or-tangent* neighbor rather than nearest overall. The wall-pull term is a proven differentiator; tuning its balance per-circle is an incremental path past the 2.42 plateau.

4. **Try a final "contact relaxation" pass after refinement**: After the best configuration is found, run a few iterations where each circle is moved to equalize its gap to its two nearest constraints (neighbor or wall), maximizing its own grown radius — a Voronoi-like local maximization. This directly targets sum_radii at convergence rather than only accepting aggregate improvements, and costs almost nothing given the 0.87s runtime budget.

5. **Enumerate non-hexagonal base lattices alongside hex**: Add square-lattice and mixed-row (e.g., alternating 5/3 rows with larger vertical spacing) candidates with their own stretch factors to the `count_sets` loop. All top programs derive from hex packing only; since the refinement machinery is layout-agnostic, diversifying the *topology* — not just stretch — is an untested axis with the same evidence base as the successful stretch enumeration.
