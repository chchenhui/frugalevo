# Recommendations for Future Program Mutations

1. **Drop the COBYLA stage entirely and adopt top-k basin deduplication with polishing.** Gen 42 scored identical 0.99 (2.61) in 4.74s vs. this program's 51.06s by polishing only the top-5 distinct basins (deduplicated at ~0.05 center-distance threshold). Refactor `construct_packing` to run cheap SLSQP on all candidates, cluster results, then run high-budget SLSQP restarts (maxiter=500, ftol=1e-14) only on the top-k distinct basins — this frees ~45s of runtime budget for the gap-closing restarts in recommendation 2.

2. **Add iterative SLSQP restart ("polish") passes from repaired endpoints.** After `_refine` returns, feed the repaired `(centers, radii)` back as `z0` into a second SLSQP call (maxiter=500, ftol=1e-14), repair again, and repeat 2–3 times or until improvement stalls below 1e-6. Restarting from a feasible, near-optimal point lets SLSQP make fine tangency adjustments unreachable from a crude lattice seed — the most direct route to closing the ~0.025 gap to AlphaEvolve's 2.635.

3. **Vectorize `_cons_all` with `np.triu_indices` and supply an analytic Jacobian.** Replace the O(n²) Python loop with `iu = np.triu_indices(26, 1)` computing `gaps = d[iu] - r[iu[0]] - r[iu[1]] - EPS`, and provide `jac` in the constraint dict (walls Jacobian is ±1/I; pairwise rows have -1 on each center coordinate and -1 on each radius). SLSQP with numerical gradients evaluates constraints ~79 times per iteration; analytic gradients improve final precision at tangency and cut runtime substantially, freeing budget for more seeds and restarts.

4. **Expand jittered-seed diversity across all valid row-set layouts, not just `cands[0]`.** Generate 2–3 deterministic Gaussian jitters (σ≈0.01–0.02, distinct fixed rng seeds) for *each* of the 9 valid row-set × 2 stretch layouts, plus radius-level diversification (scale `r_init` by 0.9/1.0/1.1). Gen 25 (fixed seeds, 0.64) vs. Gen 31/35 (jittered multi-start, 0.99) proves seed diversity gates SLSQP success, and feedback from Gen 28/34/37 all points to seed-geometry diversity — not solver depth — as the remaining bottleneck to exceed 2.61.

5. **Add post-selection greedy void-filling with SLSQP re-optimization.** After selecting the best candidate, grid-sample the unit square (e.g., 40×40), compute each point's max inscribed radius (distance to nearest circle boundary and walls), and if any void admits r > ~0.03, insert it as a 27th center, re-run SLSQP over all 27 variables with the same vectorized constraints, then drop the smallest-radius circle and repair back to n=26. This is the interior-void generalization of the greedy boundary capture known to add +0.01–0.06, with SLSQP locally reshuffling neighbors far better than the push heuristics that plateau at 2.42.
