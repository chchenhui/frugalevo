# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns
- **Joint center/radius SLSQP with true objective (current best, 0.99)**: The current best program (Gen 14, "slsqp_multistart_layouts", sum_radii=2.61) is the first to directly maximize the sum of radii via gradient-based constrained optimization (SLSQP with explicit wall + pairwise-gap inequality constraints), jumping from the previous plateau of 2.42 (Gen 16) to 2.61 — nearly closing the AlphaEvolve gap (2.635).
- **Multi-start lattice diversity remains essential**: Gen 14 generates diverse hex-lattice row-count sets plus deterministic jittered variants, then SLSQP-refines each. This confirms and extends the prior insight that layout diversity (Gen 7, 2.42; Gen 16, 2.42) beats deepening local search — the +0.19 gain came from upgrading the refinement engine while keeping the multi-start seeding.
- **Robust feasibility repair guarantees validity**: Gen 14's iterative shrink-based repair step rescues slightly infeasible SLSQP endpoints, ensuring validity 1.00 even when the optimizer terminates marginally infeasible. This de-risks the switch to nonlinear solvers.
- **Gauss-Seidel growth for initialization**: Both Gen 14 (r_init via iterative min of wall/neighbor slack) and prior top performers (Gen 3/4/7/16, 2.36–2.42) use growth-based radius computation for feasible starting points — growth consistently beats shrinking (see below).

## Ineffective Approaches
- **Proportional pairwise shrinking as the radius solver**: Gen 15 (0.30, score 0.11), Gen 19 (0.32, score 0.12), Gen 13 (0.22, score 0.08) all collapse radii to near-zero because a circle in many overlapping pairs gets multiplied by many scale factors <1 in one sweep. This confirms the earlier Gen 0/Gen 5 pattern — shrinking lands at 0.22–0.32 vs. 2.10–2.61 for growth-based peers.
- **Conservative greedy relaxation dynamics**: Gen 11's damped growth (factor 1.4) with tiny decaying relaxation steps (max 0.012) capped out at 2.29 (score 0.87) — feasible but far from optimal; under-aggressive step sizes limit convergence.
- **Crude single-neighbor contact relaxation**: Gen 18 and Gen 21 (both 1.60, score 0.61) use contact relaxation that only considers the nearest neighbor and moves mostly along x — stuck in poor local optima vs. 2.42 for the same lattice seeds with better refinement (Gen 16).
- **Too-small lattice spacing ranges**: Gen 19's spacing candidates (0.14–0.19) force r ≈ s/2 with no growth beyond spacing, capping the sum at 0.32 despite correct relaxation logic.

## Implementation Insights
- **Direct objective optimization beats heuristics**: Gen 14 encodes the exact problem (maximize Σr subject to wall/pairwise constraints) as SLSQP over a 78-dim vector (x, y, r), replacing the nudging/growth heuristics of Gen 16 (greedy neighbor-push, wall-pull, Gauss-Seidel growth) that had plateaued at 2.42. The repair step (`_repair`) plus `try/except` around each candidate guarantees the program never returns invalid output.
- **Constraint vectorization with EPS margin**: Gen 14's `_cons_all` builds all constraints as one vectorized function with EPS = 1e-9 margin in pairwise gaps — this likely also fixes the tangency bug that zeroed Gen 12 (multistart_jitter_refinement, score 0.00), which retained epsilon-level invalid configurations as "best."
- **Execution robustness is a hard precondition**: Gen 12's total failure (0.00 despite sound math) and Gen 2's earlier unconditional `visualize()` failure both show that guarded side effects and strict validity checking must wrap any optimization pipeline.
- **Speed trade-off accepted**: Gen 14's 6.12s is the slowest correct program, but prior evidence (Gen 8, 5.55s for 2.37) shows runtime isn't the bottleneck; only score matters here.

## Performance Analysis
- **Score progression**: 0.36 (Gen 0 rings) → 0.90 (Gen 3–4 hex + growth) → 0.92 (Gen 7/16 multi-layout, 2.42) → 0.99 (Gen 14 SLSQP, 2.61). Every gain in this batch came from better optimization of the same hex-lattice seeds, not better seeds.
- **Optimization-engine hierarchy on identical lattice layouts**: proportional shrink (0.22–0.32) < crude contact relaxation (1.60) < damped greedy growth (2.29) < greedy nudge + Gauss-Seidel growth (2.42) < joint SLSQP (2.61). The layout family (staggered hex rows like [5,4,5,4,5,3]/[4,5,4,5,4,4]) is common to nearly all — the refinement method determines ~10x score spread.
- **Current best vs. reference**: Gen 14 reaches target_ratio 0.99 vs. AlphaEvolve's 2.635; remaining gap (~0.025) likely requires more restarts or alternative optimizers per Gen 14's feedback.
- **Correlation confirmed across the batch**: all validity-1.00 programs cluster by radius solver; the only 0.00 (Gen 12) was an implementation/validity bug, underscoring that robustness gates everything.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Add more SLSQP restarts from repaired endpoints ("polish" passes)**: After the first SLSQP + repair of each candidate, re-run SLSQP a second time using the repaired (centers, radii) as z0 with maxiter raised to 500. Gen 14's feedback notes the remaining ~0.025 gap to 2.635 likely needs more restarts; restarting from a feasible, nearly-optimal point lets SLSQP make fine-grained contact adjustments that a single pass from a crude lattice seed cannot reach. This is purely incremental to the proven best program.

2. **Vectorize the pairwise gap constraints (kill the Python double loop in `_cons_all`)**: Replace the O(n²) Python loop building `gaps` with `iu = np.triu_indices(26, 1)` and `gaps = d[iu] - r[iu[0]] - r[iu[1]] - EPS`, and provide an analytic Jacobian (sparse or full) via `cons={'type':'ineq','fun':_cons_all,'jac':_cons_jac}`. SLSQP with numerical gradients re-evaluates the constraint function ~79 times per iteration — supplying the analytic Jacobian both speeds this up and improves convergence precision, freeing the runtime budget (currently 6.12s) for more restarts per recommendation 1.

3. **Add a trust-constr / COBYLA refinement variant per candidate**: Run a second optimizer (e.g., `scipy.optimize.minimize(..., method='trust-constr')` with `LinearConstraint`-style bound handling, or COBYQA/COBYLA on a penalized objective) on each candidate in addition to SLSQP, keeping whichever yields the higher repaired sum. The engine hierarchy shows refinement method dominates score spread (2.42→2.61); testing an alternative nonlinear solver on the identical seed pool is the highest-leverage untested axis within the proven framework. Keep the `_repair` wrapper on every solver's output.

4. **Expand the jittered variant pool with per-variant seeds from all valid row sets**: Currently only 4 jittered variants are generated from `cands[0]`. Instead, generate 2–3 deterministic jittered variants (Gaussian σ≈0.01–0.02, different fixed rng seeds) for *each* of the valid row-set layouts, and add radius-level jitter (multiply initial `r_init` by a small stretch factor 0.9–1.1) as an additional diversification axis. Gen 7/14 both confirm multi-start breadth beats deepening local search, and this cheaply doubles or triples candidate diversity without new machinery.

5. **Insert a greedy void-filling + re-refinement step after selecting the best candidate**: After the best SLSQP result is found, grid-sample the unit square (e.g., 40×40), compute the max inscribed radius at each point (distance to nearest circle boundary and walls), and if any void admits a circle with r > ~0.03, add it as a 27th center, re-run SLSQP over 27 circles, then drop the smallest-radius circle and repair back to n=26. Gen 4 showed greedy boundary capture adds +0.01–0.06; interior void-filling is the untested generalization, and SLSQP can locally reshuffle neighbors around the new circle far better than the greedy push heuristics that plateaued at 2.42.
