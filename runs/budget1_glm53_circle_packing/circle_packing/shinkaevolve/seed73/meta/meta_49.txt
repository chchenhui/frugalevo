# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- **Two-budget SLSQP (cheap screening pass → high-budget polish on deduplicated top-5 basins) is the current best architecture**: Gen 47 (`topk_basin_dedup_polish`) achieved the batch's highest sum_radii of **2.62** (combined score 0.99, validity 1.00, 8.15s) using a cheap SLSQP pass (maxiter=150) on each of ~12 row layouts, basin deduplication at a 0.05 max center-distance threshold, then up to 6 high-budget SLSQP restarts (maxiter=600, ftol=1e-14) per basin. This is the first program to break the long-standing 2.61 fixed point, confirming prior insight that multi-basin polish (Gen 42) was the right direction and adding a coarse-to-fine budget schedule on top.
- **Polishing multiple top seeds beats polishing only the best crude seed**: Gen 44 (`polish_top_candidates`) explicitly noted "seed quality is a noisy predictor of post-refinement quality" — it scored 0.99 (2.61, 13.56s) polishing top-3 seeds. Gen 41, 45, 46, 47, and 48 all replicate this (0.99 each), confirming the multi-basin/top-k polish pattern from the previous batch remains the winning structure.
- **Guaranteed-feasibility repair remains essential and free**: Every 0.99-scoring program in this batch (Gen 41, 44, 45, 46, 47, 48) uses a `_repair`-style shrink loop or Gauss-Seidel radius regrowth to guarantee validity 1.00. Gen 47's `_repair` iteratively shrinks radii against pairwise gaps until `violation.max() <= -1e-12`, matching the exact pattern validated in prior batches.
- **Widening the seed pool to 12 row-count patterns contributes to the 2.62 breakthrough**: Gen 47 uses 12 row sets (e.g., [5,4,5,4,5,3]) × 2 stretch factors plus jittered variants of 6 bases — more layout diversity than Gen 41's 8 patterns × 3 stretches, consistent with prior feedback that "seed geometry diversity, not solver depth, is the remaining bottleneck."

## Ineffective Approaches

- **Unguarded, fragile solver pipelines fail totally (0.00)**: Gen 43 (`expand_jitter_pool_all_layouts`) scored 0.00 across all metrics despite elaborate multi-start refinement — no valid output was produced (likely `best` remaining None or exception propagation), and the high-dimensional SLSQP problem (78 variables, ~351 constraints) with small `ftol` and limited iterations lacked a guaranteed-valid fallback packing. This reinforces prior insight from Gen 27 and 30: per-candidate try/except and a guaranteed-feasible fallback gate all scores.
- **Over-engineered constrained optimization with poor seeds wastes enormous compute for bad results**: Gen 39 (`slsqp_trustconstr_multistart_refiner`) achieved only sum_radii=0.78 (combined 0.30) in 96.3s — the heaviest pipeline in the batch (trust-constr/SLSQP with analytic Jacobians, 250-iteration refines) got stuck in poor local optima because its seeds lacked the diversity needed to find dense configurations. More solver machinery cannot compensate for bad attractor basins.
- **Conservative validity-first radius models sacrifice massive quality**: Gen 17 (`force_refine_hex_rows`) achieved sum_radii of only 1.72 (combined 0.65) despite validity 1.00 and 0.14s runtime — its greedy pairwise-shrink with eps margins and equal-share overlap splitting guarantees feasibility but caps density far below the 2.635 target, confirming prior insight that heuristic/greedy-only methods cannot approach the optimum.
- **COBYLA stages add no score and heavy runtime**: Gen 41 (`trust_constr_dual_solver_pack`) and Gen 44 both include penalized COBYLA second stages; Gen 41 took 17.65s while Gen 46 (`topk_basin_dedupe_polish`) reached the same 0.99 in 5.25s with no COBYLA — echoing the prior batch finding that COBYLA's contribution is "modest" at 10x cost.

## Implementation Insights

- **The current best program's key structural detail — basin deduplication via max center-distance**: Gen 47's `_basin_key` treats two configurations as the same basin if `max(||centers - c0||) < 0.05`, keeping only the top-5 distinct basins for expensive polish. This concentrates the high-budget restarts on genuinely different attractor basins rather than wasting them on near-duplicate refinements of the same lattice — the same pattern that gave Gen 42 its 10x speedup, now extended with a two-tier budget schedule.
- **Early-exit polish rounds avoid wasted compute**: Gen 47's `_polish` breaks out of its restart loop when a round fails to improve the best sum by more than 1e-12, contributing to its 8.15s runtime; Gen 46's heuristic-refine + Gauss-Seidel + top-5 basin polish achieves the same 0.99 in 5.25s, showing the pipeline stages are composable for speed.
- **Try/except per candidate + `_repair` per solver pass keeps robustness**: Gen 47 wraps each `_refine` in try/except and repairs after every SLSQP pass; contrast Gen 43's total 0.00 failure without such guarding. The bounds pattern `[(0.002, 0.998)]*(2N) + [(0.001, 0.5)]*N` plus EPS=1e-9 gap margins is shared across all 0.99 programs.
- **Simple seed generation scales better than fancy joint refinement**: Gen 39's analytic-Jacobian trust-constr approach (0.78, 96.3s) vs. Gen 47's clipped hex lattices + standard SLSQP (2.62, 8.15s) shows that investing compute in seed breadth and polish restarts, not solver sophistication, drives quality.

## Performance Analysis

- **Gen 47 broke the 2.61 ceiling to 2.62, closing the gap to AlphaEvolve's 2.635 to ~0.015 (~0.6%)**: The prior batch had every SLSQP-polished program stuck at exactly 2.61; Gen 47's combination of 12-row-pattern seed diversity, two-tier polish budgets, and basin deduplication is the first evidence the plateau is soft, not structural. - **The remaining ~0.015 gap to 2.635 consistently traces to seed geometry**: Feedback from Gen 41, 44, 46, 47, and 48 uniformly points to "seed diversity" or "finer tangency adjustment" as the gap-closer, never solver depth — Gen 47's marginal improvement over its 8-pattern predecessors is direct evidence that expanding the row-pattern pool is the lever that moves the final digits.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Expand the row-pattern seed pool with denser, larger-radius layouts.** Gen 47's breakthrough (2.62) came from widening the pool from 8 to 12 row patterns; extend this lever by enumerating *all* row sets summing to 26 with rows of 3–6 circles (there are dozens beyond the current 12, e.g. [6,4,5,4,4,3], [6,5,4,5,3,3], [4,4,4,5,4,5]), each with 2 stretch factors, and raising the base radius to a larger `min(1/10, 1/(2+5*SQRT3)) * 1.15` so seeds start closer to the tangency frontier. Seed breadth — not solver depth — is the consistently identified gap-closer.

2. **Apply jitter diversification to all lattice candidates, not just the first 6.** Currently only `cands[:6]` get 2 jittered variants each. Generate 2–3 deterministic jittered variants (σ≈0.012–0.02, distinct fixed rng seeds) for every row-set × stretch layout, plus radius-level diversification (scale initial radii by 0.9/1.0/1.1 in `_refine`). Gen 47's marginal gain over 8-pattern predecessors shows each added distinct basin is another chance at the optimum.

3. **Widen the basin-dedup funnel: keep top-8 basins and lower the dedup threshold.** Gen 47 keeps only top-5 basins at `thresh=0.05`; with the runtime freed by dropping any COBYLA-like stages (Gen 46 hit 0.99 in 5.25s with this structure), raise the basin cap to 8 and try `thresh=0.03` to admit more genuinely distinct geometries into the expensive polish stage. The polish early-exit (`break` on <1e-12 improvement) already prevents wasted restarts, so more basins costs little.

4. **Add annealed-polish perturbation restarts when the polish loop stalls.** In `_polish`, when a restart fails to improve, instead of breaking immediately, perturb `z_cur` with small deterministic jitter (σ≈0.003–0.008 on centers, one rng seed per basin) and continue for up to 2 extra rounds before giving up. This targets the "finer tangency adjustment" repeatedly cited as the ~0.015 gap-closer, letting SLSQP hop between nearly-degenerate optimal configurations reachable only via small feasible perturbations.

5. **Add a post-selection void-fill + SLSQP reshuffle pass.** After selecting the best basin, grid-sample the unit square (e.g., 40×40), compute each point's max inscribed radius (distance to nearest circle boundary and walls), and if any void admits r > ~0.03, swap it for the smallest-radius circle, re-run one high-budget SLSQP (maxiter=600, ftol=1e-14) over all 78 variables, then `_repair`. Wrap in try/except with the current best kept as fallback — guarding is mandatory per Gen 43's total 0.00 failure.
