# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- **The 0.14 tier now has three independent confirmations (Gen 12, 19, 23) sharing the same recipe**: (1) greedy sorted-descending packing filling each pack to exactly n/m items, (2) a *bounded* swap refinement between heaviest/lightest packs, and (3) `rebalance_experts` policy selection computing both global (num_groups=1, num_nodes=1) and hierarchical layouts and picking whichever minimizes per-GPU squared-deviation imbalance. The current best (Gen 23, "cheap_boundary_swap_refinement") hits combined 0.14 with GPU 0.76, expert 0.29 — identical to Gen 19's profile, showing this combination is a stable, reproducible optimum, not noise.
- **Bounded swap refinement (K=2 boundary items, O(1) per iteration) matches full pairwise-search quality at lower cost**: Gen 23's `boundary()` helper restricting candidate swaps to the 2 lightest items of the heaviest pack and 2 heaviest of the lightest pack reaches GPU 0.76 while keeping times_algorithm at 0.04 — comparable to or faster than the unbounded/20-iteration variants (Gen 14's times_algorithm 0.49, Gen 11/19's slower Python loops) that score no better.
- **The vectorized GPU-imbalance policy selector remains the highest-leverage single component**: it alone lifted GPU balancedness 0.66 → 0.76 in Gen 12, 19, and now Gen 23, and is the only mechanism in the entire history that moved expert balancedness (0.25 → 0.29).
- **Preserving the validated DeepSeek three-stage structure (groups→nodes packing, within-node replication, experts→GPUs packing) with the scatter-based `inverse()` helper and global fallback is mandatory**: all correct programs (Gen 9, 16, 23 at 0.12–0.14) retain it; the only structural rewrite attempted (Gen 13 water-filling) crashed to 0.00.

## Ineffective Approaches

- **Any per-item Python-loop packing caps the speed score at 0.00 regardless of quality**: Gen 23 (list-based greedy + Python swap loop, times_algorithm 0.04), Gen 9 (vectorized refinement but per-item loops, 0.01), and Gen 16 (argmin-over-packs loop per item, 0.01) all scored speed 0.00. Gen 16's vectorization-across-layers still left per-item iteration, confirming the loop over items — not layers — is the runtime bottleneck.
- **Pack-level refinement cannot fix expert-level balancedness**: Gen 9's coarse+fine swap refinement, Gen 16's argmin greedy with capacity constraints, and Gen 23's boundary swaps all leave expert balancedness at 0.24–0.29. Only policy selection (Gen 12/19/23) nudges it to 0.29; the replication stage (`replicate_experts` greedy max of `weight/logcnt`) is the untouched bottleneck.
- **Vectorizing replication/replica-expansion without respecting tensor API constraints is fatal**: Gen 13 ("waterfilling_lpt_swap") scored 0.00 across all metrics because `torch.repeat_interleave` was called with a 2D `cnt` tensor ("repeats must be 0-dim or 1-dim"), halting the entire pipeline before any scoring. This echoes Gen 17/22's 0.00 failures from index/dtype errors in rewritten replication code — 4 of the summarized rewrites died this way.
- **LPT seed packing without GPU-imbalance policy selection plateaus at GPU 0.66 / combined 0.12–0.13**: Gen 9 and Gen 16 both improved pack-level balance over baseline but never crossed 0.13 combined, showing swap refinement alone is insufficient without the policy-comparison layer.

## Implementation Insights

- **The current best's differentiators stack three cheap mechanisms**: (1) `balanced_packing` sorts descending, assigns each item to the lightest non-full pack via `min(candidates, key=pack_weights.__getitem__)`, then runs boundary-swap refinement with `boundary(p, heavy)` sorting only pack members by weight and taking top-K; (2) `rebalance_experts` always computes the global policy and selects via `gpu_imbalance()` — a fully vectorized single expression: `(weight / logcnt).gather(-1, phy2log)`, reshape to `[layers, gpus, -1]`, sum, squared deviation from mean; (3) the early-exit for `groups_per_pack == 1` and scatter-based `inverse()` are preserved correctness anchors.
- **The swap update is done correctly in-place in Python structures** (pack_members list mutation, pack_weights delta updates), avoiding re-sorting pack members every iteration — this is why Gen 23's refinement runs at times_algorithm 0.04 while earlier refinement variants cost 0.07–0.49.
- **The expert-balance bottleneck is precisely `replicate_experts`'s greedy loop**: `redundant_indices = (weight / logcnt).max(dim=-1).indices` iterated `num_phy - num_log` times, identical across Gen 9, 16, and 23. Gen 13's water-filling replica counts were the right *conceptual* fix but failed on 2D `repeat_interleave`; the safe fix is the documented flattening pattern `repeat_interleave(arange(rows).view(-1), cnt.view(-1))` or a per-layer loop.
- **Batched-tensor LPT with capacity masking (Gen 20's template, times_algorithm 0.02) is the known speed headroom**: masked `argmin(-1)` across all layers in lockstep achieves 3.5× faster algorithm time than Gen 23's 0.04 with identical balancedness, but Gen 16's per-item-loop version shows the masking must eliminate the item loop entirely to matter.

## Performance Analysis

- **Score ordering across the four programs: Gen 23 (0.14) > Gen 9 (0.13) > Gen 16 (0.12) > Gen 13 (0.00)**. The gap between 0.13 and 0.14 is entirely GPU balancedness (0.66→0.76) from policy selection; Gen 23's boundary-swap refinement plus policy selection ties the historical best while cutting times_algorithm from 0.07 to 0.04.
- **Expert balancedness is stuck in a narrow band (0.24–0.29) across every correct variant**: Gen 16 (0.24, argmin greedy), Gen 9 (0.25, LPT+swap), Gen 23 (0.29, policy selection). Combined score correlates almost entirely with the balancedness components; speed_score is 0.00 for all correct programs (max observed 0.01 for Gen 20's 0.02s runtime).
- **Inference time is nearly constant (~0.43–0.47) across all correct programs** (Gen 9: 0.43, Gen 16: 0.43, Gen 23: 0.47), suggesting inference time is dominated by the layout structure rather than algorithmic choices — policy selection slightly increases it without hurting the combined score.
- **Failure asymmetry is confirmed and sharpened**: every program that modified only the packing/refinement/policy layers on top of the validated structure passed validation (Gen 9, 16, 23); every program that rewrote replication or replica-expansion (Gen 13, 17, 22) scored exactly 0.00 from a single tensor-shape/dtype error before any quality measurement. The safest high-value frontier is therefore replication *strategy* changes implemented conservatively (loop-based, invariant-checked per row), not aggressive vectorization.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

# Recommendations for Future Mutations

1. **Implement the LPT-based replication rewrite in `replicate_experts` using the safe row-loop pattern**: The expert-balance bottleneck (stuck at 0.24–0.29 across all correct variants) is the `(weight / logcnt).max(dim=-1).indices` greedy loop. Replace it with LPT semantics: sort logical experts by weight descending per row, and assign each redundant replica to the replica-group with minimum *post-replication* load (masked `argmin` over `weight / (logcnt + 1)` candidates, excluding already-at-max-count experts). Critically, keep the existing per-row `logcnt` bookkeeping with the invariant `logcnt.sum(-1) == num_phy`, and do NOT use `torch.repeat_interleave` with 2D tensors (Gen 13's fatal 0.00 crash) — the current assignment loop over `range(num_log, num_phy)` is small (num_redundant iterations) and is not the runtime bottleneck.

2. **Port Gen 20's batched-tensor LPT into `balanced_packing` while keeping the K=2 boundary-swap refinement as a post-pass**: Replace the per-item Python loop (`min(candidates, key=...)` over each group) with masked-argmin LPT across all layers in lockstep: `packs = torch.full((num_layers, num_packs), inf)` plus a capacity mask that fills full packs with `inf`, then `argmin(-1)` selects the lightest non-full pack for the current sorted item across all rows simultaneously. Gen 20 proved this achieves times_algorithm 0.02 (3.5× faster) with identical balancedness; run the existing validated boundary-swap refinement (capped at 2–3 rounds, since 20 iterations gained nothing) on the result in Python to preserve GPU 0.76. This is the only known path to a nonzero speed_score.

3. **Add an expert-imbalance term to the policy-selection objective in `rebalance_experts`**: The GPU-imbalance selector (`gpu_imbalance`) delivered the only GPU-balance gains in history (0.66→0.76) and the only expert-balance movement (0.25→0.29). Extend it with a companion `expert_imbalance(logcnt)` metric — squared deviation of `weight / logcnt` per logical expert from its layer mean, fully vectorized — and select between the global and hierarchical policies by a weighted sum (e.g., `gpu_imbalance + λ * expert_imbalance`, tune λ ∈ {0.25, 0.5, 1.0}). This reuses validated plumbing, adds zero Python loops, and directly attacks the 0.29 expert ceiling that pure refinement has never moved.

4. **Add a cheap replica-migration post-pass between Steps 2 and 3 in `rebalance_experts_hierarchical`**: After `replicate_experts` fixes `mlogcnt` but before GPU packing, compute per-replica loads (`tokens_per_mlog / mlogcnt`) and perform 1–2 vectorized swap moves: find the most-loaded replicated logical expert and the least-loaded expert per row, decrement/increment their `mlogcnt` accordingly (one masked `scatter_add_`/`scatter_sub_` pair), then rebuild `phy2mlog`/`phyrank` via the validated gather/scatter plumbing. Keep moves predicate-masked and invariant-checked (`mlogcnt >= 1`, `mlogcnt.sum(-1) == num_phy // num_nodes`) to avoid the Gen 17/22 crash mode — 4 of the summarized structural rewrites died on replication code, so implement conservatively with per-row assertions.

5. **Make policy selection per-layer instead of program-global**: Currently `rebalance_experts` picks one policy (global vs. hierarchical) for all layers via a single summed `gpu_imbalance` scalar. Since the imbalance is already computed per layer (`[num_layers, num_gpus]` loads), compute the per-layer squared-deviation vector for both policies and assemble a hybrid output row-by-row using `torch.where`: for each layer, take whichever policy's row has lower deviation, gathering the corresponding rows of `phy2log`, `phyrank`, `logcnt` (and propagating to `log2phy` scatter) via a boolean mask. This is a purely vectorized, low-risk extension of the mechanism that produced the only GPU-balance improvement in history, and can only weakly decrease the selection objective — it cannot make any layer worse than the current single-policy choice.

**Priority note**: Recommendations 2 and 3 carry the highest expected value per risk. Rec 2 targets the only observed headroom in speed_score (Gen 20's 0.02s runtime proves the batched LPT works and validated at identical quality), while Rec 3 targets the expert balancedness ceiling using the proven policy-selection mechanism. Recs 1 and 4 touch the replication stage — the highest-leverage but also highest-risk area (all 4 structural rewrites that touched replication scored 0.00), so prototype them with conservative loop-based implementations and per-row invariant checks before any vectorization.
