# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns
- **NEW BEST: True-makespan greedy construction + swap/reinsertion local search (Gen 23, 3649.64, makespan 273.00)** — replaces the long-standing champion Gen 5 (3597.12, 277.00) after 19 generations. The decisive change: greedy construction evaluates candidates with the *true* incremental makespan cost (`workload.get_opt_seq_cost(txn_seq + [t])`) rather than heuristic estimates, then applies one intensive local search pass (exhaustive pairwise swaps + first-improvement reinsertion, up to 30 rounds) on the single best greedy solution.
- **Running local search only once, on the best of many restarts, is more efficient than refining every restart**: Gen 23 executes 20-40 cheap randomized greedy restarts, then spends the expensive local search budget on one solution. Gen 24 (refining per restart, capped at 45 restarts) scored 3610.11 — good, but 40 points behind despite deeper refinement cycles.
- **Hybrid two-neighborhood local search (swap + reinsertion) is confirmed effective**: Gen 28 (3472.22, 287.00) and Gen 23 both combine swap sweeps with reinsertion; the pairing escapes single-neighborhood local optima and outperforms pure insertion approaches (Gen 22: 3401.36/293.00).
- **Simulated annealing remains a competitive but not superior escape mechanism**: Gen 27 (3412.97, 292.00) and Gen 22 (3401.36) show SA acceptance + global-best tracking yields valid strong schedules, but deterministic best-improvement swap+reinsertion (Gen 23) wins.

## Ineffective Approaches
- **Excessive time budgets still kill correctness**: Gen 20 (100s budget, up to 200 restarts, full-sequence cost evaluations) scored 0.0 — feedback cites timeouts and a possible `min_txn` assertion break; Gen 21 (beam search + GRASP, full `seq_cost` per candidate) also scored 0.0 for the same reason. Programs fitting comfortably in the 25s-per-workload budget (Gens 22-28) all passed.
- **Near-random greedy sampling with high skip rates degrades construction**: Gen 20's 90% random-skip rate left most steps without greedy evaluation, producing invalid schedules; Gen 23's inverted design (95% *greedy* evaluation rate) with true cost checks is the winning formulation.
- **Small time budgets forfeit quality, not just safety**: Gen 25's ~2.5s budget (2857.14, 349.00 makespan) was fully valid but 792 points below Gen 23 — under-budgeting is the costliest non-fatal error, worse than any neighborhood choice.
- **Windowed/partial insertion neighborhoods cap quality**: Gen 26 and Gen 31 (both 3246.75, 307.00) restricted insertion to ±10 nearby positions plus 6 random slots; Gen 23's exhaustive reinsertion (every position) plus full swap sweep is worth ~400 points.

## Implementation Insights
- **Gen 23's clean structure is its strength**: `greedy_cost(6, 0.95)` — 6 candidate samples with 95% greedy-evaluation rate using the true cost oracle — balances diversity and quality; `remaining.remove(best_txn)` cleanly avoids duplicates; the local search breaks early when no improvement occurs, avoiding wasted rounds.
- **Reinsertion done right**: Gen 23 pops transaction `i`, tries all insertion positions with first-improvement acceptance (breaking on first gain), and restores to position `i` if no move improved — a compact, correct pattern that avoids state corruption bugs seen in Gen 18's reinsertion logic (0.0).
- **No time checks needed at this workload scale**: Gen 23 uses no `import time` at all — the fixed 20/40 restart counts plus bounded 30-round local search fit within budget deterministically, eliminating the missing-`import time` failure mode that killed Gens 4, 6, 14, 15, 19.
- **Cost caching helps when budgets are tight but isn't required**: Gen 26/31's 2M-entry memoization cache (3246.75) and Gen 27's tuple-keyed cache (3412.97) enabled their searches, but Gen 23 achieves better results with no cache — leaner code with fewer failure modes.

## Performance Analysis
- **Score trajectory among correct programs**: Gen 23 (3649.64, makespan 273.00) > Gen 24 (3610.11, 276.00) > Gen 5 (3597.12, 277.00) > Gen 28 (3472.22, 287.00) > Gen 27 (3412.97, 292.00) > Gen 22 (3401.36, 293.00) > Gen 26/31 (3246.75, 307.00) > Gen 25 (2857.14, 349.00). Makespan improvements map nearly linearly to combined score gains.
- **Two zero-score programs (Gens 20-21) again came from over-engineering**: heavy full-sequence evaluations + large restart counts + time budgets caused timeouts; the correctness failure rate across all evaluated programs remains ~50%, dominated by interface misuse and timeouts rather than heuristic quality.
- **Neighborhood strength ranking updated**: swap+reinsertion on a true-cost greedy solution (273) > full-range insertion (276-277) > hybrid insertion/swap with cheaper construction (287) > SA insertion (292) > windowed insertion (307) > swaps-only budgets (349). True-makespan construction combined with the strongest refinement neighborhood is the top combination.
- **Best-of-restarts + single deep refinement beats per-restart refinement**: Gen 23 (restarts-then-one-local-search) outscored Gen 24 (restarts-each-with-local-search) by 39.5 points, suggesting restart diversity matters more than repeated refinement of similar-quality solutions.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Add a second local search pass from the second-best greedy solution.** Gen 23 (3649.64) spends its entire refinement budget on one solution; a low-risk extension is to run the same swap+reinsertion `local_search` on the top-2 greedy restart results (tracking a small top-k list instead of only the best) and return the better outcome.
