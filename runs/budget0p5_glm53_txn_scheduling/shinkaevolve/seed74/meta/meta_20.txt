# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- **The current best program (Gen 14, sa_refiner_low_temp, 4081.63, makespan 244.00) confirms and extends the multi-restart greedy + refinement pattern**: randomized greedy construction under a 60% time-budget (30s × 0.6), followed by adjacent-swap first-improvement, then a low-temperature simulated annealing pass with random insertion moves. This beats the previous best (3610.11, makespan 276.00) by 471 points and 32 makespan units.
- **SA finally proved its value — as a *refinement* phase, not the main search.** Earlier SA programs (Gens 1, 11) never ran due to import errors; Gen 14's low-temperature annealer (T0 = 1% of best cost, alpha = 0.9995, floor 0.05) with mostly-downhill acceptance and insertion moves added real improvement after local search plateau. Gen 19 (simulated_annealing_refiner, 3717.47, makespan 268.00) independently confirms the same layered pattern (greedy → 2-opt → SA), ranking second among new evaluations.
- **Alternating construction modes within the restart loop provides effective diversity**: Gen 14 alternates trial%3 == 0 → fully greedy (sample_rate 1.0) with randomized trials at uniform(0.5, 0.9). This moderate diversification range avoids the excessive-randomization trap while adding more variety than fixed-rate sampling.
- **Iterated local search with ruin-and-recreate perturbation (Gen 20, 3921.57, makespan 254.00) is the third strongest pattern**: removing 3–8 transactions and greedily reinserting them outperformed plain relocation/swap moves (Gen 8: 3597.12, makespan 277.00) with the same construction method — a 23-point makespan gain from the perturbation operator alone.

## Ineffective Approaches

- **Missing imports remain the dominant failure mode**: Gen 13 (beam_grasp_perturb_search) and Gen 21 (ruin_recreate_sa_hybrid) both scored 0.00 — the former missing `import time`, the latter missing both `import time` and `import math`. This brings the total import-failure count to 5 programs (Gens 1, 9, 11, 13, 21), all with viable algorithms that never executed.
- **High random-skip rates in construction still degrade results**: Gen 10 (best_of_n_greedy_random_restarts, 2801.12, makespan 356.00) used sample rates ~0.85–1.0 with 5 samples per step and 150 restarts — the heavy restart count could not compensate for weak per-step selection. Gen 15 (or_opt_local_search, 2865.33, makespan 348.00) with 0.9 random-skip rate similarly plateaued in the 348–356 range despite strong Or-opt refinement.
- **Restarts alone, no matter how many, cannot match refinement quality**: Gen 10's 150 restarts (makespan 356) were decisively beaten by Gen 16 (or_opt_segment_moves, 3496.50, makespan 285.00) with only 30 restarts plus swap/Or-opt local search — a 71-makespan-unit gap attributable to the refiner, not restart count.

## Implementation Insights

- **Gen 14's key structural choice is phase-based time budgeting**: 60% of a 30s budget to greedy restarts, remainder split between adjacent-swap descent and an annealer that runs up to time_limit + 10s. The 2-phase refinement (deterministic descent then stochastic escape) lets each operator do what it's best at — a pattern echoed in Gen 19 (greedy → 2-opt → SA) and Gen 20 (greedy → local search → ruin-recreate).
- **Low-temperature annealing parameters matter**: T0 = max(1.0, best_cost × 0.01) ties the temperature scale to solution cost; slow cooling (alpha 0.9995, floor 0.05) keeps acceptance mostly downhill. Insertion moves (pop(i)/insert(j)) rather than pure swaps give the annealer a richer neighborhood.
- **Holdout-buffer candidate sampling is the standard construction idiom**: Gen 14 pops sampled candidates into `holdout_txns`, evaluates via full `get_opt_seq_cost` on a copied test sequence, then restores non-selected candidates — used across Gens 10, 15, 16 as well. It remains O(n) per evaluation, and Gen 19's feedback explicitly notes incremental cost evaluation could enable more iterations.
- **Moderate sample sizes (8–10 candidates per step) with moderate random-skip (0.5–0.9) are the sweet spot**: Gen 16 (8 samples, 0.9 rate, 30 restarts, makespan 285.00) versus Gen 10 (5 samples, 0.85–1.0 rate, 150 restarts, makespan 356.00) shows sample quality dominates restart quantity.

## Performance Analysis

- **Score hierarchy across the 10 evaluated programs**: Gen 14 (4081.63, 244) > Gen 20 (3921.57, 254) > Gen 19 (3717.47, 268) > Gen 8 (3597.12, 277) > Gen 16 (3496.50, 285) > Gen 15 (2865.33, 348) > Gen 12 (2898.55, 344) > Gen 10 (2801.12, 356) > Gen 13/21 (0.00, failed). The makespan-244 ceiling of the current best sets a 10-unit gap over the next-best refinement approach.
- **A third refinement layer yields monotone makespan gains**: greedy only (~356–391) → + local search (~277–285) → + ruin-recreate/2-opt (~254–268) → + SA polish (244). Each added escape mechanism is worth roughly 10–70 makespan units.
- **The full-cost evaluation overhead is now the binding constraint**: programs with per-step full `get_opt_seq_cost` evaluation (Gens 14, 19, 20 — all top-3) still scored best, but feedback on Gens 13, 19, and 21 repeatedly flags O(n²) full-sequence re-evaluation as prohibitive, suggesting the next quality leap requires incremental cost updates rather than better search operators.
- **All valid programs score perfect schedule (3.00) and validity (1.00)**, so combined score remains purely makespan-driven; correctness hygiene (imports) and makespan optimization are the only two competitive axes, with import failures costing otherwise top-tier algorithms (Gen 21's hybrid could plausibly have contended with the 250s range).

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Insert a ruin-and-recreate perturbation loop between the SA phase and the adjacent-swap descent.** Gen 20 showed that removing 3–8 transactions and greedily reinserting them (in random order at the best position) gained 23 makespan units over plain moves at identical construction settings. Keep the top-3 sequences from the greedy restart phase, apply ruin-and-recreate to each until the time budget runs out, then feed the best result into the existing SA refiner.

2. **Add Or-opt segment moves (length 1–3) to the SA neighborhood alongside the current insertion move.** The annealer currently only does single-transaction pop/insert; adding a move type that extracts a contiguous block of 1–3 transactions and reinserts it at a random position gives the annealer a richer neighborhood without changing the acceptance scheme. Alternate move types per iteration (e.g., random 50/50 choice) so single moves still dominate.

3. **Implement incremental cost evaluation to break the O(n²) full-re-evaluation bottleneck.** All top programs pay a full `get_opt_seq_cost` per candidate move, and the feedback repeatedly flags this as the binding constraint — the annealer likely completes only a few thousand iterations in 40s. Track key-state/cost deltas so a pop/insert move is evaluated in O(1)–O(n) arithmetic instead of a full simulation, then reinvest the 10–100× speedup into more restarts, more SA iterations, and per-restart light refinement (e.g., 3 rounds of adjacent-swap on each greedy restart's result rather than only the global best).

4. **Add a proper 2-opt (pairwise reversal) descent pass before SA, replacing or supplementing the adjacent-swap loop.** Gen 14 only does adjacent swaps, which is the weakest local-search neighborhood; Gen 19's greedy → 2-opt → SA pattern placed second, and 2-opt covers moves adjacent swaps cannot reach. Run first-improvement 2-opt (reverse seq[i:j] in place) with an in-place apply/undo to avoid list slicing overhead, then hand off to SA with the current low-temperature schedule.

5. **Introduce restart-level hybridization: every 5th restart, construct from a perturbed copy of the current best sequence ("shuffled best") instead of from scratch.** Take the best sequence so far, remove 4–6 random transactions, and greedily reinsert them at their cheapest positions (full cost evaluation per insertion point). This preserves the cost-guided signal that Gens 10/15 show pure randomization destroys, while generating restarts that start near a proven-good solution — cheaper than a full greedy pass and empirically stronger than plain diversity.
