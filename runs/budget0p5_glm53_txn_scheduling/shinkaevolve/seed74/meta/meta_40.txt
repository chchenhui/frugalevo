# Optimization Insights from Program Evaluations

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- **A new champion emerged: Gen 36 (perturb_reinsert_restarts, 4115.23, makespan 242)** dethroned Gen 14 (4081.63, 244) by 34 points and 2 makespan units. Its structure is a refinement of Gen 14's proven pipeline: 60%-budget greedy restarts with alternating construction diversity (`sample_rate 1.0` every 3rd trial, `uniform(0.5, 0.9)` otherwise), adjacent-swap first-improvement descent, then low-temperature SA with random insertion moves — plus a **new twist: perturbation restarts every 4th trial** (remove 4–6 txns from incumbent, greedily reinsert each at its cheapest position via full position scan).
- **The incumbent-centered perturbation restart is the key differentiator**: `get_perturbed_best` capitalizes on accumulated search knowledge by re-inserting removed transactions at their globally cheapest position (scanning all `len(base)+1` insertion slots), giving better-than-random seeds to the greedy phase. This contrasts with Gen 14's pure reconstruction restarts and appears worth the ~2-makespan improvement.
- **Simulated annealing as final phase remains the strongest refinement operator**: the top-3 correct programs this generation all end in SA — Gen 36 (4115.23), Gen 35 (4048.58, 246), Gen 31 (3968.25, 251) — all using temperature scaled to solution cost with geometric cooling and insertion/swap mixed neighborhoods.
- **Incremental cost evaluation is now validated at the top of the leaderboard**: Gen 35 (tune_sa_params_and_reheat_from_greedy_pool, 4048.58) used an incremental cost evaluator (with `get_opt_seq_cost` fallback verification) to make true greedy over all remaining transactions feasible, plus basin-hopping kicks from a diverse greedy pool — landing at #2 and confirming Gen 29's earlier signal that incremental evaluation is a top-performer lever, not just a curiosity.

## Ineffective Approaches

- **Timeout-inducing greedy construction kills whole programs**: Gen 26 (ruin_recreate_perturbation, 0.00) let its O(n²) `get_opt_seq_cost` greedy loop consume the entire 25s budget, so local search and perturbation never ran, failing all validation. Budget discipline — Gen 36's 60/40 greedy/refine split — is essential.
- **Uninitialized variable crashes remain a correctness trap**: Gen 34 (ils_pool_two_opt_oropt, 0.00) crashed on a `NameError` (`cand_cost` referenced before assignment in the `perturb_best(pool[1][1])` branch), never returning a result — a viable ILS algorithm lost entirely to one missing assignment.
- **Simply throwing more phases at the problem doesn't beat tuned baselines**: Gen 37 (lns_destroy_repair_sa, 3610.11, 276) ran a four-phase pipeline (greedy restarts + LNS + local search + multi-neighborhood SA with reheating) but scored far below the simpler Gen 36 — heavy full-cost re-evaluation per move dilutes each phase's iteration count.
- **Untuned time splits underperform**: Gen 40 (ruin_recreate_plus_sa, 3759.40, 265) allocated 2.5s/13s/rest, trading incumbent quality too cheaply early; Gen 39 (hybrid_oropt_ruin, 3906.25, 255) showed the same ruin-recreate machinery scores better when phase budgeting is more balanced.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Replace full-cost evaluation with incremental delta arithmetic in the SA loop.** Gen 36's SA pays `get_opt_seq_cost(cand)` plus a list copy per iteration, and its own feedback flags this as the scalability bottleneck; Gen 35's incremental evaluator finished only 67 points behind with a fundamentally cheaper evaluation path. Reuse `workload.get_incremental_seq_cost` state (key-map) across accepted insertion moves, keep the exact winning schedule (T0 = max(1.0, best_cost × 0.01), alpha 0.9995, floor 0.05), and reinvest the speedup into 5–10× more annealing iterations within the same 40s window.

2. **Make the perturbation restart (every 4th trial) use incremental evaluation for its cheapest-position scan.** `get_perturbed_best` currently calls `get_opt_seq_cost` on all `len(base)+1` slots per removed transaction — O(n²) full evaluations per restart. Use Gen 35's incremental evaluator to find each transaction's cheapest insertion slot in a single pass, making each perturbation restart ~10× cheaper and allowing perturbation restarts more often than every 4th trial (e.g., every 2nd–3rd trial) within the same 60% construction slice.

3. **Add Or-opt segment moves as a minority (~25%) SA neighborhood while keeping single insertion dominant.** Gen 22's #3 finish used 60% insertion / 25% adjacent-swap / 15% segment-move mixing, and all top-3 current programs use insertion-heavy annealing. Extend Gen 36's SA with a move that extracts a contiguous block of 1–3 transactions and reinserts it elsewhere at 15–25% probability; strictly avoid any reversal-based moves (Gens 17/18 scored 0.00 on 2-opt validity violations).

4. **Insert a ruin-and-recreate polishing slice between adjacent-swap descent and the SA refiner.** Gen 36 jumps directly from first-improvement swap descent into SA; the makespan ladder shows a 260s-tier (Gens 33, 40) gap between greedy (~277 after local search) and SA-polished (~242) solutions. Run repeated partial-ruin (remove 3–8 transactions from `best_seq`, greedily reinsert via the incremental evaluator) for a bounded ~3–4s slice, keep the best result, then start SA from it — the SA start temperature should be computed from this improved incumbent, preserving the tuned T0 = cost × 0.01 scaling.

5. **Enforce a zero-defect hygiene checklist targeting the new failure modes.** Gens 26/40's zero and low scores came from budget indiscipline (O(n²) greedy consuming the full 25s) and Gen 34 from a `NameError` (`cand_cost` uninitialized in a branch) — keep the 60%/40% construction/refinement split and 30s+10s hard deadlines, initialize every variable before conditional branches that reference it, place `import time`/`math`/`random` at the top of the EVOLVE block, and cap randomized-construction sample rates in the 0.5–0.9 range (avoiding the >90% random-skip branch that triggers `min_txn != -1` assertion failures). Any new code path (perturbation, ruin, incremental evaluator) must return a result on every branch before scoring is considered.
