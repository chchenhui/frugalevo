# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- **Multi-restart greedy with best-of-N selection is the dominant construction pattern.** The current best program (beam_greedy_2opt_restart, combined score 3610.11, makespan 276.00) uses greedy construction from up to 25 random starting transactions, picking the least-cost next-txn via full-sequence evaluation. This decisively beats single-pass greedy (Gen 0: 2732.24, makespan 365.00) — a gain of ~878 points and 89 makespan units from restart diversity alone.
- **2-opt segment-reversal local search outperforms adjacent-swap local search.** The best program's 2-opt refinement (30 rounds) pushed makespan from the ~316–343 range of adjacent-swap variants (Gen 6: 3154.57/makespan 316; Gen 2: 2906.98/makespan 343) down to 276.00. Larger neighborhood moves escape local optima that adjacent swaps cannot.
- **Time-budgeted restart loops work when implemented correctly.** Gen 4 (greedy_multi_restart_best_of_n, 2949.85, makespan 338.00) showed restarts within a 25-second deadline plus swap hill climbing improve on single-restart variants; the best program essentially keeps the multi-restart core and upgrades the refiner to 2-opt.
- **Per-step `get_opt_seq_cost` evaluation of candidate next-transactions is the reliable selection mechanism** across all top-scoring programs (Gens 0, 2, 4, 6, and the best program), enabling accurate local selection despite runtime cost.

## Ineffective Approaches

- **Missing imports are fatal and fully explain three zero-score failures.** Gen 1 (anneal_swap_search), Gen 9 (modular_multistart_insertion_search, missing `import time`), and Gen 11 (annealing_insertion_search, missing `import math`) all scored 0.00 despite reasonable heuristics — the programs crashed on `time.time()`/`math.exp` before producing any schedule. Correctness hygiene matters more than algorithmic sophistication.
- **High exploration/randomization rates degrade greedy quality.** Gen 3 (85% purely random steps) scored 2631.58 (makespan 379.00) and Gen 7 (15% random acceptance) scored 2551.02 (makespan 391.00) — the two worst *valid* programs. Excessive randomization dilutes the cost-based selection signal.
- **Best-of-N restarts without strong refinement plateau in the 340–395 makespan range** (Gens 3, 7), confirming that restarts alone cannot reach the 276 makespan of the current best.
- **Simulated annealing never got to demonstrate value** — both SA-based programs (Gens 1, 11) failed on import errors, so there's no evidence either way on annealing vs. 2-opt.

## Implementation Insights

- **The current best program's key implementation choice is exhaustive candidate evaluation per step**: it tests *every* remaining transaction (`for t in remaining`) rather than sampling 6–12 random candidates. This full-cost greedy construction, paired with 25 shuffled starting points, produced the lowest makespan (276.00) — suggesting that when time permits, evaluating all candidates beats random subsampling.
- **Segment reversal via slicing (`seq[:i] + seq[i:j+1][::-1] + seq[j+1:]`) is cheap to implement and effective**; the best program caps it at 30 improvement rounds, balancing refinement depth against time.
- **Reusable list-buffer trick for cost testing** (`test = txn_seq + [0]` then `test[-1] = t`) avoids reallocating a full test list per candidate — used in the best program and echoed by the pop/extend holdout pattern in Gen 0.
- **Time-budgeted loops (8s, 20s, 25s, 30s) appear in nearly every strong program** (Gens 1, 4, 6, 9), but the best program uses a fixed restart count (min(n, 25)) instead — simpler and evidently sufficient.

## Performance Analysis

- **Score progression tracks refinement strength**: single greedy (2732.24, makespan 365) → restarts + adjacent swaps (2906.98–2949.85, makespan 343→338) → restarts + swaps + more time (3154.57, makespan 316) → restarts + 2-opt (3610.11, makespan 276). Each algorithmic upgrade yields a monotone makespan reduction of roughly 20–40 units.
- **The best program (3610.11) outperforms the runner-up (Gen 6, 3154.57) by 455 points** — the entire gap attributable to upgrading adjacent-swap refinement to 2-opt segment reversal and exhaustive rather than sampled candidate evaluation.
- **All valid programs scored perfect schedule (3.00) and validity (1.00)**, so combined-score differences are driven entirely by makespan — makespan optimization is the sole competitive axis.
- **Valid-but-weak programs (Gens 3, 7: 2551–2632, makespan 379–391) confirm a clear negative correlation between randomization rate and makespan**, reinforcing that cost-guided deterministic selection with diversification only at the *restart* level (random starting points) is the sweet spot — exactly the structure of the current best program.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Apply 2-opt refinement to every restart's schedule, not just the global best.** The current best only refines the single best greedy construction, so mediocre restarts that could be polished into winners are discarded. Keep a short 2-opt (e.g., 5 rounds) per restart, select the best refined result, then run the full 30-round 2-opt on that winner — restarts give diversity, and refinement converts it into quality.

2. **Add Or-opt (segment relocation/insertion) moves alongside 2-opt.** Segment reversal is effective but can't fix sequences where a small block of transactions (1–3 txns) simply belongs elsewhere. Add a pass that removes a segment of length 1–3 and reinserts it at every other position, evaluated via `get_opt_seq_cost`, alternating with 2-opt rounds until no improvement.

3. **Reuse the best program's list-buffer trick inside 2-opt to reduce constant overhead and allow more restarts.** The `two_opt` currently builds full sliced candidate lists every iteration; instead maintain a single mutable sequence and apply/undo reversals in place (swap `seq[i]` and `seq[j]` pairwise), enabling more 2-opt rounds or more than 25 restarts within the same time budget.

4. **Implement a carefully-hygiene-checked simulated annealing refiner with low randomness.** Both SA attempts failed only on missing `import math`/`import time`, and Gens 3/7 show high randomization hurts — but SA with a low initial temperature and mostly-downhill acceptance (e.g., accept worsening moves with probability `math.exp(-Δ/T)`, T decaying fast) stays cost-guided while escaping local optima that 2-opt alone cannot. Verify all imports before anything else.

5. **Use greedy construction with a lookahead-2 candidate evaluation or perturbation-based restarts (a "shuffled best" pattern).** Instead of purely random starting transactions, generate additional restarts by taking the current best sequence, removing 3–5 random transactions, and greedily re-inserting them (ruin-and-recreate). This diversifies at the restart level while preserving the cost-guided selection signal that Gens 3/7 show is essential, and can be iterated under a time budget as in Gen 4.
