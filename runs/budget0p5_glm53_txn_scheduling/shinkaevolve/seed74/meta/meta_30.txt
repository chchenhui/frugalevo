# Optimization Insights from Program Evaluations

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- **The current best (Gen 14, sa_refiner_low_temp, 4081.63, makespan 244) remains the champion** and its three-phase structure (60%-budget greedy restarts → adjacent-swap first-improvement descent → low-temperature SA with insertion moves) is repeatedly validated by the new generation: Gen 22's SA-heavy hybrid (3891.05, 256) and Gen 30's ILS (3846.15, 259) both converge on the same layered pipeline without matching it.
- **Simulated annealing as a refinement or extended phase is the strongest scoring operator among the new programs**: Gen 22 (sa_hybrid_insert_swap_multistart, 3891.05, makespan 256) ran a 30-second SA phase with mixed neighborhoods (60% insertion, 25% adjacent swap, 15% segment swap) plus stagnation-triggered reheating — its use of insertion moves matches Gen 14's key SA design choice.
- **Ruin-and-recreate perturbation remains a reliable second-tier pattern**: Gen 28 (multi_start_and_or_opt_moves, 3802.28, 262) and Gen 30 (ils_perturb_recreate, 3846.15, 259) both scored in the 3800s using segment removal + greedy reinsertion, with Gen 28's multi-basin pool of 3 seeds plus radial/scattered dual ruin strategies effectively avoiding premature convergence.
- **Incremental delta evaluation finally proved its worth**: Gen 29 (incremental_delta_sa, 3496.50, 285) used the workload's incremental cost evaluator to enable cheap full-greedy candidate consideration across all remaining transactions each step — validating the long-standing hypothesis about evaluation cost, though the program's overall balance (35% greedy / 15% LS / 50% SA) still underperformed the simpler full-cost top performers.

## Ineffective Approaches

- **Missing `import time` reached epidemic proportions**: Gens 23 (ruin_recreate_grasp), 24 (ruin_recreate_hybrid_search), and 25 (shuffled_best_restarts) all scored 0.00 solely because `time.time()` was called without importing `time` — three viable algorithms (ruin-recreate, three-stage metaheuristic, multi-restart with Or-opt) never executed. This brings the import-failure total to 8 programs, now clearly the single dominant failure mode.
- **2-opt segment reversals break schedule validity**: Gen 17 (in_place_two_opt_search, 0.00) and Gen 18 (in_place_two_opt_local_search, 0.00) both used reversal-based local search on transaction sequences and failed validation — reversals appear to violate transaction precedence/validity constraints even when internal cost checks pass. Adjacent swaps and insertions are safe; reversals are not.
- **High random-skip rates in construction continue to starve sampling**: Gen 18's 90% random-skip branch could trigger `min_txn != -1` assertion failures, confirming the earlier finding that excessive randomization in the greedy loop (vs. Gen 14's 0.5–0.9 moderate range) is destabilizing.
- **O(n²) full-cost swap search is computationally prohibitive**: Gen 17's exhaustive pairwise-swap search with full `get_opt_seq_cost` recomputation per swap was flagged as extremely expensive with zero payoff given its correctness bugs.

## Implementation Insights

- **Gen 14's alternating construction diversity is a key differentiator**: `trial % 3 == 0 → fully greedy (sample_rate 1.0)` interleaved with `uniform(0.5, 0.9)` randomized trials within a 60% time-budget slice. None of the new programs that tried fixed high skip rates or purely random construction came close.
- **Temperature scaling tied to solution cost is the winning SA parameterization**: Gen 14 uses T0 = max(1.0, best_cost × 0.01), alpha 0.9995, floor 0.05; Gen 22 independently converges on the same idea (T0 at 2% of cost, alpha 0.9999, restart-from-best after 3000 non-improving moves). Both score in the top 3.
- **Deadline-safe refinement phases are essential**: Gen 14's SA runs to `time_limit + 10` (a 40s total window), Gen 28 uses time-sliced local search deadlines, Gen 27 a 20s budget — all top-5 programs budget phases explicitly rather than running operators to completion.
- **Holdout-buffer candidate sampling remains the standard construction idiom but is the binding cost constraint**: Gens 14, 22, 27, 28, 30 all evaluate candidates via full `get_opt_seq_cost` on copied sequences; feedback on Gens 27, 28, and 30 repeatedly flags full-cost evaluation as limiting iteration count, while Gen 29's incremental evaluator shows the path forward — though its full-greedy-per-step approach still couldn't beat tuned sampled greedy.

## Performance Analysis

- **Full ranking with new evaluations**: Gen 14 (4081.63, 244) > Gen 20 (3921.57, 254) > Gen 22 (3891.05, 256) > Gen 30 (3846.15, 259) > Gen 28 (3802.28, 262) > Gen 19 (3717.47, 268) > Gen 29 (3496.50, 285) > Gen 16 (3496.50, 285) > Gen 8 (3597.12, 277) > Gen 27 (3344.48, 298) — the best new program (Gen 22) still trails Gen 14 by 190 points and 12 makespan units.
- **The makespan ladder confirms layered refinement value**: greedy-only (~356) → +local search (~277–285) → +ruin-recreate/2-opt (254–268) → +SA polish (244). Gen 22's 256 and Gen 30's 259 fit cleanly into the "perturbation + annealing" tier without breaking through Gen 14's 244 ceiling.
- **SA variants cluster tightly in the 250s, suggesting convergence on a local optimum band**: Gen 20 (254), Gen 22 (256), Gen 30 (259), Gen 28 (262) all use greedy + perturbation + stochastic escape within 25–30s budgets; only Gen 14's specific combination (alternating full-greedy trials + very-low-temperature mostly-downhill annealing with insertion moves) reaches the 240s.
- **Failure modes are now bimodal**: 5 of 10 new programs scored exactly 0.00 — 3 from missing imports, 2 from validity-breaking 2-opt reversals — while all 5 correct programs scored 3300+. Correctness hygiene (imports, validity-preserving moves only) remains as decisive as algorithmic quality, with incremental cost evaluation (Gen 29) flagged repeatedly as the most promising unexploited lever against Gen 14's full-cost bottleneck.

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Wire the incremental cost evaluator into the SA loop's insertion moves.** Gen 14 pays a full `get_opt_seq_cost(cand)` plus a list copy per annealing iteration; Gen 29 proved `workload.get_incremental_seq_cost` works and enables far more iterations. Replace the full re-evaluation for pop/insert moves with incremental delta arithmetic (reusing the key-map state across accepted moves), and reinvest the speedup into more SA iterations and more greedy restarts within the same 60%/40s budget — keep the identical temperature schedule (T0 = best_cost × 0.01, alpha 0.9995, floor 0.05) so the acceptance dynamics stay tuned.

2. **Add a ruin-and-recreate perturbation stage between the adjacent-swap descent and the SA refiner.** Gen 20 gained 23 makespan units with this pattern, and Gens 28/30 confirmed it. Concretely: remove 3–8 random transactions from `best_seq`, reinsert each greedily at its cheapest position (using the incremental evaluator), repeat until a ~8s slice of the budget elapses, keep the best result, then hand off to the existing low-temperature SA. This bridges the greedy (~356) and SA (244) tiers that the current two-phase descent leaves untapped.

3. **Diversify the SA neighborhood with Or-opt segment moves while keeping single insertion dominant.** Gen 22's #3 finish used mixed neighborhoods (60% insertion, 25% adjacent swap, 15% segment move) and independently converged on insertion-heavy, cost-scaled annealing. Add a move type that extracts a contiguous block of 1–3 transactions and reinserts it elsewhere, chosen with ~25–30% probability per iteration, so single-transaction insertions (Gen 14's proven winning move) still dominate. Avoid any reversal-based moves — Gens 17/18 proved 2-opt reversals break validity and score 0.

4. **Introduce "shuffled best" restarts in the greedy phase: every 4th trial, perturb the incumbent instead of building from scratch.** Take `best_seq`, remove 4–6 random transactions, and greedily reinsert them at their cheapest positions using incremental evaluation. Gen 14's alternating full-greedy/randomized trials (trial % 3) is its key construction differentiator; this extends the same idea with cost-guided diversity that starts near a proven-good basin, and it's cheaper per trial than a full greedy pass, so more restarts fit in the 60% construction slice.

5. **Enforce a zero-defect hygiene checklist before any algorithmic change.** Three of ten new programs (Gens 23–25) scored 0.00 solely from missing `import time`, and the total import-failure count is now 8 — the single dominant failure mode. Any mutation must (a) place all imports (`time`, `math`, `random`) at the top of the EVOLVE block, (b) use only validity-preserving moves (swaps, insertions, block relocations — never reversals), and (c) keep moderate sample rates in the 0.5–0.9 range, avoiding the >90% random-skip branch that triggered `min_txn != -1` assertion failures in Gen 18. Correctness gates all payoff; algorithmic gains are meaningless if the program crashes.
