<DESCRIPTION>
The current greedy phase always builds sequences from scratch with a random starting transaction, which wastes trials exploring basins far from the incumbent. Following the recommendation, I add a "shuffled best" restart mechanism: every 4th trial, instead of a fresh greedy construction, take the current best sequence, remove 4–6 random transactions, and greedily reinsert each at its cheapest remaining position using full-cost evaluation (`get_opt_seq_cost`). This is cost-guided diversity starting near a proven-good solution — a form of iterated local search during construction. It's cheaper per trial than a full greedy pass (only ~5 reinsertions of ~5 position evaluations each vs. building the whole sequence), so more restarts fit in the 60% construction slice. The incumbent-based perturbation explores neighborhoods the adjacent-swap and insertion SA phases handle poorly during construction, likely yielding better starting points for the SA refiner and thus lower makespan.

Reasoning step by step:
1. Greedy from random starts is high-variance; most trials land far from optimal.
2. Perturbing the incumbent (removing a few txns, greedy reinsertion) generates candidates in the incumbent's basin — classic iterated local search, which converges much faster on permutation-style scheduling problems.
3. Mixed with the existing full-greedy/randomized trials (trial % 4 == 0 instead of building from scratch), we keep global exploration while adding local intensification during construction.
4. The SA phase then refines an already-better incumbent.
