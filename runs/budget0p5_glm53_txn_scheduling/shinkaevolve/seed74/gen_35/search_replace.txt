The current program regressed relative to the best prior generation (285 vs 251 makespan). Diagnosis: (1) the temperature schedule changed to T0 = 2% of cost with alpha = 0.99995, which cools far too slowly — the SA spends most of its budget at high temperature doing near-random walks instead of exploiting the good greedy solution; the best-performing generation used T0 = 1% and a faster cooldown, letting the search settle into deep minima. (2) The stagnation kick always restarts from `best_seq`, so after the search gets stuck it never revisits alternative greedy basins — keeping a small pool of distinct good greedy solutions and occasionally kicking from a random pool member restores basin diversity. (3) The greedy phase gets 35% of the budget but greedy solutions are quickly surpassed by SA; trimming it to 25% gives SA more time at productive (mid-to-low) temperatures.

Reasoning step-by-step: cost values here are ~100-scale makespans, so a delta of 1-5 is typical between neighbors; with T ≈ 2 initially (2% of ~100), worsening moves of delta 3 are accepted with probability e^-1.5 ≈ 0.22 — fine — but with alpha = 0.99995 and only tens of thousands of iterations within 40s, T only drops to ~30-50% of T0, never reaching the low-temperature exploitation regime where SA behaves like a proper local descent. Lowering T0 to 1% and cooling with alpha = 0.9995 brings T down to ~5% of T0 within ~6000 iterations, so the bulk of the budget is spent refining rather than randomizing. Kicks from the greedy pool (rather than always the incumbent best) implement a simple population-based iterated local search that escapes deep local optima without losing the best solution found (still tracked separately). These changes directly target the makespan quality of the final schedule.
