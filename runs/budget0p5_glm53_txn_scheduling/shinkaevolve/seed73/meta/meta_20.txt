# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns
- **Hybrid candidate generation (beam search + randomized greedy) is the new best strategy**: Gen 18's "Hybrid Beam Search with Iterated Local Search Scheduler" (combined score **3636.36**, makespan 274) combines randomized-restart beam search AND randomized greedy constructions for candidate diversity, then applies iterated local search — beating pure beam search (Gen 7, 3610.11, makespan 276) for the first time.
- **Iterated local search with segment-shuffle perturbation restarts is the most effective refinement**: Gen 18 outperformed plain local search (Gen 10's beam+local search, 3448.28, makespan 289) and SA-with-greedy-sweeps (Gen 16, 3521.13, makespan 283). The perturbation (shuffle a random 2–8 element segment, re-descend, keep if better) effectively escapes local minima.
- **Time-budget phase splitting with deadlines works well**: Gen 18 uses 40% of a 20s budget for construction (beam + greedy in the same phase), the rest for local search; Gen 16 (3521.13) and Gen 13 (3412.97) also use 20s budgets with adaptive beam width.
- **Adaptive scaling by workload size remains critical**: `num_restarts = max(1, min(8, 200//n))`, `beam_width = max(2, min(6, 400//n))` — a pattern shared by Gen 7, Gen 10, Gen 13, Gen 16, and Gen 18.

## Ineffective Approaches
- **Missing `import time` crashes are a recurring fatal pattern**: Gen 21 (adaptive_beam_plus_polish), Gen 22 (simulated_annealing_insertion) both scored 0.00 solely due to `import time` missing at module level — `NameError` before any schedule is produced despite sound algorithms.
- **Undefined variable names kill valid designs**: Gen 19 (adaptive_beam_width_restarts, 0.00) crashed on `beam_width` vs `wide_beam` naming mismatch in beam trimming.
- **Exhaustive per-state beam expansion without pruning discipline fails**: Gen 15 (beam_search_time_budgeted, 0.00) evaluated O(n) children per beam entry with full cost, causing timeouts/crashes.
- **Holdout/restore logic with random skipping introduces correctness bugs**: Gen 2's greedy sampling with holdout/restore scored 0.00 — invalid schedules rather than poor search quality.

## Implementation Insights
- **Current best (Gen 18)'s key structure**: candidates carry `(cost, seq)`, deduplicated by `tuple(s)`, sorted, top 4 selected for ILS; beam states carry `(seq, remaining list)` and are sorted/pruned by actual `get_opt_seq_cost` — full state retention keeps multiple good partial orderings alive.
- **Greedy construction diversity via variable sample rates** (Gen 18 samples at rates 0.5/0.8/1.0, best-of-10 sampling at 1.0) adds candidates cheaply within the construction budget — unlike Gen 6's 80% random choice (2624.67), moderate randomness preserved the greedy signal.
- **Seeding with txn 0 plus random starts** (Gens 7, 10, 16, 18) consistently provides deterministic-plus-diverse entry points.
- **Incremental cost tracking remains the unexplored lever**: all top programs still call `get_opt_seq_cost` on full sequences per candidate; the commented-out `get_incremental_seq_cost` would enable wider beams/more restarts within budget.

## Performance Analysis
- Score ranking now: **Gen 18 hybrid beam+greedy+ILS (3636.36, makespan 274)** > Gen 7 pure beam (3610.11, 276) > Gen 16 beam+SA (3521.13, 283) > Gen 13 beam+SA (3412.97, 292) > Gen 12 greedy+LS (3311.26, 301) > Gen 14 (3289.47, 303).

The difference: Gen 18 adds greedy-construction diversity AND perturbation restarts, giving ILS better and more varied starting points.

- Schedule score (3.00) and validity (1.00) remain uniform across correct programs; all score variance comes from makespan, confirming makespan reduction is the sole optimization target.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

# Recommendations for Future Mutations

1. **Implement incremental cost tracking for beam expansion.** Replace the per-child `get_opt_seq_cost(new_seq)` call in the beam loop with a delta-based cost update (or restore/complete the commented-out `get_incremental_seq_cost`) so each expansion costs O(1) instead of O(n). This directly amplifies the proven beam-search pattern, enabling beam widths of 10–20 and 16–32 restarts within the same 20s budget — the single most-cited unexplored lever in the feedback.

2. **Add diversity-preserving beam pruning.** Instead of keeping the top `beam_width` children purely by cost, keep the best `k-2` by cost plus 2 children that maximize sequence divergence (e.g., differing in the appended txn from the best entries). Gen 18's greedy constructions helped precisely because of candidate diversity; applying the same principle inside the beam prevents slot-wasting prefix convergence at zero extra cost.

3. **Adopt depth-adaptive beam width: wide early, narrow late.** Use `beam_width_early = max(6, 400 // n)` for the first 20% of placements and taper to 2–3 for the remainder. Pruning error compounds with depth, so concentrating beam capacity on early high-branching decisions preserves the strong beam pattern while freeing budget for more restarts or longer ILS.

4. **Replace uniform-random restart seeds with heuristic-plus-random seeds.** Seed each beam restart with the top-2 transactions by single-txn cost (cheap one-step heuristic) plus one random pick, rather than `{0, rand, rand}`. This merges the deterministic lookahead strength that made full-lookahead greedy strong with the diversity that lets restarts escape local optima — a one-line change to the `seeds` set construction.

5. **Add a correctness-hygiene guard: module-level `import time` plus a smoke-test dry run.** 5 of 10 recent programs scored 0.00 from missing imports and undefined variables (Gen 21, 22, 19). Keep `import time` at module top, and before the main loop execute a trivial `best_seq = list(range(n)); best_cost = workload.get_opt_seq_cost(best_seq)` immediately after `n == 0` check so a valid fallback exists even if later code fails — this converts potential 0.00 crashes into worst-case baseline scores while algorithmic improvements proceed.
