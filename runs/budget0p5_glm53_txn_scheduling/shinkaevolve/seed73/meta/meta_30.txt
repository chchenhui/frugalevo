# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- **Heuristic-seeded beam search restarts + greedy diversity + ILS is the new best recipe**: Gen 29 ("heuristic_seeded_beam_ils") achieves **3717.47 combined, makespan 268** — the top score so far — by seeding beam restarts with the 2 cheapest single-txn transactions (via `get_opt_seq_cost([t])`) plus random seeds, adding randomized greedy constructions, then time-budgeted (25s) iterated local search with insertion/swap plus random segment-shuffle perturbations on the top 6 deduplicated candidates. This beats the prior best (Gen 18-class hybrid, 3623.19/275 makespan) by ~94 points and −7 makespan.

- **Heuristic seeding (single-txn cost ranking) outperforms pure random/txn-0 seeding**: Gen 29's seeding by cheapest single-txn costs + random diversity improves on Gen 28 (3623.19, makespan 275) which used only randomized restarts and greedy diversity, and on Gen 31 (3521.13, makespan 283) which used "2 cheapest standalone plus random" seeds — heuristic information in seeding is a cheap, high-yield lever.

- **Rich local-search neighborhoods under a deadline**: Gen 28 ("enhanced_local_search_moves", 3623.19, makespan 275) added or-opt (block sizes 2–3) and 2-opt to insertion/swap with perturbation restarts, confirming that a broader move set in ILS improves over plain SA refinement (Gen 27, 3584.23, makespan 278; Gen 20, 3623.19 via beam+SA+greedy sweeps).

- **Time-budget phase splitting still universal among winners**: all correct top programs (Gens 20, 23, 26–29) run 20–28s budgets with ~40% for construction and remainder for refinement; Gen 29 uses 25s with a 40% construction deadline.

## Ineffective Approaches

- **Missing `import time` remains a fatal recurring crash**: Gen 24 ("greedy_pool_localsearch_pipeline", 0.00) crashed on `NameError` despite a structurally sound greedy-pool + local-search pipeline — third batch in a row showing this exact bug (previously Gens 21, 22).

- **Nested expensive full-sequence local search across many restarts is computationally prohibitive**: Gen 8 ("greedy_plus_local_search_restarts", 0.00) ran O(n²) reinsertion search per improvement iteration across 30 restarts with `get_opt_seq_cost` full evaluations — timing out/failing validation entirely rather than merely scoring poorly.

- **Dead/unused code inside evolve blocks adds risk without benefit**: Gen 25 and Gen 31 both carried unused greedy-sampled fallback code inside the evolve block (Gen 31 scored 3521.13, Gen 25 scored 3472.22) — clutter that adds crash surface but no performance.

- **Small beam widths and few restarts underperform**: Gen 25 (adaptive width tapering to 3, 4 random starts, 3472.22, makespan 287) trails Gen 29's wider beams (max(2, min(8, 600//n))) with more restarts (max(2, min(10, 400//n))) and ILS.

## Implementation Insights

- **Gen 29's winning structure**: (1) precompute single-txn costs once, sort, take top-2 as deterministic beam seeds; add 1–2 random seeds per restart; (2) beam width `max(2, min(8, 600//n))`, restarts `max(2, min(10, 400//n))`, bounded by 40% of 25s; (3) greedy constructions at sample rates 0.7/0.9/1.0 with best-of-10 sampling fill remaining construction budget; (4) dedup by `tuple(seq)`, take top 6, run first-improvement insertion/swap descent with global deadline, then segment-shuffle (2–8 elements) perturbation restarts until budget expiry.

- **Greedy diversity via variable sample rates is validated across generations**: Gen 29, Gen 18-class hybrids, and Gen 23 (3610.11) all use multi-rate (0.7–1.0) best-of-k sampling; moderate randomness preserves the greedy signal while adding candidate diversity for the ILS phase.

- **Per-candidate deadlines prevent budget starvation**: Gen 29's `local_search(seq, cur_cost, deadline)` checks `time.time() >= deadline` inside move loops, so no single candidate monopolizes the budget — a pattern shared by all top ILS programs.

- **Dedup + top-k candidate selection is a consistent winner**: Gens 26, 27, 28, 29 all deduplicate candidates (`tuple(s)`) and refine the top 4–6, rather than refining everything.

## Performance Analysis

- **Score ranking**: **Gen 29 heuristic-seeded beam+ILS (3717.47, makespan 268)** > Gen 28/Gen 20 (3623.19, makespan 275) > Gen 23 pure budgeted beam (3610.11, 276) > Gen 27 hybrid beam+SA (3584.23, 278) > Gen 26 beam_greedy_sa_hybrid (3546.10, 281) > Gen 31 heuristic+random seeds beam (3521.13, 283) > Gen 25 adaptive-width beam (3472.22, 287).

- **ILS with perturbation beats SA at equal budget**: Gen 29 (3717.47) vs Gen 27's SA approach (3584.23) — a +133 point / −10 makespan gap; perturbation restarts plus richer moves dominate annealing as the refinement phase.

- **Local search reallocation pays off only when combined with construction diversity**: Gen 23 (deliberately skipping local search, 3610.11) nearly matches Gen 20 (beam+SA, 3623.19), but Gen 28/29 show that ILS on diverse candidate pools pushes well beyond either — refinement quality depends on starting-point variety.

- **Correctness hygiene remains decisive**: 2 of 10 programs in this batch (Gen 8, Gen 24) scored 0.00 from runtime failures (timeout-level nested search; missing `import time`), while all correct programs uniformly hit schedule 3.00 and validity 1.00 — all variance comes from makespan, confirming makespan reduction is the sole optimization lever.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

# Recommendations for Future Mutations

1. **Add incremental delta-based cost updates for beam expansion.** The beam loop currently calls `get_opt_seq_cost(new_seq)` on every child (O(n) per expansion), which is the dominant construction cost. Replace with a delta/incremental cost evaluator (or cache suffix costs) so each child costs O(1); this enables scaling beam width from `min(8, 600//n)` to 10–16 and restarts from 10 to 16–20 within the same 10s construction budget, directly amplifying the proven heuristic-seeded beam pattern.

2. **Add diversity-preserving beam pruning per depth level.** Instead of keeping purely the top `beam_width` children by cost, keep the top `beam_width - 2` plus 2 children whose appended txn differs from the best entries' appended txn. Beam slots currently converge on identical prefixes, wasting restart capacity; Gen 29's success came from candidate diversity (greedy rates 0.7–1.0), and applying the same principle inside the beam prevents prefix collapse at zero extra cost.

3. **Use depth-adaptive beam width: wide early, narrow late.** Pruning error compounds with depth, so set `beam_width = max(6, 400 // n)` for the first ~20% of placements and taper to 2–3 for the remainder. This concentrates beam capacity on the high-branching early decisions that matter most, freeing budget to either increase restart count or extend the ILS phase that produced the +133-point jump over SA.

4. **Add or-opt block moves (sizes 2–3) to the ILS local search neighborhood.** Gen 28 validated that richer move sets improve over plain insertion/swap, but Gen 29's ILS dropped them. Add segment-reinsertion of blocks of length 2–3 alongside insertion/swap in `local_search`, keeping the per-candidate deadline checks — this merges Gen 28's proven neighborhood breadth with Gen 29's superior ILS framework.

5. **Expand heuristic seeding from top-2 to cost-clustered seed selection with workload-adaptive restarts.** Instead of always taking the 2 cheapest single-txn transactions, group transactions by cost quantile and pick one seed per quantile bucket per restart (rotating across restarts), so different restarts explore different seed basins. This generalizes the seeding insight that beat Gen 31's fixed top-2-plus-random approach, giving systematic coverage of seed space rather than repeated concentration on the same two seeds.
