The current program's local search regressed to only insertion + swap (best-improvement-without-break, scanning the entire neighborhood each pass), dropping the or-opt block moves and 2-opt segment reversals that made the earlier 275-makespan variant strong, and its beam keeps only cost-top children so all beam slots converge to near-identical prefixes. I restore the richer move set (insertion, swap, or-opt blocks of size 2-3, 2-opt reversals) with first-improvement early exits so each pass spends its evaluations where gains are found, and add diversity-preserving beam pruning: keep the top beam_width-2 by cost plus 2 children whose last-appended txn differs from the cost-best entries. This prevents the beam from wasting all slots on one prefix while keeping greedy-style diversity, which should lower makespan further below 274. I also add a short candidate-sequence dedup cache on get_opt_seq_cost to avoid recomputing identical sequences across passes.

Step-by-step reasoning:
1. Insertion/swap alone get stuck in shallow local optima for ordering problems; block moves (or-opt) and segment reversals (2-opt) escape wider basins, so restoring them yields lower local minima.
2. First-improvement with break exits finds improving moves faster, allowing more perturbation restarts within the fixed 20s budget — more effective search iterations means better final schedules.
3. Diverse beam pruning maintains exploration of multiple txn-ordering frontiers at zero extra cost, feeding the local search with genuinely different starting candidates (the same diversity principle that made greedy constructions effective).
4. A memo dict avoids recomputing costs of sequences already evaluated (perturbations and moves often revisit states), freeing time for additional moves.
