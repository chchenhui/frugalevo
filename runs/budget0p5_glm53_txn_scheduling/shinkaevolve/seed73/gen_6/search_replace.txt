<DESCRIPTION>
A single greedy run is highly dependent on its random choices (both the starting transaction and the random sampling of candidate transactions). Running many randomized greedy restarts and keeping the schedule with the lowest makespan is a classic and very effective way to lower the final makespan without changing the per-run cost. Each restart explores a different region of the ordering space; taking the min over restarts monotonically improves (or at least never worsens) the expected result. Additionally, lowering the sample_rate slightly (e.g., 0.8) injects occasional random "exploration" picks, which helps escape locally greedy traps, while num_samples=10 keeps the greedy evaluation strong. We run e.g. 100 restarts within the greedy and return the best found.
