<DESCRIPTION>
The current local search is a pure hill climber with insertion moves evaluated by full re-computation of the sequence cost, which gets stuck in local minima and spends budget on O(n^2) full-cost evaluations per pass. I propose replacing it with a time-budgeted simulated annealing over swap and insertion moves. SA accepts occasional worsening moves with probability exp(-delta/T), allowing escape from local minima, which for permutation scheduling problems (this is essentially a single-machine sequencing problem with conflict-dependent setup costs) is known to substantially outperform greedy descent. We also seed SA from multiple top beam candidates rather than just the best, increasing diversity of the search basins explored. Full cost evaluation per move is kept (necessary since makespan depends on the whole ordering), but the acceptance logic is cheap and the move selection is randomized so the budget is spread over promising neighborhoods rather than exhaustively scanning all O(n^2) insertions in order.
