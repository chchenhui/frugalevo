# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- **The current best, Generation 29 “Deterministic DE and Local Search,” remains tied for the top observed combined score of `0.73`.** Its unrestricted fixed-square representation combines four fixed corners, `192` population members, `460` current-to-elite DE generations, scheduled immigration, elite preservation, and extensive active-set polishing. This demonstrates that optimizing all 18 free interior coordinates can match the best compact ring methods when supported by sufficiently strong population diversity and bottleneck-aware refinement.

- **Compact three-ring triangle parameterizations also repeatedly achieve `0.73`, now with strong runtime advantages.** Generation 31 “Deterministic Ring-Based Heilbronn Search” reached `0.73` in `10.36 s`; Generation 36 “Symmetric Differential Evolution Triangle Packing” reached `0.73` in `2.73 s`; and Generation 40 “Symmetric Differential Evolution Triangle Search” reached `0.73` in `3.22 s`. This confirms the prior result that independently phased three-point rings provide enough effective freedom for the observed top score while reducing the global search to six parameters.

- **Active-constraint refinement is consistently associated with the strongest results.** Generation 29 identifies points appearing in its 14 smallest-area triangles and proposes mostly single-point moves plus occasional paired moves, reaching `0.73`. Generations 31, 36, and 40 similarly add tight-triangle-aware stochastic or coordinate refinement after DE and also reach `0.73`. By contrast, global DE without comparably successful refinement generally stays around `0.50–0.57`.

- **Surrogate-to-exact objective scheduling is a productive maximin optimization pattern.** Generation 29 begins DE using `minimum area + weighted mean of the 20 lowest areas`, reducing the tail weight from about `0.25` to `0.02`, then selects finalists by true minimum area. Generation 37 likewise uses the 18 smallest triangle areas during DE before active polishing and reaches `0.56`, supporting the value of lower-tail guidance even though its shorter or less effective search did not attain the leading score.

## Ineffective Approaches

- **Broad unconstrained barycentric evolutionary search remained substantially weaker than either fixed-square DE or ring DE.** Generation 30 “Hybrid Evolutionary Heilbronn Triangle Search” uses ten interior barycentric points, bottleneck mutations, DE-like moves, restarts, and coordinate polishing, but achieves only `0.52` in `8.22 s`. This mirrors the earlier weak barycentric result and shows that guaranteed containment alone does not compensate for the harder 20-coordinate maximin search landscape.

- **Annealing-heavy strategies were runtime-expensive without corresponding score gains.** Generation 33 “Deterministic Annealed Heilbronn Point Search” uses six simulated-annealing restarts, incremental updates, targeted active-triangle sampling, coupled moves, and multiscale polishing, but reaches only `0.57` in `18.03 s`. It is slower than Generation 29 (`9.76 s`) and much slower than the `0.73` ring variants, while remaining below their score.

- **Several fixed-square DE variants had correct evaluator alignment but insufficient optimization effectiveness.** Generation 34 reaches `0.50` in `8.72 s`, Generation 37 reaches `0.56` in `4.81 s`, and Generation 38 reaches `0.57` in `8.31 s`. All fix square corners and apply lower-tail scoring plus active-triangle local moves, but none match Generation 29’s larger DE population, long staged schedule, preserved elite set, explicit immigration events, and ten-finalist polish budget.

- **Rigid symmetry can efficiently reach the top observed score but still appears to limit geometric improvement beyond the rounded `0.02` minimum area.** Generations 31, 36, and 40 all receive `0.73`, but their feedback identifies the six-parameter ring/orbit family as potentially restrictive. Their success is strong relative to the evaluated score, yet their repeated modest displayed normalized minimum area supports the earlier finding that symmetry reduces search cost at the possible expense of access to more irregular layouts.

## Implementation Insights

- **Generation 29 is effective because its representation exactly matches the evaluator’s normalization.** The four fixed points are the unit-square corners, forcing convex-hull area to one. Therefore `_areas` computes the final normalized triangle areas directly, with no hull computation, rescaling ambiguity, or feasibility risk. Generations 33, 34, 37, and 38 share this useful property, but Generation 29 uses it most effectively.

- **The current best uses fully batched evaluation of all `286` triples for both DE and local search.** `_areas` accepts point batches, while `signature_batch` computes each candidate’s strict minimum and mean of its 20 lowest triangle areas through `np.partition`. This makes it practical to evaluate a population of `192`, batches of `16` polish proposals, and repeated active-triangle identification without Python-level triple loops.

- **Its diversity machinery is concrete and multi-stage rather than relying on mutation alone.** Initial candidates occupy shuffled jittered cells of a 3-by-3 lattice; one quarter are replaced with independent uniform starts; the top 12 pre-generation elites are restored exactly; and low-ranked candidates are replaced through deterministic stratified immigration at generations 105 and 205. This is a more complete diversity schedule than the weaker square DE programs, and aligns with the `0.73` result.

- **Generation 29 separates exploration scoring from final correctness scoring.** During DE and early local search, the lower-tail mean stabilizes the nonsmooth objective and discourages configurations with many nearly degenerate triples. Finalist ordering and global-best replacement use strict minimum area first and tail value only as a tie-breaker. This preserves alignment with the actual maximin metric while making search less brittle.

## Performance Analysis

- **Generation 29 is a top-score method but not the most time-efficient top-score method.** It achieves `0.73` in `9.76 s`, tying Generations 31, 36, and 40 in score. However, Generation 36 reaches the same score in `2.73 s` and Generation 40 in `3.22 s`, indicating that the six-parameter ring/orbit representation provides substantially lower optimization cost for the observed evaluation target.

- **Among the newly listed unrestricted or broadly parameterized methods, Generation 29 remains clearly stronger.** The barycentric evolutionary Generation 30 scores `0.52`; fixed-simplex DE Generation 32 scores `0.56` in `3.16 s`; annealed square search Generation 33 scores `0.57`; fixed-square DE Generation 34 scores `0.50`; triangle DE Generation 35 scores `0.54`; Generation 37 scores `0.56`; and Generation 38 scores `0.57`. Thus, the best unrestricted square approach has a roughly `0.16–0.23` combined-score advantage over these moderate alternatives.

- **The displayed `min_area_normalized: 0.02` is not sufficient to distinguish practical quality.** Every newly evaluated program reports `0.02`, yet combined scores range from `0.50` for Generation 34 to `0.73` for Generations 29, 31, 36, and 40. The unrounded minimum, lower-tail behavior, and runtime must therefore be materially affecting the combined metric or evaluator ranking.

- **The strongest empirical split is between mature DE-plus-active-polish pipelines and less focused global search.** The leading group—Generation 29’s unrestricted current-to-elite DE and the ring/orbit DE methods in Generations 31, 36, and 40—uses staged DE, elitism or restarts, lower-tail stabilization, and tight-triangle-aware refinement. Methods that include only some of these components, or substitute generic evolutionary/annealing behavior, cluster in the `0.50–0.57` range despite being correct and evaluator-aligned.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Add ring-derived warm starts to the fixed-square population, then deliberately desymmetrize them.** Reserve roughly 30–40% of the 192 members for the proven three-ring/orbit parameterization used by the fast `0.73` methods, map those layouts into the square representation, and perturb each of the nine free points independently by a small reflected Gaussian displacement. Keep the present stratified-cell and uniform starts for the remainder, so the full 18-dimensional DE can exploit high-quality ring basins while retaining paths to irregular improvements beyond the ring family.

2. **Use a deterministic DE mutation portfolio instead of only current-to-elite throughout the 460 generations.** In the first third, assign a meaningful fraction of trials to `rand/1` or `rand-to-pbest/1`; shift toward the current-to-elite donor currently used by Generation 29 in the middle and late phases. This preserves the best program’s successful elitism, reflection, crossover, and immigration, but reduces the risk that its elite-focused donor collapses the population into one symmetric or near-duplicate basin too early.

3. **Make the ten polishing finalists score-aware *and* geometry-diverse.** After lexicographic ordering by strict minimum and lower-tail mean, greedily choose finalists subject to a minimum distance in canonicalized free-coordinate space, optionally including several top ring-seeded candidates even if their DE rank is slightly lower. Generation 29’s expensive 1,550-step polishing is most valuable when applied to genuinely different basins rather than multiple permutations or small perturbations of the same configuration.

4. **Replace a portion of isotropic active-point proposals with area-gradient-guided moves.** For the 14–20 active triangles, compute signed doubled-area gradients with respect to every participating free point, aggregate each point’s gradients weighted by triangle tightness, and include proposals along the resulting normalized directions and their short line-search scales. Retain stochastic one- and two-point moves for escaping nonsmooth active-set changes, but target paired proposals at point pairs that co-occur frequently in bottleneck triangles; this is a direct stronger version of the active-set polishing that distinguishes the leading methods.

5. **Introduce a staged polish budget with exact-maximin finishing on only the strongest survivors.** Run a shorter surrogate-based active polish, for example 400–600 steps, over 10–12 diverse finalists using `min + 0.035 * tail`, then retain the best 2–3 and spend the remaining budget on strict lexicographic `(minimum area, tail)` acceptance with smaller gradient-guided and coordinate proposals. This should preserve the broad lower-tail stabilization that made Generation 29 effective while reducing redundant local-search work and focusing final effort on improvements that matter to the actual evaluator.
