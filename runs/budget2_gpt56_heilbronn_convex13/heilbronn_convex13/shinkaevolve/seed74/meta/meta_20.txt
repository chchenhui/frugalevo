# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- **The current best program, Deterministic Differential-Evolution Triangle Ring Search (Generation 12), establishes the strongest observed result: combined score `0.73`, normalized minimum area `0.02`, in `5.24 s`.** Its six-parameter construction—three independently phased/radius-controlled 3-point rings inside an equilateral hull—provides enough geometric flexibility to outperform both earlier square-based differential evolution (`0.67` combined score, `1.30 s`, Generation 16) and the previously cited best differential-evolution pipeline (`0.63`).

- **Population-wide differential evolution with deterministic diversity management is consistently stronger than primarily sequential annealing.** Generation 12 combines current-to-best differential mutation, forced-coordinate crossover, elitist replacement, two scheduled diversity injections, and 260 rounds of batched local refinement. This exceeded the barycentric annealing solver (Generation 10: `0.58`) and fixed-square annealing solver (Generation 14: `0.57`), despite both reaching the same rounded normalized minimum area of `0.02`.

- **Constrained geometry can work well when the parameterization retains independent phase variation.** Generation 12’s three rings have independently optimized radii and phases, unlike Generation 11’s more restrictive center/two-orbit/hexagon construction, which scored `0.00`. The successful result shows that symmetry itself is not the problem: the important distinction is whether the symmetry family has enough independent degrees of freedom to avoid repeated alignments and near-degenerate triples.

- **Structured initialization remains a high-value pattern.** Generation 12 stratifies each radial coordinate over the full allowed interval and inserts two hand-spaced seeds before optimization. This builds on the prior observation that dispersed, structured starts outperform purely random point placement and complements the current-to-best DE dynamics by supplying diverse ring scales from generation zero.

## Ineffective Approaches

- **Overly rigid symmetric orbit parameterizations can collapse the objective entirely.** Generation 11’s unit-hexagon hull with a center and only two parameterized threefold interior orbits passed validation but produced `0.00` normalized minimum area and `0.00` combined score in `1.48 s`. The evaluation feedback specifically indicates degeneracy or benchmark failure from the restricted geometry. In contrast, Generation 12’s three independently phased rings achieved `0.73`, demonstrating that the added independent ring substantially changed the search family’s viability.

- **Adding many generic hybrid-search components did not match focused DE on this benchmark.** Fixed-square hybrid methods—Generation 13 (`0.40`, min area `0.01`), Generation 15 (`0.36`, `0.01`), Generation 17 (`0.33`, `0.01`), and Generation 19 (`0.38`, `0.01`)—combined Gaussian proposals, attraction, restarts, differential moves, and polishing, yet remained well below Generation 12. Their common outcome was a feasible but modest layout, suggesting their broader nine-point coordinate search did not exploit candidate evaluations as effectively as the compact six-parameter ring family.

- **Longer annealing runs remained less score-efficient than batched evolutionary search.** Generation 14 required `6.14 s` for `0.57`, while Generation 12 reached `0.73` in `5.24 s`. Generation 10 similarly achieved only `0.58` in `4.47 s`. Their active-worst-triangle guidance and local refinement were valid, but did not produce the same final layout quality as the population-based ring optimization.

- **Correctness alone does not compensate for implementation indexing failures.** Generation 20’s batched barycentric local search received `0.00` because `candidate_chain`, a six-chain index, was incorrectly used to index proposal-level values, causing `index 113 is out of bounds for axis 0 with size 6`. Its intended multi-chain and bottleneck-polish strategy therefore never produced an evaluable arrangement.

## Implementation Insights

- **Generation 12 evaluates the exact evaluator-aligned objective in vectorized batches.** For every parameter batch, it constructs layouts of shape `(batch, 13, 2)` and computes all `286` triangle areas through indexed NumPy arrays and broadcasting. Unlike earlier successful DE approaches that used a lower-tail blended surrogate during global evolution, this program directly maximizes normalized worst-triangle area throughout. Its `0.73` result shows that, in this lower-dimensional ring parameterization, direct nonsmooth maximin scoring was effective.

- **The hull and radius bounds make feasibility inexpensive and reliable.** The fixed equilateral hull has known area `sqrt(3)/4`, while the centroid-centered disk bound of `0.278` stays inside the triangle’s inradius. Thus every candidate produced by DE or local refinement remains contained after simple clipping/wrapping of six parameters. This mirrors the robustness benefits of fixed-square corners in Generations 13–19, but preserves a different and apparently stronger search geometry.

- **The DE schedule is explicitly staged rather than static.** Across 720 generations, the differential factor decreases from `0.82` to `0.34`, best-attraction increases from `0.16` to `0.58`, and crossover falls from `0.78` to `0.56`. This transitions from broad exploration to incumbent-centered exploitation. The scheduled replacement of the bottom fifth of the population at generations `230` and `470` counteracts phase-locking, an especially relevant risk for concentric threefold ring layouts.

- **The final polishing is batch-oriented and accepts only strict true-objective improvements.** Each of 260 rounds evaluates 96 correlated sparse perturbations of the incumbent, while radial and angular step sizes decay geometrically. This is more targeted than generic coordinate annealing: it searches coupled ring changes, preserves deterministic reproducibility through the fixed RNG seed, and never replaces the incumbent with a worse candidate.

## Performance Analysis

- **Generation 12 is the new clear performance leader among the listed valid programs.** Its `0.73` combined score is higher than Generation 16’s `0.67`, the previously highlighted best DE result at `0.63`, Generation 10’s `0.58`, and Generation 14’s `0.57`. It is therefore not merely another configuration achieving rounded normalized area `0.02`; it produces a meaningfully stronger evaluator result within that same reported area band.

- **A normalized minimum area reported as `0.02` is necessary but not sufficient to explain ranking.** Generations 10 (`0.58`), 12 (`0.73`), 14 (`0.57`), and 16 (`0.67`) all report `0.02`, but differ substantially in combined score. This is consistent with the prior observation that rounded metrics hide precision-level differences in the true minimum area, possible secondary scoring behavior, and runtime effects. Generation 12’s lead indicates its actual unrounded layout quality is materially better.

- **Generation 12 trades some runtime for a substantially higher score, but remains competitive in efficiency.** Its `5.24 s` is slower than Generation 16’s `1.30 s` and the earlier `2.22 s` DE leader, yet it improves score from `0.67`/`0.63` to `0.73`. It is still faster than the `6.14 s` fixed-square annealer and avoids the 8-second-scale runtimes previously associated with less successful annealing variants.

- **The results distinguish productive from unproductive uses of symmetry.** The hexagonal-orbit model in Generation 11 scored `0.00`, while the triangular three-ring model in Generation 12 scored `0.73`. The decisive empirical difference is not simply triangular versus hexagonal containment, but the combination of three separately tunable ring radii, three phases, stratified initialization, DE diversity injection, and exact batched maximin selection.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Expand the three-ring family with one tightly bounded asymmetry parameter per ring.** Keep the equilateral hull, centroid, and three independently phased rings, but allow each ring’s three points to have small individual radial offsets (for example, base radius plus two offsets with the third chosen to keep zero mean), producing a 12-parameter rather than six-parameter search. This preserves the proven containment and structured geometry while breaking residual threefold collinearities that may limit the current exact maximin optimum.

2. **Use a two-stage objective schedule that begins with lower-tail protection and ends with the exact minimum area.** For early and middle DE generations, rank candidates by `min_area + w * mean(k smallest areas)` with `k≈8–16` and a weight decaying from roughly `0.25` to `0.02`; retain exact normalized minimum area as the final selection criterion and throughout polishing. The present ring parameterization already succeeds with direct maximin optimization, but a temporary tail term can prevent early convergence to layouts that improve one bottleneck while leaving many nearly active triangles.

3. **Replace the single current-to-best donor rule with a deterministic mutation portfolio scheduled by progress.** Early generations should retain a substantial fraction of broad difference mutations such as `rand/1`, while later generations increasingly use current-to-best/1 and top-10%-elite difference donors; choose the mode per candidate with fixed progress-dependent probabilities. This directly builds on the successful staged DE schedule and diversity injections, while giving the population both basin-discovery moves and stronger late exploitation without resorting to slower coordinate annealing.

4. **Maintain several distinct high-quality elites and polish them independently instead of only polishing one incumbent.** At the end of DE, select perhaps 6–12 candidates using both score and a minimum distance in radius/phase parameter space, then split the current 260×96 local-search budget across those basins and return the best refined layout. The existing diversity injections demonstrate that phase-locking is a real concern; preserving diverse finalists can discover a better local maximin arrangement than repeatedly perturbing a single DE winner.

5. **Make local refinement bottleneck-aware within the ring coordinates.** For the incumbent, identify the lowest-area triangles and count which rings and parameter dimensions participate most in their interior points; bias candidate perturbations toward those rings, include paired radius-phase moves, and occasionally apply coordinated changes to two rings. Continue accepting only strict exact-objective improvements, but spend more of the fixed batched-polish budget on the parameters controlling active constraints rather than uniformly perturbing all six coordinates.
