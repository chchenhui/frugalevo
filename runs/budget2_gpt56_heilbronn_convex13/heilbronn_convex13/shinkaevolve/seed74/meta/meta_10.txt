# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- **Batched differential evolution plus focused polishing is the strongest observed pattern.** The current best, **Deterministic Differential Evolution for Heilbronn 13** (Generation 1), reached the top combined score of **0.63** with normalized minimum area **0.02** in only **2.22 s**. Its key combination was: stratified initial configurations, fully vectorized population-wide triangle scoring, differential-evolution proposals, elite preservation, and final direct local search using the actual normalized objective.

- **A lower-tail surrogate improves global-search behavior relative to raw minimum-only fitness.** The current best scores each candidate as `minimum area + 0.16 * mean(12 smallest areas)`. This guards against candidates that improve one active worst triangle while leaving many nearly degenerate triangles. Comparable lower-tail approaches also produced relatively good results: the triangular-hull **Simulated Annealing Convex 13-Point Search** scored **0.58**, and the incremental annealing method scored **0.57**, both with normalized area **0.02**.

- **Stratified initialization appears materially better than purely random placement.** The best program initializes most of its population by selecting 13 of 16 grid cells and adding bounded jitter, while reserving one quarter for independent uniform starts. This produces dispersed candidates immediately. In contrast, **Seeded Random 13-Point Placement** had normalized minimum area effectively **0.00** and combined score **0.01**.

- **Constraint-safe geometry supports robust scoring.** Fixed-corner unit-square methods consistently ensure hull area one, so raw triangle area directly corresponds to normalized area. The best program instead keeps points in the square through reflection and computes normalized hull scores for finalists; this retains flexibility while preserving feasible coordinates. The successful triangular-hull annealing variant likewise guaranteed feasibility through barycentric coordinates and reached **0.58**.

## Ineffective Approaches

- **Unoptimized random generation is decisively inadequate.** Generation 0 performed no minimum-area search and achieved only **0.01** combined score. Uniformly random points readily create near-collinear triples, so validity alone does not produce meaningful Heilbronn-style quality.

- **Longer annealing budgets did not translate to better configurations.** The six-restart **Deterministic Annealed Triangle-Area Search** required **8.06 s** for score **0.57**, while the four-restart annealed search took **8.95 s** and reached only **0.47** with normalized area **0.01**. The current best exceeded both quality and speed, indicating that extensive sequential local moves can be less effective than broad batched exploration.

- **Population methods based largely on Gaussian perturbations and attraction to the incumbent were weaker than differential evolution.** Generations 3, 7, and 9 all used variants of annealed mutations, best-layout attraction, differential proposals, restarts, and polishing, yet scored only **0.20**, **0.37**, and **0.37**, respectively, all with normalized area **0.01**. Their feedback consistently identifies modest discovered layouts despite substantial generation counts or multi-stage search.

- **Fixed-corner constructions are reliable but may constrain discovery when paired with weak search dynamics.** Island evolution (Generation 8) did reach normalized area **0.02**, but scored **0.54**, below the current best’s **0.63**, despite a more elaborate island/restart/polish pipeline. Corners simplify normalization, but that alone did not overcome the quality gap.

## Implementation Insights

- **The current best’s vectorized objective is a central efficiency advantage.** `_all_triangle_areas` evaluates all 286 triples for an entire `(population, 13, 2)` batch using NumPy broadcasting. With population size 160 and 360 generations, this enables broad candidate evaluation without Python loops over candidates or triangles, contributing to the best observed **2.22 s** runtime.

- **Its initialization deliberately balances structure and diversity.** The 4×4 cell assignment with jitter provides separated starting points; replacing the final quarter of the population with uniform samples prevents the structured prior from becoming overly restrictive. This is a more purposeful starting distribution than the all-random baseline and more population-efficient than multiple costly independent annealing restarts.

- **Reflection is preferable to clipping for candidate repair.** Both differential-evolution donor points and local-polish proposals are reflected at `[0, 1]` boundaries. This avoids the artificial accumulation of points exactly on square edges that ordinary clipping can create, preserving variation near boundaries.

- **The best program separates global surrogate optimization from final objective selection.** During evolution it uses the low-tail blended batch score for stability; afterward it ranks finalists with `_normalized_signature`, which computes convex-hull-normalized minimum triangle area and uses the mean of the 12 smallest normalized areas only as a tie-breaker. This aligns the final returned configuration with the evaluator’s true metric rather than permanently optimizing only a surrogate.

## Performance Analysis

- **The current best is the clear Pareto leader among the evaluated valid programs.** Its **0.63** score is above the next-best **0.58** from triangular-hull simulated annealing and **0.57** from incremental annealing. It also runs in **2.22 s**, faster than those alternatives at **5.03 s** and **8.06 s**, respectively.

- **Normalized minimum area of 0.02 is a meaningful threshold in these evaluations.** The top five stronger methods—the current best (**0.63**), simulated annealing convex (**0.58**), incremental annealing (**0.57**), island evolution (**0.54**), and the original annealed search result at **0.47** only had 0.01—show that achieving reported **0.02** generally corresponds to the higher score tier. However, identical rounded normalized values still yield different combined scores, indicating that unreported precision, runtime, or secondary evaluator behavior distinguishes these configurations.

- **Runtime alone is not the determining factor.** The rank-based search was relatively fast at **2.60 s** but scored only **0.37** and found normalized area **0.01**. Conversely, expensive 5–9 second evolutionary/annealing variants often remained in the **0.20–0.58** range. The best program’s efficiency stems from vectorized population search and a limited, targeted polishing phase rather than from simply increasing search iterations.

- **Search design matters more than adding optimization components.** Generation 8 combined islands, differential evolution, Gaussian mutation, restarts, tail objectives, and stochastic polishing but reached **0.54**. The current best used a comparatively compact pipeline—stratified initialization, differential evolution, elitism, lower-tail scoring, finalist normalization, and local reflected random search—and achieved **0.63**.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Replace the single fixed lower-tail weight with a staged surrogate schedule.** During early differential-evolution generations, score candidates with a stronger tail term such as `min_area + 0.25–0.35 * mean(smallest 12)` to promote broadly nondegenerate layouts, then anneal toward the current `0.16` or even `0.05` late in the run to focus on the true bottleneck triangle. This directly extends the current best program’s successful min-plus-lower-tail objective while reducing the risk that early search overcommits to one active triangle.

2. **Use a mixed differential-evolution mutation portfolio rather than only `rand/1`.** For each trial vector, randomly choose between the existing `x_r1 + F(x_r2 - x_r3)`, a current-to-best form such as `x + F*(elite - x) + F*(r1-r2)`, and an elite-difference form using members of the top 10–20% population. The weaker attraction-heavy population methods should not replace the current broad DE core, but limited use of elite-guided donors may improve late-stage exploitation while retaining the vectorized global search responsible for the 2.22-second result.

3. **Make stratified initialization more geometry-aware by seeding a small fraction with boundary/hull-biased layouts.** Retain the successful 4×4 jittered-cell population and uniform-diversity quarter, but add perhaps 10–20% configurations containing points sampled near the four edges/corners plus dispersed interior points. Since normalized scoring benefits from a large convex hull and fixed-corner approaches reliably reached normalized area `0.02`, these seeds can expose high-hull-area basins without imposing corners permanently or sacrificing the current method’s flexibility.

4. **Upgrade the final polish from single-point greedy moves to active-constraint targeted proposals.** After computing all normalized triangle areas, identify the points occurring in the lowest 8–16 triangles and preferentially mutate those points, occasionally perturbing two points jointly or applying a small affine-like displacement to a local group. Preserve the current true-objective acceptance rule and reflected boundaries; this focuses costly local evaluations on the coordinates controlling the current minimum while avoiding the broad, slow annealing schedules that underperformed.

5. **Add diversity-aware survivor selection to prevent the population from collapsing around near-duplicates.** Keep the existing top elites, but fill a few survivor slots from candidates that are both high-scoring and sufficiently distant from selected survivors in flattened coordinate space or sorted pairwise-distance signature space. The best implementation already balances structured and uniform starts; maintaining that diversity through later generations can yield more distinct normalized-objective finalists for polishing, rather than spending all six local restarts in essentially the same basin.
