# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- **Generation 29 “Deterministic DE and Local Search” remains a top-scoring evaluated design at `0.73`, tied with the best ring-based methods.** Its fixed-corner unit-square representation, `192`-member population, `460` DE generations, current-to-elite mutation, exact elite restoration, two immigration events, and ten-finalist polishing collectively outperform the other unrestricted fixed-square searches. This is substantially better than Generation 34 (`0.50`), Generation 37 (`0.56`), Generation 38 (`0.57`), and the newly reported Generation 39 anchored hybrid DE (`0.45`).

- **Staged lower-tail surrogate scoring plus exact final selection is a repeated high-value pattern.** Generation 29 scores candidates as `minimum area + tail_weight * mean(20 smallest areas)`, decreasing the tail weight from roughly `0.25` early to `0.02` late. This stabilizes early DE selection without abandoning the true maximin objective: finalists and global-best updates are ultimately determined by strict minimum triangle area, with tail quality used only as a tie-breaker. Generation 37’s use of the lowest 18 triangle areas and its `0.56` result provide weaker but consistent evidence that lower-tail guidance is useful.

- **Active-small-triangle local refinement is present in every `0.73` result.** Generation 29 repeatedly identifies the 14 smallest-area triangles, extracts their involved free points, and generates batches of mostly one-point perturbations plus occasional two-point moves. The same bottleneck-aware refinement theme appears in the `0.73` ring/orbit programs: Generation 31 (`10.36 s`), Generation 36 (`2.73 s`), and Generation 40 (`3.22 s`). In contrast, global evolutionary methods without similarly effective polishing generally remain in the `0.45–0.57` range.

- **Representation reduction can preserve peak score while greatly reducing runtime.** The compact three-ring parameterizations in Generations 31, 36, and 40 reach the same `0.73` as Generation 29 while using only six effective parameters instead of 18 free Cartesian coordinates. The best evidence is Generation 36, which matches Generation 29’s score in `2.73 s` versus `9.76 s`, and Generation 40, which does so in `3.22 s`.

## Ineffective Approaches

- **The new Generation 39 “Hybrid Differential Evolution for Heilbronn-13” underperforms despite being evaluator-aligned and correct.** It fixes the four corners, uses batched areas, stratified/random/Beta initialization, DE mutation, elitism, and active-triangle perturbations, yet obtains only `0.45` in `5.08 s`. Relative to Generation 29’s `0.73`, this indicates that merely combining DE with local refinement is insufficient; the stronger long-horizon diversity schedule, surrogate staging, larger search budget, and finalist polish in Generation 29 matter materially.

- **Broad high-dimensional unconstrained or barycentric global search has not matched the leading fixed-square or ring methods.** Generation 30’s ten-interior-point barycentric evolutionary search reaches `0.52` in `8.22 s`, despite including bottleneck mutations, restarts, DE-like moves, and coordinate polishing. Generation 32’s fixed-simplex DE reaches `0.56` in `3.16 s`. Containment guarantees from barycentric/simplex parameterizations do not compensate for the more difficult global landscape compared with the fixed-square representation.

- **Annealing-heavy search provides limited score return for substantial runtime.** Generation 33 uses six simulated-annealing restarts, incremental updates, targeted active-triangle samples, coupled moves, and multiscale polishing, but reaches only `0.57` in `18.03 s`. It is almost twice as slow as Generation 29 while scoring `0.16` lower, and over six times slower than Generation 36 while also scoring lower.

- **Partial versions of the successful pipeline remain moderate performers.** Fixed-square DE programs such as Generation 34 (`0.50`), Generation 37 (`0.56`), Generation 38 (`0.57`), and Generation 39 (`0.45`) share useful ingredients—fixed corners, batched triangle evaluation, DE-like search, and local movement—but lack the empirically successful full combination seen in Generation 29: a large population, long staged schedule, elite preservation, deterministic immigration, ten true-objective finalists, and extensive active-set batch polish.

## Implementation Insights

- **Generation 29’s geometry exactly removes normalization cost and ambiguity.** By permanently placing points 0–3 at `(0,0)`, `(1,0)`, `(1,1)`, and `(0,1)`, every candidate has convex-hull area one. Its `_areas` function therefore evaluates the final normalized objective directly as raw triangle areas across all `286` triples, avoiding hull calculations or post-search normalization errors. Generation 39 also benefits from this design, confirming that it is correctness-enabling, though not alone score-determining.

- **The current best’s batched NumPy evaluation supports a much stronger search budget.** `_areas` gathers all triangle vertices with indexed arrays and computes cross products across whole candidate batches. `signature_batch` uses `np.partition(values, 19, axis=1)` to obtain the lowest 20 areas efficiently, allowing all `192` DE candidates and `16` local proposals per step to be scored without Python loops over triangles. This implementation makes Generation 29’s `460` generations and `10 × 1550` local-polish iterations practical within `9.76 s`.

- **Generation 29 implements diversity explicitly rather than relying only on DE donor variation.** Its initial population combines shuffled 3-by-3 stratified layouts with random replacements for one quarter of the population. During evolution, it preserves the top 12 pre-generation elites exactly and replaces the bottom 10% with fresh stratified candidates at generations 105 and 205. This is a concrete difference from weaker fixed-square variants and is consistent with its `0.73` result.

- **The local polish is carefully aligned with changing active constraints.** At each iteration, Generation 29 finds the 14 worst triangles, perturbs only free points that participate in those triangles, and chooses the best candidate from a 16-proposal batch. A decaying tolerance permits early movement between competing active-triangle sets, while late iterations become strict hill climbing. This avoids blind coordinate perturbation and helps explain why active refinement in Generation 29 and the `0.73` ring methods is more effective than generic local search in lower-scoring programs.

## Performance Analysis

- **Generation 29 is the strongest unrestricted fixed-square result and remains tied for the best observed score.** Its `0.73` exceeds Generation 30 (`0.52`) by `0.21`, Generation 32 (`0.56`) by `0.17`, Generation 33 (`0.57`) by `0.16`, Generation 34 (`0.50`) by `0.23`, Generation 37 (`0.56`) by `0.17`, Generation 38 (`0.57`) by `0.16`, and Generation 39 (`0.45`) by `0.28`. The comparison strongly favors the mature DE-plus-active-polish pipeline over less developed unrestricted alternatives.

- **The best score is not uniquely tied to expensive unrestricted search.** Generation 29 obtains `0.73` in `9.76 s`, but ring-based symmetric programs reach the same score much faster: Generation 36 in `2.73 s` and Generation 40 in `3.22 s`; Generation 31 reaches it in `10.36 s`. Thus, the six-parameter ring family is the most runtime-efficient route to the observed evaluation ceiling, while Generation 29 demonstrates that broad 18-coordinate optimization can still match it.

- **Runtime alone does not explain score differences among DE methods.** Generation 39 runs in `5.08 s` but scores only `0.45`; Generation 37 runs in `4.81 s` and scores `0.56`; Generation 32 runs in `3.16 s` and scores `0.56`; Generation 36 runs in `2.73 s` and scores `0.73`. The strong correlation is instead with representation quality and the completeness of the optimization pipeline: tail-aware selection, diversity management, elitism, and bottleneck-specific refinement.

- **Rounded displayed minimum area does not distinguish solution quality.** Generation 29, the `0.73` ring programs, and many weaker programs all display `min_area_normalized: 0.02`, including Generation 39 at `0.45`. The score differences therefore reflect unrounded bottleneck area, lower-tail triangle distribution, and evaluator-sensitive geometric quality rather than the two-decimal displayed minimum alone.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Hybridize the fixed-square DE population with the proven six-parameter three-ring family.** Seed 30–50% of the 192 candidates from the Generation 36/40 ring-orbit construction, then convert them to the nine free square points and apply small independent reflected perturbations so DE can break symmetry. Keep stratified and uniform candidates for the remaining population; this preserves global coverage while starting many searches in basins already known to reach `0.73` much faster.

2. **Replace the single current-to-elite DE operator with a deterministic staged mutation portfolio.** During approximately generations 0–150, use a mixture such as 40% `rand/1`, 35% current-to-pbest/1, and 25% current-to-elite; progressively shift to mostly current-to-elite or current-to-pbest in the final half. Generation 29’s diversity mechanisms are clearly valuable, and a portfolio directly addresses premature convergence without removing its successful elite restoration, tail schedule, or immigration events.

3. **Make finalist selection explicitly basin-diverse before spending 1,550 local steps per candidate.** Rank candidates lexicographically by `(strict minimum area, lower-tail mean)`, but greedily retain only candidates separated by a threshold under a canonicalized free-point distance—e.g., matching-sorted coordinates or nearest-neighbor point-set distance—until ten distinct basins are selected. This makes the expensive active-triangle polish less redundant, especially because DE populations can contain many close copies of one elite geometry after 460 generations.

4. **Add deterministic area-gradient proposals to the active-triangle polishing batch.** For each active free point, aggregate gradients of signed doubled triangle area over the 14–20 worst triangles, weighting more tightly constrained triangles more heavily; include normalized ascent and short line-search scales alongside the existing 12 random one-point and 4 random two-point proposals. Retain random perturbations for active-set transitions, but gradient-based candidates should improve the efficiency of the bottleneck-aware refinement shared by all `0.73` designs.

5. **Use a two-stage local-polish funnel with a late exact-maximin solver phase.** First polish 10–12 diverse finalists for roughly 400–700 iterations using the current `min + 0.035*tail` acceptance, then retain only the best 2–3 and devote the remaining iterations to strict lexicographic acceptance on `(minimum area, tail)` with smaller proposal scales and gradient/paired-active-point moves. This extends Generation 29’s successful surrogate-to-exact philosophy while concentrating the most expensive fine search on configurations that already demonstrate the strongest true bottleneck quality.
