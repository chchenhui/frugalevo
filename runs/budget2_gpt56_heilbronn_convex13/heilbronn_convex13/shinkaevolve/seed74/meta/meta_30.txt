# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- **The current best supplied program, Generation 29 “Deterministic DE and Local Search,” confirms that a fixed unit-square hull plus active-constraint DE can reach the top observed combined score of `0.73`.** Fixing all four corners makes hull area exactly one, so the raw triangle-area objective is evaluator-aligned. Its `0.73` matches the prior leading Generation 12 triangular-ring DE result, although Generation 29 takes `9.76 s` versus Generation 12’s `5.24 s`.

- **Current-to-elite DE with staged exploration/exploitation is repeatedly effective.** Generation 29 uses a declining differential factor (`0.82` to `0.48`), decreasing crossover probability, elite preservation, and early immigration. This parallels the successful staged DE design previously identified in Generation 12. Square-based DE variants with comparable ingredients were valid but weaker or less efficient: Generation 18 reached `0.55` in `7.43 s`, while Generation 26 reached `0.52` in `1.34 s`.

- **Targeting points involved in low-area triangles is a productive local-search pattern when paired with population search.** Generation 29’s polishing repeatedly identifies the 14 worst triangles, extracts participating free-point indices, and perturbs mostly one active point with occasional two-point moves. This produced `0.73`, outperforming primarily annealing-oriented active-constraint methods such as Generation 24 (`0.57`, `8.09 s`) and Generation 23 (`0.52`, `7.39 s`).

- **The six-parameter triangular three-ring family remains a highly successful compact parameterization.** Generations 21, 22, 25, and 28 all reached `0.73`; notably Generation 22 did so in only `1.15 s`. This reinforces the previous finding from Generation 12 that three independently phased/radius-controlled rings retain enough flexibility for strong layouts, unlike more rigid symmetric constructions.

## Ineffective Approaches

- **Broader barycentric interior-coordinate searches underperformed despite guaranteed containment.** Generation 27’s ten-point barycentric evolutionary search achieved only normalized minimum area `0.01` and combined score `0.43` in `3.04 s`. Generation 23 improved to `0.52` with hybrid perturbations, bottleneck targeting, DE moves, restarts, and polishing, but still trailed the `0.73` DE/ring leaders. Feasibility alone did not translate into stronger maximin configurations.

- **Sequential annealing and extensive generic hybrid search were less score-effective than focused DE.** Generation 24’s six-restart simulated annealing with incremental triangle updates and active-bottleneck polishing reached `0.57` but required `8.09 s`. Generation 18’s DE version likewise reached only `0.55` in `7.43 s`. Their valid fixed-square designs did not equal the current program’s combination of large population DE, elite retention, immigration, and extensive batched active-set polishing.

- **Strong symmetry can reduce search dimensionality but can also cap layout quality if the family is too restrictive.** The three-ring programs reliably reach `0.73`, but their feedback consistently attributes the modest `0.02` reported minimum area to the constrained ring family. The previous Generation 11 result is the extreme case: its more rigid center/two-orbit/hexagon arrangement scored `0.00`, showing that independent phases and a sufficient number of independently tunable rings are essential.

- **Lower runtime alone did not imply better objective quality.** Generation 26’s vectorized fixed-square DE completed in `1.34 s`, but scored `0.52`; Generation 27 completed in `3.04 s` but scored `0.43`. In contrast, the very fast symmetric-ring Generation 22 (`1.15 s`) still achieved `0.73`, indicating that compact, evaluator-aligned geometry—not merely fewer iterations—is what enabled both speed and quality there.

## Implementation Insights

- **Generation 29 evaluates all `286` triangles in vectorized NumPy batches and explicitly separates global surrogate selection from final exact-maximin selection.** During DE it scores `minimum + tail_weight * mean(lowest 20 areas)`, with tail weight annealed from roughly `0.25` toward `0.02`. After evolution, it ranks finalists by the true minimum area, breaking ties with the lower-tail statistic. This directly addresses the nonsmoothness of pure maximin optimization while ensuring final candidate choice is governed by the actual primary metric.

- **The current program’s fixed-corner representation makes boundary and normalization handling exceptionally simple.** Only nine points move; the corners are restored before return; and `_reflect_unit` maps even large differential donors back into `[0,1]^2` through repeated reflection. This avoids invalid hulls and avoids costly convex-hull-area computation. The same evaluator alignment was a strength in Generations 18 and 26, though their optimization schedules did not attain Generation 29’s score.

- **Generation 29’s diversity mechanisms are concrete rather than purely random.** It begins with shuffled 3-by-3 stratified cells and jitter, replaces one quarter of the initial population with uniform random starts, preserves the top 12 pre-generation elites, and injects fresh stratified candidates at generations 105 and 205. This is consistent with the previous Generation 12 insight that structured initial coverage plus scheduled diversity replacement mitigates phase-locking or premature convergence.

- **The active-set local polish is computationally heavy but tightly focused.** For each of ten finalists, Generation 29 runs 1,550 iterations of 16 proposals, selecting the best proposal by a near-maximin score and gradually reducing perturbation scale. Unlike blind coordinate search, proposals are concentrated on points in the current worst 14 triangles. This targeted mechanism helps explain its `0.73`, but also contributes to its `9.76 s` runtime. Generation 22 shows that a more compact six-parameter representation can reach the same score with much lower evaluation cost.

## Performance Analysis

- **Generation 29 is tied for the highest observed combined score at `0.73`, but it is not the most time-efficient top scorer.** It matches Generation 12 and Generations 21, 22, 25, and 28, all of which report normalized minimum area `0.02` and combined score `0.73`. Its `9.76 s` runtime is substantially slower than Generation 22 (`1.15 s`), Generation 28 (`5.07 s`), Generation 21 (`6.01 s`), and the earlier Generation 12 leader (`5.24 s`).

- **Reported normalized minimum area rounded to `0.02` continues to conceal meaningful score differences.** Generation 29, the ring-layout programs, Generation 18 (`0.55`), Generation 24 (`0.57`), and Generation 26 (`0.52`) all report `0.02`, yet their combined scores range from `0.52` to `0.73`. This supports the prior conclusion that unrounded maximin values, secondary behavior, and runtime influence ranking beyond the displayed two-decimal minimum-area metric.

- **The new results strengthen the evidence that both structured-ring DE and robust fixed-square DE are viable paths to the top score, but with different efficiency profiles.** Ring layouts reached `0.73` repeatedly using only six radius/phase variables; the fixed-square current program reached the same score while optimizing 18 free coordinates. The latter demonstrates that the square representation is not inherently inferior, but it required a much longer search and polish schedule.

- **The performance hierarchy is consistent across the evaluated set.** The strongest group is the `0.73` DE/ring and current fixed-square active-DE methods. Mid-tier methods include fixed-square annealing or less refined DE at `0.52–0.57`. Barycentric evolutionary search is lower at `0.43` with `0.01` normalized area. Thus, the main empirical differentiator is not simply hull choice or deterministic seeding, but the combination of evaluator-aligned constraints, population-level DE diversity, and targeted treatment of active low-area triangles.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Seed the fixed-square DE population with high-quality three-ring layouts, then let the nine free square coordinates break their symmetry.** Generate perhaps 25–40% of the initial population from the proven six-parameter triangular three-ring construction, optionally with small independent Cartesian perturbations, while retaining stratified and uniform starts for diversity. This combines Generation 22’s very fast access to `0.73`-quality basins with Generation 29’s more flexible 18-dimensional representation, which may allow improvement beyond the ring family’s apparent geometric ceiling.

2. **Replace the single current-to-elite donor with a deterministic, progress-scheduled DE mutation portfolio.** In early generations, use a substantial `rand/1` or `rand-to-elite/1` fraction to discover distinct basins; transition through current-to-pbest/1 and finish mainly with current-to-elite/1 as in the present best program. Keep the existing declining factor, elite preservation, reflection, and immigration, but choose mutation modes from fixed progress-dependent probabilities so exploration is broader without sacrificing the demonstrated late-stage exploitation strength.

3. **Make finalist selection explicitly diversity-aware before the expensive active-set polish.** Rather than taking the ten highest lexicographic `(minimum area, lower-tail mean)` candidates, greedily select finalists that are both high-scoring and separated in free-coordinate space, for example after point-order canonicalization or via pairwise RMS distance. The current program already demonstrates that multiple basins can be polished successfully; ensuring those starts are genuinely distinct should reduce repeated polishing of near-duplicate DE solutions and improve the chance of escaping a shared local optimum.

4. **Upgrade active-triangle polishing from random active-point perturbations to sensitivity-guided coordinated moves.** For the worst 14–20 triangles, estimate for each involved free point the signed area gradient contributions, then propose directions that increase several active triangle areas simultaneously rather than isotropic Gaussian displacements alone. Retain a minority of two-point proposals, but make them target pairs that co-occur frequently among bottleneck triangles; this directly extends the current successful active-set idea while giving each expensive batch a more purposeful direction.

5. **Use a two-level local-search schedule that shortens broad polishing and reserves a final exact-maximin phase for the strongest few candidates.** First apply a cheaper surrogate-guided active polish to all 10–12 diverse finalists using the existing `min + tail` score, then retain only the best 2–3 and run a stricter final refinement accepting improvements by exact minimum area first and lower-tail area only as a tie-break. Generation 29 shows active polishing is valuable but costly (`9.76 s`), while the fast ring results show that reaching strong basins efficiently matters; this allocation should preserve quality while reducing redundant late local search.
