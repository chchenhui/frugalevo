<DESCRIPTION>
Replace part of the isotropic random coordinate proposals with directions derived from the currently most restrictive incident triangles.  For a limiting triangle containing the moving point, its area changes most directly by moving that point along the normal to the opposite edge; choosing the sign away from that edge increases its altitude.  Additional directions repel the point from its nearest neighbors, helping resolve clustered low-area triples.  Random directions are retained for escaping competing-constraint local traps.  This makes the same proposal budget substantially more aligned with the determinant-area objective, especially in late-stage refinement.
</DESCRIPTION>

                # Area of a triangle incident to p is proportional to the
                # altitude of p over the edge joining its other two vertices.
                # Therefore normals to the opposite edges of the tightest
                # incident triangles are much more useful than isotropic
                # proposals when resolving an active determinant constraint.
                directions = np.empty((12, 2), dtype=float)
                local_order = np.argsort(areas[incident[p]])
                for slot, local_index in enumerate(local_order[:3]):
                    tight_triangle = tri[incident[p][local_index]]
                    other = tight_triangle[tight_triangle != p]
                    edge = points[other[1]] - points[other[0]]
                    normal = np.array([-edge[1], edge[0]], dtype=float)
                    side = np.cross(edge, current - points[other[0]])
                    if side < 0.0:
                        normal = -normal
                    directions[slot] = normal

                # Nearest-neighbor repulsion supplies geometry-aware escape
                # directions for several nearly limiting triangles at once.
                distances = np.linalg.norm(points - current, axis=1)
                distances[p] = np.inf
                for slot, neighbor in enumerate(np.argsort(distances)[:3], start=3):
                    directions[slot] = current - points[neighbor]

                # Keep a random component: active triangle normals can point
                # into another constraint, so exploration remains necessary.
                directions[6:] = rng.normal(size=(6, 2))
                directions /= np.maximum(
                    np.linalg.norm(directions, axis=1, keepdims=True), 1e-12
                )
                radii = step * (0.25 + 1.15 * rng.random(12))
                proposals[1:] = current + directions * radii[:, None]
