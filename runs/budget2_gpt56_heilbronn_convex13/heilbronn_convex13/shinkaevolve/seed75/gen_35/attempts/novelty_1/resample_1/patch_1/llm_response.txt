<DESCRIPTION>
Replace the final single-minimum polishing rule with a strict lexicographic lower-tail comparator. The raw minimum remains the dominant objective: candidates are first filtered to those attaining the best feasible minimum area (up to a tiny numerical tolerance). Within that plateau, the sum of the smallest eight triangle areas and then the second-smallest area are used as deterministic tie-breakers.

This is useful for Heilbronn configurations because many active or nearly active triangles create nonsmooth max-min plateaus. Pure `argmax(candidate_min)` repeatedly selects the unchanged point or an arbitrary first tied direction, whereas lower-tail shaping redistributes pressure among near-active constraints without sacrificing the achieved bottleneck. The phased 48-direction stencil also avoids repeatedly sampling the same compass directions on successive rounds.
</DESCRIPTION>
