# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- **The current best program’s fixed-corner square formulation remains a top-performing, reliable pattern.** The current program, *Deterministic Soft-Min Heilbronn Search* (Generation 26), fixes all four unit-square corners and optimizes only nine interior points. It achieved the top reported combined score of **0.74** with normalized minimum area **0.02**. Fixing the corners makes the hull area exactly one, so raw triangle-area comparisons are exactly aligned with the normalized evaluator objective.

- **Soft lower-tail exploration followed by strict max-min refinement consistently reaches the leading score tier.** Generation 26 uses temperature-annealed soft-min candidate scoring during broad search, then switches to exact minimum-area directional polishing. This matches the successful structure identified for Generation 2 (**0.74**), Generation 23 (**0.74**), and Generation 27 (**0.74**). In contrast to pure surrogate optimization, these methods use the smooth objective only to navigate early nonsmooth regions and ultimately optimize the actual bottleneck metric.

- **Moderate multi-restart coordinate search is more consistently effective than elaborate global metaheuristics.** Generation 26’s ten deterministic restarts with lattice, staggered, and interleaved seeds attained **0.74**. Generation 27 similarly used ten restarts and plateau-aware strict polishing for **0.74** in **4.53 s**. Generation 23 also reached **0.74** with active-triangle directions and coupled repairs, but needed **8.32 s**. These results reinforce that diverse but structured initialization plus coordinate-level refinement is sufficient to reach the observed score ceiling.

- **The fixed-hull idea generalizes, but square implementations are usually more efficient.** Generation 20’s fixed equilateral triangle hull and ten interior-point optimization also reached **0.74** and normalized area **0.02**, confirming that fixing a known hull eliminates normalization ambiguity. However, its **14.00 s** runtime was far larger than square-based score ties such as Generation 27 (**4.53 s**) and the earlier Generation 2 (**4.02 s**), so the square layout remains the more efficient confirmed formulation.

## Ineffective Approaches

- **Differential-evolution islands did not translate global-search diversity into a strong bottleneck solution.** *Deterministic Island Differential Evolution* (Generation 29) used four seeded DE islands, continuation scoring, migration, and final exact polishing, but achieved only **0.39** with normalized minimum area **0.01** in **0.89 s**. It was fast, but its population-based mechanism did not reach the quality obtained by structured coordinate-restart searches.

- **Additional geometric proposal complexity did not improve on simple stochastic coordinate proposals.** Generation 23’s active-triangle normals, nearest-neighbor repulsion, edge proposals, coupled repairs, and circular polish tied the best score at **0.74**, but took **8.32 s**. Generation 28 added periodic coupled bottleneck moves and scored only **0.64** in **8.47 s**. Generation 30 used signed-area gradients, lower-tail weighting, and joint moves but reached **0.61** in **5.44 s**. The evaluated evidence does not show that these more specialized moves improve the score ceiling.

- **Symmetry-constrained and highly structured global parameterizations limited attainable quality.** Generation 21’s threefold-symmetric orbit search plus local polishing was robust and fast (**0.82 s**) but scored only **0.64**. The reduced eight-parameter orbit representation likely improves search efficiency at the expense of the positional flexibility needed to resolve all limiting triples.

- **Longer search budgets alone did not improve the observed normalized minimum-area tier.** Generation 20’s 16-restart triangle search required **14.00 s** for **0.74**, while Generation 22 used twelve restarts and 520 sweeps for **0.72** in **9.54 s**. Both remained in the same rounded **0.02** area cluster as the faster leading square methods, showing that additional sweeps, restarts, or proposal families did not produce a verified improvement beyond the established score level.

## Implementation Insights

- **The current program efficiently combines cached global areas with exact incident-triangle updates.** In Generation 26, moving one interior point recalculates only its incident triangles, while all unaffected triangle areas are reused from the cached `areas` vector. Candidate evaluation still reconstructs the full 286-triangle distribution for exact soft-bottleneck ranking. This preserves correct global comparisons without recomputing every triangle from scratch for each candidate.

- **The current program’s proposal schedule is deliberately multiscale and non-destructive.** Each coordinate update includes the current position among 13 proposals, so the selected soft-min candidate cannot be worse than staying put under that search score. Early sweeps use random directional moves and occasional global replacements, while late sweeps reduce both step size and temperature. The final four-scale circular polish uses strict `np.min` candidate comparison, matching the actual evaluation objective.

- **Fixed corners are both an objective simplification and a validity mechanism.** Generations 22, 23, 25, 26, 27, 28, 29, and 30 all retain square corners, guaranteeing a unit convex hull and avoiding hull-area degeneration. Generation 26 additionally clips interior points to a safe range, ensuring that exploratory proposals remain inside the square while preserving the corner-defined hull.

- **Tie-breaking and lower-tail shaping are useful only when subordinate to the strict bottleneck.** Generation 26’s stable soft bottleneck incorporates the full lower tail during exploration, but its final polishing accepts moves based on exact candidate minimum area. Generation 27 similarly uses a lower-tail sum only to resolve max-min plateaus and tied the top score at **0.74**. By comparison, Generation 25’s stronger annealing emphasis reached only **0.65** in **5.47 s**, supporting the conclusion that the true minimum must dominate late-stage selection.

## Performance Analysis

- **Generation 26 is a score leader, but not the most efficient member of the leading cluster.** The current program achieved **0.74**, normalized area **0.02**, in **7.07 s**. It tied Generation 23 (**0.74**, **8.32 s**) and Generation 20 (**0.74**, **14.00 s**) while outperforming Generation 25 (**0.65**) and Generation 28 (**0.64**). However, Generation 27 reached the same **0.74** in **4.53 s**, and the previously reported Generation 2 reached **0.74** in **4.02 s**, making those implementations more efficient at the same observed quality level.

- **The dominant result cluster remains fixed-hull local search at normalized minimum area 0.02.** Generations 20, 22, 23, 25, 26, 27, 28, and 30 all passed validation and mostly reported **0.02** normalized minimum area, with combined scores ranging from **0.61** to **0.74**. The score differences within this rounded area tier indicate that implementation details, exact unrounded geometry, and runtime all still matter even when the displayed minimum-area metric is the same.

- **Runtime and search sophistication have weak positive correlation with quality.** The fast orbit approach, Generation 21, ran in **0.82 s** but scored **0.64**; DE islands, Generation 29, ran in **0.89 s** but scored only **0.39**. Conversely, the longest run, Generation 20 at **14.00 s**, only tied the score of much faster coordinate-search methods. The strongest efficiency-quality tradeoff remains the simpler fixed-square restart-and-polish family.

- **Square methods remain the most consistently strong class, although the new triangle result shows the hull shape is not the sole determinant.** Generation 20’s triangle-hull method tied the leading **0.74**, unlike prior weaker triangle approaches, but required substantially more runtime. The square methods produced several **0.74** results across Generations 2, 23, 26, and 27, with the fastest documented score tie at **4.02–4.53 s**. Thus, the evidence favors fixed-hull normalization plus exact bottleneck-focused coordinate refinement; the square version has delivered that pattern more efficiently and repeatedly.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Replace final-polish `argmax(candidate_min)` with a strict lexicographic lower-tail comparator.** Rank each candidate by `(minimum_area, mean_of_smallest_k_areas, second_smallest_area)` for a small `k` such as 4–8, accepting only lexicographic improvements with a tiny tolerance on the primary minimum. This preserves exact max-min optimization while allowing productive plateau moves, matching the successful lower-tail shaping used during the current program’s soft-min phase and the efficient plateau-aware refinement pattern of Generation 27.

2. **Use successive-halving restart allocation rather than running all ten seeds for 520 sweeps.** For example, launch 16 structured seeds for 180–220 sweeps, rank them by exact minimum area plus lower-tail tie-breaks, retain the best 5–6, and give only those survivors the remaining sweeps and strict polish. This extends the already successful deterministic-restart strategy, increases basin coverage through more initial geometries, and can plausibly stay within the current runtime budget because weak starts are terminated early.

3. **Add active-triangle normal proposals as a small deterministic supplement to the 12 random coordinate directions.** For each movable point, identify its 2–4 smallest incident triangles and generate displacement directions perpendicular to the opposite edge, in both signs, with lengths proportional to the current sweep step; retain some random directions for diversity. Triangle area changes directly with the moving point’s altitude above the opposite edge, so these proposals target the actual bottlenecks while avoiding the heavy geometry machinery that made broader specialized-move systems slower without improving scores.

4. **Introduce rare stall-triggered two-point repairs during late search only.** If the strict minimum has not improved for roughly 30–50 sweeps, identify the movable vertices appearing most often among the globally smallest 4–6 triangles, choose a pair, and test a compact Cartesian set of joint displacements such as 8 compass directions at one or two small radii. Accept a joint move only if its full exact triangle-area vector improves the strict minimum or a lexicographic lower-tail key; this can escape coordinate-wise local optima without making expensive coupled search a constant part of every sweep.

5. **Expand initialization diversity with inexpensive near-solution perturbation seeds, not just noisy lattice variants.** Retain several existing lattice, staggered, and interleaved starts, but add seeds based on shifted-row/hexagonal layouts, rotated staggered grids, and small asymmetric perturbations of the best configuration found by an earlier restart. Fixed square corners should remain unchanged, since they guarantee unit hull area and align raw area optimization with the evaluator, while the additional seeds target different high-quality local basins at low implementation cost.
