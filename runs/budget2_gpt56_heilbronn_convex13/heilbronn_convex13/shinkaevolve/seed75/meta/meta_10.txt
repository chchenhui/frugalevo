# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- **Fixed unit-square corners plus direct raw-area optimization was the strongest pattern.** The current best, **Deterministic Multi-Restart Heilbronn Search** (Generation 2), fixed all four corners, guaranteeing convex-hull area exactly one and removing normalization uncertainty. It reached **min_area_normalized 0.02** and the best **combined score 0.74**. The closely related Generation 7 fixed-corner strict-bottleneck method matched the same area and score, reinforcing that this formulation is robust.

- **Bottleneck-first selection outperformed soft-min-only optimization.** Generation 2 used soft-min during broad coordinate search but ended with **strict max-min directional polishing** on the actual minimum-area metric. Generation 7 explicitly prioritized the true minimum area throughout candidate selection and also scored **0.74**. This contrasts with triangle-hull soft-min annealing approaches such as Generation 5, which scored only **0.07** and reported effectively zero normalized minimum area.

- **Multiple deterministic restarts with perturbed structured initialization were effective.** The current best combined **10 seeded restarts**, perturbed 3×3 interior-lattice starts, broad early coordinate proposals, and decreasing step sizes. The similar Generation 6 approach—with four annealing restarts, poor-triangle targeting, and polishing—achieved **0.58**, while Generation 11 achieved **0.65**. The stronger 10-restart configuration in Generation 2 correlated with the best observed score.

- **Local refinement after global exploration materially helped.** Generation 2’s final polishing searched fixed directions at four shrinking scales and evaluated strict candidate minima. The best fixed-corner programs all used a late-stage exact-objective refinement: Generation 2 and 7 scored **0.74**, versus general stochastic/evolutionary triangle-hull searches that remained at **0.16–0.53**.

## Ineffective Approaches

- **Unoptimized random placement failed completely on the geometric objective.** **Seeded Random 13-Point Placement** (Generation 0) was valid and fast but had **min_area_normalized 0.00** and **combined score 0.01**. Uniformly sampled points leave near-collinear or nearly coincident triples with no mechanism to repair them.

- **Fixed triangular hulls consistently underperformed fixed square corners.** The triangle-based simulated annealing program in Generation 1 and differential-evolution programs in Generations 3 and 4 reached only **0.16–0.27** combined score with normalized area **0.01**. Even the stronger adaptive triangle DE in Generation 8 reached **0.53**, below the square-corner approaches. Fixing three vertices constrains the layout to a triangular hull and leaves hull-area normalization/search geometry less favorable than the unit-square construction.

- **Soft-min objectives without sufficiently strong strict max-min correction were weak.** Generation 5 used a fixed triangular hull, six annealing restarts, and a soft-minimum objective but attained only **0.07** with reported area **0.00**. Its surrogate could improve the lower tail broadly without reliably raising the single limiting triangle that determines evaluation.

- **More expensive evolutionary search did not necessarily improve quality.** Generation 4 used deterministic DE for 1,800 generations and required **4.06 s**, yet achieved only **0.16**. Generation 8 increased sophistication with current-to-pbest DE, restarts, and refinement, requiring **5.21 s** for **0.53**. These results show that larger evolutionary budgets did not match the effectiveness of targeted coordinate-wise bottleneck search.

## Implementation Insights

- **The current best efficiently exploits locality of point moves.** In Generation 2, moving point `p` only changes triangles incident to `p`; `incident_areas` recomputes those local determinants while retaining previously computed areas for all unaffected triples. This is well aligned with coordinate search over nine movable points and supports evaluating 13 candidates per update without recomputing all 286 areas from scratch.

- **Its objective schedule combines smooth early search with exact late optimization.** The current best computes a numerically stable soft bottleneck,
  `minimum - temperature * log(sum(exp(...)))`,
  with a cooling temperature from roughly `0.010` to `0.00022`. This makes early moves responsive to several small triangles rather than only one discontinuous limiting triple. Its later polishing switches to `np.min`, directly matching the evaluator’s maximin-area target.

- **Candidate construction is deliberately non-destructive and multiscale.** Each current-best update includes the existing coordinate as proposal zero, ensuring the smooth score cannot decline merely because all sampled moves are worse. Early Gaussian directional proposals and occasional uniform long moves provide escape behavior, while final 24-direction moves at steps from `0.008` down to `0.0008` resolve local bottlenecks precisely.

- **The fixed-corner representation simplifies both correctness and scoring.** By preserving `(0,0)`, `(1,0)`, `(1,1)`, and `(0,1)`, the current best ensures all returned points are in the unit square and the convex hull has area one. This is specifically cited in feedback for Generations 2, 6, 7, and 11 as eliminating normalization ambiguity; the raw smallest triangle area is the normalized metric.

## Performance Analysis

- **The current best is the top observed configuration.** Generation 2 scored **0.74 in 4.02 s**, tied only by Generation 7’s related fixed-corner strict-bottleneck implementation at **0.74**, though Generation 7 was slower at **5.81 s**. Thus, the current best achieves the strongest measured score while also being the faster of the two top-scoring methods.

- **Fixed-corner local search formed a clear high-performing cluster.** Generation 2 scored **0.74**, Generation 7 **0.74**, Generation 11 **0.65**, and Generation 6 **0.58**; all reported normalized minimum area **0.02**. Their score differences despite the same rounded area indicate the combined metric likely preserves more precision and/or runtime influence, with Generation 2 benefiting from its lower 4.02-second evaluation time.

- **Triangular-hull methods formed a weaker cluster.** Generations 1 and 3 both reached **0.27**, Generation 4 reached **0.16**, Generation 5 fell to **0.07**, and Generation 8 reached **0.53**. Their normalized minimum areas were generally **0.01** or rounded to **0.00**, versus **0.02** for the leading square-corner methods.

- **Runtime alone was not predictive of quality.** The random baseline was negligible-time but scored **0.01**; Generation 1 achieved **0.27** in **1.85 s**; the current best achieved **0.74** in **4.02 s**; and slower searches did not improve on it—Generation 11 took **5.90 s** for **0.65**, while Generation 4 took **4.06 s** for **0.16**. The strongest correlation is not search duration but the combination of fixed unit-square hull, incremental coordinate updates, multi-restart exploration, and strict bottleneck polishing.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Keep the four fixed unit-square corners, but expand the deterministic restart portfolio with structured non-lattice seeds.** Retain the current 10 perturbed 3×3 starts, while adding several restart families such as staggered/hexagonal interior patterns, rotated-grid patterns, and seeds generated from a previously strong point set plus small perturbations. Fixed corners are consistently responsible for the strongest results, while broader high-quality initial geometry may help the coordinate search enter different max-min basins without abandoning the proven formulation.

2. **Use a lexicographic late-stage objective that maximizes the minimum area first and then improves the lower tail.** During strict polishing, rank proposals by a tuple such as `(minimum_area, mean_of_k_smallest_areas, second_smallest_area)` rather than only `minimum_area`; only accept a candidate if this tuple improves lexicographically. This preserves exact bottleneck-first behavior that succeeded in Generations 2 and 7, but reduces neutral moves among candidates with the same limiting area and may create room for later bottleneck improvements.

3. **Add targeted joint moves for points participating in the current worst triangles.** After each sweep or every few sweeps, identify the bottom 3–10 triangle areas and select their non-corner vertices; propose coordinated displacements for pairs or triples of those points rather than only one-point moves. The current local incident-area update is efficient for coordinate search, but the nonsmooth bottleneck landscape can require several points to move together to escape configurations where every individual move temporarily harms another near-minimum triangle.

4. **Make proposal directions geometry-aware in addition to random Gaussian directions.** For each movable point, derive candidate directions from the normals of lines through the other two vertices of its currently smallest incident triangles, plus directions toward/away from nearby points and square boundaries; mix these with the existing random directions. Because the limiting quantity is triangle determinant area, directions that directly increase altitude relative to the limiting triangle’s opposite edge should be more sample-efficient than purely isotropic proposals, especially during the final third of search.

5. **Replace part of the expensive broad search with adaptive restart allocation and longer refinement of promising runs.** Run all restarts through an initial reduced sweep budget, rank them by strict minimum triangle area or a low-tail score, then spend the remaining budget only on the best few states using the existing cooling schedule and strict polishing. Generation 2 already benefits from deterministic multi-restart exploration, and this successive-halving variation should preserve diversity while concentrating computation on configurations most likely to exceed the current `0.02` bottleneck.
