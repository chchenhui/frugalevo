# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- **The current best remains Generation 35 `lexicographic_polish`: fixed-square coordinate search with separated soft exploration and exact final optimization achieved the top score of 0.75 in 4.71 s.** Retaining `(0,0), (1,0), (1,1), (0,1)` fixes hull area at one, so the raw smallest triangle area exactly matches the normalized objective. Its ten restarts, 520 sweeps, and five-scale directional hard-polish stage improve slightly but materially over the 0.74 cluster.

- **A smooth lower-tail objective is useful in the exploratory phase only when hard max-min selection governs final moves.** Generation 35 uses a stable soft-min over all 286 triangle areas during the main search, then uses exact minimum area as the primary criterion in polishing, with the sum of the eight lowest areas and second-lowest area only as tie-breakers. This outperformed Generation 41 `stall_pair_repair`, which also used soft-min tie-breaking and exact max-min protection but reached 0.74 in 4.58 s, and Generation 37’s more mixed repair approach at 0.65.

- **Moderate deterministic multi-restart search on a square is both stronger and more efficient than the triangular-hull alternative.** Generation 39 `vectorized_triangle13` used 16 restarts and 560 sweeps on an equilateral triangular hull, but reached only 0.67 in 19.25 s. Earlier triangular Generation 33 reached 0.74 but needed 25.15 s. The fixed-square design used by Generations 35 and 41 reaches 0.75/0.74 in approximately 4.7/4.6 s.

- **Simple per-point directional proposals continue to outperform elaborate coupled repair logic.** Generation 35’s search uses 12 random radial proposals per movable point, includes the current position, and adds only two early global relocation proposals. Its straightforward final 48-direction, two-radius polish achieved 0.75. Generation 41’s late coupled two-point repairs did not exceed 0.74, while prior active-triangle and paired-repair designs similarly added complexity without improving the leader.

## Ineffective Approaches

- **Triangular hull reformulation did not improve solution quality and imposed a major runtime cost.** Generation 39 fixes an equilateral triangle and optimizes ten remaining points, yet scored 0.67 with a 19.25 s evaluation time. This is 0.08 below Generation 35 while taking over four times longer. The prior triangular Generation 33 also failed to surpass the square leader despite a 25.15 s runtime.

- **Increasing restart and sweep budgets alone was not productive.** Generation 39’s 16 restarts and 560 sweeps are more extensive than Generation 35’s 10 restarts and 520 sweeps, but the result is substantially worse. This reinforces the existing evidence from Generation 34 and Generation 33 that search-budget expansion cannot compensate for a less effective objective/hull/search structure.

- **Coupled repair mechanisms have not shown a measurable gain over strict coordinate-level polishing.** Generation 41 adds late two-point repairs and multiscale circular polishing, but remains at 0.74 rather than improving beyond Generation 35’s 0.75. Earlier Generation 37’s critical-triangle paired repairs reached 0.65, and Generation 40’s two-point active-normal repair design reached 0.62. These results suggest the added proposal complexity is not reliably resolving the true bottleneck better than sequential point moves.

- **Alternative global-search families remain weak on this nonsmooth constraint problem.** The previous DE programs remain far below the local-search leaders: Generation 31 scored 0.49 and Generation 38 scored 0.33. The new Generation 39 result further supports that changing geometry and adding longer global search does not displace the fixed-square local-search pattern.

## Implementation Insights

- **Generation 35’s cached incident-triangle update is a central efficiency mechanism.** A move of point `p` recomputes only areas for triangles incident to `p`, while all other triangle values remain cached. It still reconstructs the full candidate area spectrum before evaluating soft-min or lexicographic criteria. This combines local geometric recomputation with globally correct ranking and helps explain the 4.71 s runtime despite evaluating many proposals.

- **The current best makes every exploratory coordinate update non-destructive under its current objective.** Proposal index zero is the current coordinate. The selected proposal therefore cannot have a lower soft-bottleneck score than leaving the point in place. During early sweeps, two proposals are replaced by uniformly sampled interior relocations, which provides controlled escape from lattice-derived local basins without making the whole search population-based or destructive.

- **The final polish is tightly aligned to the evaluator’s objective.** For each candidate move, Generation 35 first rejects any move that decreases the exact current minimum area. It then maximizes candidate minimum area; only candidates effectively tied at that level are ranked by the sum of their eight smallest triangle areas and then their second-smallest area. This strict hierarchy is more directly aligned with the score than a late soft-min objective and is the key distinction behind the best observed 0.75 result.

- **Generation 41 confirms the value of vectorized exact area calculations but not the need for heavier repair logic.** Its NumPy-vectorized incident-triangle and batched candidate calculations produce a competitive 4.58 s runtime and a 0.74 score. Thus, vectorized local evaluation is a broadly successful implementation pattern; the remaining gap versus Generation 35 appears associated with optimization behavior—especially the latter’s explicit global relocations, restart seeding, and strict lexicographic hard polish—rather than raw array-processing efficiency.

## Performance Analysis

- **Generation 35 is still the clear quality-efficiency leader.** Its combined score of 0.75 is the best reported result, with a 4.71 s evaluation time. Generation 41 is closest in runtime at 4.58 s and quality at 0.74, but does not exceed it. The 0.01 score difference is small, yet it is meaningful because both report a rounded normalized minimum area of 0.02; the distinction likely comes from unrounded bottleneck quality and/or the combined runtime-quality calculation.

- **The leading performance band is concentrated in fixed-square local search.** Generation 35 scored 0.75, Generation 41 scored 0.74, Generation 36 scored 0.74 in 7.01 s, and Generation 32 scored 0.71 in 8.98 s. All use fixed square corners, interior-coordinate optimization, and exact or incident-based triangle evaluations. This is a much tighter and stronger group than triangle-hull, DE, or complex-repair variants.

- **The new triangular program is substantially dominated by the current best.** Generation 39’s 0.67 at 19.25 s is worse by 0.08 score and slower by roughly 14.5 s relative to Generation 35. Even though it passed all validity and determinism checks, its extensive 16-restart/560-sweep configuration did not generate a stronger minimum-area arrangement.

- **Runtime is useful only when paired with the right local-search structure.** Generation 41 demonstrates that a competitive fixed-square implementation can run in 4.58 s, near Generation 35’s 4.71 s, whereas Generation 39 spends 19.25 s for lower quality. The strongest observed correlation is not raw budget, but fixed-hull objective alignment, cached local exact evaluation, diversified coordinate restarts, and strict late bottleneck preservation.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Strengthen the hard-polish comparator with a richer, explicitly ordered lower-tail signature.** Keep exact minimum area as an inviolable first key, but rank tied candidates by a tuple such as `(sum of smallest 8, second smallest, sum of smallest 16, third smallest)` rather than only `(sum8, second)`. This is a low-risk extension of Generation 35’s successful strict lexicographic polish and can improve movement across equal-minimum plateaus without ever accepting a bottleneck reduction.

2. **Add a small set of geometry-aware normal proposals to the existing random/circular candidates during only the final third of search and polish.** For each movable point, identify its 2–3 smallest incident triangles, take the edge opposite that point, and append moves in both perpendicular directions to that edge at `step`, `0.45*step`, and perhaps `0.2*step`. Preserve the current 12 random exploratory moves and 48-direction polish as the main proposal pool; this supplements the proven simple coordinate search with targeted directions likely to enlarge active triangles without adopting the unsuccessful heavy coupled-repair machinery.

3. **Convert two or three of the ten restarts into incumbent-centered perturbation restarts.** After several independent lattice/staggered/interleaved runs, seed later restarts from the current global best and apply structured low-amplitude perturbations: alternating row shifts, small rotations about `(0.5, 0.5)`, anisotropic scaling around the center, or sparse perturbations to only 3–5 interior points. This retains deterministic diversification while allocating some budget to escaping and refining the strongest basin already found, rather than spending every restart rebuilding from broad grid-derived starts.

4. **Make proposal radius selection responsive to local lower-tail participation.** For each point, count appearances among the current smallest 8–16 triangles and, for highly active points, append precision radii such as `0.15*step` and `0.35*step`; retain the broader current radius distribution for inactive points. The existing global annealing schedule is effective, but active bottleneck points need finer late-scale moves while noncritical points remain useful for larger structural reshaping.

5. **Use a rare sequential two-point escape only after a measurable late-search stall, with full lexicographic acceptance.** If a restart’s exact minimum has not improved for around 40–60 late sweeps, choose two movable points occurring most often in the smallest 8 triangles, test a compact candidate pool for the first, and conditionally polish the second using the normal/random directions. Invoke this at most once or twice per restart and accept the resulting pair move only if the complete configuration improves the same hard lower-tail tuple; this gives a controlled escape mechanism while keeping the successful single-coordinate method as the dominant search behavior.
