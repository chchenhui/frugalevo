# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- **The current best’s fixed-square, bottleneck-first coordinate search remains the strongest confirmed formulation.** The current best (**Generation 2**, score **0.74**, normalized minimum area **0.02**, **4.02 s**) fixes all four unit-square corners, uses ten deterministic restarts from jittered 3×3 interior lattices, and optimizes only the nine interior points. This guarantees hull area one, so the search optimizes exactly the scored raw minimum area without normalization variation. Generation 19’s structured-restart portfolio also reached **0.74** at area **0.02**, but took **12.82 s**, reinforcing the formulation while showing the current best is substantially more efficient.

- **Strict max-min polishing after smoother exploration is consistently effective.** The current best uses a temperature-cooled soft bottleneck during its 520 broad-search sweeps, then switches to strict `np.min`-based directional polishing at four scales. This same bottleneck-first pattern appears in Generation 10 (**0.73**, **5.10 s**), Generation 15 (**0.72**, **4.97 s**), Generation 13 (**0.70**, **9.55 s**), and Generation 17 (**0.70**, **7.37 s**), all with normalized area **0.02**. Their consistent results support the earlier observation that soft scoring is useful as a search aid, but exact minimum-area acceptance is essential near convergence.

- **Fixed-corner square layouts outperform all reported fixed-triangle layouts.** Square methods in Generations 2, 10, 13, 14, 15, 17, 18, and 19 largely reached normalized area **0.02** and combined scores from **0.50–0.74**. By contrast, the triangle-hull searches—Generation 9 differential evolution (**0.29**, area **0.01**) and Generation 12 annealing (**0.34**, area **0.01**)—remained substantially weaker despite vectorized triple evaluation and targeted local moves.

- **Moderate deterministic restart counts with perturbed structured starts are productive.** The current best’s ten jittered-lattice restarts balance diversity and runtime effectively. Generation 10’s ten seeded jittered-lattice restarts nearly matched it at **0.73**, while Generation 19 expanded to 16 restarts and multiple grid families but only tied the score at much higher cost. This extends the prior result: restart diversity is valuable, but a larger portfolio did not improve the observed quality ceiling beyond the current best.

## Ineffective Approaches

- **Soft-min/annealing emphasis without sufficiently strong exact-objective refinement underperformed.** Generation 12’s critical-triangle-guided simulated annealing optimized a log-sum-exp soft minimum over 18 restarts but reached only **0.34** in **5.62 s**. Generation 14 combined true minimum area and soft-min annealing in a square layout but only scored **0.50** in **5.87 s**. These results support the earlier finding that optimizing a surrogate can improve several small areas while failing to improve the single worst triangle that controls evaluation.

- **More complex geometry-aware proposal mechanisms did not beat simpler coordinate proposals.** Generation 18 added tight-triangle normals, neighbor repulsion, coupled two-point repairs, and circular polishing, yet scored **0.67** in **9.10 s**. Generation 15’s targeted joint moves did better at **0.72** in **4.97 s**, but still did not exceed the current best’s simpler ten-restart coordinate-search schedule. The feedback indicates local-optimum limitations persisted despite these additional mechanisms.

- **Active-constraint gradient bias was particularly unsuccessful.** Generation 16 used soft-min optimization, gradient-guided proposals, annealed acceptance, tail polishing, incident-triangle updates, and eight restarts, but achieved normalized area **0.00**, combined score **0.01**, and required **6.54 s**. Although valid and robust, its additional local-search structure apparently converged to poor configurations rather than reliably resolving the true limiting triples.

- **Triangle-hull evolutionary search remained weak relative to square coordinate search.** Generation 9’s two-phase differential evolution plus multiscale stochastic polishing achieved **0.29** in **1.75 s**, and the prior triangle-based DE/annealing results similarly lagged the square cluster. The reduced runtime of Generation 9 is favorable, but its area **0.01** is only half the reported rounded area attained by the leading square-corner methods.

## Implementation Insights

- **The current best efficiently exploits move locality while retaining exact global scoring context.** For each moved point, `incident_areas` recomputes only triangles containing that point, rather than all 286 triples. It then combines these local values with unchanged cached areas to evaluate each candidate’s full area distribution. This gives the soft-bottleneck search an exact candidate comparison without full recomputation, and matches the successful locality pattern cited in Generations 10, 13, 15, 16, 17, and 19.

- **The current best’s proposal construction is deliberately safe and multiscale.** Every 13-candidate coordinate update includes the point’s current location as candidate zero, so soft-score selection cannot force a detrimental move. Early updates use 12 randomized directions and variable radii, with two occasional global replacements in the first third of sweeps; late search reduces the step size and temperature. The final pass uses 24 fixed circular directions at steps `0.008`, `0.004`, `0.002`, and `0.0008`. This combines escape behavior with exact local resolution more economically than the larger restart/sweep schedules of Generations 13 and 19.

- **Fixed corners simplify both feasibility and the objective.** The current best permanently retains `(0,0)`, `(1,0)`, `(1,1)`, and `(0,1)` and clips movable points to an interior range. As confirmed repeatedly in feedback for the square programs, this guarantees a convex hull of area exactly one, makes raw and normalized areas equivalent, and prevents search-time hull degeneration. The triangular programs must instead work under a less favorable fixed hull geometry and showed only **0.01** normalized minima.

- **The strongest implementations treat soft-min as a tie/shaping mechanism, not the final definition of success.** The current best’s stable soft bottleneck subtracts `temperature * log(sum(exp(...)))` from the candidate minimum. As temperature cools from roughly `0.010` to `0.00022`, it progressively emphasizes the actual bottleneck. Its final strict polishing then uses only candidate minimum area. Generation 10’s soft-min tie-breaking and Generation 15’s lower-tail tie-breaker follow the same principle and both scored above **0.70**.

## Performance Analysis

- **Generation 2 is still the best efficiency-quality point among the reported successful methods.** It achieved the top score **0.74** in **4.02 s**. Generation 19 matched **0.74** but took **12.82 s**, over three times longer. Generation 10 came close at **0.73** in **5.10 s**, and Generation 15 reached **0.72** in **4.97 s**. Thus, the current best’s specific combination of ten restarts, 520 sweeps, incident updates, and compact polishing produced the best observed result without the high cost of broader portfolios or more elaborate geometry handling.

- **A normalized minimum area of 0.02 defines the competitive square-method cluster, but score and runtime still differentiate implementations.** Generations 2 (**0.74**), 19 (**0.74**), 10 (**0.73**), 15 (**0.72**), 13 and 17 (**0.70**), and 18 (**0.67**) all report **0.02** after rounding. The combined score distinguishes their underlying precision and/or evaluation-efficiency effects. In particular, the current best is stronger than the slower coordinate approaches: Generation 13 at **9.55 s**, Generation 17 at **7.37 s**, and Generation 18 at **9.10 s**.

- **Search complexity and runtime show weak correlation with solution quality.** Generation 19’s 16 restarts, varied initialization families, and radial polishing did not exceed the current best’s score. Generation 18’s coupled repairs and geometry-aware moves were slower and scored lower. Conversely, Generation 9 was fast at **1.75 s** but weak at **0.29**. The actual evidence favors the particular fixed-square and exact-bottleneck structure over simply increasing search budget or proposal sophistication.

- **The performance gap between square and triangle formulations remains pronounced in the newly reported results.** The best square methods attained score **0.74** and normalized area **0.02**, while Generation 9’s triangle differential evolution reached **0.29** and Generation 12’s triangle annealing reached **0.34**, both at **0.01**. Generation 14 confirms that square containment alone is not sufficient—its annealed soft-min setup scored only **0.50**—but square corners combined with strict bottleneck-driven coordinate refinement form the consistently dominant pattern.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Add low-tail lexicographic selection to the final strict polish while retaining minimum area as the primary key.** Replace `np.argmax(candidate_min)` with comparison tuples such as `(minimum_area, mean_of_smallest_6, second_smallest_area)` computed from the candidate’s full area vector; accept only lexicographic improvements. This preserves the current best’s exact max-min objective while giving productive neutral moves a principled direction, consistent with the success of soft-tail shaping in Generations 2, 10, and 15.

2. **Use successive-halving restart allocation instead of giving all ten starts the full 520 sweeps.** For example, run 16–20 structured starts for 180–240 sweeps, retain the best 5–6 based on strict minimum area plus a lower-tail tie-break, then spend the remaining search budget on those survivors. This extends the proven deterministic-restart strategy and may explore more basins without exceeding the current best’s runtime, unlike Generation 19’s expensive full portfolio.

3. **Inject a small number of targeted pair moves only when coordinate search stalls.** Every 30–50 late sweeps with no strict-minimum improvement, identify vertices shared by the bottom 3–6 triangles and jointly perturb a pair of movable vertices using a small Cartesian product of displacement directions; accept only if the exact global minimum improves. This is a controlled version of Generation 15’s useful joint-move idea, designed to escape one-coordinate local optima without paying the constant overhead of Generation 18’s broad geometry machinery.

4. **Augment random proposal directions with determinant-improving directions from active triangles.** For each updated point, find its few smallest incident triangles and add candidate displacements along the normals to the opposite edges, in both signs, alongside the existing random directions and current-location safeguard. Such moves directly alter the altitude term controlling triangle area, but should remain a minority of proposals because the current random multiscale coordinate search is already the strongest baseline.

5. **Seed several restarts from symmetry-broken near-solutions rather than only jittered 3×3 lattices.** Keep most of the ten lattice-jitter starts, but replace two or three with staggered-row, hexagonal-like, and rotated/interleaved grid seeds, each lightly perturbed and clipped to the same interior range. This keeps the fixed-square hull and efficient local scoring intact while testing different high-quality geometric basins; it is a lower-cost refinement of Generation 19’s initialization diversity rather than its much slower full search portfolio.
