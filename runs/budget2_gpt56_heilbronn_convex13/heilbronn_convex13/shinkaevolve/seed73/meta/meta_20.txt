# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- **The current best, Simulated Annealing Heilbronn 13-Point Search (Generation 16), establishes the strongest observed combination of structured initialization, hull-adaptive scoring, and long annealing.** It reached `min_area_normalized = 0.03` and combined score **0.84**, exceeding the prior top tier reported around **0.74–0.76** and the next strongest newly listed result, Deterministic Hybrid Heilbronn Search’s **0.75**. Its nine approximately circular boundary points plus four interior points avoid the many nearly collinear triples that arise from a uniformly random initial cloud.

- **Optimizing against the actual normalized evaluation geometry appears especially effective.** Generation 16 computes every triangle determinant and divides by the candidate’s convex-hull doubled area on every proposal. This differs from the fixed-reference-triangle programs, which generally reached only normalized areas of **0.01–0.02** and combined scores from **0.44 to 0.64**. Allowing the hull to adapt appears materially better than forcing three points to fixed simplex corners.

- **A mixed maximin/lower-tail annealing objective is consistently stronger than strict bottleneck-only local movement.** Generation 16 accepts moves using `min_area + 0.16 * mean(12 smallest areas)` while selecting its final answer strictly by minimum normalized area. This gives it a smoother search signal when many nearly active constraints compete. Related lower-tail-guided approaches were also comparatively strong: Generation 19 scored **0.64**, Generation 20 scored **0.64**, and the previous tail-annealed square search reached **0.66**.

- **Persistent exploration and occasional global mutations help the active bottleneck change.** The best program retains a nonzero final step (`0.0018`) and temperature (`0.000015`), and every 701 iterations perturbs all 13 points rather than one coordinate pair. This matches earlier evidence that nonzero late-stage mobility improves bottleneck exchange rather than freezing a configuration around one currently smallest triangle.

## Ineffective Approaches

- **Orientation-sign-locked alternating LP optimization was fast but produced the weakest valid result.** Alternating LP Triangle Point Optimizer (Generation 10) completed in only **0.23 s**, but had normalized minimum area reported as **0.00** and combined score **0.08**. Preserving all 286 triple orientation signs during coordinate-block LP updates constrained the search to its initial combinatorial arrangement and, according to the feedback, trapped it in weak local layouts.

- **Fixed-simplex annealing with bottleneck guidance did not by itself yield high-quality packings.** Generations 13 and 15 both produced `min_area_normalized = 0.01`, with scores **0.45** and **0.44**, despite soft-min/lower-tail objectives and active-triangle polishing. Generations 9, 14, 18, 19, and 20 improved this to approximately **0.02**, but still remained below Generation 16’s **0.03** result and **0.84** score.

- **More iterations without a stronger geometric prior did not guarantee better outcomes.** Generation 12 used six seeded restarts, **84,000** stochastic iterations, and final pattern search, yet scored **0.75** in **19.86 s** with rounded normalized area **0.02**. Generation 16’s circular-hull initialization and normalized hull-aware objective achieved a higher **0.84** score, albeit with a longer **27.23 s** runtime.

- **Soft-min-driven simplex search can optimize the wrong local tradeoff for final strict maximin selection.** Generation 13 used multiscale annealing and soft-min continuation but reached only **0.45**. The current best uses its lower-tail value only as an acceptance surrogate while explicitly retaining the best candidate according to the strict minimum normalized area, avoiding final selection by the surrogate.

## Implementation Insights

- **Generation 16’s geometry evaluation is directly aligned with the evaluator.** It precomputes the 286 index triples once, vectorizes determinant calculation with NumPy gathers, computes convex-hull area using Andrew monotone chain, and evaluates `double_areas / hull_double_area`. This directly targets scale-invariant minimum triangle area rather than raw area in an arbitrary fixed domain.

- **The best program separates search guidance from final ranking correctly.** `quality()` returns both the strict primary value and a lower-tail augmented score. Acceptance uses the augmented score to distinguish candidates with the same or similar worst triangle, but `best_primary` is updated only from the strict minimum. This is a concrete implementation advantage over methods whose optimization surrogate can dominate their reported objective.

- **Its initialization encodes a plausible high-quality hull before stochastic refinement begins.** Nine noisy angular positions with radii near `0.455` produce an approximately circular boundary, while four independently sampled interior polar points populate the center. The feedback specifically credits this structured circular initialization with reliable search quality; it is substantially more geometry-aware than random barycentric or lattice/random seed clouds.

- **The runtime cost is identifiable and corresponds to the quality gain.** Generation 16 performs **7 × 26,000 = 182,000** annealing proposals. Each proposal copies the point array, recomputes all triangle areas, and recomputes the monotone-chain hull. This explains its **27.23 s** evaluation time, but also accompanies the only reported **0.03** normalized minimum area and the highest combined score.

## Performance Analysis

- **Generation 16 is the clear new leader and supersedes the previously reported best score near 0.76.** Its combined score of **0.84** is higher than Deterministic Hybrid Heilbronn Search’s **0.75**, the previously cited batched-simplex result at **0.74**, and the current set’s fixed-domain annealers, which mostly lie between **0.44 and 0.64**. Its reported minimum normalized area of **0.03** is also visibly above the common rounded **0.02** plateau.

- **The performance gap supports hull-adaptive, circularly seeded search over fixed reference-triangle formulations.** Generation 16 reaches **0.84**; Generation 12’s unconstrained unit-square, hull-normalized method reaches **0.75**; while reference-triangle searches such as Generations 9, 14, 18, 19, and 20 range from **0.54 to 0.64** despite using targeted moves, restarts, and active-set polishing.

- **Score and runtime show a quality/cost tradeoff rather than a simple “faster is better” relation.** The LP approach is extremely fast at **0.23 s** but scores only **0.08**. Moderate annealers in the **5.65–10.48 s** range score roughly **0.44–0.64**. The strongest methods consume substantially more time: Generation 12 takes **19.86 s** for **0.75**, and Generation 16 takes **27.23 s** for **0.84**.

- **The rounded normalized-area metric still masks meaningful differences among the 0.02 group, but Generation 16 breaks out even at displayed precision.** Programs with reported `0.02` span combined scores from **0.54** (Generations 14 and 18) through **0.64** (Generations 19 and 20) to **0.75** (Generation 12). Generation 16 not only improves the combined score but also raises the displayed normalized minimum area to **0.03**, providing stronger evidence of a genuinely better geometric configuration rather than only a runtime-related score advantage.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Replace single-worst-triangle targeting with an adaptive active-set proposal distribution.** Modify `quality()` to return the sorted low-area triangle indices, then on targeted iterations choose a triangle from the lowest `k` normalized areas rather than implicitly selecting a uniformly random point; use `k≈3–5` early and increase toward `12–20` late. Select one of that triangle’s vertices, or weight active triangles inversely by area, so the search improves the whole near-bottleneck set instead of repeatedly exchanging only the current minimum constraint.

2. **Add coordinated two-point and geometry-aware displacement proposals.** On roughly 10–20% of non-global iterations, choose two distinct vertices appearing in the lowest-area triangles and perturb both with correlated small moves; for example, move a vertex away from the opposite edge’s line normal while slightly moving another active vertex tangentially. Keep ordinary one-point Gaussian proposals as the majority, but these coupled moves can cross local barriers where moving one bottleneck vertex merely makes a neighboring triangle become minimal.

3. **Use staged adaptive restarts rather than seven equal-length independent anneals.** Run a larger first stage of short circular-hull anneals, rank endpoints by a lexicographic criterion such as `(strict minimum, mean of lowest 12 areas)`, and spend the remaining budget reheating only the best two or three configurations. This retains the successful structured ring-plus-interior initialization while concentrating the expensive hull-normalized evaluation budget on promising combinatorial layouts instead of weak restarts.

4. **Add a strict-improvement active-vertex local polish after each annealing run.** Starting from each restart’s best strict-primary configuration, identify vertices occurring in the lowest 12–20 triangles and test a deterministic stencil of small axis, diagonal, radial-from-centroid, and tangential-to-hull perturbations. Accept only candidates that improve the strict normalized minimum area, shrink the probe radius over several rounds, and retain the current lower-tail score only for ordering equal-primary candidates; this complements annealing’s exploration with reliable late maximin refinement.

5. **Preserve the circular-hull prior but explicitly mutate hull structure and interior roles.** Introduce occasional proposal types that rotate or radially adjust one boundary candidate relative to the hull centroid, swap a near-hull interior point with a boundary-role point, or jointly perturb neighboring hull points while preserving broad angular spacing. The best result strongly supports a roughly circular nine-point hull with four interior points, but allowing controlled changes in which points occupy those roles may discover better hull/interior arrangements without reverting to weak unstructured random clouds.
