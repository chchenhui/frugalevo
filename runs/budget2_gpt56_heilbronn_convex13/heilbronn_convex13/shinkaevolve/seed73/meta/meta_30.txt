# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- **The current best, Generation 26’s _Deterministic Annealed Heilbronn Configuration Search_, confirms that hull-adaptive annealing from a structured rotational prior is the strongest observed recipe.** It achieved the top combined score, **0.86**, with `min_area_normalized = 0.03` in **17.83 s**. Its initialization uses a center plus four perturbed three-point rings at increasing radii, which gives a non-collinear, approximately threefold-symmetric configuration before optimization. This preserves the geometric-prior advantage previously seen in Generation 16’s circular-hull initialization, while improving the observed score/time balance.

- **Survivor selection followed by long reheated refinement is particularly effective.** Generation 26 runs ten short 3,000-step searches, retains only the top four, and gives those four 21,000-step refinements. This concentrates 84,000 of its 114,000 total proposals on promising determinant-orientation and hull structures. The same staged pattern was effective in Generation 21 (`0.86`, 15.00 s), whose feedback likewise credits “staged survivor refinement,” and it outperformed broad fixed-budget local approaches such as Generation 27 (`0.61`, `0.02`, 15.85 s).

- **Moving vertices from the currently worst triangle remains a consistently successful active-constraint heuristic when paired with Metropolis acceptance.** Generation 26 chooses a worst-triangle vertex 82% of the time, with random point moves retained for exploration. Generation 21 and Generation 22 used the same bottleneck-focused principle and reached `0.03`, with scores **0.86** and **0.85**, respectively. This is substantially stronger than fixed-simplex bottleneck variants, which generally plateaued near `0.02`.

- **Free hull geometry is materially better than constraining all points to a fixed reference triangle or fixed polygon hull.** Generations 21, 22, 23, 24, and 26 all use hull-normalized scoring and reached `0.03`, with scores from **0.81 to 0.86**. In contrast, fixed-hull Generation 25 reached only `0.02` and **0.65**, and fixed-reference-triangle Generations 27 and 29 reached `0.02` with **0.61** and **0.65**. The result strengthens the earlier conclusion that allowing hull vertices and hull area to co-evolve is important for this normalized objective.

## Ineffective Approaches

- **The coordinate-feasibility/half-plane method was geometrically valid but competitively ineffective.** Generation 17’s coordinate feasibility pump passed validation but produced an effectively `0.00` normalized minimum area and a **0.13** score despite taking **118.82 s**, by far the largest runtime in this set. Its coordinate-wise clipping and binary-search feasibility updates appear to preserve local feasibility without discovering a globally strong, nondegenerate arrangement.

- **Fixing the containing hull sacrifices too much useful geometry.** Generation 25 fixes a 7–8 vertex regular-polygon hull and restricts interior points to its incircle; it avoids hull recomputation and finishes in **9.66 s**, but only reaches `0.02` and **0.65**. The speed improvement does not compensate for the quality loss relative to Generation 26’s `0.03` / **0.86** adaptive-hull result.

- **Reference-simplex parameterizations remain weaker even with sophisticated local moves.** Generation 27 adds lower-tail annealing, active-constraint gradient moves, coordinated perturbations, and barycentric projection, but scores only **0.61**. Generation 29 similarly adds guided moves and greedy bottleneck polish but ends at **0.65**. The fixed hull corners constrain the search to a less favorable family of normalized configurations than the ring-based, unconstrained searches.

- **More local-search machinery is not automatically productive.** Generation 28 combines critical-triangle moves, coupled area-increasing steps, reheating, and coordinate pattern search, yet reaches `0.02` and **0.81** in **20.35 s**. It is solid, but below Generation 26 despite taking longer. This supports the evidence that strong initialization and survivor allocation can matter more than adding a larger final local-search stack.

## Implementation Insights

- **Generation 26 directly matches the scoring geometry with vectorized triple determinants and per-candidate monotone-chain hull area.** It precomputes all **286** triples, computes all determinant magnitudes with NumPy array gathers, identifies the active minimum triangle, and evaluates `0.5 * min_double_area / hull_area`. This makes every proposal consistent with the evaluator’s scale-invariant normalized objective, rather than optimizing raw areas in an arbitrary enclosing domain.

- **Its acceptance and reporting objective are aligned more tightly than lower-tail-surrogate variants.** The current best accepts proposals based directly on the strict normalized minimum-area score. This avoids optimizing a tail aggregate that can trade away the true bottleneck. Generation 23’s lower-tail auxiliary objective remained competitive (`0.03`, **0.82**) but did not exceed Generation 26; Generation 26 demonstrates that active-triangle targeting plus stochastic acceptance can navigate the nonsmooth objective without requiring a separate tail term.

- **The annealer preserves late exploration deliberately.** Its step schedule bottoms out at `0.0020`, and its temperature bottoms out at `0.000012`, rather than falling to zero. Combined with the 18% chance of moving an arbitrary point rather than an active-triangle vertex, this retains a mechanism for changing the active bottleneck set late in search. This is consistent with the earlier Generation 16 insight that persistent nonzero mobility helps avoid premature freezing.

- **The current best’s runtime is controlled through search-budget allocation rather than cheaper geometry.** It performs 10 short discovery runs plus only 4 extended refinements, rather than fully refining every restart. Its **17.83 s** runtime is lower than Generation 22’s **24.69 s**, Generation 23’s **19.41 s**, Generation 28’s **20.35 s**, and the previously reported Generation 16’s **27.23 s**, while matching or exceeding their quality. The key efficiency mechanism is refining good survivors, not approximating or omitting hull/triple calculations.

## Performance Analysis

Generation 26 nevertheless provides strong corroboration that perturbed threefold ring initialization, worst-triangle targeting, and survivor reheating are a repeatably high-performing design rather than a one-off seed result.

- **The leading adaptive-hull group is tightly clustered at the top.** Generation 21 scores **0.86** in 15.00 s; Generation 26 scores **0.86** in 17.83 s; Generation 22 scores **0.85** in 24.69 s; Generation 23 scores **0.82** in 19.41 s; and Generation 24 scores **0.81** in 18.40 s. All report `0.03`, indicating that the displayed normalized-area precision compresses meaningful quality differences; combined score and runtime expose distinctions among configurations in the same rounded area band.

- **Fixed-domain variants form a clearly weaker `0.02` tier.** Generation 25’s fixed hull reaches **0.65** in 9.66 s; Generation 27’s fixed simplex reaches **0.61** in 15.85 s; and Generation 29 reaches **0.65** in 15.80 s. Even Generation 28’s unconstrained hybrid search remains at `0.02`, though its **0.81** combined score indicates it may be close numerically. The decisive visible jump remains reaching `0.03`, which is associated with ring/circular priors and adaptive hull normalization.

- **Runtime alone does not predict quality.** Generation 17 is the strongest negative example: **118.82 s** for only **0.13** score. Conversely, Generation 21 reaches the best observed score in **15.00 s**. Among competitive annealers, the distinction is not simply proposal count: Geometry-aware initialization, direct normalized scoring, active-triangle mutations, and selective survivor refinement correlate more strongly with the top results than exhaustive coordinate feasibility or elaborate fixed-hull local polishing.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Replace single-worst-triangle selection with a sampled low-area active set.** Extend `evaluate()` to return the sorted indices of, for example, the lowest 8–16 triangle double-areas; on the 82% targeted moves, sample among the lowest `k` with probability proportional to `1 / (area + eps)` rather than always using `worst`. Start with `k≈4–6` during the 3,000-step discovery runs and broaden to `k≈12–20` during refinement, reducing repeated oscillation between a small number of exchanged bottleneck triangles while retaining the successful active-constraint focus.

2. **Add a modest mixture of two-vertex active-set moves during survivor refinement.** On roughly 10–15% of targeted proposals in the 21,000-step phase, choose two distinct vertices from low-area triangles and perturb both with correlated displacements, while retaining ordinary one-point Gaussian moves as the dominant proposal. Favor a displacement that moves one selected point away from the opposite edge normal and gives the other a smaller tangential perturbation; this directly addresses local barriers where improving one minimum triangle simply causes an adjacent low-area triangle to become active.

3. **Use reheating cycles inside each long survivor refinement instead of one monotone cooling pass.** Split the 21,000 steps into three 7,000-step epochs, retaining the best configuration seen so far but restarting each epoch from the current state with a partially restored step size and temperature, such as `step=0.05–0.06` and `temperature=0.0008–0.0011`. This preserves Generation 26’s successful survivor allocation while making it easier to change determinant-orientation patterns late, consistent with the observed benefit of persistent nonzero mobility and reheated refinement.

4. **Add a deterministic strict-score polishing pass to each refined survivor.** After annealing, identify vertices appearing in the lowest 12–20 triangles and test a small stencil of candidate moves: axis/diagonal offsets, radial directions from the point centroid, tangential directions around the hull centroid, and normals to opposite edges of active triangles. Accept only strict improvements in the existing normalized minimum-area objective, halve the stencil radius over several rounds, and run this only on the top survivors so the added evaluation cost is concentrated where Generation 26 already demonstrates refinement is valuable.

5. **Diversify the structured ring prior while preserving its successful threefold geometry.** Keep the center-plus-four-rings construction, but make a subset of discovery restarts use controlled alternative role layouts: e.g., one slightly elliptical ring, independently jittered angles rather than only ring phases, or occasional radial swaps between adjacent rings before annealing. The evidence strongly favors structured rotational initialization and free hull evolution over random clouds or fixed hulls, so this should broaden the reachable hull/interior combinatorics without abandoning the proven geometric prior.
