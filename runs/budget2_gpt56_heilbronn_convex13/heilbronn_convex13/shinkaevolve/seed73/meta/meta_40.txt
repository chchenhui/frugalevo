# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- **The current best, Generation 38’s _Deterministic Annealed Heilbronn Configuration Search_, improves the previously leading adaptive-hull recipe through structured restart diversity and selective long refinement.** It achieved the highest observed combined score, **0.90**, with `min_area_normalized = 0.03` in **17.98 s**. Like the earlier best adaptive-hull methods, it uses threefold ring structure, normalized hull-area scoring, seeded annealing, and worst-triangle-biased moves. Its distinguishing change is cycling among structured initialization families—including anisotropically scaled starts and independently perturbed ring variants—rather than repeatedly sampling nearly identical ring configurations.

- **Survivor refinement remains a high-value search-budget allocation strategy.** Generation 38 runs ten short **3,000-iteration** discovery anneals, then refines four selected survivors for **21,000 iterations** each. This preserves one strong trajectory from each of its three initialization families before using the fourth slot on the best remaining trial. This family-preserving selection is a concrete improvement over merely taking the top candidates from similar starts: Generation 34 and Generation 37 used related ring-based survivor annealing and scored **0.86**, while Generation 38’s broader basin coverage reached **0.90**.

- **Free, hull-adaptive geometry consistently outperforms fixed-hull or fixed-domain formulations.** Generations 34, 37, and 38 all reached `0.03` normalized minimum area with scores **0.86**, **0.86**, and **0.90**, respectively. By contrast, the fixed nine-hull/four-interior-point variants—Generations 30, 31, 33, and 35—remained at `0.02` and scored only **0.63–0.67**. The adaptive convex hull permits the optimization to jointly choose hull structure and interior spacing rather than accepting a restricted regular-polygon geometry.

- **Worst-triangle targeting plus nonzero random exploration is repeatedly effective for the nonsmooth maximin objective.** In the current best program, **82%** of proposals move a vertex of the presently minimum-area triangle, while **18%** move an arbitrary point. The same active-constraint principle is credited in Generations 34 and 37, both at **0.86**, and is materially stronger than relying on generic local coordinate moves alone. Retaining random moves and nonzero late-stage step/temperature floors helps change bottleneck-triangle identities rather than freezing around one local active set.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Replace single-worst targeting with low-tail active-set sampling.** Have `evaluate()` additionally expose the indices of the lowest roughly 8–16 triangle areas, then on targeted proposals sample one of those triangles with inverse-area or rank-biased probability instead of always using `worst`. Keep the current 82% targeted / 18% random mixture, but use a narrow active set during 3,000-step discovery and a broader one during 21,000-step refinement to reduce cycling among just two exchanged bottleneck triangles.

2. **Introduce occasional geometry-aware two-point proposals in the long refinement stage.** For about 10–15% of targeted moves, select two distinct vertices appearing in low-area triangles and move both: push one away from the opposite-edge normal of its selected triangle, while giving the second a smaller correlated tangential or normal displacement. This extends the successful active-constraint principle beyond one-coordinate repair and can cross barriers where moving one bottleneck vertex merely activates a neighboring small triangle.

3. **Turn each 21,000-step survivor polish into several reheated refinement epochs.** Split the current long anneal into three approximately 7,000-step phases, carrying forward the best/current state but restoring a moderate step and temperature at the beginning of phases two and three, such as `step≈0.050–0.060` and `temperature≈0.0008–0.0011`. The existing positive cooling floors already show that late mobility is useful; controlled reheating should better change active-triangle and hull-membership patterns without abandoning the strong survivor-refinement allocation.

4. **Expand the structured restart portfolio with role-level threefold distortions rather than generic random clouds.** Retain center plus four three-point layers, but add a fourth family that perturbs each ring by a low-amplitude three-harmonic deformation—e.g. `radius_j = r * (1 + a*cos(3*angle_j + phi))`—and another that deliberately makes one ring mildly elliptical before annealing. Generation 38’s advantage came from structured family diversity, so these variants are likely to expose new hull/interior combinatorics while preserving the productive rotational density prior.

5. **Apply a strict deterministic local stencil polish only to the best one or two refined survivors.** After annealing, collect vertices occurring in the bottom 12–20 triangle constraints and test a compact set of strict-improvement moves: opposite-edge normals, centroid-radial directions, tangential directions, and small axis/diagonal offsets at several successively halved radii. Accept only improvements in the same normalized minimum-area objective and repeat until a full pass fails, concentrating the extra exact evaluations on configurations already shown by survivor selection to be near high-quality basins.
