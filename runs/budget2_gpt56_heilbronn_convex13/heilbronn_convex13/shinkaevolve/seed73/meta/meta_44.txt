# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- **Generation 38, Deterministic Annealed Heilbronn Configuration Search, remains the strongest evaluated design** at `min_area_normalized = 0.03`, combined score **0.90**, and **17.98 s**. Its decisive pattern is a structured but diverse global-search pipeline: ten 3,000-step discovery anneals from center-plus-four-threefold-ring seeds, followed by 21,000-step refinement of four survivors. Unlike earlier ring methods, it deliberately preserves the best survivor from each of three initialization families—base, anisotropically scaled, and independently perturbed rings—before filling the final refinement slot with the strongest remaining candidate.

- **Active bottleneck-triangle targeting is consistently productive when paired with global mobility.** Generation 38 moves a vertex from the currently worst triangle on **82%** of proposals and selects a random point on the other 18%. This matches the favorable feedback for Generation 39’s active-set sampling and correlated moves (**0.77**) and Generation 43’s active-constraint targeting (**0.82**). The random component remains important because the identity of the minimum-area triangle changes during search; purely local active-set updates can otherwise remain trapped in one determinant pattern.

- **Adaptive convex-hull optimization is the high-quality regime.** Generation 38’s unconstrained coordinates and per-proposal convex-hull normalization outperform the fixed-simplex Generation 41 (**0.74**, normalized area `0.02`) and fixed-square Generation 39 (**0.77**, normalized area `0.02`) formulations. Generation 43 also permits a flexible hull and reaches `0.03`, confirming that hull adaptation is associated with the stronger normalized-area tier, even though its score of **0.82** does not match Generation 38.

- **Structured restart diversity is more effective than simply increasing annealing effort.** Generation 42 uses structured concentric three-point rings and survivor continuations but scores only **0.65** despite **22.48 s** and 114,000 total iterations. Generation 38 obtains a substantially higher **0.90** in less time by combining ring structure with controlled family variation and family-aware survivor selection, rather than relying primarily on a larger raw iteration budget.

## Ineffective Approaches

- **Fixed-domain parameterizations continue to limit final packing quality.** Generation 41 fixes three hull vertices in a simplex and optimizes ten remaining points, reaching only **0.74** and `0.02` normalized area in **9.53 s**. Generation 39 uses 13 points in a unit square and similarly remains at `0.02` with score **0.77**. These methods are valid and relatively targeted, but their fixed external geometry restricts the hull shapes available to the optimizer.

- **Broad local-polishing machinery did not exceed the focused survivor-refinement strategy.** Generation 43 combines exploratory restarts, reheating, lower-tail energy, coordinate pattern search, and active-set directional polling, yet scores **0.82** in **22.23 s**. It achieves the same rounded `0.03` normalized area as Generation 38 but is slower and lower-scoring, indicating that substantial late-stage refinement alone does not compensate for weaker basin discovery or restart selection.

- **More elaborate active-set surrogates have not surpassed direct maximin acceptance.** Generation 39 rewards the 16 smallest triangle areas in addition to the minimum and scores **0.77**; Generation 41 uses soft-min/tail scoring and scores **0.74**. These surrogate objectives can stabilize movement across multiple near-active constraints, but Generation 38’s direct normalized-minimum-area Metropolis score is better aligned with the evaluator and achieves **0.90**.

- **Long multi-restart annealing without sufficiently diverse trajectories can be inefficient.** Generation 42’s extensive ring-based annealing takes **22.48 s** for **0.65**, below even the faster fixed-simplex Generation 41. Its structured initialization avoids generic random basins, but the reported result suggests that its restart/refinement organization did not provide the same effective oriented-matroid diversity as Generation 38’s three-family design.

## Implementation Insights

- **Generation 38 evaluates the exact evaluator-aligned objective at every proposal.** It precomputes all \(\binom{13}{3}=286\) triangle index triples, vectorizes doubled-area determinant computation with NumPy gathers, chooses the minimum-area triangle, and divides its area by a monotone-chain convex-hull area. This exact adaptive normalization costs time relative to fixed-domain methods, but it directly supports the best observed score.

- **The current best uses a simple, efficient state transition per annealing step.** Each proposal copies the \(13 \times 2\) point array, perturbs only one point with isotropic Gaussian noise, recomputes score and worst triangle, and applies standard Metropolis acceptance. Despite the objective’s nonsmooth active-constraint changes, maintaining the returned `worst` triangle as proposal-targeting state avoids gradient complexity while preserving directed search pressure.

- **Its annealing schedules preserve late-stage exploration instead of decaying to zero.** Discovery uses initial step `0.115` and temperature `0.0022` for 3,000 iterations; survivor refinement uses `0.062` and `0.0011` for 21,000 iterations. Both retain floors of step `0.0020` and temperature `0.000012`. This is consistent with the need to change active bottleneck triangles late in the search and contrasts with strict directional polishing in Generation 41, which was effective but remained at **0.74**.

- **Family-aware survivor selection is a concrete implementation advantage.** Generation 38 stores `(score, points, worst, family)` after every discovery run, sorts candidates by score, then keeps the strongest candidate from every initialization family before selecting the best unused fourth candidate. This is more deliberate than selecting only the top global trials and helps explain why it improves over related survivor-ring methods from Generations 34 and 37, both **0.86**, to **0.90**.

## Performance Analysis

- **Generation 38 is the clear leader in the observed quality/runtime tradeoff:** score **0.90** in **17.98 s**. It exceeds the newly evaluated flexible-hull hybrid Generation 43 by **0.08** combined-score points while also running about **4.25 s faster**. Both display `0.03` normalized area after rounding, so the combined score captures meaningful sub-rounded objective improvement.

- **The new programs reinforce that `0.03` is necessary but not sufficient for top performance.** Generation 43 reaches the same displayed normalized value as Generation 38 but scores **0.82**, whereas Generation 38 scores **0.90**. By comparison, Generations 39, 41, and 42 remain at `0.02` and score **0.77**, **0.74**, and **0.65**, respectively. The higher-scoring results therefore correlate with both flexible hull geometry and stronger optimization of the precise unrounded minimum-area ratio.

- **Runtime alone is not predictive of quality.** Generation 41 is the fastest new active-set method at **9.53 s** but reaches only **0.74**. Generation 42 is the slowest at **22.48 s** yet performs worst at **0.65**. Generation 43 also spends over **22 s** for **0.82**, while Generation 38 remains faster than both and better than all evaluated alternatives, demonstrating that restart organization and objective alignment dominate raw search budget.

- **Active-set logic improves over unguided search, but its surrounding geometry and search allocation determine the ceiling.** Generation 39’s active low-area triangles, correlated proposals, and polishing reach **0.77**; Generation 41’s determinant-gradient moves reach **0.74**; and Generation 43’s directed normal/radial/tangential polls reach **0.82**. Generation 38’s less elaborate single-point active-triangle perturbations nevertheless reach **0.90**, because they are embedded in a high-value combination of adaptive hulls, structured restart diversity, direct objective acceptance, and selective long survivor refinement.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Replace single-worst-triangle selection with rank-biased low-tail targeting while retaining the 82%/18% targeted-random split.** Modify `evaluate()` or an auxiliary routine to identify the lowest 8–16 triangle areas, and on targeted moves sample a triangle with probability biased toward smaller areas rather than always using `worst`. This directly extends Generation 38’s productive bottleneck targeting while addressing active-triangle cycling observed in more local methods; use a smaller tail, such as 6–8, in discovery and 12–16 during long refinement.

2. **Add a small mixture of geometry-aware directional proposals to the existing Gaussian proposal kernel.** On perhaps 10–20% of targeted proposals, choose one vertex of a low-area triangle and propose a displacement partly along the normal to its opposite edge, with a randomized sign and Gaussian tangential component; retain ordinary isotropic moves for all other proposals. Generation 43 indicates that normal/radial/tangential active-constraint moves are useful, but they should be subordinate to Generation 38’s globally mobile Metropolis search rather than replacing it.

3. **Split each 21,000-step survivor refinement into staged annealing epochs with controlled reheating.** For example, run three 7,000-step epochs, preserving the best state between epochs but resetting the current step/temperature near `0.050–0.062` and `0.0008–0.0011` at each new epoch, while keeping the same positive floors. The current best already benefits from nonzero late-stage mobility, and reheating can help alter the active determinant pattern or convex-hull membership after a survivor settles into a strong but narrow basin.

4. **Expand the initialization portfolio with additional structured threefold-deformation families, then preserve one survivor per family.** Keep the base, affine-scaled, and independently perturbed ring families, but add variants such as per-ring harmonic distortions `r_j = r * (1 + a*cos(3*theta_j + phi))`, one deliberately elliptical inner or outer layer, and a mild angular shear between successive rings. Generation 38’s family-aware selection is a principal advantage over raw-restart approaches, so increasing structured oriented-matroid diversity is more promising than merely adding generic random starts.

5. **Apply an exact strict-improvement stencil polish only to the best one or two fully annealed survivors.** Build the active vertex set from the bottom roughly 12–20 triangle constraints, then repeatedly test small normal-to-edge, centroid-radial, tangential, axis, and diagonal displacements at decreasing radii, accepting only moves that improve the direct normalized minimum-area objective. This borrows the strongest part of Generation 43’s targeted local polling without spending its broad polishing budget on weak basins, preserving Generation 38’s efficient survivor-refinement strategy.
