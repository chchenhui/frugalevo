# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- **Generation 20 “Exact-Cost Beam Search with Local Repair” is the new strongest validated design.** It reached **240 makespan** and **4149.38 combined score**, with perfect schedule score (**3.00**) and validity (**1.00**). This improves materially on the previous best reported in the global insights, Generation 17, which had **253 makespan / 3937.01 score**: a **13-point makespan reduction** and **212.37 score increase**.

- **Exact simulator scoring at every decision level is strongly supported by the top results.** Generation 20 evaluates beam prefixes, local-search candidates, block moves, and destroy-and-repair insertions with `workload.get_opt_seq_cost`, using cached results. This outperformed Generation 21’s similarly exact-cost beam/local-search approach (**268 / 3717.47**) and Generation 22’s hybrid beam/GRASP approach (**256 / 3891.05**), indicating that accurate evaluation alone is not enough, but it is a common foundation of every high-quality valid scheduler.

- **Preserving diverse beam states appears more effective than simply expanding a broad greedy or randomized construction pool.** Generation 20 explicitly keeps beam prefixes with distinct *final transactions* before filling remaining beam slots by cost. Its **240** makespan is better than Generation 23’s conflict-aware multi-start greedy and pairwise-precedence seeding (**251 / 3968.25**) and Generation 21’s diversity-preserving beam variant (**268 / 3717.47**). The result reinforces the prior insight that retaining competing partial precedence choices reduces commitment to a poor early ordering.

- **Combining local descent with larger coordinated moves remains the dominant successful pattern.** Generation 20 uses relocation, arbitrary swaps, reversals, and short block moves, then applies randomized contiguous or dispersed destroy-and-repair. This extends the previously successful Generation 17/14/16 pattern of VND plus ruin-and-recreate, but now produces a substantial improvement over Generation 14’s **254** and Generation 17’s **253** makespans.

## Ineffective Approaches

- **A beam-plus-local-search design can still be substantially weaker when its construction and repair behavior are less effective.** Generation 21 “Beam-Guided Local Search Scheduler” used exact-cost greedy prefix expansion, a diverse beam, caching, swaps, insertion moves, long-range exchanges, and destroy-and-repair, but reached only **268 makespan / 3717.47**. Relative to Generation 20, that is **28 makespan points worse** and **431.91 score lower**, showing that merely including these components does not reproduce the benefit of Generation 20’s particular beam diversity rule and block-move neighborhood.

- **Hybridization through additional seed mechanisms did not automatically improve quality.** Generation 22 added exhaustive small-instance search, beam search, diversified greedy/GRASP construction, VND, and repair, yet scored **3891.05** with **256 makespan**. That is valid and strong, but still **16 makespan points behind** Generation 20. The evaluated outcome suggests that the current best’s more focused exact-cost beam-to-elite-refinement pipeline was more effective than a broader mix of construction strategies.

- **Pairwise precedence probes and ranking-based seeds were helpful but did not beat direct beam preservation.** Generation 23’s pairwise conflict signals and several seed schedules yielded **251 makespan / 3968.25**, outperforming several earlier methods but trailing Generation 20 by **11 makespan points** and **181.13 score points**. Pairwise information can diversify starts, but it is less directly aligned with full-prefix simulator cost than Generation 20’s repeated exact prefix evaluation.

- **Correctness failures remain categorically ineffective regardless of heuristic sophistication.** As noted in the previous insights, Generations 13 and 18 received **0.0** despite using randomized greedy construction, local moves, and destroy/repair methods. In contrast, Generations 20–23 all have schedule metric **3.00** and validity **1.00**. The evaluation history continues to show that optimization complexity is only valuable when output behavior remains fully validation-safe.

## Implementation Insights

- **Generation 20’s single shared memoization cache is central to making exhaustive neighborhoods practical.** `cost_cache` is keyed by `tuple(sequence)` and is reused for singleton starts, every beam prefix, relocations, swaps, reversals, block moves, and repair insertions. This preserves exact objective fidelity while avoiding repeated simulator calls for revisited schedules. The same exact-cost caching pattern existed in earlier successful programs, but Generation 20 applies it across an especially broad set of candidate evaluations.

- **The beam implementation is deliberately structured to avoid near-duplicate search trajectories.** At each depth, Generation 20 sorts all expansions by exact prefix cost, then first retains entries with distinct ending transactions through `represented_last`; only afterward does it fill unused slots by score. This is a concrete refinement over a simple top-\(k\) beam, because a cheap local prefix family cannot entirely consume the beam. The resulting **240 makespan** is strong evidence that this diversity criterion retained useful alternative precedence orientations.

- **The local search is a strict best-improvement VND rather than a first-improvement walk.** For each refinement pass, Generation 20 evaluates a full neighborhood and accepts only the best strict reduction found among relocation, arbitrary swaps, reversals, and short block relocations. The block relocation of sizes two and three is a notable addition: it can preserve a useful transaction subgroup while changing its position relative to conflicting transactions. This richer move geometry distinguishes the current best from programs summarized primarily around insertion/swap/reversal search.

- **Destroy-and-repair is tightly integrated with the incumbent rather than applied indiscriminately.** Generation 20 alternates between removing contiguous intervals and randomly dispersed positions, then reinserts transactions by evaluating every transaction-position combination and randomly choosing among tied best insertions. It only locally refines a repaired schedule when the repair already improves the incumbent. This bounded, simulator-guided repair complements local descent without allowing random perturbation to dominate the search.

- **Generation 20 establishes a clear new leading performance tier.** Its **240 makespan / 4149.38 score** beats Generation 23 (**251 / 3968.25**) by **11 makespan points** and **181.13 score points**, Generation 22 (**256 / 3891.05**) by **16** and **258.33**, and Generation 21 (**268 / 3717.47**) by **28** and **431.91**, respectively. All four programs were fully valid, so these score differences are attributable primarily to schedule-quality differences rather than validation effects.

- **The new result materially improves the former top cluster identified in prior insights.** Earlier strong validated designs were tightly grouped around **253–257 makespan**: Generation 17 at **253**, Generation 14 at **254**, Generation 22/12 at **256**, and Generation 16 at **257**. Generation 20’s **240** is not a marginal improvement; it moves **13 points below** the former best and indicates that the added beam diversity and expanded local neighborhood produced a meaningful quality gain.

- **Across the valid programs, score is consistently aligned with makespan because schedule and validity metrics are saturated.** Generations 20–23 all achieved schedule score **3.00** and validity **1.00**, while their combined scores range from **3717.47** to **4149.38**. The ordering is exactly consistent with makespan: Generation 20’s 240 is best, followed by Generation 23’s 251, Generation 22’s 256, and Generation 21’s 268.

- **The strongest designs all combine exact cost evaluation, diversified construction, and multi-scale repair, but implementation details determine the final gap.** Generation 21, 22, and 23 all include several of these ingredients and remain competitive. However, Generation 20’s specific composition—distinct-last-transaction beam diversity, elite beam refinement, strict best-improvement across four move types including blocks, and dual-mode destroy-and-repair—corresponds to the best observed validated result.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Upgrade beam diversity from “distinct final transaction” to a staged prefix-signature policy.** Keep the current distinct-last rule, but reserve beam slots across distinct first transaction, first-two orderings, and/or recent suffix signatures during the first 6–10 depths; fill remaining slots by exact prefix cost. This directly extends Generation 20’s strongest differentiator—preserving competing precedence choices—while reducing the chance that low-cost prefixes sharing the same early ordering dominate the beam.

2. **Add selective exact depth-2 or depth-3 rollout scoring during early beam expansion.** For only the best immediate extensions at shallow depths, score an extension by its prefix cost plus the minimum exact cost among a bounded set of second extensions, rather than ranking solely by `cost(prefix + [txn])`. Since the simulator cost is already cached and exact prefix evaluation is validated, this targets the remaining append-only myopia without making every beam layer prohibitively expensive.

3. **Refine beam elites with path-relinking between distinct local optima.** After VND refinement of the top beam survivors, choose pairs of structurally different elite schedules and progressively transform one ordering toward the other using exact-cost-tested relocations or swaps, retaining the best intermediate schedule. Generation 20 already proves that multiple beam survivors can lead to different basins; path relinking exploits those basins more deliberately than restarting destroy-and-repair only from the single incumbent.

4. **Make destroy-and-repair impact-guided and include two-segment destruction.** Estimate transaction or position impact from the best relocation/swap/reversal improvements observed during VND, then alternate current contiguous and dispersed removals with removals of high-impact items or two short separated intervals. Reinsert removed transactions in decreasing estimated insertion difficulty, evaluating all insertion positions exactly as in the current implementation; this preserves the successful exact repair mechanism while perturbing likely bottleneck precedence decisions more effectively.

5. **Add adaptive larger coordinated neighborhoods around local-search plateaus.** Retain the current exhaustive relocation, swap, reversal, and 2–3 transaction block moves, but when VND stalls, selectively test 4-item block relocations, block swaps between two short segments, or an ejection-chain move involving two removed transactions and two reinsertion positions. Apply these expensive neighborhoods only to the best incumbent and a few elite refined schedules, because Generation 20’s advantage already appears tied to coordinated block-level changes that ordinary one-transaction moves cannot express.
