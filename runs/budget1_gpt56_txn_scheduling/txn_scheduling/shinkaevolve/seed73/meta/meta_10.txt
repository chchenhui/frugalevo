# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- **Hybrid exact-small / heuristic-large search is the strongest pattern.** The current best, **Hybrid Beam-Greedy VND Scheduler** (Generation 9), reached **3816.79** with **makespan 261** and full validity. It exhaustively evaluates all permutations for `num_txns <= 8`, eliminating heuristic variance where exhaustive optimization is affordable, then switches to bounded beam/greedy search for larger cases. This outperformed the earlier correct hybrid beam approach in Generation 7 (**3436.43**, makespan **290**) by a substantial **29 makespan points**.

- **Combining diverse construction methods produces better seeds than relying on one greedy trajectory.** Generation 9 combines a beam initialized from shuffled transactions with multiple randomized greedy constructions. Its beam preserves up to 8 promising prefixes, while independent greedy starts add schedules that beam sampling may miss. This improves on the multi-start-only/local-search family: Generation 5 scored **3460.21** (makespan **288**), Generation 4 scored **3401.36** (makespan **293**), and Generation 3 scored **3344.48** (makespan **298**).

- **Exact simulator-guided local refinement consistently improves greedy schedules.** The best program uses best-improving VND over insertion, arbitrary exchange, and subsequence-reversal neighborhoods. Earlier correct programs already showed the value of this direction: adding richer distant exchanges in Generation 4 improved makespan from Generation 3’s **298** to **293**, and full relocation/swap refinement in Generation 5 further reached **288**. Generation 9’s added reversal neighborhood and refinement of multiple elite seeds correlate with the best observed **261** makespan.

- **Bounded sampling preserves scalability without abandoning exact objective evaluation.** Generation 9 samples candidate expansions only when remaining candidate sets are large, but every sampled prefix and local-search move is scored by `workload.get_opt_seq_cost`. This retains objective fidelity while controlling evaluation volume. The approach was both correct and stronger than Generation 0’s limited sampled greedy construction (**2645.50**, makespan **377**).

## Ineffective Approaches

- **Complex search alone was not sufficient to ensure correctness.** Generation 1’s **Cost-Guided Beam Search with Insertion Refinement** received a score of **0.0** and failed validation despite beam search, cached simulator costs, greedy seeds, and insertion refinement. The evaluator gave no precise diagnostic, but this establishes that schedule-generation mechanics and edge-case handling must remain compatible with validation requirements; optimization sophistication does not compensate for invalid output or unmet interface assumptions.

- **Overly expensive exact-style search/refinement can fail evaluation even when objective-guided.** Generation 6, **Exact-Greedy Local Schedule Search**, also scored **0.0** and failed validation. Its exhaustive permutations for small workloads plus multiple greedy paths and best-improving relocation/swap refinement likely imposed excessive computational work or violated an unreported evaluator constraint. In contrast, Generation 9 explicitly bounds beam width, candidate expansion, sampled large-instance moves, refinement rounds, and elite seed count.

- **Single-path or narrowly sampled greedy construction underperforms hybrid diversification.** Generation 0’s randomized sampled greedy scheduler was valid but had the weakest correct result, **makespan 377** and score **2645.50**. Sampling at most 10 next candidates limits construction cost, but it can miss essential conflict-resolving choices. Even exhaustive-next-step multi-start greedy programs substantially improved this, with Generation 2 at **304** makespan and later local-search variants at **288–298**.

- **Prefix-cost optimization without sufficiently broad recovery mechanisms has limited quality.** Beam search with local refinement in Generation 7 was correct and strong (**290**), but remained well behind Generation 9 (**261**). The performance gap indicates that Generation 9’s combined beam diversity, separate greedy restarts, and three local neighborhoods was more effective than a narrower beam-plus-bounded-first-improvement approach.

## Implementation Insights

- **The current best program uses a per-call tuple-keyed exact-cost cache throughout all phases.** `schedule_cost(sequence)` memoizes `workload.get_opt_seq_cost(sequence)`, and the cache is shared by exhaustive search, beam expansion, greedy construction, and VND. This is particularly important because local refinement repeatedly evaluates related complete schedules and beam search repeatedly evaluates overlapping prefixes. Similar caching was present in the successful Generation 2–8 programs, but Generation 9 uses it across the broadest combined search pipeline.

- **Construction effort is deliberately bounded according to instance size.** For greedy construction, the program evaluates all remaining transactions up to 30 and otherwise samples `max(16, 3 * num_seqs)` candidates. Beam expansion similarly evaluates all remaining choices up to 26 and otherwise samples a width tied to beam size. This is a concrete balance absent from the failed Generation 6-style more exhaustive strategy: objective calls remain exact, while the number of sequences evaluated is controlled.

- **The beam implementation maintains diversity rather than merely keeping deterministic top prefixes.** It starts from shuffled initial transactions, removes duplicate prefix orders with `seen`, sorts by exact prefix cost, and shuffles equal-cost groups before filling the beam. Randomized tied selection is also used in greedy construction. This avoids fixed tie-breaking bias while retaining score-based pruning, matching the feedback that beam diversity and randomized tie handling improved robustness.

- **Refinement is structured as true best-improvement VND, not a single move type.** For up to five rounds on workloads of at most 32 transactions, the current best program exhaustively evaluates all insertion, exchange, and reversal moves; for larger workloads it evaluates `7 * num_txns` sampled moves per neighborhood. It accepts only strict improvements and then repeats from the new incumbent. This directly extends the successful relocation/swap pattern of Generation 5 and the exchange-enhanced pattern of Generation 4, while keeping large-workload work bounded.

## Performance Analysis

- **Performance improved steadily across correct multi-start/local-search variants before the major hybrid gain.** Makespan decreased from **377** in Generation 0 to **304** in Generation 2, then **298** in Generation 3, **293** in Generation 4, and **288** in Generation 5. Corresponding combined scores rose from **2645.50** to **3278.69**, **3344.48**, **3401.36**, and **3460.21**. This progression aligns with increasingly diverse starts and broader local-search neighborhoods.

- **The current best is materially better than every other validated program.** Generation 9’s **261** makespan is **27 lower** than Generation 5’s **288**, **29 lower** than Generation 7’s **290**, **32 lower** than Generation 4’s **293**, and **116 lower** than the initial sampled greedy program’s **377**. Its score of **3816.79** exceeds the next-best listed valid score, Generation 5’s **3460.21**, by **356.58**.

- **Generation 7 confirms hybrid beam search is valuable, while Generation 9 shows the importance of broader hybridization.** Generation 7’s exact-small, memoized beam-search, and bounded insertion/swap refinement achieved **3436.43** and makespan **290**, validating the scalable beam approach. Generation 9 improved this to **3816.79** and **261** by adding diversified greedy seeds, elite-seed refinement, best-improvement rather than first-improvement VND behavior, and reversal moves.

- **All successful high-performing programs maintained full schedule metrics and validity.** Generations 3–5 and 7–9 each reported schedule score **3.00** and validity **1.00**. Therefore, the score differences among successful programs are primarily explained by makespan quality rather than schedule-format correctness. Conversely, Generations 1 and 6 demonstrate a sharp binary failure mode: despite sophisticated optimization logic, failed validation reduced the combined score to **0.0**.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Add adaptive beam width and elite allocation based on search-stage uncertainty.** Instead of a fixed beam width capped at 8, retain additional prefixes when the cost gap between the current beam boundary and nearby discarded states is small, then narrow the beam when there is a clear prefix-cost separation. This extends the current program’s successful diversity mechanism while concentrating expensive refinement on instances where multiple structurally different prefixes remain competitive.

2. **Use a second construction policy that scores short lookahead completions rather than only one-step prefix cost.** For selected greedy and beam expansions, evaluate each candidate next transaction by appending one or two sampled follow-up transactions and rank it by the best resulting depth-2/depth-3 prefix cost. The current greedy/beam logic is exact for the immediate prefix but can be myopic; bounded lookahead preserves exact simulator scoring and may avoid early choices that look cheap locally but create expensive downstream conflicts.

3. **Replace purely random large-instance VND sampling with conflict-focused move sampling.** Keep some uniform random insertion, exchange, and reversal moves, but bias most samples toward transaction positions that contribute most to cost degradation, inferred by probing removals, adjacent swaps, or local segment perturbations against `get_opt_seq_cost`. Generation 9 already benefits from three neighborhoods; targeting likely problematic positions should improve the sampled regime for `num_txns > 30` without reintroducing the excessive exhaustive work associated with failed exact-style approaches.

4. **Introduce a destroy-and-repair large-neighborhood phase for the top refined seeds.** After VND stalls, remove a small subset of transactions—e.g., 3–6 positions chosen from high-impact or random regions—and greedily reinsert them into the best positions using exact sequence costs, repeating for a tightly bounded number of attempts. This provides an escape from local minima that insertion/swap/reversal VND cannot cross, while building directly on the proven simulator-guided greedy construction and cached exact scoring.

5. **Diversify seed generation with complementary deterministic transaction orderings in addition to shuffled starts.** Seed the beam or greedy constructor using starts/order priorities derived from transaction metadata or cheap simulator probes, such as each singleton cost, pairwise ordering preference, or estimated conflict degree, alongside the existing randomized starts. The current best’s major gain came from heterogeneous seeds; structured seeds can expose useful conflict-resolving schedules that random shuffles and immediate-prefix greedy choices may not encounter.
