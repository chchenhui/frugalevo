# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- **Direct simulator-objective optimization is consistently associated with strong valid schedules.** The current best, **Exact Beam and Local-Search Scheduler** (Generation 8), scores **3861.00** with makespan **258.00** by ranking beam prefixes and every local-search candidate through `workload.get_opt_seq_cost()`. The other strong beam variants using the same true objective—Generations 3, 4, and 7—each reached **3636.36** with makespan **274.00**.

- **Beam search plus multi-neighborhood refinement produced the best improvement.** Generation 8 extends the insertion-and-swap approach of Generations 4 and 7 with **interval reversal** moves. This corresponds to a makespan reduction from **274 to 258** and a combined-score increase from **3636.36 to 3861.00**. The current implementation explicitly searches insertion, swap, and reversal neighborhoods from several top beam seeds.

- **Exact search for small instances, deterministic beam retention for larger ones, is a successful hybrid.** Generations 3, 4, 7, and 8 all use exhaustive permutation search up to seven transactions and switch to bounded beam search afterward. These programs all passed validation. The exact branch guarantees quality for small workloads without burdening larger ones, while beam search maintains multiple promising partial schedules.

- **Refining multiple final beam candidates is better than refining only one schedule.** The current best refines up to `min(len(beam), max(3, min(10, num_seqs)))` final beam seeds. This preserves alternatives that may have similar prefix costs but different local-search potential. Generation 5 used a similar multi-finalist insertion-refinement pattern and achieved **3472.22**, substantially above the simple sampled greedy scheduler’s **2898.55**.

## Ineffective Approaches

- **Random sampled greedy selection limited schedule quality despite being correct.** Generation 0, **Random-Start Sampled Greedy Scheduler**, passed validation but scored only **2898.55** with makespan **344.00**. It evaluates only up to 10 sampled remaining transactions at each step, so high-quality next transactions outside the sample are never considered. Its random start and diversity were insufficient relative to deterministic beam retention.

- **Correct-looking beam/local-search designs still failed entirely when validation or interface behavior was wrong.** Generations 1 and 2 both scored **0.0** and were marked incorrect despite using cached `get_opt_seq_cost` beam methods. This shows that simulator-guided search quality is irrelevant unless the implementation always meets schedule and interface requirements. Generation 6 has a concrete example: for workloads over seven transactions it references `best_cost` and `best_schedule` before initialization, while its intended beam implementation is below an unconditional `return`; it consequently scored **0.00**.

- **Increasing search complexity without preserving robustness did not guarantee success.** Generation 9 expanded exact search to nine transactions and used insertion, swap, reversal, and block relocation, yet scored **0.0** due to validation failure. In contrast, Generation 8 achieved the highest valid score with a more controlled exact-search cutoff of seven, capped beam width, bounded rounds, and structured neighborhood subsampling for large schedules.

- **Insertion-only local refinement was helpful but weaker than broader move sets.** Generation 5’s beam-plus-insertion approach reached **3472.22** and makespan **287.00**. Adding swap search in Generations 4 and 7 improved makespan to **274.00**, and adding reversal in the current best improved it further to **258.00**. The evaluated results support the current program’s comment that insertion-local optima can remain improvable through moves that simultaneously alter multiple relative transaction orders.

## Implementation Insights

- **The current best combines bounded global search with expressive local search.** Its beam width is scaled by `num_seqs` but bounded to `[8, 48]` via `max(8, min(48, max(1, num_seqs) * 5))`. This avoids both the narrow candidate retention of sampled greedy search and unbounded expansion. Each depth expands all unused transactions from retained prefixes, sorts by `(cost, prefix)`, and preserves the best beam entries deterministically.

- **Tuple-keyed memoization supports the large number of simulator calls.** The `evaluate` helper caches `workload.get_opt_seq_cost(list(key))` by immutable schedule tuples. This applies to prefix expansion, full beam schedules, and repeated local-search candidates. The same pattern appears in the successful Generation 3/4/7 family and is especially important in Generation 8, where three neighborhood types create many repeated schedule evaluations.

- **The best implementation deliberately scales local-search cost with problem size.** For at most 30 transactions, it examines full insertion neighborhoods; above 30, it uses evenly spaced source/destination positions plus the final position. This retains long-distance moves while avoiding the full quadratic neighborhood cost. It also caps descent at `max(2, num_txns)` rounds, preventing uncontrolled search.

- **Strict-improvement logic and deterministic tie behavior improve stability.** Beam states are sorted by `(cost, sequence)`, while local search only accepts candidates with strictly lower cost. This avoids random behavior and cycling. The correct, high-scoring Generation 8 thereby differs sharply from Generation 0’s randomized-start sampled strategy, which trades reproducibility and exhaustive candidate comparison for lightweight diversity.

- **Beam-based simulator scoring materially outperformed sampled greedy construction.** Relative to Generation 0’s **344.00** makespan, the current best reduced makespan by **86** and raised combined score from **2898.55** to **3861.00**. The evidence favors retaining and comparing multiple exact-cost-ranked prefixes over selecting only from random candidate samples.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Add a bounded block-relocation neighborhood to the existing insertion/swap/reversal descent.** For each local-search round, try removing contiguous blocks of length 2–4 from selected positions and reinserting them at selected destinations, accepting only strict improvements under `evaluate`. This extends the proven reversal benefit by enabling coordinated movement of several transactions while remaining much safer than Generation 9’s overly broad, validation-risky implementation.

2. **Use a two-stage beam width: retain a wider beam in early prefix depths, then narrow it near completion.** For example, use up to 64–96 states while the prefix is shorter than half the schedule, then reduce toward the current 48-state cap for later depths. Early ordering choices can determine large conflict structures, so preserving more diverse partial schedules may produce stronger local-search seeds without multiplying the expensive full-length refinement workload.

3. **Diversify final local-search seeds by schedule distance, not only beam rank.** Keep the best beam member, then greedily choose additional seeds that differ substantially in transaction positions or pairwise ordering from already selected seeds, while still restricting selection to a strong cost-ranked beam subset. The current program already benefits from refining multiple finalists; explicitly selecting structurally different candidates can avoid spending local-search budget on nearly identical schedules that converge to the same local optimum.

4. **Add a deterministic perturb-and-reoptimize phase for the best local optimum.** After ordinary descent stalls, create a small number of candidates by applying controlled non-improving perturbations—such as reversing a medium interval, relocating a 2–3 transaction block, or swapping distant positions—then rerun the current strict-improvement insertion/swap/reversal descent from each. This is a bounded iterated-local-search extension that can cross local-optimum barriers while preserving the best program’s deterministic simulator-objective ranking and validity-safe schedule construction.

5. **Improve large-instance neighborhood sampling with conflict-informed positions rather than only evenly spaced indices.** Retain the current evenly spaced source/destination positions, but supplement them with positions whose transactions have high estimated conflict impact, derived from one-transaction removal/reinsertion costs or cached prefix-cost changes. The existing structured sampling preserves long-distance moves efficiently; prioritizing likely bottleneck transactions should make the limited local-search evaluations more effective on schedules above 30 transactions.
