# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- **The current best result comes from diversified greedy construction followed by exhaustive multi-neighborhood descent.** Generation 18, **Multi-Neighborhood Greedy Schedule Descent**, achieved the best validated outcome: **3787.88 combined score** and **263.00 makespan**. It constructs several schedules from different starts using exact `workload.get_opt_seq_cost` evaluations, then repeatedly applies strict best-improvement relocation, swap, and adjacent two-transaction block-relocation moves. This improves materially on the previous strong diversified greedy result, Generation 14, at **3623.19 / 275.00**, reducing makespan by **12**.

- **Adding adjacent-pair block relocation appears to be a meaningful improvement over relocation/swap-only descent.** The current best explicitly searches blocks of two adjacent transactions and continues descent until no relocation, swap, or block move improves the simulator cost. Generation 12, which also added a contiguous two-transaction block relocation neighborhood to beam refinement, achieved **3690.04** and **270.00 makespan**, supporting the value of this operator. Generation 18’s greedy multi-start construction plus the same class of block move produced the stronger **263.00** result.

- **Exact simulator scoring is consistently present in the strongest valid programs.** Generation 18 evaluates every greedy append candidate and every local-search candidate through the authoritative simulator, as did Generation 12 (**270.00**), Generation 19 (**273.00**), Generation 16 (**272.00**), and Generation 14 (**275.00**). There is no evidence that proxy conflict metrics outperform direct `get_opt_seq_cost` optimization in these evaluations.

- **Diversified greedy seeds are more competitive than beam construction in this set of accepted results.** Generation 18’s multi-start greedy approach reached **263.00**, while the best reported beam-oriented valid result, Generation 12, reached **270.00**. Other beam/VND variants were lower still: Generation 16 at **272.00**, Generation 19 at **273.00**, Generation 11 at **276.00**, and Generation 15 at **279.00**.

## Ineffective Approaches

- **Validation failures remain a complete performance failure regardless of search sophistication.** Generation 10, **Simulator-Scored Beam Local Search**, and Generation 17, **Beam Search Transaction Scheduler**, both received **0.0** and were marked incorrect despite simulator-scored beam construction, caching, and several local-search neighborhoods. Their failure shows that valid permutation/scheduler behavior is a hard gate: optimization quality is irrelevant when validation does not accept the result.

- **Bounded or sampled neighborhood search was weaker than the current best’s exhaustive descent.** Generation 13, **Multi-Start Greedy VND Scheduler**, achieved only **3278.69** with a **304.00 makespan**. It used exhaustive neighborhoods only through 30 transactions and randomized sampling on larger workloads. In contrast, Generation 18 evaluates full relocation, swap, and adjacent-block neighborhoods and reached **263.00**, a **41-unit makespan reduction**.

- **Beam search did not overcome the apparent value of stronger local optimization from diversified greedy seeds.** Although valid beam variants were strong, they generally trailed the greedy-descent family: Generation 11 scored **3610.11 / 276.00**, Generation 15 **3571.43 / 279.00**, and Generation 19 **3649.64 / 273.00**. Beam prefix selection can provide candidate diversity, but the measured results indicate that it did not produce better final basins than Generation 18’s multi-start greedy construction plus exhaustive descent.

- **Additional perturbation mechanisms did not clearly exceed exhaustive deterministic local descent.** Generation 19 added destroy-and-repair restarts and reached **273.00**, while Generation 16 added bounded reversals, block relocation, and two-move perturbations for **272.00**. Both were valid and effective, but neither surpassed Generation 18’s simpler strict-improvement search over complete relocation, swap, and block neighborhoods.

## Implementation Insights

- **Generation 18’s cost cache is used across all search stages and is central to making exhaustive moves feasible.** The `cost(sequence)` wrapper keys results by `tuple(sequence)` and is called during greedy construction, relocation, swaps, block moves, and final evaluation. This avoids repeatedly invoking `workload.get_opt_seq_cost` for schedules recreated by overlapping starts and neighborhood moves, enabling full local-neighborhood evaluation without abandoning the exact objective.

- **The current best preserves schedule validity structurally.** Each construction starts with one transaction, removes selected transactions from `remaining`, and appends each transaction exactly once. Relocation removes one existing transaction and reinserts it; swap exchanges two elements; block relocation removes and reinserts an existing adjacent pair. These operations preserve a complete transaction permutation, consistent with its **validity 1.00** and unlike Generations 10 and 17, which failed validation.

- **Strict improvement gives the descent loop clear termination and avoids unstable tie cycling.** In Generation 18, a candidate is accepted only when `candidate_cost < best_value`; equal-cost schedules do not replace the incumbent. The outer loop returns to relocation after any accepted swap or block move, because these moves can expose new improving single-transaction relocations. This exhaustive variable-neighborhood control flow is stronger than a fixed number of passes and aligns with the best observed makespan of **263.00**.

- **Construction diversity is targeted rather than purely random.** The current best uses `num_seqs` as a seed budget, tries every start when feasible, otherwise includes transaction `0` and samples additional starts. Candidate order is shuffled only to diversify equal-cost greedy choices, while the objective comparison itself remains exact. This avoids pure transaction-ID bias while maintaining reproducible schedule coverage.

## Performance Analysis

- **Generation 18 is the clear leader among all valid reported programs.** Its **263.00 makespan** and **3787.88 combined score** beat the next-best valid result, Generation 12’s **270.00 / 3690.04**, by **7 makespan units** and **97.84 score points**. It also improves on Generation 16’s **272.00** by **9**, Generation 19’s **273.00** by **10**, and Generation 14’s **275.00** by **12**.

- **The ranking supports multi-neighborhood local descent more strongly than beam sophistication.** The top valid outcomes are Generation 18’s greedy relocation/swap/block descent (**263**), Generation 12’s beam plus relocation/swap/block refinement (**270**), Generation 16’s beam VND with blocks and perturbation (**272**), and Generation 19’s beam with destroy-repair (**273**). The common factor is exact simulator-scored local refinement; the differentiator favoring Generation 18 is its diversified greedy seeds and complete, repeatedly applied neighborhoods.

- **All accepted high-performing programs have identical schedule and validity metrics, so makespan drives their score differences.** Generations 11–19 that passed validation report **schedule metric 3.00** and **validity 1.00**. Therefore, Generation 18’s score advantage is directly attributable to its lower measured makespan rather than a formatting or validation advantage.

- **Search-cost bounding can trade away solution quality.** Generation 13’s workload-dependent sampling produced **304.00**, while the more exhaustive current-best procedure produced **263.00**. Conversely, beam methods with bounded widths and sampled large-instance moves remained in the **270–279** range. The evaluation data therefore associates greater exact neighborhood coverage, when supported by caching, with the strongest accepted makespans.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Run exhaustive VND from several distinct greedy constructions instead of refining only `constructed[0]`.** Deduplicate the constructed schedules by `tuple(schedule)`, retain the best 2–4 seeds (or all seeds when `num_seqs` is small), and apply the existing relocation/swap/adjacent-pair-block descent independently to each before returning the best result. The current program’s diversified starts are already valuable, but choosing only the cheapest unrefined greedy seed can discard a schedule whose local-search basin reaches a lower makespan.

2. **Generalize block relocation to include length-3 contiguous blocks on small or moderate workloads.** Keep the proven length-2 block neighborhood, then, after relocation/swap/pair-block stagnation, remove every contiguous triple and reinsert it at every legal position using the same cached exact simulator evaluation and strict-improvement rule. Generation 12 and 18 support the importance of coordinated adjacent moves; a bounded triple-block neighborhood is a direct validation-safe extension that can cross barriers involving three mutually dependent transactions.

3. **Add a deterministic exact-cost destroy-and-repair restart from the locally optimal schedules.** Remove a small contiguous segment or a small set of 2–4 positions from the incumbent, preserve the residual order, then greedily reinsert removed transactions by testing every insertion position with `cost`; run the full existing VND after each repaired schedule. This builds on the exact-scoring and permutation-safe principles of Generation 18 while using the promising, though not yet superior, escape mechanism seen in Generation 19 to reach a different local-optimum basin.

4. **Improve greedy-seed diversity by explicitly branching on tied or near-tied append choices.** During `construct_from`, collect all transactions with equal best incremental cost—or, conservatively, costs within a tiny absolute threshold—and produce several deterministic/randomized tie branches rather than selecting the first shuffled minimum. Exact append evaluation remains unchanged, but this produces genuinely distinct high-quality seeds in workloads with flat prefix costs, which can increase the chance that one seed descends below the current best basin.

5. **Add a restricted two-transaction relocation neighborhood that is broader than adjacent blocks but cheaper than unrestricted multi-moves.** After ordinary neighborhoods stagnate, choose pairs from a small set of promising positions—such as high-impact transactions identified by trying each single removal—and remove both, then test reinserting them in their original relative order at all destinations. This can move non-adjacent cooperating transactions together while preserving a valid permutation and exact simulator scoring, extending the successful “interacting transactions may need to move jointly” rationale behind pair-block relocation.
