<DESCRIPTION>
The current bottleneck is slope changes (82.2) and false reversals (60.4). The Kalman's reversal gate is the analog of the "second pass" window: its comparison horizon (reversal_window = window_size // 4) is the least-tuned parameter and is too narrow — a short slope-history lookback makes the gate trigger off noise-induced slope sign flickers rather than genuine turning points. Two coordinated changes: (1) widen the lookback to window_size // 3, so the gate compares against a more statistically stable slope estimate; (2) add sign-hysteresis — only fire the process-noise boost when the *new* slope has been sign-consistent for two consecutive steps AND the sign change is significant, which suppresses single-sample noise spikes from inflating process noise and causing lag/drift. Together these reduce spurious reversals (helping both slope_changes and false_reversals) while preserving the genuine-turning-point responsiveness via the retained significance test (sig > 1.5, inno_sig > 1.0).</DESCRIPTION>
