The metrics show lag_error (0.26) is the dominant weakness, while correlation is already saturated at 0.97. The current slope-lead compensation only advances the level by a first-order term (lead_gain * sg_half * slope). Because the signal uses a constant-acceleration model and the smoother already estimates curvature (state[2]), we can compensate the group delay more accurately with the second-order Taylor term: y(t+d) ≈ y(t) + d·slope + ½d²·curvature. Using the already-computed smoothed curvature channel costs nothing extra and reduces lag on curved segments (peaks/troughs) precisely where lag error is largest. To keep false reversals in check, the curvature term is damped (factor 0.25 of its nominal coefficient) and lead_gain is raised modestly from 0.2 to 0.3 — the curvature correction makes the higher lead safe because it no longer overshoots on curved trajectories. This should lower lag_error without increasing slope_changes or false_reversals, improving the multi-objective composite score.</DESCRIPTION>
