The Stage 3 lead compensation injects a first-difference term that amplifies curvature spikes at turning points, which is exactly what produces spurious directional reversals. Adding a curvature (second-difference) damper directly penalizes those oscillation spikes without adding lag, since np.gradient is a symmetric (zero-phase) operator. To avoid noise-induced over-damping, the curvature is clipped robustly at ±3·median(|d2|). The coefficient is scaled by sg_half so the damping strength scales with the smoothing window (consistent with Stage 3's lead scaling). This targets the false-reversal and slope-change metrics while leaving the lag error and tracking accuracy essentially untouched (the damper is zero at inflection-free regions).

    # --- Stage 3: conservative slope-lead compensation + curvature damper ---
    # Multi-scale SG attenuates sharp corners; restore dynamics by adding a
    # conservative fraction of the zero-phase estimated slope (no added lag,
    # since the slope is estimated symmetrically via np.gradient).
    slope = np.gradient(smoothed)
    lead = (0.5 * sg_half * 0.25)
    # Curvature damper: penalize second-difference spikes (oscillation /
    # false reversals) injected or amplified by the lead compensation.
    # Zero-phase, O(n), and robustly clipped so genuine turning points
    # (large |d2|) are not over-flattened.
    d2 = np.gradient(np.gradient(smoothed))
    med_abs_d2 = np.median(np.abs(d2)) + 1e-12
    d2 = np.clip(d2, -3.0 * med_abs_d2, 3.0 * med_abs_d2)
    smoothed = smoothed + lead * slope - 0.1 * sg_half * d2
