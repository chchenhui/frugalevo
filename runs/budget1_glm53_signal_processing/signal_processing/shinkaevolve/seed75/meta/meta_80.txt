Gen 72's volatility-scaled R achieved the round's best noise reduction (0.82) and lag (0.27) while keeping corr 0.97 — but paid with smoothness 0.23, confirming the trade-off.
- **Double zero-phase SG with mirrored (2·edge − reflection) padding is confirmed as the optimum SG stage:** none of this round's alternatives (three-scale kernel banks in Gens 73/75/77, triple SG cascade in Gens 21/76/78, percentile/Gaussian volatility blending in Gens 73/80/81) beat the simple double cascade.

## Ineffective Approaches
- **Triple SG cascades are net-negative or break correctness:** Gen 21's "triple_sg_gated_lead" scored **0.00** (failed validation entirely — complexity-induced failure), while Gen 78's third zero-phase SG pass (0.63) and Gen 76's full/half/third-window triple (0.64) both fell short of the best's 0.65. Adding a third SG pass adds failure surface without measurable gain.
- **Multi-scale SG kernel banks (2×half / half / half//2) trade correlation for smoothness and lose net score:** Gens 75/77 showed the pattern — smoothness up to 0.34–0.38 but correlation down to 0.88–0.89 and noise reduction down to 0.42–0.45, netting 0.62–0.65 with no improvement. The narrower kernels re-inject the noise the wider kernels removed.
- **Slope-lead stages are the dominant reversal-injection mechanism:** Gen 80's sigmoid-gated lead produced 66.8 slope changes / 49.2 false reversals (smoothness 0.23) despite corr 0.97 and NR 0.81; Gen 72's confidence-gated lead hit 65.2 slope changes / 45.4 reversals. Only the best's conservative parameters (lead = 0.5·sg_half·0.25, gate threshold 0.25·q_scale) keep reversals at 34.
- **Removing the gradient component from the slope blend regressed correlation:** Gen 77 ("remove_gradient_slope_blend", using pure RTS slope in the lead) fell to corr 0.88, NR 0.45 — empirically confirming the 0.7/0.3 RTS/gradient blend in the best is needed for responsiveness. Conversely, Gen 21's widened/two-pass SG before lead compensation was over-aggressive and contributed to its 0.00.

## Implementation Insights
- **The best's Stage 4 remains the sole tuning lever and the round's closest variants bracket it:** Gen 74's use of the RTS slope directly in the lead stage (0.64, corr 0.97, 67.8 slope changes) and Gen 75's tightened gate + stronger final polish (0.65 but NR only 0.42, lag 0.45) show that gain magnitude and gate threshold are the two sensitivity knobs; the best's settings sit at the local optimum.
- **Degenerate-case guards are prerequisites, not optional:** Gen 21's 0.00 (likely short-signal/alignment failures in its multi-pass pipeline) matches the prior round's pattern of Gens 65/66/67 crashing on hysteresis slicing, degenerate percentiles, and transposed RTS solves. The best's guards (`m < 2*half+1` early return, `len(out) >= m` fallback, `output_length` fallback to `kf_out`, 1e-9/1e-12 denominators) are why it survives validation while heavier pipelines don't.
- **Fixed scalar Q = 0.05·q_scale² with 2-state CV model remains the stable recipe:** Gen 73's constant-acceleration model with MAD noise estimation and trend-reversal Q-boosting scored 0.62 with smoothness 0.21 and 56.2 false reversals — the third confirmation (after Gens 62, 68) that adaptive Q and acceleration states amplify slope ripple.
- **RTS via `np.linalg.inv(Pm)` gain on [level, slope] state is universally replicated** across all nine correct programs this round (runtimes 0.03–0.04s, efficiency saturated at 1.00); no alternative backward-smoother formulation was attempted or needed.

# Recommendations for Future Program Mutations

1. **Apply the solve-based RTS gain as a single minimal change to the current best.** Replace `G = P_filt[i] @ F.T @ np.linalg.inv(Pm)` with `G = np.linalg.solve(Pm, (P_filt[i] @ F.T).T).T` in Stage 2, keeping everything else byte-identical. The solve form was historically more numerically stable (Gen 35) and costs nothing; if smoothness improves even marginally, it compounds with the saturated 0.96 correlation for a free composite gain.

2. **Soften the lead-gate pair to attack false reversals below 34:** in one mutation change only the sigmoid temperature from `0.15 * q_scale2` to `0.10 * q_scale2`, and in a separate mutation change only the lead magnitude from `0.5 * sg_half * 0.25` to `0.4 * sg_half * 0.25`. Gens 80/71 showed lead/gate aggressiveness is the dominant reversal-injection mechanism (45–66 reversals at higher gains); a slightly softer setting should push reversals under 34 without touching the saturated correlation, since Gen 75's over-softening (26 reversals) paid in NR/lag — the best's values sit near, but perhaps not at, the local optimum.

3. **Remove the 0.3 gradient-slope blend in Stage 4 (`slope = 0.7*slope_rts + 0.3*slope_grad` → `slope = slope_rts`) as a clean single-variable test.** Gen 77 confirmed the blend is needed for responsiveness in the *pure-RTS-lead* configuration, but no round has tested the gradient-free blend *with the best's exact gate and lead constants intact* — Gen 77 also changed other elements simultaneously. Keep `q_scale2`, gate center `0.25·q_scale`, temperature `0.15·q_scale`, and lead `0.5·sg_half·0.25` verbatim so the score delta isolates the blend effect alone.

4. **Add a light unconditional third zero-phase SG pass after the lead compensation, before the end-aligned trim.** Use `h3 = max(2, sg_half // 3)`, order 2, reusing the existing `_zero_phase_smooth` helper (it already has mirrored reflect-padding and the `m < 2*half+1` degenerate fallback). With correlation and efficiency saturated, smoothness (0.30) and reversals (34) are the only headroom; unlike Gen 78's heavier third pass (window `h2`), the `//3` half-width keeps lag cost negligible. Guard the trim with the existing `len(out) >= output_length` fallback.

5. **Try a percentile-mixed two-scale SG cascade in Stage 3:** replace the second pass's fixed `h2 = sg_half // 2` with a per-sample blend of two zero-phase SG outputs (`h2` and `sg_half`) weighted by a sigmoid of local volatility (convolved `|diff|`), using the existing `1e-9` denominator guards. This is a lighter-weight version of the Gen 45×46 kernel-bank idea that avoids the correlation collapse Gens 75/77 suffered from *fixed* narrow kernels — adaptively widening the kernel in high-volatility regions preserves responsiveness where the signal genuinely moves while keeping wide-kernel smoothness elsewhere. Validate output shape after each stage to avoid the Gen 65/66/67 crash pattern.
