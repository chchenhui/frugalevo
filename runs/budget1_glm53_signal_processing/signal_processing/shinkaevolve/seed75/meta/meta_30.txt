## Successful Algorithmic Patterns
- **The best program (Gen 7, 0.64) remains unbeaten after ten more attempts.** Its winning formula — constant-velocity Kalman (MAD noise, 3σ gating with R×9) → unconditional double zero-phase SG pass → conservative slope lead (0.25×sg_half×0.5·slope) → end-aligned trim — still holds the highest smoothness (0.27) and lowest false reversals (37.4) among all 16 evaluated programs.

## Ineffective Approaches
- **Slope-lead amplification consistently degrades the smoothness frontier.** Gens 24 (0.53), 26 (0.54), 29 (0.46), and 30 (0.51) all added stronger lead compensation (lead = window/12, 0.35–0.4×window) and all fell below 0.58, with false reversals 66–93 and smoothness 0.14–0.20. The best program's lead is 0.125·sg_half·slope — every attempt to increase it lost more on smoothness than it gained on lag.
- **Localized/selective smoothing keeps failing against full-signal smoothing.** Gen 28's hysteresis "only to weak gradient-flip neighborhoods" (0.56, 71.2 false reversals) and Gen 32's version (0.57, 69.2) both underperform Gen 7's unconditional double SG pass (37.4). This reconfirms across two more generations that gating smoothing to detected problem regions cannot suppress micro-oscillations.
- **Complex multi-stage pipelines are still the top failure mode.** Gen 27 (0.00) — Kalman + RTS + double SG + lead — failed entirely with "All test signals failed," mirroring Gens 13/14/18. Its edge reflection slicing `sig[-2:-half-2:-1]` (broken for small half) is exactly the fragile boundary-handling class of bug; sophisticated machinery is worthless without validated alignment.
- **Forward/backward Kalman fusion underperforms expectation.** Gen 29's 50/50 fused passes (0.46) produced lag error 0.73 and 118.6 slope changes — worse lag than causal+RTS designs, showing naive fusion does not cancel delay as effectively as an RTS pass or lead compensation.

## Implementation Insights
- **The best program's simplicity is its robustness:** a single 2-state Kalman loop with vectorized SG convolution via `pinv(AᵀA)Aᵀ` row 0, edge-safe guards (`if m < 2*half+1: return sig.copy()`), and a one-line end trim `smoothed[-output_length:]`. None of the ten new, more complex programs (five-stage pipelines, fusion, hysteresis gates, cumulative-sum reconstruction) beat it — architectural simplicity with correct boundary handling wins.

The reversal gate actively injects jitter at sign flips — the same pathology flagged in Gens 15–20. The 2-state [level, slope] model of Gen 7 remains optimal.
- **Q tuning matters more than state complexity:** Gen 7's `Q = 0.05·(median|diff(x)|)²` balances tracking and damping; Gen 29's feedback explicitly identified Q/R scaling and unsmoothed slope channels as its weak points (noise reduction 0.29). Denoising the slope channel before lead compensation (per Gen 24's feedback) is the recognized fix no program has yet implemented correctly.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Add a third zero-phase SG pass with a short window (h3 = max(2, sg_half // 3), quadratic kernel) applied unconditionally to the full signal.** The 0.59→0.64 jump came from adding the second pass, and false reversals track smoothness inversely across all 16 programs (37.4→0.27 vs 58→0.20 vs 116→0.12). Since correlation (0.96) and responsiveness (0.74) are saturated, an extra light pass costs almost nothing on those metrics and directly attacks the score frontier. Reuse `_zero_phase_smooth` exactly as-is and keep the end trim `smoothed[-output_length:]` after the final stage.

2. **Replace the raw `np.gradient(smoothed)` in Stage 3 with the Kalman slope state, or apply one short SG pass to the gradient before lead compensation.** Gen 24's feedback identified the unsmoothed slope channel as re-injecting high-frequency energy that undoes the double smoothing; the forward loop already computes `s[1]` per step, so storing it in a `slope_arr` array is free. With a denoised slope, the lead can be safely raised from `0.125·sg_half·slope` toward 0.3–0.4×sg_half to improve lag error (0.35) without raising false reversals — every prior attempt to increase lead failed *because* it used noisy slopes, not because lead itself is bad.

3. **Store `s[i]` and `P[i]` for every step in the forward Kalman loop, then run a standard RTS backward recursion on the [level, slope] state before the SG stages.** Gens 25/32 showed RTS variants beat the best program on lag error (0.31 vs 0.35) and noise reduction while matching correlation 0.94; they lost only on smoothness due to their strong lead/hysteresis add-ons. Keeping Gen 7's exact double-SG + conservative-lead + end-trim stages after the RTS pass combines the two proven winners without the failed extras. Keep Q = 0.05·(median|diff|)² and the 2-state model — 3-state [level, slope, curvature] with reversal gates demonstrably injected jitter in Gens 25/26/28/32.

4. **Widen the second SG pass from `sg_half // 2` to `(2 * sg_half) // 3` (or try `sg_half // 2 + 1`), holding the third pass constant.** This is the lowest-risk single-knob mutation: the second pass exists purely to suppress slope reversals, and its window is the least-tuned parameter in the program (h2 = sg_half//2 was never varied across ten generations). Compare against h3 variants from recommendation 1 in separate mutations, since both target the same 37.4-reversal bottleneck and only one width direction can win.

5. **Harden and validate alignment as the very first priority in any mutation: assert the output is exactly `len(x) − window_size + 1` samples, end-aligned, after every stage, and never touch the `adaptive_filter` baseline or `process_signal` signature.** Gen 27 scored 0.00 purely from a broken edge-reflection slice (`sig[-2:-half-2:-1]` fails for small half), while nine architecturally identical siblings passed — the existing `_zero_phase_smooth` guards (`if m < 2*half+1: return sig.copy()` and the `len(out) >= m` fallback) are proven safe and should be copied verbatim into any new smoothing stage rather than rewritten. Any new stage must also degrade gracefully with `sig.copy()` on short inputs.
