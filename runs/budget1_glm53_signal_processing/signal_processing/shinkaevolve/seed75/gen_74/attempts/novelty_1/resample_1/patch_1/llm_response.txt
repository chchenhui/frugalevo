<DESCRIPTION>
The last identified noise leak in the pipeline is Stage 5's slope channel: it is derived from `np.gradient(smoothed)`, i.e., a finite difference of an already-smoothed signal. Finite differencing amplifies any residual ripple left by the SG cascade, so the sigmoid gate (`gate = 1/(1+exp(-(|slopes|/(2*mad_sl) - 2)))` opens on noise-induced slope spikes, injecting lead into quiet regions and causing spurious directional reversals (false_reversals 51, slope_changes 69). Meanwhile the RTS smoother already produces an optimal, low-noise, zero-phase slope estimate `sm[:, 1]` (the smoothed slope state), which is discarded by the current code. This is exactly the "gradient vs. RTS-slope" blend issue flagged in prior generations: pure RTS-slope historically held lag at 0.25-0.40 with best-in-class reversals. As a minimal, single-attribution mutation I replace only the slope source in Stage 5 with `sm[:, 1]`, keeping the 5-tap slope polish, the MAD noise scale, the sigmoid gate constants, and `lead = max(1, window_size/8)` exactly as-is, so any score delta is attributable to the slope-source swap alone. The RTS slope is smoother (fewer gate openings on noise -> fewer false reversals and slope changes) yet is a genuine smoothed trend estimate (trend tracking / lag preserved), directly improving the reversal and slope-change objectives that dominate the combined score.</DESCRIPTION>
