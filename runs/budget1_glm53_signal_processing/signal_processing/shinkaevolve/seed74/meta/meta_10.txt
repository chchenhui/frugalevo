# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

# Analysis of Program Evaluation Results

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- **Forward-backward Kalman fusion with velocity-weighted blending is the current best approach (0.55 combined score, Gen 5 kalman_zero_phase_hybrid_filter, the current best program)**. Running a constant-velocity Kalman filter in both directions and fusing with velocity-confidence weights `1/(1+|velocity|)` achieves the best balance: correlation 0.96, noise reduction 0.70, lag error 0.22, responsiveness 0.82 — all the best or near-best values across all generations.
- **Zero-lag local quadratic least-squares endpoint fitting (evaluating the fit at the newest sample, t=1)** is a reliably good pattern: Gen 3 (0.51, correlation 0.92), Gen 7 (0.51), and Gen 9 (0.51) all achieved strong responsiveness (0.71–0.73) with moderate lag (0.36–0.41).
- **Innovation-driven adaptive measurement noise** (r = 0.5·r₀ + 0.5·innovation²) consistently supports high correlation (0.90–0.96) across Gen 4, 5, and 11 — but only when paired with adequate smoothing.
- **Correct output-length contract is mandatory**: the best program slices `smooth[window_size-1:]` to yield exactly `n − window_size + 1` samples; Gen 5 (and the current best) passed all validation via this convention.

## Ineffective Approaches

- **Raw squared innovations directly as measurement noise cause jitter-chasing**: the current best program's feedback explicitly notes this — feeding innovations un-smoothed into r produces a jagged output (smoothness 0.06, 302.8 slope changes, 237.8 false reversals), capping its score despite excellent accuracy. Temporal smoothing (EWMA of innovation variance) is identified as the fix.
- **Output-format violations produce total failure regardless of algorithm quality**: Gen 1 (kalman_adaptive_filter, score 0.00) failed from misaligned re-indexing (`start = window_size - 1 - delay`, possibly negative/empty slices); Gen 6 (zero_phase_kalman_savgol_filter, score 0.00) returned 500 samples when 481 were expected ("Invalid filtered signal length: expected 481, got 500").
- **Overly aggressive slope-correction/hysteresis stages add artifacts**: Gen 4 (0.50) with 9× process-noise amplification and forward gain 0.6 produced 267.8 false reversals; Gen 8 (0.38) showed that stacking a Kalman fusion and slope stabilizer on an already-good zero-lag Stage 1 actively degraded performance (noise reduction fell to 0.04, correlation to 0.41).
- **Narrow/negligible post-smoothing fails to fix smoothness**: Gens 3, 7, 9 all used a light one-pole smoother (α ≥ 0.75) or short SG window (window_size//6 ≈ 3 in Gen 11), leaving smoothness stuck at 0.06–0.09 across every program.

## Implementation Insights

- **The current best program's key structural choices**: (1) dual forward/backward Kalman passes cancel phase lag without explicit delay compensation; (2) velocity-based blend weights prioritize the pass with more stable slope estimates; (3) r₀ initialized from `np.var(np.diff(x))/2` gives a data-driven noise scale; (4) trimming `smooth[window_size-1:]` satisfies the validation contract exactly.
- **Slicing convention matters more than cleverness**: successful programs (Gens 5, 11, current best) all trim to `n − window_size + 1`; the two scoring-0.00 programs violated either length (Gen 6) or alignment (Gen 1).
- **Blend-weighted dual-pass fusion outperforms both single-pass Kalman (Gen 4: 0.50) and single-pass quadratic endpoint fits (Gen 3/7/9: 0.51)** by achieving the same accuracy while cutting lag error to 0.22 — the lowest of all correct programs.
- **Every correct program has efficiency 1.00 and execution times of 0.01–0.04s**, so speed is not a differentiator; the remaining gains are entirely in the smoothness/false-reversal dimension.

## Performance Analysis

- **Smoothness is the universal bottleneck**: every correct program scores 0.06–0.14 on smoothness with 100–330 slope changes and 100–330 false reversals, while accuracy metrics are strong (correlation 0.84–0.96). The current best (0.55) leads despite the second-worst smoothness (0.06) purely on accuracy/noise-reduction/lag strength.
- **Score trend across generations**: 0.50 (Gen 0 EWMA) → 0.51 (Gen 3 quadratic endpoint) → 0.50 (Gen 4 Kalman+hysteresis) → **0.55 (Gen 5 forward-backward Kalman, current best)** → 0.51 → 0.51 → 0.38 (Gen 8 over-engineered pipeline) → 0.52 (Gen 11 Kalman+SG). Zero-phase fusion delivered the single largest gain (+0.04 over the best alternatives).
- **Trade-off pattern**: lag error and false reversals are inversely related to smoothness in all programs — e.g., current best has lag 0.22 but 237.8 false reversals, while Gen 0 (simplest filter) has lag 0.38 with only 175.4 false reversals. The frontier program must suppress reversals without adding lag.
- **Pipeline complexity is non-monotonic in benefit**: adding stages helped once (Gen 5's backward pass) but hurt elsewhere (Gen 8's three-stage pipeline dropped to 0.38). The failures of Gen 6 and Gen 8 confirm that validation compliance and stage simplicity are prerequisites before algorithmic sophistication pays off.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

# Actionable Recommendations for Future Program Mutations

1. **Smooth the innovation-driven adaptive measurement noise with an EWMA before feeding it into r.** The current best's feedback explicitly identifies raw squared innovations as the cause of its 302.8 slope changes and 237.8 false reversals (smoothness 0.06). Replace `r = r0*0.5 + innov²*0.5` with `r = r0*0.5 + ewma_var*0.5` where `ewma_var = 0.9*ewma_var + 0.1*innov²`, keeping everything else (dual pass, velocity weights, slicing) identical. This directly targets the one remaining bottleneck without touching the accuracy machinery that already achieves correlation 0.96 and lag 0.22.

2. **Add a gentle zero-lag post-smoother on the fused output with a tuned (not negligible) smoothing strength.** Gens 3/7/9/11 all failed to improve smoothness because their post-smoothing was too weak (α ≥ 0.75, SG window ≈ 3). Apply a Savitzky-Golay filter with a moderately longer window (e.g., window_size//3, odd, order 2–3) or a two-pass forward-backward one-pole smoother (run it forward, then reversed, to cancel added lag) with α ≈ 0.4–0.6. The bidirectional application is critical to preserve the current lag error of 0.22.

3. **Replace hard velocity-weighted blending with covariance-weighted RTS-style fusion.** The current `w = 1/(1+|velocity|)` heuristic works, but the forward and backward passes already maintain covariance matrices P_f and P_b; fusing levels with weights `1/P_f[0,0]` and `1/P_b[0,0]` is the statistically optimal combination and could improve noise reduction (currently 0.70) while keeping the zero-phase property. Keep the `smooth[window_size-1:]` output convention exactly as-is to preserve validation compliance.

4. **Suppress small slope reversals with a deadband on the fused velocity, not an extra pipeline stage.** Gen 8 showed that stacking a separate slope-stabilizer stage actively degrades performance (score 0.38). Instead, add a minimal, local mechanism: recompute the fused velocity as `vel = sign(vel_f+vel_b) * max(0, |vel_f+vel_b| - threshold)` where `threshold ≈ 0.2 * std(diff(x))`, and correct the fused level slightly when the deadband activates. This targets false reversals (237.8) with a single lightweight mechanism rather than a stacked stage.

5. **Try a true RTS smoother as a third variant and compare against the blend.** Run the forward pass, then perform the proper backward RTS recursion (using stored filtered states and covariances: `G = P_f F' P_pred^{-1}`, gain-weighted correction) instead of an independent backward filter. The true RTS smoother is the minimum-variance fixed-interval estimate and may deliver better noise reduction and fewer false reversals than the current independent-backward-pass blend, at the same O(n) cost and same output slicing. Validate length (`n − window_size + 1`) explicitly to avoid the Gen 1/Gen 6 class of total failures.
