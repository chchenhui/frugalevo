## Implementation Insights

- **The best program's differentiators are all "guarded post-processing"**: correlation-guarded velocity clamp (slack doubles up to 4 attempts if corr < 0.97) and guarded sub-noise reversal damping (disabled if corr < 0.97). # Actionable Recommendations for Future Program Mutations

1. **Interpolate the core toward Gen 53's proven high-accuracy operating point while keeping the guarded suppressor stack intact.** Raise the acceleration-noise cap from `0.6·sigma_r` to ~`0.85·sigma_r` and lower the blend floor from `w_refine ≥ 0.25` to ~`0.10` (i.e., `clip(1.25 − 1.25·dyn_ratio, 0.10, 1.0)`), optionally preceded by a centered Hampel despiker (window 7, 3·local-MAD, skip if >5% of samples flagged) to mirror Gen 53's core. Gen 53 proved correlation 0.97 / noise reduction 0.81 / lag 0.28 is reachable and costs only −0.06 score through smoothness 0.28 and 36 reversals, so a partial move (correlation ~0.93) with the existing guarded clamp+damp holding reversals near 14 should net above 0.72. Do not remove the final dampers when doing this — Gen 43/52's jitter collapse (smoothness 0.17, ~70 reversals) came from responsive cores *without* strong terminal suppression.

2. **Fix the polarity flaw in `_reversal_damp`: `keep=0.25` shrinks sub-threshold flips but preserves their sign, so the output difference sequence still reverses.** Because `out[k] = out[k−1] + keep·d` retains d's sign opposite to `prev_d`, the reversal counter still sees a flip — which explains why false_reversals sat at 14.2 byte-identically across four programs with *different* suppressor stacks. Replace with true flip elimination: on a detected sub-threshold flip, hold the line (`out[k] = out[k−1]`, keep=0) or average the two conflicting steps, applied in a forward sweep plus a mirrored backward sweep for zero phase, updating `prev_d` from the pre-damp difference to avoid zero-stall; retain the corr ≥ 0.97 guard with a keep=0.5 retry if it trips.

3. **Add a guarded zero-phase curvature clamp as the missing complement to `_velocity_clamp`.** The velocity clamp bounds |Δy| ≤ 2|vel| + slack·σ_r but permits *alternating* ±limit steps — oscillation entirely within the displacement bound — which is exactly how 20.4 slope changes survive it. Clamp the second difference |y[k+1] − 2y[k] + y[k−1]| ≤ c·(robust d2 scale from the current output, c ≈ 1.5) in forward+backward sweeps with the same slack-doubling corr ≥ 0.97 retry pattern as the existing clamp; this directly bounds the derivative's oscillation rate (the stated "next real lever") without adding lag or shifting the level.

4. **Move the bilateral one-pole damper between the two RTS passes instead of running it after them.** Apply alpha ≈ 0.65–0.75 to the pass-1 output, then let the Q/4 refinement re-sharpen genuine corners the damper rounds while the removed ripple stays removed — the four-way tie at 0.72 (Gens 54/57/58/61) all varied *terminal* polish with zero metric movement, so ordering is the untested structural lever, and Gens 42/45 showed terminal damper stacking caps at 0.65–0.68. Monitor lag_error; if it exceeds 0.40, raise the inter-pass alpha toward 0.8 or gate a light alpha=0.9 final pass on residual MAD > 0.3·σ_r.

5. **Replace fixed-λ Whittaker calibration with a guarded λ-escalation loop whose stopping criterion is an internal slope-change counter.** The zero-score cluster shows the safe λ window is narrow and signal-dependent — Gen 55's weak λ gave 107.8 reversals while Gen 60's strong λ flattened the signal to where noise-floor-dominated derivatives flipped 679 times at correlation 0.00, and Gen 61's fixed λ was too weak to matter. Loop: solve Whittaker (banded, O(n), bandwidth-5 Cholesky) at λ₀, count sign flips of `np.diff(y)`, double λ while flips decrease by >5% and corr(y, pre-polish) ≥ 0.97, and keep the best iterate — the flip-count criterion halts automatically before the over-smoothing regime where flips rebound, auto-calibrating per signal.
