# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- **The iterated two-pass RTS refinement (Q/4 second pass) remains the highest-leverage design element**: the current best program (Gen 37 `dynamics_aware_blend_second_pass`, combined 0.72) retains exactly the architecture of Gen 27 — full forward Kalman storing `xp/xf/Pp/Pf`, true RTS recursion with `G = Pf[k]FᵀPp[k+1]⁻¹` + pinv fallback, then a second forward+RTS pass with `q2 = 0.25·qa`, finishing with the bidirectional one-pole damper (alpha 0.55–0.95 via tanh on residual-noise ratio). Its metrics: correlation 0.89, noise_reduction 0.55, smoothness 0.50, lag_error 0.34, only 14.2 false reversals, efficiency 1.00 at 0.05s.
- **The whole 0.68–0.72 scoring cluster shares one skeleton**: Kalman/RTS + multi-pass refinement + zero-phase damper. Gen 35 (third gated RTS pass, 0.71), Gen 40 (Whittaker with bilateral damper, 0.71), Gen 41 (velocity-clamped RTS, 0.71), Gens 33/39/42 (0.68) all confirm the pattern — every correctly-wired member of this family scores 0.68–0.72, while alternative families (fusion+deadzone, SG) sit at 0.40–0.51.
- **Data-driven robust noise statistics are validated across every top scorer**: MAD-of-first-differences for R, quadrature-subtracted acceleration variance for Q (floor 0.05σ_r, cap 0.6σ_r), covariance symmetrization `P = 0.5(P+Pᵀ)`, and 2×2 fixed-size operations keep execution at 0.05s with efficiency 1.00.
- **Diversity around the RTS core can add value but not breakthroughs**: Gen 35's convergence-gated third RTS pass (0.71), Gen 40's curvature-adaptive Whittaker solve with bilateral damper (0.71), and Gen 41's velocity clamping (0.71, smoothness 0.56 — best in cluster) all match or slightly exceed the 0.68 variants, showing the core architecture is near a plateau and remaining gains are in smoothness/noise-reduction tuning.

## Ineffective Approaches

- **Interface non-compliance is instantly fatal**: Gen 26 (`adaptive_kalman_hybrid_filter`, 0.0) never defined `process_signal()` despite the sophisticated causal-SG + Kalman hybrid underneath — a NameError zeroed everything. This repeats the Gen 18/19 pattern; interface wiring failures remain the most common total-loss mode.
- **Stacking an extra SG polish on the zero-lag smoother breaks the contract**: Gen 23 (`savgol_post_smoother`, 0.0) added a quadratic Savitzky-Golay polish after the double-RTS + bilateral one-pole stack and failed validation entirely — echoing Gen 25's collapse to negative correlation. The `y[window_size-1:]` slicing is fragile when additional smoothing distorts phase/edge structure.
- **Deadzone smoothing with carry/leak logic still fails**: Gen 34 (`ewma_adaptive_r_kalman`, 0.50) applied "20% of small steps plus carry" rather than rejecting sub-threshold steps, yielding noise_reduction 0.17, smoothness 0.17, 99.2 slope changes, 75.4 false reversals — despite high correlation (0.85) and efficiency (1.00).
- **Over-conservative damper alpha and over-adaptive noise leave residual ripple**: Gens 39/42 (0.68 each) show 24.4 false reversals and 31.6 slope changes with smoothness 0.39 — the alpha floor at 0.55 and EWMA-adaptive R over-trusting noisy segments cap their scores ~0.04 below the best. Gen 40's curvature-relaxed λ similarly leaks noise where the penalty relaxes (noise_reduction 0.36).

## Implementation Insights

- **What makes the best program (Gen 37, 0.72) effective**: (1) the dynamics-aware *blend* of the second RTS pass rather than full replacement — the 4x-reduced Q refinement is blended by a dynamics ratio, preserving genuine turns while sharpening smooth segments; (2) all work happens in the minimum-variance RTS stage rather than aggressive one-pole damping, achieving the best balance (14.2 reversals, lag 0.34, correlation 0.89); (3) all entry points (`adaptive_filter`, `enhanced_filter_with_trend_preservation`, `process_signal`) funnel through one core with the explicit `len(y) = n − window_size + 1` contract asserted.
- **Measured small mutations around the core cluster tightly**: Gen 42's lower alpha floor with correlation guard and cascade fallback still scored 0.68 — heuristic additions to the damper stage don't outperform doing more smoothing in the RTS stage itself (Gen 27/37's approach).
- **Velocity clamping is a cheap smoothness win**: Gen 41's clamp (per-step displacement ≤ 2|v| + 0.5σ) achieved the best smoothness in the cluster (0.56) at 0.71 overall with only 12 false reversals — a candidate to combine with the dynamics-blend refinement.
- **Causal SG pre-smoothing inside a Kalman (Gen 26) was never fairly tested** — its fatal NameError means the "SG endpoint-evaluated pre-filter + volatility-scaled Q + lag-compensated output" idea remains unvalidated and carries noted secondary defects (off-by-one residual alignment, window 11 vs harness's 20).

- **Score ceiling analysis**: the correct RTS-family programs span 0.68–0.72 with a tight spread. # Actionable Recommendations for Future Program Mutations

1. **Fuse Gen 41's velocity clamping into Gen 37's two-pass core — this is the highest-priority combination.** After the second RTS pass (before the bilateral damper), clamp per-step displacement using the second-pass velocity state: limit |y[k]−y[k−1]| ≤ 2·|xs[k,1]| + 0.5·σ_r, applied in a forward sweep plus a mirrored backward sweep to stay zero-phase. Guard with corr(clamped, unclamped) ≥ 0.97, doubling the 0.5σ_r slack if violated, and keep all three entry points plus the `y[window_size-1:]` slice untouched.

2. **Add a third RTS pass that is dynamics-blended, not a wholesale replacement.** Gen 35's third pass (0.71, smoothness 0.54, lag 0.37) fell 0.01 short of Gen 37 because full replacement over-smooths genuine turns; instead run a third forward+RTS cycle with q3 = 0.0625·qa (continuing the validated 4× geometric decay) and blend y ← y2 + w(k)·(y3 − y2), where w(k) = tanh-squashed ratio of local |second difference of y2| to σ_r (w→1 in noise-dominated segments, w→0 near true turns). Skip the pass entirely if max|y2 − y1| < 0.05·σ_r (convergence gate) and hard-cap at 3 passes to protect the 0.05s runtime; optionally re-derive q3 from the MAD of y2's second differences — a self-consistent, EM-style decay may beat the hardcoded 1/16 factor.

3. **Swap the final one-pole damper for a fixed-λ Whittaker–Henderson polish.** Gen 40 validated the Whittaker family at 0.71, and its only diagnosed defect was the curvature-relaxed λ leaking noise (noise_reduction 0.36) where the penalty relaxed — so use a *constant* λ = clip(25·(r_std/σ_r)², 1, 200) from the existing residual MAD, solved with a banded (bandwidth-5) O(n) Cholesky to preserve the 0.05s execution. A second-order-difference penalty has a flatter passband and sharper stopband than the cascaded one-pole, which should cut residual ripple (false reversals, slope changes) without the lag a stronger one-pole would add. Critically, apply it to the full-length signal and slice last with the `len(y) = n − window_size + 1` assertion — Gen 23's zero came precisely from a length-changing post-smoother breaking that contract.

4. **Reorder the pipeline: place the bilateral damper *between* the two RTS passes, with RTS as the final operator.** The evidence is consistent that smoothing in the minimum-variance RTS stage beats damper-stage heuristics (Gen 37's win; Gen 42's damper tweaks capped at 0.68) — so run the one-pole at moderate alpha (~0.65) between pass 1 and pass 2, then let the Q/4 refinement pass finish the signal, re-sharpening corners the damper rounded while the ripple it removed stays gone. Keep R = σ_r² and all noise statistics unchanged, and monitor lag_error — if it exceeds 0.40, back the inter-pass alpha off toward 0.75 or add a light alpha=0.9 final bilateral only when the post-pass-2 residual MAD exceeds 0.3·σ_r.

5. **Make Q locally adaptive via robust block-MAD statistics — explicitly not recursive/EWMA.** Compute σ_acc in overlapping blocks of length ≈ window_size (50% overlap) from the *first-pass smoothed output's* second differences (y1 is already denoised, so its d2 reflects genuine dynamics like the 5 Hz component a single global Q under-serves), subtract the existing (σ_r/√w)·√6 noise floor per block, floor at 0.05σ_r, raise the local cap to ~1.5σ_r, and linearly interpolate to per-sample Q[k] used at each prediction step (RTS needs no change; second pass uses 0.25·Q[k]). Gen 42's EWMA-adaptive R (0.68) shows recursive noise estimates chase transients and over-trust noisy segments — block medians are the robust counterpart, and this targets lag_error (0.34) and avg_error (0.39) by granting busy segments larger process noise while smoothing slow segments harder.
