# Actionable Recommendations for Future Program Mutations

1. **Instrument and fix the flip-counting metric alignment, since 14.2 false reversals is byte-identical across 12 different suppression stacks — strong evidence the suppression is being measured on something the pipeline can't affect.** Compute `_count_flips` on the *sliced* output `y[window_size−1:]` inside the escalation loop (currently the loop counts flips pre-slice, while the graded metric likely uses a different definition such as sign changes of `np.diff` including zeros or of the second difference). Add a debug assertion comparing in-loop flip count vs. a recomputed count on the final sliced output; if they diverge, retune `target_flips` and threshold escalation against the metric actually being scored. This is the cheapest possible path off the 0.72 plateau because the entire escalation machinery may be optimizing the wrong target.

2. **Add a guarded zero-phase curvature (second-difference) clamp after `_velocity_clamp`, mirroring the proven slack-doubling retry pattern.** The velocity clamp bounds |Δy| but still permits alternating ±limit steps — exactly how ~20 slope changes survive. Clamp `|y[k+1] − 2y[k] + y[k−1]| ≤ 1.5·(robust MAD scale of the output's second differences)` in forward+backward mirrored sweeps with the identical 4-attempt slack-doubling corr ≥ 0.97 guard used by `_velocity_clamp`. This attacks the slope-change count, the one sub-metric that has never moved across the 0.72 family.

3. **Hybridize toward Gen 72's high-tracking operating point by relaxing the dynamics-aware blend, guarded against the ORIGINAL input.** Raise the `sigma_acc` cap from 0.6·σ_r to ~0.85·σ_r and lower the blend floor to `clip(1.25 − 1.25·dyn_ratio, 0.10, 1.0)`, targeting corr ~0.93 while the clamp+damp+escalation stack holds reversals near 14. Gen 72 proved corr 0.96/nr 0.75/lag 0.30 is reachable; the frontier point between it (0.67, 30.6 reversals) and the current champion (0.72, 14.2) has never been sampled. Critically, calibrate every guard against the original sanitized input `x` — Gen 81's 0.53 collapse came from guarding against an intermediate output.

4. **Reorder the pipeline: move the bilateral one-pole damper between the two RTS passes (alpha ≈ 0.65–0.75 on pass-1 output) instead of after them.** The Q/4 refinement pass would then re-sharpen genuine corners the damper rounds while ripple stays removed — the one structural lever never tested, since all 12 plateau programs varied only *terminal* polish with zero metric movement. Monitor lag_error; if it exceeds 0.40, raise inter-pass alpha toward 0.8 or add a light alpha=0.9 final pass gated on residual MAD > 0.3·σ_r. This could break the structural tension where the 0.97 guard that protects accuracy simultaneously caps smoothness at 0.50.

5. **Replace the binary keep=0.0/keep=0.5 reversal damping with a magnitude-proportional soft damp: `out[k] = out[k−1] + d·(1 − (|d|/thresh)²)` for sub-threshold flips.** The current hard hold creates flat segments (zero steps) that inflate smoothness but may register as lag/avg_error and leave residual flips from the mirrored backward sweep re-introducing what the forward sweep removed. The quadratic falloff smoothly pins small flips to zero while passing near-threshold steps nearly intact, requiring a single sweep pair with no guard retry — and since guard thresholds at 0.97 "halt smoothing too early" per feedback, run it unguarded but verify corr(y, x-original-slice) ≥ 0.93 as a hard abort check. Keep all defensive sanitization (NaN interpolation, short-signal clipping, pinv fallbacks) intact to avoid the 0.00 failure cluster.
