<DESCRIPTION>
The current pipeline's bottleneck is noise reduction (0.61) and average error (0.37) while slope changes (29) and false reversals (23) are already very low, and lag is small (0.33). This means the smoother is being slightly under-smoothed: there is residual noise left in the output that costs accuracy without helping responsiveness. A zero-phase Savitzky–Golay (local polynomial) post-filter is the ideal complement to the RTS smoother: it fits low-order polynomials in symmetric windows, so it adds no phase delay, preserves local trends (quadratic dynamics pass through exactly), and removes white residual noise more effectively than a one-pole pass. Applying it after the bilateral one-pole pass, with a window scaled to the estimated noise level, should improve noise reduction, correlation and smoothness while leaving lag and slope-change metrics essentially unchanged. I also widen the adaptive one-pole alpha floor slightly (0.55 → 0.50) since the SG stage now handles trend-preserving denoising.
