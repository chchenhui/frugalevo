<DESCRIPTION>
Make the process noise Q locally adaptive via robust block-MAD statistics rather than a single global (or EWMA/recursive) estimate. After the first Kalman+RTS pass produces the denoised y1, compute second differences of y1 in overlapping blocks of length ≈ window_size (50% overlap). Because y1 is already denoised, its d2 reflects genuine dynamics (e.g., the 5 Hz component) that a single global Q under-serves. Per block: robust σ from MAD, subtract the (σ_r/√w)·√6 propagated noise floor, floor at 0.05·σ_r, cap at 1.5·σ_r, then linearly interpolate block values to a per-sample q_k sequence used in the second (refinement) pass's prediction step as 0.25·q_k. Busy segments get larger process noise (better tracking, lower lag_error and avg_error in oscillatory regions) while quiet segments are smoothed harder (fewer slope_changes / false_reversals). Block medians are robust to transients, unlike EWMA which chases bursts — the Gen-42 EWMA-adaptive R scored 0.68 with lag_error 0.34; the robust block counterpart targets the same objectives without over-trusting noisy segments.
