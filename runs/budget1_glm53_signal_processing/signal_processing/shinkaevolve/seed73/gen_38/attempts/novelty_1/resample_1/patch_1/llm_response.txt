<DESCRIPTION>
Replace the per-sample Kalman + Holt cascade (which incurs lag on trend segments and only achieves 0.33 noise reduction / 103 slope changes) with a vectorized Savitzky-Golay (order-2 polynomial) FIR smoother convolved with an exponentially decaying recency-weighting kernel (e^-1.5 tilt). The polynomial fit preserves local trend dynamics (low lag, good tracking), the recency tilt shifts effective weight toward the most recent samples to cut group delay, and the convolution is fully vectorized via np.convolve "valid" mode preserving the 1.00 efficiency score. A post-pass zero-phase slope gate suppresses noise-induced false reversals by detecting slope sign flips smaller than a local noise threshold and re-integrating the gated slopes. This directly targets slope_changes (103), false_reversals (85.4), and lag_error (0.42) simultaneously.</DESCRIPTION>

    # Least-squares quadratic fit over the window: polynomial order 2 preserves
    # local level + slope (trend dynamics), giving low lag and good tracking.
    half_w = max(2, window_size // 2)
    W = 2 * half_w + 1
    k = np.arange(W) - half_w  # centered offsets

    # Least-squares design matrix for polynomial order 2
    V = np.vander(k, 3, increasing=True)  # columns: 1, k, k^2
    VtV_inv = np.linalg.inv(V.T @ V)
    # Coefficients for evaluating the fit at the *last* sample (k = half_w):
    # pulls the effective weight toward recent samples (zero extra lag).
    c_last = VtV_inv @ (V.T @ (np.ones(W)))  # level coefficient row at k=half_w
    a_last, b_last, _ = (V @ c_last)  # equivalent FIR taps for endpoint eval
    # ... simpler: build endpoint evaluation directly
    # FIR taps = row of V @ VtV_inv @ V.T corresponding to k = half_w
    P_end = V @ VtV_inv @ V.T
    taps = P_end[half_w, :]  # evaluates fitted polynomial at most recent sample

    # ---- Recency tilt: exponential weighting e^{-1.5 * |lag|/half_w} ----
