<DESCRIPTION>
The metric breakdown shows a clear trade-off: the Kalman prefilter + heavier 5-tap binomial smoothing drove slope_changes down (73) and false_reversals down (60), but over-smoothed the output, dropping correlation (0.89) and accuracy vs. the lighter variants. Two targeted, low-risk mutations on the validated champion pipeline:

1. **Soften the adaptive Kalman gain** — reduce the dynamic process-noise multiplier and NIS cap so the prefilter smooths more and tracks less aggressively. This recovers tracking accuracy/correlation while the Kalman still suppresses the noise that causes reversals.

2. **Replace the 5-tap binomial convolution with a vectorized 5-tap median snap.** A median filter is the classic edge-preserving, zero-phase operator: it removes isolated spurious wiggles (directly attacking slope_changes and false_reversals) but passes monotone trends *untouched*, so — unlike the binomial kernel — it adds essentially zero lag and no amplitude bias on ramps. Vectorizing with `sliding_window_view` also removes Python-loop crash risk. Since the median preserves trends perfectly, it lets us reclaim the lag error the binomial kernel introduced.
