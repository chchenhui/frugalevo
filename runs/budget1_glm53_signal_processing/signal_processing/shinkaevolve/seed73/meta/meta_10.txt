# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns
- **Multi-stage pipeline (adaptive Kalman + Savitzky-Golay)**: The current best (Gen 4, "kalman_savgol_hybrid", combined 0.58) combines an innovation-adaptive [level, velocity] Kalman filter with NIS-scaled process noise, then Savitzky-Golay smoothing, then exponential-weighted averaging biased toward recent samples. This balances responsiveness (0.71) and accuracy (0.78) while keeping efficiency perfect (0.03s) and success rate 1.00.
- **Zero-lag endpoint evaluation**: Gen 7's robust quadratic endpoint filter (0.54) and Gen 6's weighted linear regression (0.52) show that evaluating window regressions at t=0 (most recent sample) strongly eliminates lag (0.29, 0.30 lag error) and yields top correlation (0.94, 0.92).
- **Robust MAD-based noise estimation**: Both the best program and Gen 9's pipeline use MAD of first differences to estimate measurement noise — a cheap, robust foundation that supports high correlation (0.91 for Gen 9) and efficiency.
- **Strict output-length contract compliance**: All top scorers (0.50+) maintain exactly len(x) − W + 1 output with fallback logic; every failed program (Gen 1, Gen 3, score 0.00) violated it.

## Ineffective Approaches
- **Over-aggressive adaptive gain**: Gen 4's NIS process-noise scaling (up to 10× q_base) and Gen 9's EMA-based Q adaptation let noise leak into the slope estimate — Gen 9 suffered 304.80 slope changes and 247 false reversals with smoothness of only 0.06 despite correlation 0.91.
- **Permissive trend/slope gating**: Gen 8 (0.53) gated reversals at 0.5×slope-noise std with a 3-tap median filter — insufficient, yielding 85.4 false reversals; Gen 5's 0.25·σ gate similarly failed (167.40 false reversals, smoothness 0.08).
- **Edge-padding/slicing bugs**: Gen 1 and Gen 3 scored 0.00 ("all tests failed") purely from implementation errors (wrong slice length `n − 2·half`, unsafe reflective padding `x[-2 : n - half - 2 : -1]`), despite structurally sound designs. Correctness robustness matters more than algorithmic sophistication.
- **Fixed exponential weighting without noise adaptation**: Gen 0 (0.50) over-weighted recent samples, leaving residual noise (smoothness 0.08, 175.40 false reversals).

## Implementation Insights
- **The best program's core strength is stage separation**: Kalman handles lag/adaptive tracking, Savitzky-Golay handles trend-preserving smoothing, and the final exponential-weighted window average suppresses residual lag — each weakness of a single stage is covered by the next, unlike single-mechanism approaches.
- **Noise-adaptive smoothness is the key unexploited lever**: The best program's weakest metrics are noise_reduction (0.33) and smoothness (0.33). Gen 7 shows robust Huber reweighting improved noise reduction to 0.57 (best observed), suggesting combining Gen 4's pipeline with Gen 7's robust reweighting is the natural path.
- **Light post-smoothing is insufficient**: Gen 7's 3-tap [0.25, 0.5, 0.25] smoother barely improved smoothness (0.11) while its quadratic fit overfits noise curvature; heavier inter-window consistency constraints are needed for slope churn (162.80 slope changes).
- **Efficiency is a solved problem**: All correct programs score 1.00 efficiency (0.01–0.09s); per-sample loops with precomputed sums are fast enough — optimization effort should go entirely to smoothness/noise metrics.

## Performance Analysis
- **Best vs. rest**: Gen 4 (0.58) leads Gen 7 (0.54), Gen 8 (0.53), Gen 6 (0.52), Gen 0 (0.51), Gen 9 (0.51), Gen 5 (0.42), Gen 2 (0.43); failures score 0.00. The spread among correct programs is narrow (0.42–0.58), indicating the remaining gains are marginal tuning rather than paradigm shifts.
- **Clear tradeoff pattern**: Responsiveness/accuracy vs. smoothness/noise is the dominant tension. Programs with high responsiveness (0.77–0.81: Gen 6, 9, 7) all have smoothness ≤ 0.11 and 125–304 slope changes; the best program accepts moderate responsiveness (0.71) for the best smoothness among high scorers (0.33).
- **Correlation is achievable, smoothness is not**: Most correct programs hit correlation 0.83–0.94, but smoothness never exceeds 0.33 and noise_reduction never exceeds 0.57 — these two metrics are the binding constraints on combined score.
- **Correctness gate is binary**: Two of nine programs (22%) scored 0.00 from output-length/edge-handling bugs, more than wiping out any algorithmic advantage; safe padding and length validation are prerequisites, not optimizations.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Add a robust Huber/IRLS reweighting stage to the Kalman+SG pipeline.** The best program's weakest metrics are noise_reduction (0.33) and smoothness (0.33), while Gen 7's robust quadratic filter achieved the best observed noise_reduction (0.57) via Huber reweighting. Insert an IRLS pass (2–3 iterations, MAD-based residual scale) between the Kalman stage and the Savitzky-Golay stage to downweight outlier samples before polynomial fitting, keeping the same output-length contract.

2. **Cap the NIS-based process noise scaling to reduce slope churn.** The current `q_scale = q_base * (1.0 + 10.0 * min(nis, 50.0) / 10.0)` can inflate Q up to 50×q_base, letting noise leak into the velocity state (a known failure mode from Gen 9's 304 slope changes). Try capping the multiplier at 3–5× and raising q_base slightly so genuine dynamics still track but noise-driven velocity spikes are suppressed — directly targeting the 41.2 slope changes and 36.6 false reversals.

3. **Apply a strict, hysteresis-based slope-reversal gate as a final stage.** Gen 5 (0.25·σ) and Gen 8 (0.5·σ) gates failed because they were permissive; make the gate two-sided: require the estimated slope sign change to exceed 1.0–1.5× MAD-estimated slope-noise σ AND persist for 2–3 consecutive windows before allowing a reversal, otherwise hold the previous slope direction. This suppresses false reversals (currently 36.6) without adding lag to genuine turns.

4. **Increase Savitzky-Golay smoothing strength on the trend component while preserving endpoints.** Try lowering poly_order to 2 and enlarging sg_window (e.g., 1.5–2× window_size, kept odd) — Gen 7 showed zero-lag endpoint evaluation at t=0 achieves correlation 0.94 with minimal lag (0.29). Combine with the existing exponential weighting so heavier smoothing is compensated by the recency bias, targeting smoothness beyond the current 0.33 ceiling.

5. **Vectorize the final windowing stage and add a light inter-window slope-consistency constraint.** Replace the per-output-sample exponential-weight loop with a precomputed weight vector and `np.convolve` (or stride tricks) for efficiency headroom, then spend it on a penalized smoothing: when consecutive window means differ by less than ~0.5× noise σ, snap them toward their local median (3–5 tap). This enforces inter-window consistency that Gen 7's single 3-tap smoother couldn't achieve, addressing slope churn without a paradigm change.
