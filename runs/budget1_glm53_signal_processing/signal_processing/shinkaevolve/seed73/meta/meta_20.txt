# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns
- **Multi-stage Kalman + SG pipeline remains the champion architecture**: The current best (Gen 16, "vectorize_window_median_snap", combined 0.58) keeps the adaptive Kalman → Savitzky-Golay → exponential-weighted convolution structure, and adds a 5-tap median-snap pass with a noise-scaled threshold (0.5·σ from MAD of first differences). This yielded the best smoothness among top scorers (0.33) and the lowest false reversals of any high-scoring program (36.4 vs. 60–268 for others).
- **Noise-scaled median snapping works better than slope gating**: Gen 16's approach — snap samples whose local step is < 0.5·σ toward a running median — cut false reversals to 36.4 while preserving responsiveness (0.71). This outperforms all slope-gate designs (Gen 18: 116.8 reversals; Gen 20: 93.2; Gen 11: 114.6).
- **Robust local quadratic with zero-lag endpoint evaluation delivers top accuracy**: Gen 13 ("zlag_robls_trendlock", 0.53) and Gen 17 (0.54) achieved the highest correlations (0.93) via leading-edge quadratic evaluation with Huber IRLS; Gen 15 combined this with a Kalman prefilter for 0.57 with fewer reversals (60.4) — the closest competitor to the best.
- **MAD-of-first-differences noise estimation is universal**: Every correct program scoring 0.49+ uses it as a robust, cheap foundation.

## Ineffective Approaches
- **Slope gates that catch only small deviations are useless**: Gen 18's median gate condition (`abs(slope - med) < gate`) only replaced slopes already near the median, letting extreme noise spikes through — 116.8 false reversals, smoothness 0.12. Similarly, Gen 21's elaborate hysteresis/gated-slope reconstruction actively amplified noise (lag_error 10.47, correlation 0.25, score 0.30 — a collapse from Gen 13's 0.53 baseline).
- **Fixed forward extrapolation for lag compensation trades smoothness for little gain**: Gens 11, 18 all used 2-step slope extrapolation; all three scored 0.50–0.53 with smoothness ≤ 0.12 and 93–117 false reversals. Gen 21 shows the failure mode worsens when the extrapolated slope itself is noisy.
- **Inverted gating logic crash**: Gen 12 ("zerophase_sg_trendgate") scored 0.00 from likely output-length/padding edge-case failures — a structurally similar design to the best program, confirming that correctness robustness (safe slicing, small-n handling) is a binary gate, not an optimization.
- **Single 3–5 tap post-smoothing is too weak**: Gen 17's 3-tap pass (smoothness 0.14) and Gen 15's 5-tap binomial (0.21) both confirm light smoothing cannot fix slope churn from aggressive lag removal.

## Implementation Insights
- **Vectorized convolution replaced the O(n·W) loop in the best program** — execution time 0.03s with efficiency 1.00, matching prior best while enabling the heavier median-snap stage at no cost. All correct programs run 0.03–0.13s; efficiency is saturated.
- **Level-domain snapping beats slope-domain gating**: Gen 16 snaps the *output level* toward a median when steps are noise-sized, rather than trying to gate/reintegrate slopes. Slope reconstruction schemes (Gens 18, 21) consistently reintegrate residual slope noise and cause reversals; level snapping avoids the integrator entirely.
- **The best program's weakness profile is now well-defined**: noise_reduction 0.33 and smoothness 0.33 are the binding constraints, while correlation (0.78) is actually *below* several competitors (0.93 for Gens 13/17) — heavier SG smoothing likely trades correlation away. Gen 13's robust quadratic (noise_reduction 0.59, correlation 0.93) suggests robust reweighting could raise both.
- **Median-snap threshold at 0.5·σ with 50/50 blend is the sweet spot observed**: full replacement would over-flatten; the blend preserves dynamics (responsiveness 0.71).

## Performance Analysis
- **Score distribution**: Gen 16 (0.58) > Gen 15 (0.57) > Gen 4 (0.58, previous best) > Gen 17 (0.54) > Gen 13 (0.53) > Gens 20/8 (0.53) > Gens 11/18 (0.50) > Gen 10 (0.49) > Gen 21 (0.30) > Gen 12 (0.00). Correct programs cluster in 0.49–0.58; the gap is entirely smoothness/noise-reduction tuning.
- **The reversal–smoothness axis predicts rank almost perfectly**: false reversals correlate inversely with score — 36.4 (best, 0.58), 60.4 (0.57), 99.4 (0.54), 116.8 (0.50), 268.4 (0.53 but composite dragged down). Programs with reversals < 60 all score ≥ 0.57.
- **Zero-lag leading-edge evaluation maximizes correlation (0.93) but not overall score**: Gens 13/17 lead on accuracy yet lose on smoothness (0.06–0.14). The best program accepts lower correlation (0.78) for the best smoothness balance — confirming the tradeoff identified previously, now with the threshold quantified at roughly correlation 0.85 vs. smoothness 0.33.
- **Complexity is now net-negative**: Gen 21 added hysteresis + gated reconstruction + blending to Gen 13's design and dropped it from 0.53 to 0.30. Simpler, level-domain, noise-scaled mechanisms (Gen 16) outperform elaborate slope machinery.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Add a Huber-style iterative reweighting pass between the Kalman and Savitzky-Golay stages.** Run `savgol_filter` once on `x_kalman`, compute residuals, estimate robust σ via `MAD(residual)/0.6745`, winsorize residuals to ±2.5σ (i.e., `x_adj = fit + clip(res, -2.5σ, 2.5σ)`), and re-run SG on the adjusted signal — 2 iterations, fully vectorized. This directly attacks the best program's two weakest metrics (noise_reduction 0.33, correlation 0.78), and is supported by Gen 15's robust quadratic achieving correlation 0.93 with the best noise_reduction (~0.59) at 0.57 combined.

2. **Cap the NIS-driven process-noise multiplier at 3–5× instead of the current ~51×.** The existing `q_scale = q_base * (1.0 + 10.0 * min(nis, 50.0) / 10.0)` can inflate Q to 51×q_base, leaking noise into the velocity state (the likely source of the 41 slope changes). Replace with something like `q_scale = q_base * min(1.0 + nis / 10.0, 4.0)` and raise `q_base` from 0.01 to ~0.02 so genuine turns still track — this targets false reversals (36.4), the metric that predicts rank almost perfectly (all programs under 60 reversals score ≥ 0.57).

3. **Strengthen the SG stage: lower `poly_order` to 2 and enlarge `sg_window` to 2×window_size−1 (kept odd), while steepening the exponential recency weighting to `np.exp(np.linspace(-1.5, 0.0, window_size))` to compensate the added group delay.** Correlation is 0.78 versus the 0.93 achieved by Gens 13/17, so there is measurable headroom to trade accuracy for the binding smoothness (0.33) and noise_reduction (0.33) constraints. The recency-weighted convolution is the proven lag-compensation mechanism here — unlike slope extrapolation, which consistently failed (Gens 11, 18, 21).

4. **Make the median-snap threshold locally adaptive instead of global.** Replace the single MAD-of-diffs σ with a rolling robust σ (e.g., MAD over ±25-sample blocks of `x_smooth` diffs, computable vectorized via `sliding_window_view`), so noisy non-stationary segments get aggressive snapping while clean segments keep their dynamics. Also sweep the proven sweet spot's neighborhood — threshold 0.5σ → 0.75σ and blend 0.5 → 0.6 — since responsiveness (0.71) has margin and the current settings were the best of only a small explored set.

5. **Add a second snap point: apply the same 5-tap, noise-scaled median snap to `x_smooth` *before* the exponential convolution, keeping the existing post-convolution snap.** Removing noise-sized steps prior to window averaging compounds the smoothing effect on the output (targeting smoothness 0.33) at negligible cost, since efficiency is saturated (1.00, 0.03s). This stays entirely within the level-domain, noise-scaled mechanism that beat all slope-gating designs (Gens 18, 21), respecting the lesson that added slope machinery is net-negative.
