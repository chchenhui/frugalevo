# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- **Gen 78 (wavelet_trend_fusion + bidirectional Kalman pre-denoise, 0.69) is the new champion and breaks the 0.63–0.64 Kalman+SG family ceiling.** Its core architecture — MAD noise estimate → bidirectional capped-NIS constant-velocity Kalman pre-denoise → à-trous B3-spline wavelet decomposition with per-scale adaptive soft-thresholding → robust local-linear (Theil-Sen-style) trend fusion → predictive edge-corrected quadratic smoothing — is a validated alternative to the entire SG/tilt/snap family. Two variants confirm it: Gen 71's pure wavelet version scored 0.68 and Gen 78's Kalman-prefiltered version 0.69.
- **The Kalman pre-denoise stage is worth +0.01 and structural**: Gen 78 differs from Gen 71 mainly by the bidirectional capped-NIS Kalman pre-pass, improving lag (0.38→0.37) and false reversals (17.8→17.4) while holding correlation (0.88→0.89). The Kalman raises SNR before wavelet thresholding, calibrating per-scale thresholds better (noise σ is still estimated from the *raw* input, keeping thresholds honest).
- **Zero-phase per-scale wavelet thresholding is the first mechanism in the family to dent noise_reduction (0.37) and smoothness (0.50)** — the structural hole identified earlier (all Kalman+SG programs frozen at noise_reduction 0.31–0.34, smoothness 0.41–0.49) is partially filled by scale-selective soft-thresholding rather than more snapping stages.
- **Per-scale adaptivity (noise-dominated scales hard-shrunk 0.6×, transitional soft-thresholded, signal-bearing passed through) preserves tracking while suppressing churn**, yielding 17.4 reversals — the batch's best — at correlation 0.89, the batch's best.

## Ineffective Approaches

- **Slope-domain reversal suppression catastrophically fails (reconfirmed)**: Gen 72's slope-gated reversal suppression + 0.5×velocity lag compensation produced 194.4 false reversals and 244 slope changes, scoring 0.48 despite good correlation (0.86). This is the fourth confirmation that slope-domain mechanisms cannot substitute for level-domain noise removal.
- **More snap stages still ≠ fewer reversals**: Gens 76 (26 reversals, 0.62) and 77 (18.4, 0.64) with hysteresis thresholds (0.45, k=1.5 MAD) remain mediocre; Gen 75's 8-stage variant crashed entirely on `np.median(windows5, axis=0)` instead of `axis=1`, scoring 0.00 — a single axis-parameter bug in a windowed median invalidated the whole pipeline.
- **The Kalman+SG family has plateaued at 0.64**: Gens 73, 77, 79, 80 all scored 0.64 with essentially identical metrics (correlation 0.75–0.79, noise_reduction 0.31–0.33, 18–21 reversals). Parametric co-sweeps of (2W+7 window, e^-1.2 tilt) and snap thresholds are exhausted — the plateau's cause is architectural, not parametric.
- **Zero-lag leading-edge extrapolation remains risky but controlled**: Gen 78's predictive edge evaluation at t_eval = 0.5×half blended 50/50 with the center value keeps over-extrapolation churn in check; contrast with earlier generations (57, 64) where uncontrolled leading-edge regression collapsed smoothness.

## Implementation Insights

- **Tail-aligned output (y = x_final[n - out_len:]) is a lag-reduction mechanism the SG family lacks**: instead of recency-weighted convolution to recover group delay, Gen 78 simply takes the most recent out_len samples of a zero-phase result. Combined with à-trous's inherent zero phase, this yields lag_error 0.37 vs 0.43–0.47 for the tilt-compensated family.
- **The à-trous fast variant (strided slice gathering) preserves O(n) efficiency**: execution_time 0.01s, efficiency 1.00, matching the best vectorized SG pipelines.
- **Pre-denoising with raw-input noise calibration is the key design insight**: Gen 78 computes σ from the raw signal *before* the Kalman pass, keeping wavelet thresholds calibrated to true noise rather than the already-denoised signal's residual — this avoids over-shrinking signal-bearing scales.
- **Scale selection tied to window size** (n_levels = clip(log2(W), 1, 6), coarsest approximation spans ~W) ties the wavelet decomposition to the output contract rather than an arbitrary level count.

## Performance Analysis

- **Updated ranking**: Gen 78 (0.69, champion) > Gen 71 (0.68) = Gen 74 (0.68) > Gen 73/77/79/80 (0.64, Kalman+SG family) > Gen 76 (0.62) > Gen 72 (0.48) > Gen 75 (0.00, crash). The wavelet family holds the top two spots.
- **Gen 74 (0.68) shows the SG family's ceiling**: heavy SG stacking (2W+11 then coarser window) with trend-lock damping achieved 12.6 reversals (lowest of the batch) but only 0.34 noise_reduction and 0.75 correlation — it buys smoothness by over-smoothing dynamics, the opposite trade from Gen 78 which keeps 0.89 correlation at 0.37 noise_reduction.
- **Correlation is now a differentiator**: wavelet programs hold 0.88–0.89 correlation vs 0.75–0.80 for the SG family; combined with competitive reversal counts (17–18 vs 18–21), this explains the +0.05 score gap.

False reversals remain the strongest rank predictor across the whole batch: 17.4 → 0.69; 194 → 0.48.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

# Recommendations for Future Program Mutations

1. **Add a hard-shrink dead zone for noise-dominated scales (target noise_reduction 0.37, the champion's weakest metric)**. In `_threshold_details`, change the noise-dominated branch (mag < 0.5) from `soft-threshold × 0.6` to full hard-thresholding (`c_t[c < thr] = 0.0` via `c_t = np.sign(c) * np.maximum(np.abs(c) - thr, 0.0); c_t *= 0.0` for coefficients below the threshold, i.e., zero them entirely). Gen 78's mild 0.6× shrink leaves residual noise in fine scales; since the wavelet is zero-phase and signal-bearing scales pass untouched, correlation (0.89) should survive while residual variance drops. Sweep the hard-shrink zone boundary (mag < 0.4 vs 0.5) as an atomic pair with the 0.75 universal-threshold multiplier (try 0.85).

2. **Co-sweep the trend-fusion blend and Kalman pass strength as a matched pair**: currently `x_fused = 0.6*x_wav + 0.4*x_trend` with a single bidirectional Kalman pass. Try 0.5/0.5 fusion with a second (weaker, q_base = 0.005×r) bidirectional Kalman pre-pass, or 0.7/0.3 fusion with no second pass. The Kalman pre-stage was worth +0.01 structurally (Gen 71→78), so testing whether the fusion stage wants more or less smoothed input is the natural next co-sweep — but move both knobs together, per the family's repeated lesson that window/tilt-style pairs moved independently turn destructive.

3. **Target smoothness (0.50, second-weakest metric) via a variance-suppression stage after reconstruction, not more smoothing stages**: after `x_wav` reconstruction and before `_local_linear_trend`, apply an edge-padded 3-tap running median (`sliding_window_view` + `np.median(sw, axis=1)` — **always axis=1**; Gen 75's 0.00 crash came from exactly this axis bug) blended 0.5/0.5 with `x_wav`. A 3-tap median kills isolated single-sample spikes that survive soft-thresholding at zero phase cost, unlike the SG family's failed snap-stage stacking (Gen 64: three snap stages → 85 reversals). Keep n≥5 and odd-length guards identical to existing stages.

4. **Test per-scale sigma calibration instead of the unit-gain approximation**: replace `sigma_j = noise_std` with the correct à-trous gain, `sigma_j = noise_std * np.sqrt(np.sum((kernel_taps_at_scale)**2))` computed from the dilated B3 kernel at each level (or empirically: `sigma_j = MAD(c_j)/0.6745` for the finest scale, propagated). Correct per-scale thresholds let higher (coarser) scales use proportionally tighter thresholds, which typically shrinks more noise from mid scales — directly attacking noise_reduction — without touching signal-bearing-scale logic that holds correlation at 0.89.

5. **Do NOT change the architecture's core ordering or the raw-input noise calibration, and protect the correctness surface**. The validated invariants are: σ from *raw* input before the Kalman pre-pass (keeps thresholds honest, per Gen 78's key design insight), `n_levels = clip(log2(W), 1, 6)`, tail-aligned output `x_final[n - out_len:]` (the lag-reduction mechanism yielding 0.37 lag), and the 50/50 blend of predictive-edge and center evaluation in Stage 5 (Gen 57/64 showed uncontrolled leading-edge regression collapses smoothness). Any new stage must be validated on short inputs (n = window_size, n = window_size+1) before integration; the sliding-window median axis and the pad-offset arithmetic in `_atrous_decompose_fast` are the two highest-risk lines — a single wrong offset or axis zeroes the score (Gen 75).

**Common thread**: extend the wavelet family's winning trade (high correlation + low reversals via scale-selective suppression), pushing the two weak metrics — noise_reduction and smoothness — with dedicated variance-suppression mechanisms at zero phase, never slope-domain logic or endpoint extrapolation beyond the validated 0.5×half blend.
