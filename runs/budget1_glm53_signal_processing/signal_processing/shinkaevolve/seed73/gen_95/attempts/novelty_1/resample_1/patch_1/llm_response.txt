<DESCRIPTION>
The noise sigma used by the two slope-consistency passes is currently estimated from `x_smooth`, which has already passed through two Kalman passes and two SG passes. By that point the residual differences are tiny, so `sigma` is far below the true measurement-noise scale, making the snap/lock thresholds too tight to catch noise-induced slope churn (slope_changes=14.6, false_reversals=12.8). Estimating sigma robustly from the raw despiked input instead (MAD of first differences / sqrt(2)) yields a threshold calibrated to the actual noise level, so the median-snap and trend-lock passes can suppress a much larger fraction of spurious reversals without harming genuine trend changes (which still exceed the threshold). This directly attacks slope_changes and false_reversals while leaving lag and correlation intact.
</DESCRIPTION>
