# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- **Continuation from smooth to sharp extrema objectives consistently produced near-optimal packings.** The current best, **Generation 8 / Multi-Start Softmin Diameter Packing**, reached the maximum **combined score 1.00** and `min_max_ratio = 0.24` by annealing the soft-extrema parameter from `beta=5` to `120` during 8,000 global-search steps, then from `120` to `600` in a 2,200-step contact-polishing phase. This directly addresses the nonsmooth transitions between active closest and farthest pairs.

- **Independent deterministic restarts materially improved robustness against local optima.** The best program uses **12 seeded starts**, retaining the highest exact squared-distance ratio after full optimization. Other strong restart-based approaches confirm the pattern: Generation 3 used 12 starts and reached **0.99 / 0.24**, while Generation 4 used 18 starts and also reached **0.99 / 0.24**. The feedback repeatedly attributes high performance to escaping symmetric or contact-graph local optima.

- **Explicit removal of translation and scale directions is shared by every high-scoring point-packing method.** The current best centers the points and RMS-normalizes after every Adam update. Generation 2 instead uses zero-centroid and unit-diameter normalization and achieved **0.98 / 0.23**; Generation 7 uses diameter normalization and obtained **0.99 / 0.24**. Maintaining a canonical scale lets the optimizer devote updates to configuration shape rather than irrelevant translations or uniform expansion.

- **A dedicated sharp final refinement improves peak quality.** The current best’s 2,200-step high-beta polish distinguishes it from otherwise similar methods. Generation 3, which also included high-beta polishing, achieved **0.99**, while Generation 12 combined continuation with accepted exact-ratio polish and achieved **0.98 / 0.24**. This indicates that the final contact structure benefits from a more exact approximation than the broad global-search objective.

## Ineffective Approaches

- **Unstructured Gaussian sampling is dramatically insufficient.** Generation 0, **Seeded Random 3D Point Generation**, merely generated a fixed `(14, 3)` Gaussian cloud and scored only **0.02** with `min_max_ratio = 0.00`. Validation correctness and deterministic seeding alone do not improve the geometric packing objective; the absence of distance-aware optimization was the decisive limitation.

- **Restricting the geometry to antipodal line pairs underperformed unrestricted point packing.** Generation 1, **Deterministic Antipodal Line Packing**, used seven lines plus antipodes and substantial optimization—80 restarts with staged softmax refinement—but reached only **combined score 0.88** and `min_max_ratio = 0.21`. The imposed antipodal structure reduces the search space but also excludes stronger unrestricted 14-point configurations, which the `0.24` point-packers found.

- **Less extensive or less sharply resolved optimization tended to plateau around `0.23`.** Generation 5 performed 12 restarts and 2,200 Adam iterations with a beta schedule of 5 to 50, achieving **0.97 / 0.23**. Generation 2 similarly reached **0.98 / 0.23**. Their strong but lower ratios relative to the best program are consistent with insufficiently sharp late-stage treatment of the true closest/farthest contacts.

## Implementation Insights

- **The current best computes gradients over all 91 unordered pairs efficiently and correctly.** It precomputes `iu, ju = np.triu_indices(n, 1)`, forms pair displacements in one indexed operation, obtains squared distances with `einsum`, and scatters pair forces back to vertices with `np.add.at`. This vectorized all-pairs structure supports repeated optimization while correctly accumulating each pair’s equal-and-opposite force.

- **The optimized objective is scale invariant in log-distance space.** The best program uses weights from softmin and softmax of `log_dist = 0.5 * log(dsq)`, and its pair force is proportional to `(w_min - w_max) * delta / dsq`. Near pairs receive repulsion through `w_min`; far pairs receive contraction through `w_max`. This is a smooth surrogate for maximizing `log(min distance) - log(max distance)`, matching the evaluated min/max-distance criterion.

- **Adam stabilization is important at contact switches.** In the best implementation, separate first and second moments are maintained during both the broad search and final polish, then reset before polishing. The comments and evaluation feedback identify Adam as more stable than raw force updates when the identity of active shortest-pair contacts changes. This implementation choice aligns with the best score despite the highly nonsmooth underlying maximin objective.

- **Exact scoring is reserved for selecting completed candidates rather than differentiating through hard extrema.** After each restart and polish, the current program computes the exact squared ratio `dsq.min() / dsq.max()` and saves only the best configuration. This combines stable smooth optimization internally with exact evaluation externally. Generation 7’s periodic exact-score tracking and Generation 12’s exact-objective acceptance polish similarly support strong final results.

## Performance Analysis

- **The score progression shows that optimization quality, not just validity, dominates results.** The baseline random configuration moved from **0.02 / 0.00** in Generation 0 to **0.88 / 0.21** under structured antipodal optimization in Generation 1, then to **0.97–0.99 / 0.23–0.24** under unrestricted annealed point-packing searches. The current best reached **1.00 / 0.24**, confirming that the best-performing family is unrestricted, multi-start, normalized soft-extrema optimization.

- **The current best improves score slightly over several faster near-equivalents, at a substantial runtime cost.** Generation 8 requires **9.38 s**, versus Generation 7’s **1.64 s** for **0.99 / 0.24**, Generation 4’s **3.45 s** for **0.99 / 0.24**, and Generation 6’s **3.90 s** for **0.99 / 0.24**. Thus, its 12 × (8,000 + 2,200) iteration schedule and high-beta polish provide the evaluated maximum score, but the numerical ratio is reported at the same rounded `0.24` level as multiple faster methods.

- **The distinction between `0.23` and `0.24` tracks objective resolution and final contact handling.** Generation 2 (`0.98 / 0.23`) and Generation 5 (`0.97 / 0.23`) used annealed smooth objectives but had comparatively lighter or lower-beta schedules. Programs with sharper late optimization—Generation 3, 4, 6, 7, 8, and 12—consistently reached reported `0.24` ratios.

- **Maximum combined score was not determined by runtime alone.** The 9.38-second current best achieved **1.00**, but the similarly structured Generation 3 took **6.13 s** and achieved **0.99**, while Generation 7 achieved **0.99** in only **1.64 s** through hybrid random and structured seeds plus focused refinement. The evaluation therefore rewards the marginal packing-quality gain from the current best’s longer continuation and contact-polish process, even though several alternatives are much more time-efficient.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Add an exact-ratio accepted-move polish after the existing \(\beta=600\) phase.** Starting from each polished candidate, repeatedly propose small tangent-space perturbations (zero-center and RMS-normalize afterward) and accept only moves that improve the exact `dsq.min() / dsq.max()` ratio; adapt the perturbation scale downward when no moves are accepted. Generation 12 indicates that combining continuation with an exact-objective acceptance stage can improve final contact handling without differentiating through hard extrema.

2. **Use a mixed seed portfolio rather than twelve purely Gaussian starts.** Retain several random normalized starts, but seed additional restarts from perturbed high-symmetry configurations such as icosahedral-like subsets, staggered rings, previously successful saved configurations, and antipodal-pair configurations with independently perturbed partners. Generation 7’s hybrid random/structured initialization reached `0.24` much faster, while the poor antipodal-only result shows these should be seeds for unrestricted optimization rather than a permanent constraint.

3. **Allocate optimization effort adaptively using intermediate exact scores.** Run all starts through a shorter shared global schedule—for example 2,000–3,000 steps through moderate beta—evaluate exact ratios, then spend the full 8,000-plus-polish budget only on the top 3–5 candidates. This preserves the current method’s restart robustness and sharp refinement while freeing runtime for either more diverse starts or additional exact polishing of configurations that have already demonstrated promising contact structure.

4. **Introduce intermittent contact-graph refinement at high beta.** During the final continuation, periodically identify a small set of near-minimum and near-maximum pairs, then perform several steps using a restricted objective focused on equalizing shortest-contact distances while preventing diameter-pair expansion. The current soft-extrema objective already naturally emphasizes these pairs; an explicit contact-set phase may resolve the remaining small discrepancy between near-optimal `0.99` solutions and the current maximum-score result.

5. **Use beta-dependent optimizer settings and restart Adam moments when the active pair distribution becomes sharp.** Keep the current broad-phase Adam settings, but at selected beta thresholds—such as 50, 120, and 300—reset moments and reduce learning rate more aggressively, potentially increasing `beta2` toward `0.9995` in the highest-beta stage. The best program already benefits from resetting moments before polish, and extending that stabilization to intermediate sharpness transitions may prevent stale momentum from pushing the configuration away from newly active closest/farthest contacts.
