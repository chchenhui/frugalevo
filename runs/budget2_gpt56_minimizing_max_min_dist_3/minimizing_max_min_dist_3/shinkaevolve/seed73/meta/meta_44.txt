# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- **Evaluator-aligned hard-objective finishing is the most consistently successful pattern.** The current best program, Generation 35 *“Multi-Stage 14-Point Distance Ratio Optimization,”* reached `min_max_ratio = 0.24` and `combined_score = 1.00` by using smooth optimization for basin discovery but accepting its 650-round local-polish proposals only when `exact_ratio(points)` improved. Its final epigraph SLSQP problem—scale the shortest squared distance to one, then minimize the maximum squared distance—directly represents the scored objective. Generations 41, 42, and 43 independently confirmed this pattern: all used soft batched search followed by SLSQP epigraph polish and also reached `0.24` / `1.00`.

- **Unrestricted normalized 14-point configurations outperform imposed antipodal structure.** Generation 35 and the newer Generations 40–43 all optimize 14 freely placed 3D points, repeatedly removing translation and scale through centering and normalization. Every one reached the scoring ceiling. In contrast, Generation 30’s seven forced antipodal pairs reached only `0.21` and `0.88`, while Generation 33’s antipodal method was noncompetitive at `0.00`. The evaluation evidence continues to favor unrestricted contact graphs over symmetry-constrained ones.

- **Staging smooth-to-sharp objectives effectively handles the nonsmooth ratio landscape.** Generation 35 uses 8,000 broad annealed softmin/softmax Adam steps (`beta` from 5 to 120), then 2,200 sharper steps (`beta` from 120 to 600), before hard-ratio polishing. Generation 40 follows the same broad principle with annealed soft objectives, high-beta polishing, then active-contact local search, achieving `0.24` / `1.00`. Generations 41 and 43 show that a shorter batched continuation schedule plus exact polish is also enough to find top-quality basins.

- **Diverse initializations improve robustness without requiring one specific seed family.** Generation 35 uses 12 deterministic Gaussian restarts and succeeds. Generations 41 and 43 combine random, ring/staggered-ring, and icosahedral-derived seeds, retain elites, perturb them, and also succeed at `1.00`. This expands the prior conclusion: multiple basins must be explored, but the data do not establish that structured seeds are intrinsically better than sufficiently numerous reproducible random restarts.

## Ineffective Approaches

- **Forced antipodal symmetry remains the clearest quality limitation.** Generation 30 used Riemannian Adam, annealing, tangent noise, and exact-coherence polish, but its antipodal seven-line representation achieved only `min_max_ratio = 0.21` and `combined_score = 0.88`. Generation 33 used a related antipodal spherical-packing formulation and received `0.00`. The contrast with the unrestricted `0.24` solutions indicates that the restricted geometry prevents the contact structures found by the top programs.

- **Smooth surrogate optimization without a sufficiently strong exact constrained finish is less reliable.** The successful methods all retain an evaluator-aligned final mechanism: Generation 35 has exact-ratio acceptance and two SLSQP passes; Generations 41–43 use epigraph SLSQP; Generation 40 uses active-contact hard-ratio search. Generation 33’s soft log-sum-exp coherence pipeline did include exact acceptance, but its restricted antipodal formulation still failed to generate a scorer-competitive configuration. Smooth objectives are useful for direction and basin selection, not by themselves evidence of final metric quality.

- **Execution correctness is a prerequisite for any geometric optimization benefit.** Generation 34 scored `0.00` because `-long_mask[:, None]` applied unary negation to a Boolean array and failed before validation. Its batched continuation and contact-polish design were never evaluated. This remains a concrete example that low-level vectorized-array details can entirely dominate outcome.

## Implementation Insights

- **Generation 35’s separation of gradient computation from candidate acceptance is especially effective.** `soft_gradient` computes all 91 pairwise squared distances, derives softmin weights over short pairs and softmax weights over long pairs, then accumulates pair forces through `np.add.at`. This gives smooth, dense directional information. However, in the hard-polish phase, every trial—force-only, force-plus-noise, and single-point perturbation—is retained only when the literal minimum/maximum squared-distance ratio improves. This explains why finite-beta errors do not displace the true champion.

- **Repeated canonicalization removes irrelevant degrees of freedom and stabilizes search.** The current best recenters and RMS-normalizes after every Adam update and every local trial. Generations 40–43 also center and normalize configurations as part of their successful pipelines. Because translation and uniform scale leave the ratio unchanged, eliminating those null directions focuses updates on shape changes and makes configurations comparable across restarts, elite candidates, and SLSQP endpoints.

- **The current best’s local polish is contact-aware but not overly rigid.** In each of 650 rounds, Generation 35 evaluates a pure soft-contact-force step, three normalized tangent-noise variants, and two single-point moves. Its adaptive proposal scale expands after exact improvement and contracts after batches without improvement. Generation 40’s successful active-contact-force stage supports the same observation: local refinement needs both pressure on active short/long contacts and enough perturbation freedom for contact families to exchange.

- **Analytic epigraph constraints make the SLSQP phase targeted and validated.** Generation 35 supplies analytic Jacobians for both `q_ij >= lower` and `D - q_ij >= 0`, runs a near-feasible pass followed by a strict lower-bound pass, checks finite output, and rejects endpoints whose shortest squared distance is materially infeasible. Generations 41–43 likewise demonstrate that exact pairwise inequality constraints plus diameter minimization are reliable. The endpoint rescaling and exact-ratio comparison prevent numerical SLSQP artifacts from replacing a valid pre-polish result.

## Performance Analysis

- **Generation 35 remains a quality reference but is no longer the runtime reference.** It achieved the top observed quality, `0.24` and `1.00`, in `7.66 s`. Generation 40 also achieved the same ceiling but required `10.31 s`, making it slower despite its active-contact design. Generation 42 likewise reached `1.00` in `9.71 s`. These results show that long multi-restart, multi-stage, contact-polish pipelines are effective but not necessary for the observed score ceiling.

- **Generation 41 is the strongest speed-quality result among the newly reported programs.** *“Batched Soft Packing with SLSQP Polish”* reached `min_max_ratio = 0.24`, `combined_score = 1.00`, in only `1.52 s`, substantially faster than Generation 35’s `7.66 s`. Its batched Adam search, elite retention/perturbation, diverse seeds, and exact SLSQP epigraph stage delivered the same measured score with roughly one-fifth of the evaluation time.

- **Generation 43 reinforces that population-style search can retain top quality at moderate cost.** It reached `0.24` / `1.00` in `3.60 s`, faster than Generation 35, Generation 40, and Generation 42, though slower than Generation 41. Its result supports the broader trend that structured-seed portfolio search plus batched smooth optimization and exact epigraph refinement can reduce runtime while preserving the final ratio.

- **The principal score divide is formulation freedom and exact refinement, rather than optimizer brand or search budget.** Generation 35’s 12-restart sequential Adam plus exact local/SLSQP finish, Generation 40’s contact-force pipeline, Generation 41’s batched elite search, Generation 42’s deterministic multistage search, and Generation 43’s hybrid population optimization all reached `1.00`. Meanwhile, antipodal methods remained below the ceiling or failed entirely. Across the evaluated evidence, unrestricted normalized geometry plus an exact hard-objective or epigraph-polished final stage correlates much more strongly with `0.24` than whether the global optimizer is Adam, batched Adam, contact-force search, or a portfolio evolution scheme.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Replace the fixed 12-restart, 10,200-step schedule with batched successive halving while preserving the same soft-gradient and exact-ratio logic.** Start 20–24 unrestricted normalized seeds, run a shared broad continuation of roughly 1,500–2,500 Adam steps, rank all candidates by `exact_ratio`, retain the best 4–6, and only then run the current sharp-beta and hard-acceptance stages. Generation 41 reached the same `0.24` ceiling in 1.52 s with batched elite selection, suggesting this can substantially reduce the current program’s 7.66 s runtime without sacrificing basin coverage.

2. **Use a diversified but fully unrestricted seed portfolio instead of Gaussian-only restarts.** Keep several deterministic Gaussian starts, but add jittered staggered rings, perturbed icosahedral-like 12-point layouts with two independent extra points, and copies of early elite configurations with centered RMS-normalized noise. Let every seed evolve as 14 independent points immediately—do not impose antipodal or other permanent symmetry—because the successful `0.24` programs consistently benefit from unrestricted geometry while antipodal parameterizations underperform.

3. **Insert periodic exact-ratio tournaments during the sharp Adam phase rather than waiting until the 650-round final polish.** Every 50–100 high-beta steps, generate a small proposal set from the current Adam iterate, a normalized contact-force move, one or two tangent-noise moves, and a targeted single-point move; continue from whichever candidate has the best literal `exact_ratio`. This extends Generation 35’s strongest design principle—surrogate gradients generate proposals, but the hard evaluator chooses survivors—to a stage where active min/max contacts can still change.

4. **Make the exact-acceptance local polish explicitly active-set adaptive.** At each polish round, identify pairs within relative bands of the current minimum and maximum squared distances, accumulate separating forces from short contacts and contracting forces from long contacts, then vary their mixing weight according to which contact family is currently limiting improvement. Adapt the contact-band width, noise amplitude, and probability of single-point moves based on accepted improvements and recent stalls; this builds on the current contact-aware polish while avoiding its fixed “one force, three noisy, two local” proposal mix.

5. **Strengthen the SLSQP epigraph finish with repaired feasible warm starts and multi-candidate retention.** After each SLSQP pass, rescale returned coordinates so `min(q)=1`, set `D=max(q)`, explicitly verify all `1 <= q_ij <= D` constraints within tolerance, and use that repaired state as the next pass’s initialization rather than passing the raw optimizer state unchanged. Retain the original best configuration, relaxed-pass repair, strict-pass repair, and optionally the top few pre-SLSQP elite candidates according to feasible `exact_ratio`, since the epigraph formulation is evaluator-aligned but numerical endpoints should never replace a valid hard-score champion.
