## Implementation Insights

- **Generation 35 separates gradient generation from optimization acceptance in a technically effective way.** Its `soft_gradient` computes all 91 pairwise squared distances vectorially, derives softmin weights for short distances and softmax weights for long distances, and accumulates pairwise forces with `np.add.at`. - **Canonicalization is integral to the current best program’s numerical behavior.** Generation 35 recenters and RMS-normalizes after every Adam update and every trial proposal. # META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Replace the fixed 12 × 10,200-step smooth-search budget with exact-ratio successive halving.** Run 18–24 diversified unrestricted seeds for a shorter broad phase (for example 1,800–2,500 steps), rank every candidate by `exact_ratio`, retain the top 4–6, and give only those survivors the current high-beta phase and exact-acceptance polish. This preserves Generation 35’s successful surrogate-gradient basin discovery while adopting the faster selection strategy validated by Generation 36 and reducing time spent on inferior contact graphs.

2. **Expand the restart portfolio with structured but non-antipodal initial configurations.** Keep Gaussian starts, but add jittered staggered-ring seeds, 12-point near-icosahedral seeds plus two independently placed points, and perturbed copies of the best early exact-ratio candidate. Generations 37 and 39 show that mixing seed families improves basin coverage, while the poor antipodal results indicate these templates should remain fully unrestricted after initialization.

3. **Introduce periodic exact-ratio tournament selection during the late Adam continuation, not only after it.** Every 50–100 high-beta steps, evaluate a small batch consisting of the Adam iterate, a normalized contact-force step, several tangent-noise variants, and one or two single-point proposals; replace the current iterate only with an exact-ratio improvement. This extends the current program’s strongest principle—smooth forces provide proposals, hard `min(q)/max(q)` chooses survivors—to the stage where the active contact graph is still evolving.

4. **Make final contact polishing active-set adaptive rather than using a fixed mixture of proposal types.** Compute near-minimum and near-maximum pair sets using a relative contact band, accumulate each point’s separation and contraction forces, and select force mixing based on whether short or long contacts dominate the current bottleneck. Retain strict exact-ratio acceptance, but adapt contact width, noise level, and local-move probability from recent accepted improvements; this should improve exchanges among nearly tied contacts more efficiently than the current fixed three noisy and two local trials.

5. **Strengthen the two-pass SLSQP epigraph finish with feasibility-aware warm starts and candidate retention.** After the relaxed first solve, explicitly rescale to `min(q)=1`, recompute the epigraph variable as `max(q)`, and use that repaired feasible state as the initial point for a tighter second solve; retain both the pre-polish and each pass’s repaired result based on feasible exact ratio. The current constrained formulation is already evaluator-aligned and repeatedly validated by Generations 32, 35, and 38, so improving endpoint feasibility and warm-start quality is a high-value refinement without changing the successful core geometry.
