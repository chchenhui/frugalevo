# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- **The current best program, Generation 17 / “Multi-Stage 14-Point Distance Optimizer,” combines three complementary objective regimes and reaches the maximum score efficiently.** Its 10-restart annealed soft log-distance search reaches broad promising basins, SLSQP then solves the equivalent fixed-minimum-distance/diameter-minimization formulation for the top three candidates, and final accepted-move hill climbing optimizes the evaluator’s exact `min(d²)/max(d²)` metric. This produced `min_max_ratio = 0.24`, combined score `1.00`, in `3.49 s`.

- **Smooth continuation followed by a sharper or exact contact stage remains the strongest recurring design.** Generation 17 anneals `beta` from `8` to `528` across 4,600 steps, then uses a highly sharp `beta=900` active-contact force during exact-ratio local search. This extends the previously identified pattern from Generation 8’s long soft-to-sharp schedule and is consistent with other perfect-score programs: Generation 14 (`1.00`, `0.24`, `2.57 s`), Generation 15 (`1.00`, `0.24`, `8.77 s`), and Generation 19 (`1.00`, `0.24`, `10.57 s`).

- **Constrained diameter polishing is a particularly effective bridge between smooth search and the true packing geometry.** Generation 17 fixes all pairwise squared distances at or above one, minimizes a scalar diameter bound, and preserves zero centroid. This mirrors the successful SLSQP formulations in Generation 10 (`1.00`, `0.24`, `7.43 s`) and Generation 18 (`1.00`, `0.24`, `5.64 s`), while avoiding reliance on constrained optimization alone by feeding it only strong soft-optimization candidates.

- **Deterministic multi-start search continues to provide robustness without requiring very large restart counts.** The current program uses 10 seeded starts and retains the best exact-ratio candidates before polishing. This achieved the same maximum score as 12–14 restart alternatives, including Generation 13’s 13-start method (`1.00`, `0.24`, `9.21 s`) and Generation 18’s 14-start method (`1.00`, `0.24`, `5.64 s`), suggesting that staged refinement quality can offset a modestly smaller restart budget.

## Ineffective Approaches

- **Unstructured random point generation remains decisively inadequate.** The earlier Generation 0 Gaussian-cloud baseline scored only `0.02` with a near-zero reported ratio. Deterministic seeding and valid output shape were not enough; no mechanism existed to alter the closest and farthest pair structure.

- **Hard antipodal symmetry materially limits solution quality.** Generation 11 / “Antipodal Softmin-Softmax Diameter Packing” used seven antipodal generator pairs, continuation, and 12 restarts, yet reached only `0.88` with ratio `0.21` in `9.96 s`. This confirms the prior evidence from Generation 1: antipodal symmetry can stabilize optimization but excludes better unrestricted 14-point contact graphs found by the `0.24` solutions.

- **Smooth optimization without direct exact-metric or constrained finishing is less reliable at the final contact arrangement.** Earlier lower-resolution continuation methods plateaued around `0.23`, such as the prior Generation 5 (`0.97`, `0.23`) and Generation 2 (`0.98`, `0.23`). The current Generation 17 specifically addresses this failure mode by accepting a trial only when the hard ratio itself improves, rather than assuming its smoothed surrogate remains accurate at late contact exchanges.

- **Long optimization schedules do not automatically yield better evaluated results.** Generation 19 required `10.57 s` and Generation 11 required `9.96 s`; only Generation 19 reached `1.00`, while the antipodal method stayed at `0.88`. Generation 17 reaches the same perfect score as the long unrestricted searches in `3.49 s`, indicating that the quality of final constrained/exact polishing is more consequential than iteration count alone.

## Implementation Insights

- **Generation 17’s vectorized pairwise computation is well aligned with the 14-point problem size.** It precomputes the 91 upper-triangular pairs using `np.triu_indices`, evaluates all pair displacements and squared distances in vectorized form, and accumulates equal-and-opposite pair forces using `np.add.at`. This preserves correct geometry gradients while keeping the repeated 4,600-step, 10-restart search practical.

- **Centering and RMS normalization after every update remove null directions that otherwise waste optimizer effort.** The current program applies `normalize` after each Adam-style update, after stochastic kicks, after SLSQP output, and after every exact-ratio trial. This matches the shared successful pattern across Generations 9, 13, 15, 16, and 19: translation and uniform scale are irrelevant to the ratio, so canonicalization directs numerical effort toward the configuration’s shape and contact graph.

- **The current program uses separate optimization mechanisms for surrogate stability and true-objective fidelity.** Its soft objective is based on weighted log distances, with pair force proportional to `(wm - wx) * delta / q`; near pairs are emphasized by softmin weights and far pairs by softmax weights. In contrast, the final hill climber computes the exact ratio for every proposed move and uses strict improvement acceptance. This is a concrete implementation of the previously observed principle that smooth extrema are useful for search, but the actual min/max evaluator should govern final selection.

- **Its SLSQP formulation is technically stronger than a generic post-processing call because it supplies analytic derivatives.** The scalar objective is the diameter variable, constraints enforce both `q >= 1` and `D >= q` for all 91 pairs, and the code provides explicit Jacobians for the pair constraints and centroid equality. Generation 16 also used analytic gradients/Jacobians and was exceptionally fast (`1.00`, `0.24`, `0.67 s`), reinforcing that derivative-aware constrained polishing can substantially improve both reliability and runtime.

## Performance Analysis

- **Generation 17 is a high-quality speed/score balance, but it is not the fastest perfect-score program.** It achieves `1.00` and `0.24` in `3.49 s`, substantially faster than Generation 10 (`7.43 s`), Generation 13 (`9.21 s`), Generation 15 (`8.77 s`), Generation 18 (`5.64 s`), and Generation 19 (`10.57 s`), all of which also achieved `1.00` and `0.24`. However, Generation 16’s continuation + L-BFGS-B + SLSQP + exact hill-polish pipeline attained the same result in only `0.67 s`, and Generation 14 did so in `2.57 s`.

- **The current program improves materially over the previously highlighted long-running soft-continuation best.** The earlier Generation 8 benchmark required about `9.38 s` for `1.00` and `0.24`, using 12 restarts and roughly 10,200 optimization steps per restart. Generation 17 obtains the same reported quality in `3.49 s` using 10 × 4,600 smooth steps plus selective top-three constrained and exact polishing. The result indicates that selective high-value post-processing can replace much of a uniformly long annealing schedule.

- **Reported ratio `0.24` is now consistently attainable across several unrestricted formulations, whereas constrained symmetry remains below that level.** Generations 10, 13–19 all report perfect combined score with `0.24` ratio except the antipodal Generation 11, which reaches only `0.21`. The dominant divide is therefore not Adam versus L-BFGS-B versus SLSQP, but unrestricted point geometry plus a sufficiently accurate final treatment of closest/farthest contacts.

- **Exact-objective finishing correlates with maximum score, while smooth-only schedules show more variance.** Generation 17’s strict hard-ratio acceptance, Generation 15’s exact-ratio hill climb, Generation 16’s batched exact hill polish, and Generation 19’s hard-ratio local search all reach `1.00`. In comparison, surrogate-heavy approaches without equally explicit final exact refinement were historically more likely to land at `0.97–0.99` or remain at `0.23` despite otherwise similar normalization, annealing, and restart designs.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Adopt an adaptive two-tier restart budget instead of running all 10 starts for 4,600 iterations.** Run 16–24 diverse starts through a cheaper 1,800–2,400-step schedule ending near moderate sharpness (for example, \(\beta\approx120\)), rank them by exact `min(q)/max(q)`, then continue only the best 4–6 through the existing \(\beta=528\) phase and SLSQP polish. This directly extends the current selective top-three polishing strategy and should improve contact-graph diversity without substantially increasing runtime.

2. **Seed part of the restart portfolio from perturbed high-quality geometric templates, while keeping all coordinates unrestricted.** Preserve several Gaussian starts, but add centered/RMS-normalized seeds based on staggered rings, icosahedral-like 12-point subsets plus two displaced points, and jittered copies of the best candidate generated during the run. The evidence against permanent antipodal symmetry is strong, but structured seeds have previously reached the \(0.24\) basin faster and can help the soft stage discover favorable unrestricted contact graphs.

3. **Make constrained polishing multi-pass with an active-set reinitialization.** After the first SLSQP solution, rescale it so `q.min() == 1`, identify pairs within a small tolerance of the minimum or diameter bound, then rerun SLSQP from that result with a slightly relaxed feasibility margin and tighter termination settings. Generation 17 already benefits from the diameter formulation; a second solve after contact sets stabilize may reduce small residual violations or allow SLSQP to exchange nearly active contacts before exact hill polishing.

4. **Replace the late random-kick rule with exact-ratio-guided batched proposals at sharp beta.** During the final 800–1,100 soft-search steps, generate a small batch of normalized perturbations combining the current soft-contact force, random tangent noise, and individual-point moves; accept the best candidate only if its hard ratio improves. This imports the current program’s successful exact acceptance criterion earlier into the pipeline, where it can resolve contact exchanges without waiting for the post-SLSQP hill climber.

5. **Use contact-aware exact polishing with separate moves for shortest and diameter pair memberships.** In the final accepted-move stage, explicitly form near-minimum and near-maximum pair sets, then alternate proposals that push apart only short-contact endpoints and pull together only diameter-contact endpoints, followed by normalization and strict hard-ratio acceptance. The existing `beta=900` force already approximates this behavior; making it set-based and alternating with noise should provide more decisive local moves when soft weights are split among several nearly tied contacts.
