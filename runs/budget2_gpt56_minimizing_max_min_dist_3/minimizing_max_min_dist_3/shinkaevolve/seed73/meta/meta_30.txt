# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- **The current best program (Generation 25, “Multi-Stage 14-Point Distance Ratio Optimization”) confirms that a three-phase pipeline is highly reliable:** 10 seeded softmin/softmax searches, selective constrained SLSQP refinement, and hard-ratio contact polishing achieved `min_max_ratio = 0.24`, `combined_score = 1.00` in `4.12 s`. Its decisive feature is that smooth optimization is used for basin discovery, while final proposals are accepted only when the evaluator-aligned exact `min(q)/max(q)` improves.

- **Unrestricted 14-point optimization consistently outperforms forced antipodal constructions.** Generation 25 reaches `0.24`, as do Generations 22, 24, 26, 27, 28, and 31. By contrast, Generation 20’s seven-direction antipodal Riemannian search received no performance credit, and prior antipodal evidence plateaued near `0.21`. The successful configurations evidently require contact graphs unavailable under mandatory seven-pair antipodal symmetry.

- **Continuation plus elite/selective refinement repeatedly reaches the scoring ceiling.** Generation 22’s population annealing with elite descendants and exact backtracking scored `1.00` in `2.94 s`; Generation 24’s multistage search with contact-set polishing scored `1.00` in `2.67 s`; and Generation 26’s random/antipodal seeds followed by constrained diameter polish scored `1.00` in `1.45 s`. Across methods, staged smoothing and a late sharp/exact phase are more important than the particular first-order optimizer.

- **Constrained diameter minimization is an effective intermediate representation of the target ratio.** The current best program rescales a candidate so its shortest squared distance is one, then minimizes a diameter variable subject to all 91 squared distances satisfying `1 <= q_ij <= D`. This same principle underlies Generation 26’s final constrained phase and contributes to its perfect result. It converts the nonsmooth ratio objective into a geometrically direct feasibility-and-diameter problem before returning to exact-ratio local moves.

## Ineffective Approaches

- **Hard antipodal symmetry is the clearest quality limitation in the evaluated set.** Generation 20 was valid but received `min_max_ratio: 0.00` and `combined_score: 0.00` in `0.57 s`, indicating that its fast Riemannian minimax search did not find a scorer-competitive code. Generation 21 could not be evaluated geometrically because it returned shape `(6,)` rather than `(14, 3)`. Even when antipodal formulations are correctly implemented, prior results show them below the unrestricted `0.24` level.

- **Correct geometry is irrelevant if the output contract fails.** Generation 21’s intended seven directions plus antipodes never received a packing score because validation rejected the array shape. This is distinct from Generation 20’s issue: Generation 20 was valid but weak under the performance scorer, whereas Generation 21 was invalid before optimization quality could be assessed.

- **Surrogate-only optimization is weaker at the last contact-graph adjustments.** The successful unrestricted programs all include some evaluator-aligned final mechanism: Generation 25 uses accepted exact-ratio moves; Generation 22 uses exact-score-monotone backtracking; Generation 23 uses accepted-step exact polishing; Generation 24 uses active shortest/longest contact polishing; and Generation 26 uses constrained diameter minimization plus exact backtracking. This reinforces the earlier observation that smooth softmin/softmax objectives can reach promising basins but do not alone reliably resolve the active min/max pair structure.

- **Longer raw iterative budgets do not guarantee a better score or runtime tradeoff.** Generation 28 reaches `1.00` but takes `8.71 s`, while Generation 25 reaches the same ratio in `4.12 s`, Generation 24 in `2.67 s`, and Generation 26 in `1.45 s`. The performance evidence favors focused constrained or exact-contact finishing over simply adding more restarts or hard-objective perturbation iterations.

## Implementation Insights

- **Generation 25 is effective because it separates surrogate gradients from hard-objective decisions.** During the 4,600-step search, it computes all 91 pairwise squared distances vectorially, assigns softmin weights to short log-distances and softmax weights to long log-distances, and forms pair forces proportional to `(wm - wx) * delta / q`. In the final phase, however, it does not trust this surrogate for acceptance: every normalized trial is scored with the exact `q.min() / q.max()` ratio.

- **Repeated canonicalization is a shared high-value implementation detail.** The current best program subtracts the centroid and RMS-normalizes after every Adam-like update, stochastic kick, constrained-solver result, and hard-ratio proposal. Generations 22, 23, 24, 26, 27, 28, and 31 likewise normalize throughout. Because translation and uniform scale do not alter the ratio, this removes irrelevant numerical drift and keeps the optimizer focused on shape.

- **The current best program’s SLSQP phase is technically targeted rather than generic.** It optimizes a scalar diameter bound with analytic objective derivatives, analytic inequality Jacobians for both lower and upper pairwise-distance constraints, and centroid equality Jacobians. It applies this expensive stage only to the top three candidates from the 10-restart smooth search. This selective use of derivative-aware constrained refinement is consistent with the prior finding that constrained polishing is valuable when seeded from strong configurations rather than used as the sole global search method.

- **Contact-set-specific motions improve the final nonsmooth search.** Generation 25 explicitly identifies near-shortest and near-longest pairs, then alternates between separating short contacts, contracting long contacts, and combined moves. The alternating scheme avoids direct cancellation when both active sets share vertices. Generation 24 similarly uses active shortest/longest pairs in its contact-aware polishing, and both programs obtain `1.00` at ratio `0.24`.

## Performance Analysis

- **Generation 25 is a perfect-score solution but is not the fastest evaluated perfect-score implementation.** It reaches `1.00` and `0.24` in `4.12 s`. It is faster than Generation 23 (`0.99`, `0.24`, `4.74 s`) and Generation 28 (`1.00`, `0.24`, `8.71 s`), but slower than Generation 22 (`1.00`, `2.94 s`), Generation 24 (`1.00`, `2.67 s`), and especially Generation 26 (`1.00`, `1.45 s`). Thus, its score quality is fully competitive, while its broad 10 × 4,600 smooth-search budget and top-three SLSQP calls impose a moderate runtime cost.

- **The `0.24` ratio is now robustly reproduced by multiple unrestricted architectures.** Population Adam with elite resampling (Generation 22), two-stage softmin with exact polish (Generation 23), contact-aware multistage optimization (Generation 24), constrained diameter minimization (Generation 26), batched continuation (Generation 27), multi-restart hard-ratio hill climbing (Generation 28), and Generation 31’s hybrid contact/SLSQP approach all report `0.24`. The evidence indicates that this quality level is not tied to one optimizer, but to the common unrestricted, normalized, staged-exact design.

- **Generation 23 shows that matching the reported ratio is not necessarily sufficient to match the maximum combined score.** It reports `min_max_ratio = 0.24` but a `combined_score = 0.99`, unlike the `1.00` achieved by Generations 22 and 24–28 and 31. This suggests the scorer may retain precision or secondary sensitivity beyond the rounded ratio value, making the current best program’s exact acceptance, feasibility refinement, and best-candidate tracking meaningful even when headline ratios appear equal.

- **The antipodal experiments are sharply separated from the unrestricted perfect-score group.** Generation 20’s valid antipodal code scored zero performance credit, while Generation 21 was invalid due to interface shape. In contrast, every valid unrestricted staged optimizer in Generations 22 and 24–28/31 achieved either `0.99` or `1.00`. The evaluation results support the earlier conclusion that output validity is necessary but unrestricted geometry and exact-contact refinement are what correlate with top packing performance.
