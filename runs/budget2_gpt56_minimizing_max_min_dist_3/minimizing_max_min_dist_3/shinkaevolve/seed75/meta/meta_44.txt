# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- **The current best program, Multi-Seed Contact Flow Sphere Packing (Generation 41), confirms that broad seed diversity plus exact constrained polishing reliably reaches the top quality tier.** It obtains `min_max_ratio = 0.24` and score `1.00` in `2.49 s`. Its seed portfolio is especially broad: perturbed cube–axis configurations, eight twisted two-layer seven-point layouts, 12 perturbed antipodal-line starts, and 20 unconstrained Gaussian clouds. This supports the earlier finding that asymmetric, fully free-coordinate packings are necessary to access the `0.24` basin.

- **A smooth-to-exact optimization pipeline is the consistent full-score pattern.** Generation 41 uses 580 iterations of contact-homotopy force flow to locate promising basins, then reformulates the exact objective as maximizing `t` subject to every squared pair distance satisfying `t <= q_ij <= 1`. This same exact-diameter constrained approach is shared by Generation 42 (`1.00`, `0.24`, `2.93 s`) and the earlier constrained-packing successes. Smooth-only methods had generally stalled near `0.23`.

- **Persistent active-contact weighting improves smooth basin finding.** Generation 41 does not rely only on instantaneous softmin/softmax weights. It combines log-distance soft weights with exponentially decayed `low_memory` and `high_memory` contact-band estimates, increasingly concentrating force on near-minimum and near-maximum pairs. Generation 42’s persistence-weighted contact flow also reaches `0.24`, indicating that retaining contact history stabilizes the transition from broad separation forces to active-contact optimization.

- **Candidate screening before SLSQP preserves quality while limiting expensive refinement.** Generation 41 runs contact flow for all seeds but polishes only up to 12 candidates, selecting high-ratio candidates while retaining coordinate-separated basins. This follows the prior successful strategy of using exact-ratio screening and diversity filtering rather than sending every multistart result to SLSQP. It achieves the same `1.00` score as broader staged searches while avoiding the `5.25 s` runtime of Generation 40.

## Ineffective Approaches

- **Antipodal-only geometric restrictions remain a quality limitation.** The previous Generation 32 antipodal line-packing method completed very quickly (`0.14 s`) but only achieved `min_max_ratio = 0.21`, score `0.88`. Generation 41 uses perturbed antipodal line configurations only as seeds, then allows independent point positions and radii during flow and SLSQP. Its `0.24` result reinforces that antipodal structure is useful for initialization but harmful as a final constraint.

- **Smooth surrogate optimization without exact constrained feasibility enforcement tends to plateau below the full-score frontier.** Earlier smooth continuation methods such as Generations 34 and 38 reached approximately `0.23` and score `0.94`, despite sophisticated annealing and multistart exploration. Generation 41, Generation 42, and Generation 43 all reach `0.24` only after exact active-contact or constrained-diameter refinement.

- **More refinement is not automatically better once the `0.24` basin is found.** Generation 40’s Multi-Start Smooth Distance-Ratio Optimizer achieved the same `0.24` / `0.99` quality regime but required `5.25 s`, largely due to extensive staged refinement and accept-only polishing. Generation 41 reaches the full `1.00` score in `2.49 s` by screening candidates and applying direct SLSQP refinement, while Generation 43 shows an even leaner path to the same result in `0.92 s`.

## Implementation Insights

- **Generation 41’s SLSQP formulation exactly matches the evaluator’s ratio after normalization.** It anchors point zero, normalizes the diameter to one, introduces scalar `t`, and solves `max t` under `q_ij - t >= 0` and `1 - q_ij >= 0` for all 91 pairs. With unit squared diameter, `t` is precisely the minimum/maximum squared-distance ratio. This direct alignment is a central reason its final polished candidates attain `0.24`.

- **Analytic constraint Jacobians are an important efficiency feature in the current best program.** The code provides derivatives for all `182` inequalities over 39 free point-coordinate variables plus `t`, rather than relying on numerical differentiation. This matches the earlier observation that analytic Jacobians help constrained packing searches reach full score in a practical runtime; Generation 41’s `2.49 s` is substantially faster than Generation 40’s `5.25 s` despite using exact constrained polishing.

- **The contact flow is numerically stable and geometrically targeted.** Generation 41 computes pair weights in `log(q)` space, clamps squared distances at `1e-12`, and subtracts the maximum logit before exponentiation. Its pair force is proportional to `(low - high) * delta / q`, so short pairs receive repulsion and long pairs attraction in log-distance geometry. RMS normalization after each step removes translation and scale modes, while per-point momentum clipping prevents transient contacts from producing destabilizing moves.

- **The program includes deterministic symmetry breaking without sacrificing repeatability.** A fixed RNG seed ensures reproducibility, but structured seeds receive small early random perturbations and Gaussian starts are included explicitly. This implementation detail avoids getting trapped in exact cube-axis or layered symmetries, consistent with the broader evidence that the best `0.24` configurations are unrestricted and asymmetric rather than strictly symmetric.

## Performance Analysis

- **Generation 41 is a full-quality result but is no longer the fastest reported full-score implementation.** It achieves `min_max_ratio = 0.24`, score `1.00`, in `2.49 s`, matching Generation 42 (`2.93 s`) in quality and outperforming Generation 40’s longer `5.25 s` run. However, Generation 43 reaches the same `0.24` / `1.00` outcome in only `0.92 s`, showing that its checkpointed multistage continuation, branching, and lighter accept-only finishing are substantially more runtime-efficient.

- **Exact constrained refinement separates the `1.00` methods from the smooth-only quality tier.** Generation 41 (`2.49 s`), Generation 42 (`2.93 s`), and Generation 43 (`0.92 s`) all achieve `0.24` with full score through an exact or active-contact finishing stage. In contrast, previous smooth-only continuation approaches remained at roughly `0.23` / `0.94`, despite using many seeds and advanced soft objectives.

- **Generation 41 provides a favorable exploration/refinement tradeoff compared with the more expensive Generation 40 design.** It evaluates a large and structurally diverse seed set—46 total seeds—but retains at most 12 candidates for SLSQP. This delivers full score in less than half Generation 40’s time (`2.49 s` versus `5.25 s`) while avoiding the lower-quality risk of relying on a single structured geometry.

- **The evaluations show a clear structural quality/runtime frontier.** Restrictive antipodal search is fastest but weaker (`0.21`, `0.88`, `0.14 s` in Generation 32); unrestricted smooth search is stronger but often plateaus around `0.23`; and unrestricted search followed by exact diameter/contact refinement consistently reaches `0.24`. Within that final tier, Generation 43 is the runtime leader, while Generation 41 remains a strong example of robust multi-family basin discovery combined with a mathematically exact final solve.

1. **Adopt Generation 43-style checkpoint branching while retaining the current contact-flow and exact SLSQP finish.** During the last half of `contact_flow`, save promising states around iterations 320, 410, and 500 for only the strongest seed families, perturb each checkpoint with small centered noise (`0.003–0.012` after RMS normalization), and run shortened remaining schedules with slightly varied final sharpness.
