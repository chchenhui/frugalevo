# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- **Unrestricted 3D point coordinates plus exact diameter-constrained polishing are the strongest repeated pattern.** The current best, **Hybrid Annealed Diameter Packing (Generation 26)**, reached `min_max_ratio = 0.24`, combined score `1.00`, in only `2.42 s`. It starts from free 14-point coordinates, removes only translation/scale through RMS and diameter normalization, and then solves the direct feasibility form \(t \leq \|x_i-x_j\|^2 \leq 1\). This matches the top-quality outcomes of **Generation 21** and **Generation 31**, both contact-graph/SLSQP variants at `0.24` and `1.00`.

- **Diverse basin generation is valuable when followed by strong candidate screening and local constrained optimization.** Generation 26 uses 40 seeds: cube-axis, three twisted two-ring layouts, 26 Gaussian point clouds, and 10 perturbed antipodal line packings. The perturbation of line packings is important: it retains useful geometric structure without preserving the restrictive antipodal condition. This broad seed population produced the same `0.24` tier as Generation 21’s Fibonacci/antiprism/spherical/Gaussian multi-start search.

- **A smooth global surrogate is effective as an initializer, not as the final objective.** Generation 26’s 520-step anneal optimizes a soft-minimum minus soft-maximum of log squared distances, creating candidates with improved separation before exact optimization. On its own, comparable smooth-force approaches were generally lower: **Persistent-Contact Point Packing (Generation 28)** reached `0.22` in `2.80 s`, while **Multi-Start Smoothed Sphere Packing (Generation 25)** reached `0.23` in `2.46 s`. The current best’s final constrained polish converts a good smooth candidate into a top-tier exact-ratio candidate.

- **Free radial motion continues to distinguish the `0.24` solutions from spherical or antipodal ones.** Generation 26, Generation 21, and Generation 31 all permit different point radii after centering/normalization and achieved `0.24`. By contrast, both antipodal line programs—**Generation 20** and **Generation 22**—remained at `0.21` and score `0.88`, despite using multi-start search and SLSQP.

## Ineffective Approaches

- **Hard antipodal line formulations remain limited for this objective.** **Antipodal Seven-Line Minimax Packing (Generation 20)** and **Multistart Antipodal Line Packing (Generation 22)** both fix the solution as seven opposite point pairs and achieved only `min_max_ratio = 0.21`, combined score `0.88`. Their line-correlation objectives correctly optimize within that restricted family, but fixing every opposite pair fixes the diameter structure too rigidly and prevents discovery of the asymmetric unrestricted configurations at `0.24`.

- **Cube-axis symmetry, even with deterministic perturbations and contact polishing, plateaued near `0.22`.** **Generation 23**, **Generation 24**, and **Generation 27** all use cube/cube-octahedron plus axial-point families, staged soft forces, and exact-ratio acceptance. They were reliable and fast (`0.70–0.94 s`) but stayed at `0.22`, score `0.93`. Their seed family is useful, but it did not by itself access the stronger basin reached by the broader population searches.

- **Smooth continuation without an explicit exact feasibility stage generally underperformed.** Generation 28 used 3,600 continuation steps with persistent contact weights but stayed at `0.22`; Generation 25 added late basin hopping and improved to `0.23`, still below the top tier. This supports the prior finding that soft min/max forces are useful for restructuring contact graphs, but the nonsmooth min/max ratio benefits from exact final constraints or exact-objective polishing.

- **Higher runtime alone does not guarantee better packing quality.** Generation 21 reached `0.24` but required `4.85 s`, and Generation 31 required `4.08 s`; meanwhile Generation 28 used `2.80 s` but achieved `0.22`. The current best demonstrates that quality depends more on the sequence of population search, screening, and exact polishing than on raw iteration count.

## Implementation Insights

- **Generation 26’s objective sequence is especially well matched to the geometry.** During annealing, it computes all 91 squared pair distances and forms stable shifted softmax weights over `log(q)`: short pairs receive repulsion through `low`, while diameter pairs receive attraction through `high`. The pairwise forces include the factor `d / q`, corresponding to gradients in log-distance space, which prevents large distances from dominating solely due to their magnitude. RMS normalization after each step removes translation and scale degeneracies without imposing a unit-sphere constraint.

- **The current best uses a concrete exact constrained formulation rather than only evaluating the ratio after heuristic updates.** Its SLSQP variable contains 13 free point coordinates plus a scalar lower bound `t`; point 0 is anchored at the origin. It maximizes `t` subject to all pairwise constraints:
  \[
  q_{ij} - t \ge 0,\qquad 1-q_{ij}\ge 0.
  \]
  After diameter normalization, maximizing feasible `t` is directly equivalent to maximizing the squared minimum-distance/diameter ratio. This is a stronger final formulation than merely maintaining a soft surrogate.

- **Analytic constraint Jacobians likely contribute to the current best’s favorable runtime.** Generation 26 explicitly builds derivatives for all `2 × 91` inequality constraints, including the signs for each endpoint coordinate and the `-1` derivative with respect to `t`. This avoids finite-difference cost and instability during up to 700 SLSQP iterations per selected candidate. It helps explain why it achieved the same `1.00` score as Generations 21 and 31 while running faster (`2.42 s` versus `4.85 s` and `4.08 s`).

- **Candidate selection balances quality with population diversity before the expensive stage.** Rather than polishing every one of the 40 annealed starts, Generation 26 sorts by exact ratio and selects up to 11 candidates, retaining early alternatives even if they are close and requiring a mean-coordinate separation threshold thereafter. This preserves multiple potential contact-graph basins while containing SLSQP cost. It is a more targeted version of the multi-start/contact-graph principle seen in Generations 21 and 31.

## Performance Analysis

- **The observed quality frontier remains `min_max_ratio = 0.24`, but Generation 26 is the most runtime-efficient member of that tier in the supplied results.** Generation 26 achieved score `1.00` in `2.42 s`; **Generation 31** achieved the same score in `4.08 s`; **Generation 21** achieved it in `4.85 s`. Thus, the current best matches the highest measured packing quality while reducing evaluation time by roughly 41% relative to Generation 31 and 50% relative to Generation 21.

- **The programs separate into clear performance tiers.** Antipodal line methods are consistently at `0.21` / `0.88` (Generations 20 and 22). Deterministic cube-axis and persistent smooth methods concentrate at `0.22` / `0.93` (Generations 23, 24, 27, and 28). More flexible smooth multi-start search can reach `0.23` / `0.94` (Generation 25). The full `0.24` / `1.00` tier is reached by unrestricted point-coordinate methods with exact constrained or contact-aware polishing (Generations 21, 26, and 31).

- **Generation 26 improves on nearby approaches by combining, rather than replacing, their strengths.** It retains the stable soft-extrema annealing seen in Generation 25 and Generation 28, preserves the broad population diversity emphasized by Generation 21 and Generation 31, and adds a direct SLSQP feasibility formulation. This combination avoids the `0.22–0.23` smooth-optimization plateau while requiring less runtime than the other full-score contact-graph implementations.

- **The scoring evidence supports exact lower/upper distance enforcement as the defining final-stage difference.** Generation 26’s direct constraints enforce every squared distance below normalized diameter `1` while maximizing a common lower distance bound. Generation 21 and Generation 31 similarly use constrained diameter formulations and score `1.00`. In contrast, programs that only use accept-only exact-ratio checks or contact-force polishing without this explicit feasibility solve generally remained at `0.22–0.23`.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Add a second exact-polishing pass with adaptive active-set tightening from the first SLSQP result.** After each successful feasibility solve, identify pairs within a small tolerance of the minimum and diameter (for example \(q_{ij} < 1.01t\) or \(q_{ij} > 0.99\)), then rerun constrained optimization from that solution with the full constraints retained but a contact-focused objective or trust-region perturbations around the active graph. This extends the current program’s strongest feature—direct \(t \le q_{ij}\le1\) optimization—while exploiting the contact-graph behavior that also produced `0.24` in Generations 21 and 31.

2. **Use survivor-based scheduling before expensive SLSQP, replacing fixed polishing of up to 11 candidates.** Anneal all 40 starts initially, retain the best 6–8 by exact ratio plus one or two deliberately diverse candidates, then spend the saved budget on 2–4 asymmetric perturb-and-reanneal descendants of the top 2–3 survivors before polishing. Generation 26 already benefits from broad basin generation and screening; reallocating late work toward near-`0.24` basins may improve the chance of finding a better contact graph without increasing total runtime substantially.

3. **Introduce late-stage checkpoint branching during annealing rather than only branching at seed creation.** Save configurations at roughly 65%, 80%, and 92% of the 520-step schedule for high-performing seeds, perturb each checkpoint by a small free-coordinate noise such as `0.01–0.03`, and finish with a sharper, shorter continuation schedule. This preserves unrestricted radial motion and creates local basin hopping around promising asymmetric structures, addressing the possibility that the current monotone anneal commits too early to one soft-objective contact arrangement.

4. **Add structured but non-rigid free-radial seeds derived from successful cube/octahedral families.** Build seeds from cube vertices plus six axial points, but independently vary cube radius, axis radius, axis offsets, and point-specific radial factors before applying non-antipodal Gaussian perturbations and RMS normalization. Cube-axis methods alone plateaued around `0.22`, but they are reliable sources of geometric structure; using them only as perturbed inputs to the proven unrestricted anneal-plus-SLSQP pipeline could expose additional high-quality basins.

5. **Make the smooth annealing force contact-persistent near the end of the schedule.** During the final 25–35% of iterations, maintain exponentially decayed persistence scores for pairs repeatedly assigned substantial `low` or `high` softmax weight, then multiply their force coefficients by a modest persistence factor while retaining all pair contributions. This builds on Generation 26’s successful log-distance soft-extrema gradient while reducing force dilution from transient near-extreme pairs and stabilizing the contact graph delivered to exact constrained polishing.
