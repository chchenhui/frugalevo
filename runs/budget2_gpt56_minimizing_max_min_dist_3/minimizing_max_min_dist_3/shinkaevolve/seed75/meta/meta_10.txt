# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- **The current best, Multi-Start Smoothed 14-Point Packing, combines diverse deterministic seeds with smooth continuation and exact-score selection effectively.** Its Fibonacci, ring-and-cap, and random spherical starts, followed by seven 3,600-step refinements, reached `min_max_ratio = 0.22` and the top combined score of `0.93`. This substantially improves on fixed symmetric constructions such as Cube–Octahedron (`0.21`, `0.88`) by allowing the contact graph to depart from the initial symmetry.

- **Optimizing a differentiable surrogate while checkpointing the evaluator’s exact nonsmooth ratio consistently works.** The current program uses soft minimum/maximum squared distances with an increasing inverse temperature (`beta` from 7 to 80), but retains candidates based on exact `min(d²) / max(d²)`. Programs 3, 5, 7, and 8 use the same broad softmin/softmax-plus-exact-selection pattern and all reached `0.22` ratios with `0.91–0.92` combined scores.

- **Scale and translation normalization are consistently beneficial implementation constraints.** The current best recenters every update and RMS-normalizes the configuration. Programs 5 and 7 likewise center/RMS-normalize during force-based optimization, while Programs 3 and 8 enforce unit-sphere positions by tangent-space updates and reprojection. These remove objective-invariant degrees of freedom and keep optimization numerically stable.

- **Structured seeds are valuable even when final solutions are not fully symmetric.** The cube-plus-axis seed supported strong outcomes in Program 6 (`0.22`, `0.93`), Program 3 (`0.22`, `0.92`), and Program 5 (`0.22`, `0.91`). The current best broadens this principle: its ring-and-cap and Fibonacci seeds supply alternative contact structures, while deterministic random starts and early kicks permit symmetry breaking.

## Ineffective Approaches

- **Unoptimized random sampling is decisively inadequate for this geometric objective.** Seeded Random 3D Point Generator generated valid Gaussian points but had `min_max_ratio = 0.00` and combined score `0.02`. It had no mechanism to repel close pairs or control the diameter, so random clustering and outliers dominate the ratio.

- **A fixed geometric construction can be fast but leaves measurable objective value unclaimed.** Cube–Octahedron 14-Vertex Construction was nearly free to evaluate and attained `0.21` / `0.88`, but remained below the optimized `0.22` / `0.93` methods. Its chosen axial radius produces a valid symmetric diameter-controlled arrangement, yet the evaluator favors a slightly less constrained geometry.

- **Not all symmetric packings are equally compatible with the min/max-distance criterion.** Cuboctahedron with Axial Pair achieved only `min_max_ratio = 0.15` and score `0.61`, despite direct symmetry and negligible runtime. Its stated minimum distance of \(\sqrt{2}\) versus diameter \(2\sqrt{2}\) intrinsically gives a weak distance ratio; symmetry alone does not ensure a good limiting-pair structure.

- **Restricting the search to antipodal line codes is competitive but constraining.** Deterministic Seven-Line Spherical Code Search reached `0.21` and `0.88` in `0.35` seconds. Its antipodal construction fixes the maximum squared distance through opposite pairs and directly controls coherence, but its `0.21` result did not match the `0.22` found by unconstrained 14-point optimizers, indicating that forced antipodality limits the best attainable configuration here.

## Implementation Insights

- **The current best’s force computation directly reflects the ratio objective.** For all 91 point pairs, it computes squared distances and applies a signed pair force:
  - close pairs receive soft-min-derived repulsion;
  - large-distance pairs receive soft-max-derived contraction;
  - force weights are scaled by `1 / soft_min` and `1 / soft_max`, corresponding to ascent on `log(soft_min / soft_max)`.
  
  This is more objective-aligned than generic repulsion because it improves the closest pairs while preventing a few distant pairs from expanding the denominator.

- **Continuation is implemented concretely rather than merely using a single smooth approximation.** The current program raises `beta` from `7` to `80` over 3,600 iterations. Early low-sharpness weighting distributes forces across many pairs and permits contact-graph restructuring; late high-sharpness weighting increasingly targets true limiting close and far pairs. This matches the strong results of staged-sharpening implementations in Programs 3, 7, and 8.

- **Exact objective tracking protects against surrogate mismatch.** The current best evaluates the exact squared-distance ratio every 20 iterations and preserves the global best point array. This is important because the soft objective is only an approximation, particularly at early beta values. Programs 3, 5, 7, and 8 also explicitly retain the best exact candidate, and all reached the same leading `0.22` ratio tier.

- **Determinism is maintained despite stochastic exploration.** The current implementation uses `np.random.default_rng(20250308)`, making its three random seeds and scheduled early perturbation kicks reproducible. This retains the robustness benefit noted for Program 6’s deterministic SLSQP/restart strategy while still obtaining the diversity usually associated with stochastic multistart search.

## Performance Analysis

- **The evaluated results form three clear quality tiers.** Pure random generation is an extreme outlier at `0.00` ratio and `0.02` score. The weaker fixed constructions span `0.15–0.21` ratio and `0.61–0.88` score. Objective-driven optimization consistently reaches `0.22`, producing scores from `0.91` to `0.93`.

- **The current best is tied for the highest combined score but not uniquely best in ratio.** Multi-Start Smoothed 14-Point Packing achieved `0.22` / `0.93` in `2.26` seconds. Deterministic 14-Point Max-Min Packing also achieved `0.22` / `0.93`, but in only `0.56` seconds. Thus, both methods reached the observed top quality level, while Program 6 did so with substantially lower measured evaluation time.

- **More extensive multistart optimization did not improve the observed ratio beyond `0.22`.** Program 5 used 18 starts × 2,200 iterations and took `3.58` seconds for `0.22` / `0.91`. Program 8 used cube/Fibonacci plus 14 random starts with multi-stage spherical refinement, taking `2.68` seconds for `0.22` / `0.92`. The current best’s seven starts × 3,600 iterations scored slightly better overall (`0.93`) but still plateaued at the same reported ratio.

- **Runtime correlates more with search budget than with final score among the optimized methods.** The current best (`2.26 s`, `0.93`), spherical optimizer (`2.76 s`, `0.92`), force packing (`3.58 s`, `0.91`), and Adam-style multistart relaxation (`1.76 s`, `0.92`) all converge to approximately the same ratio. In contrast, Program 6’s constrained SLSQP refinement reaches the same leading score in `0.56 s`, and the no-optimization geometric constructions are effectively instantaneous but remain below the optimized quality tier.

2. **Replace the fixed seven-start allocation with staged survivor selection and restart budget reallocation.** Run every seed through a shorter first continuation stage, retain the top 2–3 candidates by exact ratio, and spend the remaining iterations on only those survivors with sharper beta and smaller steps. The current method already checkpoints the exact objective successfully; using that signal earlier should preserve seed diversity while avoiding 3,600 iterations on starts that have clearly entered inferior contact structures.

3. **Seed the search explicitly with the cube-plus-axis / cube–octahedron-derived 14-point configuration, then apply deterministic asymmetric perturbations.** This seed family was repeatedly productive in Programs 3, 5, and 6, while the current best currently uses Fibonacci, rings, and random spherical starts but omits this demonstrated strong basin. Include several variants with independently perturbed axial radius, cube radius, and small non-antipodal coordinate offsets, then let the existing continuation break symmetry where beneficial.

4. **Use a two-level local objective near the end of continuation that focuses on active contact pairs but still protects the diameter.** After beta reaches a moderately sharp value, identify pairs within a small tolerance of the current minimum and maximum squared distances, and alternate the smooth log-ratio gradient with a targeted equalization step that pushes near-minimum pairs outward and near-maximum pairs inward. This extends the current objective-aligned force mechanism: it should reduce the residual spread among limiting pairs rather than allowing soft weights to remain diluted by noncritical distances.

5. **Introduce deterministic basin-hopping around high-quality exact checkpoints rather than only early random kicks.** Whenever a run improves the exact ratio materially, generate a few small centered, RMS-normalized perturbations of that checkpoint and perform short high-beta refinements from each; retain the best exact candidate. The current kicks occur only in the first 500 iterations, but late-stage perturb-and-refine can escape a nearly optimal contact graph without abandoning the strong scale-normalized continuation framework.
