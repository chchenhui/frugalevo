# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- **The current best program, Multi-Start Contact-Graph Point Packing (Generation 13), establishes that direct active-contact optimization can reach the top observed quality tier.** It achieved `min_max_ratio = 0.24` and the maximum combined score of `1.00` in `4.48 s`. Unlike smooth-extrema-only methods, it explicitly forms lower contacts `q <= qlo(1+band)` and upper contacts `q >= qhi(1-band)`, then applies repulsion to the near-minimum pairs and contraction to the near-diameter pairs. Its broad-to-narrow band schedule—`0.24` down to `0.007`—successfully transitions from global contact-graph restructuring to near-exact nonsmooth optimization.

- **Removing unnecessary spherical or antipodal constraints is associated with the jump from the earlier `0.21–0.22` plateau to `0.24`.** Generation 19, Multi-Start Smoothed Ratio Point Optimizer, also reached `0.24` and `0.99`, specifically because it retained independent radial motion instead of projecting every point to the unit sphere. Generation 15 likewise achieved `0.24` / `0.99` using centered, RMS-normalized unrestricted points. In contrast, the spherical and antipodal-line formulations—Generations 12, 14, 17, and 18—remained at `0.21`, while the earlier soft-extrema point methods generally reached `0.22`.

- **Diverse structured and random starts remain essential, but the strongest results combine them with a contact-aware refinement method.** The current best uses Fibonacci, three antiprism variants, seven spherical random starts, and five Gaussian starts. This extends the previous successful seed-diversity pattern from the `0.22` methods, but reaches a better result because the optimizer can select among substantially different initial contact graphs. The best configuration is retained according to the exact ratio throughout all starts.

- **Exact-objective local polishing materially complements surrogate or banded optimization.** The current program finishes with 5,500 accepted coordinate perturbation trials evaluated strictly using `min(q) / max(q)`. Generation 15 also used deterministic perturbation refinement and reached `0.24`; Generation 19’s exact candidate tracking reached the same ratio tier. This confirms that final progress comes from optimizing the evaluator’s actual nonsmooth ratio rather than relying only on a smoothed proxy.

## Ineffective Approaches

- **Forced antipodality remains a measurable limitation for this particular 14-point ratio objective.** Seven-Line Spherical Packing Optimization (Generation 12) reached only `0.21` / `0.88` despite tangent-projected gradients, multiple starts, and sharp softmax continuation. Evolutionary Antipodal Line Packing (Generation 18) also remained at `0.21` / `0.88`, and the Hybrid Point and Antipodal Line method (Generation 11) reached `0.22` / `0.91` but required `9.28 s`. Fixing opposite pairs conveniently fixes the diameter, but constrains the contact graph more than the best unrestricted configurations permit.

- **Unit-sphere projection similarly appears restrictive relative to centered/RMS-normalized free-radius point optimization.** Multi-Stage Spherical 14-Point Packing (Generation 14) achieved `0.21` / `0.88` in `1.70 s`, and Multi-Start Spherical Ratio Optimization (Generation 16) achieved `0.22` / `0.91` in `1.54 s`. These are valid and efficient, but they did not match the `0.24` attained by Generations 13, 15, and 19, where radial positions can adapt while only translation and global scale are normalized away.

- **Smooth min/max continuation alone appears insufficient to break the `0.22` quality plateau.** Multi-Start Smoothed 14-Point Packing (Generation 10) used stable shifted log-sum-exp, annealing, and exact checkpointing, but reached `0.22` / `0.93`. Its approach was strong relative to fixed constructions, yet the newer active contact-band formulation improved the ratio to `0.24`. The key difference is the late-stage emphasis on explicit near-minimum and near-maximum pair sets instead of continuing to distribute force according to a global soft-extrema weighting.

- **Fixed symmetric constructions are fast but leave objective value unclaimed.** The prior Cube–Octahedron construction and related deterministic symmetric methods were around the `0.21` / `0.88` tier. Generation 17’s cube-axis refinement remained at `0.21` / `0.90`, even with multistage soft optimization. Symmetric seeds are useful starting points, but retaining the symmetry or only weakly perturbing it does not reliably discover the stronger asymmetric contact structure found by the current best.

## Implementation Insights

- **Generation 13’s gradient construction is tightly aligned with the exact ratio geometry.** For all 91 pairs, it identifies active short and long contact bands, assigns positive coefficients proportional to `1 / qlo` for low pairs and negative coefficients proportional to `1 / qhi` for high pairs, and converts those pair weights into a graph-Laplacian gradient:
  `2 * ((row_sum * x) - cmat @ x)`.
  This directly raises limiting short distances while reducing limiting long distances. The explicit contact graph likely explains why it outperformed smooth-only alternatives at the actual ratio metric.

- **The current best removes only objective-invariant directions, rather than imposing extra geometry.** Each update subtracts the translation component and radial scale component of the gradient, then calls `normalize()` to center and RMS-normalize the point array. This preserves numerical conditioning without forcing every point to have equal radius. That design matches Generation 19’s reported advantage from “releasing” the spherical constraint and is consistent with both programs reaching `0.24`.

- **Its optimization schedule separates exploration, stabilization, and exact polishing.** Broad early bands (`0.24`, `0.15`, `0.085`) involve many contacts and include deterministic random kicks every 95 steps, making basin changes possible. Narrow later bands (`0.042`, `0.018`, `0.007`) approximate the true active min/max contacts. Finally, the coordinate-wise random polish accepts only strict exact-ratio improvements while reducing `sigma` every 550 trials. This staged structure is more concrete than merely increasing a softmax temperature and is the defining implementation difference behind the `1.00` score.

- **Adaptive momentum is used in a controlled, deterministic way.** The program combines momentum (`0.88/0.12`) with an elementwise running squared-gradient accumulator (`0.992/0.008`), effectively scaling updates by local gradient magnitude. It uses a fixed RNG seed (`918273645`) for reproducible random starts, kicks, and polishing. This preserves the robustness benefits of multi-start stochastic search while satisfying deterministic validation requirements, as did the earlier successful seeded methods.

## Performance Analysis

- **The current best materially improves the observed quality frontier.** Generation 13 is the only listed program with a combined score of `1.00`, reaching `min_max_ratio = 0.24`. It improves over the earlier best soft-extrema methods at `0.22` / `0.91–0.93`, including Generation 10 (`0.22`, `0.93`, `2.42 s`) and Generation 16 (`0.22`, `0.91`, `1.54 s`). The increase from `0.22` to `0.24` is accompanied by a full-score result rather than merely a small runtime-quality tradeoff.

- **The `0.24` tier is consistently associated with unrestricted point packing, not a single isolated implementation.** Generation 15 achieved `0.24` / `0.99` in `6.83 s`; Generation 19 achieved `0.24` / `0.99` in `3.59 s`; and the current contact-graph program achieved `0.24` / `1.00` in `4.48 s`. Thus, the performance evidence supports a robust pattern: centered/RMS-normalized unrestricted coordinates plus multi-start refinement can exceed spherical and antipodal models.

- **Runtime does not increase monotonically with result quality, but contact-band optimization has a meaningful cost.** Generation 13 takes `4.48 s`, faster than Generation 15’s `6.83 s` while scoring slightly higher, but slower than Generation 19’s `3.59 s` with nearly the same quality. The fastest methods—Generation 17 at `0.79 s`, Generation 18 at `1.13 s`, and Generation 16 at `1.54 s`—remain in the `0.21–0.22` ratio tier. The extra time in the current best is spent on 18 starts, 2,760 staged updates per start, repeated exact checks, and 5,500 exact-ratio polishing trials.

- **The evaluated programs now divide into clearer quality groups than the earlier results suggested.** Antipodal and strictly spherical methods cluster around `0.21` / `0.88`; efficient smoothed unrestricted methods cluster around `0.22` / `0.90–0.93`; and active-contact or free-radial multi-start methods reach `0.24` / `0.99–1.00`. The current best demonstrates that the decisive improvement is not just more iterations or more restarts, but the combination of unconstrained radii, explicit evolving contact sets, and exact-objective polishing.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Add late-stage basin hopping from the best contact-graph checkpoints, not just early random kicks.** Whenever a start improves the exact ratio by a meaningful threshold, clone that centered/RMS-normalized configuration 3–5 times with small asymmetric perturbations, then run only the final two narrow-band stages (`0.018`, `0.007`) on each clone. The current method’s early kicks help broad exploration, while Generation 15’s deterministic perturbation success suggests that perturb-and-refine around already strong asymmetric contact graphs is a promising way to exceed the current local basin.

2. **Replace uniform active-pair averaging with contact persistence and violation-weighted coefficients.** Track how often each pair remains in the lower or upper band over recent iterations, then assign larger force to persistent contacts and to pairs closest to the actual minimum or maximum—for example, weight low contacts by `exp(-(q/qlo - 1)/tau)/qlo` and high contacts analogously. This preserves the successful explicit contact-graph mechanism while reducing dilution from transient band members, especially in the narrow stages where the true nonsmooth objective is controlled by a small stable set of pairs.

3. **Introduce survivor-based multi-start scheduling to reallocate iterations toward promising contact graphs.** Run all 18 existing structured/random starts through the first three broad stages, rank them by exact `ratio_sq`, and retain only the top 4–6 for the expensive `0.042`, `0.018`, and `0.007` stages plus polishing. Use the saved computation either for more random/asymmetric starts or for additional narrow-band refinements; this keeps the demonstrated seed diversity but spends the contact-optimization budget on configurations already approaching the `0.24` tier.

4. **Expand the seed portfolio with free-radial cube–octahedron and cube-axis variants, immediately perturbed to break symmetry.** Add 14-point seeds based on cube vertices plus axial/octahedral points, but independently vary cube radius, axis radius, point offsets, and apply small non-antipodal Gaussian perturbations before normalization. Earlier structured cube-derived constructions reached strong basins even though rigid symmetry plateaued near `0.21`; combining them with the current unrestricted-radii contact optimization may expose contact graphs that the Fibonacci, antiprism, and purely random starts miss.

5. **Add a constrained active-set polishing phase after coordinate perturbation search.** From the best exact checkpoint, identify a stable set of near-minimum and near-diameter pairs, then perform a small projected optimization that maximizes a slack variable \(r\) subject to selected short pairs satisfying \(q_{ij}\ge r\), selected long pairs satisfying \(q_{ij}\le 1\), centering, and RMS scale normalization. This directly targets equalization of the limiting contacts using the evaluator’s geometry, extending the current exact polishing beyond one-point random mutations while retaining free radial coordinates rather than reintroducing spherical or antipodal restrictions.
