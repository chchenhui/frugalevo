# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- **The current best, Generation 22 “Multistart Smoothed Distance-Ratio Optimization,” confirms that full-space soft-extrema optimization with exact-objective retention is the strongest observed design.** It reaches `min_max_ratio = 0.24`, combined score **1.00**, in **2.60 s**. Its broad deterministic multistart phase optimizes smooth approximations to both the minimum and maximum squared distances, while its high-sharpness polish accepts candidates based on the exact nonsmooth evaluator ratio. This directly reinforces the prior result from Generation 19 and earlier projected Adam-style methods: optimizing the ratio surrogate is materially better than optimizing only short-contact repulsion.

- **Exact-ratio checkpointing, rollback, and regression handling are repeatedly present in top-quality results.** Generation 22 periodically saves the exact best configuration during all 20 × 1,500 broad-search trajectories, then tracks both a global best and per-attempt local best during polishing. Its rollback after two worsening exact checkpoints restores `local_point`, clears momentum, damps the second moment, and shrinks the step scale. This mirrors the evaluator-aligned protections in Generation 24 (`1.00`, 1.74 s), Generation 27 (`1.00`, 2.34 s), and Generation 30 (`1.00`, 1.04 s), demonstrating that smooth-surrogate improvements alone are not sufficient to preserve final packing quality.

- **Continuation from diffuse to sharp pair-contact weighting consistently reaches the 0.24 tier.** Generation 22 increases beta from `9` to `254` over the main search and from `260` to `1110` during polishing. This initially distributes force across many pair distances, then concentrates updates on the actual closest and farthest contacts. Generations 20, 23, 24, 25, 27, and 29 use comparable soft-min/soft-max sharpening schedules and all report approximately `0.24`, with combined scores from `0.99` to `1.00`.

- **Unconstrained 14-point refinement outperforms purely antipodal formulations.** Generation 30 shows that antipodal geometry can be valuable as a seed when followed by full-configuration optimization, reaching `1.00` / `0.24`. In contrast, the more line-packing-centered Generation 21 reaches `0.23` / `0.94`, and Generation 26 reaches `0.22` / `0.94`. The successful pattern is therefore not antipodal symmetry itself, but allowing later optimization to break or move beyond that constraint.

## Ineffective Approaches

- **Restricting the search too strongly to antipodal line packings lowers attainable quality.** Generation 21’s seven antipodal-line portfolio plus general force search reaches only `min-max ratio = 0.23` and score `0.94` in **4.56 s**. Generation 26’s icosahedral/antipodal construction with later full-space refinement remains at `0.22` / `0.94` in **1.54 s**. Even with sophisticated tangent-space optimization and symmetry-breaking stages, these methods trail the unrestricted soft-ratio optimizers at `0.24` / `1.00`.

- **More computation without a more evaluator-aligned refinement strategy does not improve the result.** Generation 21 is the slowest among the listed new methods at **4.56 s** yet does not reach the 0.24 plateau. By comparison, Generation 22 uses a substantial 20-start, 1,500-step search plus four 850-step polish attempts, but its exact-score logic is tightly integrated into candidate retention. The data continue to support the prior finding that optimization dynamics and exact-best preservation matter more than raw restart or iteration volume.

- **Rigid or mostly structured geometries are useful initializations but not sufficient as final search spaces.** Generation 26’s icosahedral line structure gives a good geometric starting point, but its `0.22` ratio shows that preserving too much of that contact structure limits final flexibility. Generation 23 and Generation 24 instead use structured seeds only as basin-entry mechanisms, then optimize all 14 points freely; both reach about `0.24`, with scores `0.99` and `1.00`.

## Implementation Insights

- **Generation 22’s gradient is explicitly matched to a log soft-min / log soft-max distance-ratio surrogate.** For all 91 pairs, it computes squared distances once, creates numerically stable shifted exponentials around the observed minimum and maximum distances, and forms the coefficient  
  `low_weights / soft_low - high_weights / soft_high`.  
  The resulting pair forces increase short distances while reducing long distances. This is more aligned with the evaluator than a short-distance-only repulsion field, explaining the repeated superiority of the soft-min/soft-max family.

- **Projection is applied both to states and gradients.** The current best centers and RMS-normalizes every configuration after each update. In `smooth_gradient`, it additionally removes mean-gradient translation and subtracts the radial component parallel to `x`. Thus, Adam-like updates act in the tangent space of the centered, fixed-RMS manifold rather than wasting updates on translation or scale. This is consistent with the successful implementation pattern identified for Generations 11, 17, 19, 24, and 27.

- **The current best separates global basin search from local contact polishing.** The main phase uses 20 independent Gaussian starts, relatively broad soft-extrema weights, and a learning-rate decrease from `0.048` to `0.004`. The later phase begins from the best exact candidate or controlled tangent-space perturbations of it, uses much higher beta, smaller learning rates, and four attempts. This two-scale structure combines broad contact-graph discovery with local adjustment of active shortest/farthest pairs.

- **Rollback is implemented as optimizer-state control, not merely best-point storage.** When Generation 22 sees two exact-score regressions relative to the local best, it restores the local exact-best point, resets the first moment, multiplies the second moment by `0.15`, and reduces `step_scale` by `0.62`. Conversely, stable checkpoints slowly recover step scale up to `1.10`. Generation 24’s rollback refinement and Generation 27’s adaptive scaling report the same practical benefit: preventing high-beta surrogate gradients from carrying momentum through a harmful contact-graph change.

## Performance Analysis

- **Generation 22 is quality-optimal but not the fastest quality-optimal program in the observed set.** Its `1.00` combined score and `0.24` ratio match Generation 24 (`1.00`, 1.74 s), Generation 27 (`1.00`, 2.34 s), and Generation 30 (`1.00`, 1.04 s). At **2.60 s**, it is slightly faster than the prior Generation 19 benchmark at **2.87 s**, but slower than the geometry-seeded Generation 30. Thus, its added 20-start broad search and four-attempt robust polish preserve maximum quality, while simpler or more structured portfolios can achieve the same rounded ratio with lower evaluation time.

- **The 0.24 ratio remains the decisive performance boundary.** Generation 20, 23, 25, and 29 all report `0.24` but receive `0.99` rather than `1.00`, with runtimes of 3.13 s, 1.04 s, 1.48 s, and 1.05 s respectively. Generation 22, 24, 27, and 30 receive the maximum score at the same reported two-decimal ratio. This indicates that unrounded geometric quality and/or runtime-sensitive combined scoring distinguish methods even when the reported ratio rounds identically.

- **Antipodal-first methods occupy a lower observed quality tier unless they transition successfully into unrestricted point refinement.** Generation 21 (`0.23`, `0.94`, 4.56 s) and Generation 26 (`0.22`, `0.94`, 1.54 s) trail all full soft-ratio methods that reach `0.24`. Generation 30 is the important exception: it starts from optimized antipodal lines but then runs multistart full-configuration smooth ratio optimization and exact contact polishing, reaching `1.00` in 1.04 s.

- **The new evaluations strengthen the prior runtime-versus-method conclusion.** Generation 22’s 2.60-second perfect result, Generation 24’s 1.74-second perfect result, and Generation 30’s 1.04-second perfect result all substantially outperform Generation 21’s 4.56-second, `0.23` result. The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:
