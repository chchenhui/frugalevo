# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- **Annealed smooth optimization of the actual ratio is the strongest observed pattern.** The current best, **Annealed Soft-Extrema 3D Point Packing** (Generation 8), reached `min_max_ratio = 0.24` and combined score `0.98`. Its objective approximates `log(min distance) - log(max distance)` using separate softmin and softmax weights over all pairwise distances, then progressively raises sharpness from `6` to `100`. This directly targets both terms of the evaluator’s ratio rather than optimizing only minimum distance or a surrogate based on line coherence.

- **Combining structured layouts with deterministic random multistart improves basin coverage.** Generation 8 retains the best result from two explicitly structured seeds—staggered hexagonal layers with poles, and cube-plus-axis points—plus four fixed Gaussian seeds. This outperformed the purely structured **Symmetric Cube-and-Axis 14-Point Packing** (Generation 3), which achieved only `0.18`, and the mostly random **Stochastic 3D Fourteen-Point Packing** (Generation 6), which reached `0.23` but required `6.94 s`.

- **Normalization throughout optimization is consistently beneficial.** The current best recenters every update and normalizes RMS radius, removing translation and uniform scale degrees of freedom. Generation 4’s direct maximin relaxation used the same general recenter/rescale principle and reached `0.23`; Generation 8’s more refined soft-extrema objective and initialization portfolio improved this to `0.24`.

- **Unconstrained full-point optimization can exceed antipodal line packing.** Antipodal methods reliably achieved about `0.21`: Generations 1, 2, and 5 all scored `0.88`, while the symmetry-breaking extension in Generation 9 rose to `0.22` and `0.94`. The current best’s fully unconstrained 14-point refinement avoids the fixed antipodal geometry and produces the best measured ratio, `0.24`.

## Ineffective Approaches

- **Unoptimized random sampling is decisively inadequate.** **Seeded Random 3D Point Generator** (Generation 0) had a near-zero `min_max_ratio = 0.00` and combined score `0.02`. Determinism alone made the output valid and fast (`0.01 s`), but Gaussian samples do not control either close pairs or extreme diametric pairs.

- **Closed-form symmetry without refinement leaves substantial objective value unrealized.** **Symmetric Cube-and-Axis 14-Point Packing** (Generation 3) was effectively instantaneous but only reached `0.18` / `0.75`. Its radius balances selected distance classes, but its rigid construction cannot adapt all pairwise contacts to the exact minimum/maximum objective.

- **Restricting the configuration to antipodal pairs limits the observed ratio.** The seven-line spherical-code family in Generations 1, 2, and 5 used sophisticated Riemannian optimization, annealing, and multiple restarts, yet remained at `0.21`. Even Generation 9’s subsequent full-point symmetry-breaking reached only `0.22`, below the `0.24` achieved by optimizing unconstrained point arrangements from the outset.

- **Long stochastic local search did not beat the smoother direct method.** Generation 6 achieved `0.23` but cost `6.94 s`, over six times the current best’s `1.09 s`. Its strict-improvement greedy hill climbing, perturbation batches, and occasional two-point moves were robust, but the evaluation data show no score advantage over Generation 8’s differentiable annealed updates.

## Implementation Insights

- **The current best’s gradient matches its smoothed objective closely.** `_soft_extrema_gradient` constructs pairwise displacements and squared distances once, computes log distances, and assigns normalized softmin weights to short pairs and softmax weights to long pairs. The gradient uses `near_weights - far_weights`, so it simultaneously pushes apart close pairs and pulls in far pairs. This is more evaluator-aligned than antipodal coherence minimization, which optimizes maximum absolute line correlation rather than the complete point-set ratio.

- **Numerical stabilization and scale-independent updates support reliable refinement.** Generation 8 shifts log-distance values before exponentiation (`low_shift`, `high_shift`), preventing avoidable overflow in sharp soft extrema. It then subtracts the mean gradient, normalizes gradient RMS magnitude, uses momentum (`0.82` retention), and normalizes positions after every move. These details make a fixed schedule usable across highly different initializers.

- **The annealing schedule is concrete and progressive rather than static.** In the current program, sharpness increases quadratically from `6.0` to `100.0`, while the step size decreases from `0.045` to roughly `0.01575`. Early iterations therefore optimize a broad distance distribution; later iterations concentrate on active shortest and longest pairs. This is consistent with Generation 4’s successful annealed direct relaxation (`0.23`) and is likely part of why Generation 8 gains another `0.01` ratio.

- **Exact selection is separated from surrogate optimization.** After optimizing each of six initializers, Generation 8 uses `_exact_ratio`—the unsmoothed true minimum/maximum pairwise-distance ratio—to choose the returned candidate. This prevents a candidate from winning merely because it optimized the finite-sharpness approximation better, an important practical distinction when sharpness is capped at `100`.

## Performance Analysis

- **Generation 8 is the best balance of solution quality and runtime among evaluated correct programs.** Its `0.24` ratio and `0.98` combined score exceed Generation 6’s `0.23` / `0.96`, while taking `1.09 s` instead of `6.94 s`. It also exceeds Generation 4’s `0.23` / `0.95` at `2.02 s`, indicating that its initializer mix and smooth log-ratio formulation improved both quality and measured efficiency.

- **Direct full-configuration ratio optimization trends above antipodal formulations.** Antipodal optimizers scored `0.88` with `0.21` in Generations 1, 2, and 5, despite different restart counts, icosahedral initialization, continuation schedules, and polishing. Allowing full configuration refinement improved the result: Generation 9 reached `0.22`, Generation 4 and 6 reached `0.23`, and the current best reached `0.24`.

- **More computation did not monotonically improve results.** Generation 5’s 20 restarts and polishing cost `1.53 s` but remained at `0.21`; Generation 6 spent `6.94 s` for `0.23`; the current best used six candidates × 1,800 iterations and reached the highest ratio in `1.09 s`. The results favor objective alignment, stable annealing, and effective initializer diversity over simply adding search iterations or restarts.

- **The score scale is strongly correlated with the reported geometric ratio.** The transition from Generation 0’s `0.00` ratio / `0.02` score to Generation 3’s `0.18` / `0.75`, antipodal approaches’ `0.21` / `0.88`, direct relaxation’s `0.23` / `0.95–0.96`, and Generation 8’s `0.24` / `0.98` shows that combined-score improvements closely track small gains in `min_max_ratio`.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Add a final active-contact polishing phase after the annealed soft-extrema refinement.** Starting from each `_refine` result, identify the few shortest and longest pairs, then run 100–300 smaller projected/normalized updates using much higher sharpness (for example, `150–500`) or an explicit objective based on the current minimum and maximum distance classes. Generation 8 already benefits from exact-ratio selection after surrogate optimization; a short local polish can reduce the remaining mismatch between the sharpness-100 surrogate and the true nonsmooth ratio.

2. **Use a continuation portfolio with several annealing schedules rather than one fixed schedule for every initializer.** Keep the successful `6 → 100` quadratic sharpness path, but additionally try a slower late-stage schedule (such as `6 → 200` or `8 → 300`) and a piecewise schedule that holds moderate sharpness before rapidly concentrating on contacts. Evaluate all resulting candidates with `_exact_ratio`; this extends the current multistart strategy while exploring whether different basins benefit from different soft-extrema continuation paths.

3. **Introduce deterministic perturb-and-refine restarts around the best structured candidates.** After refining the hexagonal-layer and cube-axis seeds once, generate a small number of perturbed copies—e.g., add RMS-scaled Gaussian noise of `0.02–0.08`, renormalize, and rerun a shorter 500–900 iteration annealed refinement. This preserves the demonstrated value of structured initializers while allowing controlled symmetry breaking, which is important because fully unconstrained layouts outperform rigid symmetric and antipodal constructions.

4. **Adapt the step size and momentum using exact-ratio checkpoints during late optimization.** Every 25–50 iterations after the midpoint, compute `_exact_ratio`; if it declines repeatedly, reduce the step size or reset/dampen velocity, and if it improves, retain the current schedule. The current normalized momentum method is stable and fast, but late high-sharpness gradients can cause oscillation among changing active pairs; checkpoint-based damping targets that failure mode without changing the evaluator or abandoning the successful smooth objective.

5. **Expand initializer diversity with near-optimal geometric families, not additional purely random seeds.** Add a few deterministic seeds derived from the existing staggered-layer construction by varying layer height, angular offset, ring radius, and pole position, plus slightly distorted cube-axis layouts with independently scaled axes. This is likely more efficient than many Gaussian restarts because prior results show that structured seeds plus limited random multistart outperform either rigid symmetry alone or broad stochastic search.
