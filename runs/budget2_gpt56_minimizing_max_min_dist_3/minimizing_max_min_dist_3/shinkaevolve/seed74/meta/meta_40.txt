# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- **Projected full-space soft min/max optimization with exact-objective retention remains the dominant successful family.** The current best, Generation 22, reaches `min_max_ratio = 0.24` and combined score `1.00` in **2.60 s** by optimizing a log-soft-min/log-soft-max squared-distance ratio while retaining points only when the exact nonsmooth ratio improves. New programs reinforce this: Generation 33 (*Deterministic 14-Point Sphere Packing Search*) reaches `1.00`, `0.24` in **2.02 s**, and Generation 39 (*Antipodal Line-Packing Distance Optimizer*) reaches `1.00`, `0.24` in **1.85 s** after transitioning from structured lines into unrestricted point-level optimization.

- **Continuation from diffuse pair weighting to concentrated contact weighting consistently reaches the top quality tier.** Generation 22 ramps `beta` from `9` to `254` in broad search, then from `260` to `1110` during polish. This same staged sharpening appears in Generation 31 (`1.00`, `0.24`, **2.16 s**), Generation 34 (`1.00`, `0.24`, **3.21 s**), Generation 35 (`1.00`, `0.24`, **3.64 s**), and Generation 40 (`1.00`, `0.24`, **3.63 s**). The repeated result supports the interpretation that early smooth forces help identify viable contact graphs, while late sharp forces adjust the limiting nearest/farthest contacts.

- **Structured geometry works best as a basin-entry portfolio rather than as a permanent constraint.** Generation 22 uses 20 randomized full-space starts, while the new successful methods diversify this with layered, cube-axis, antiprism, ring-and-pole, and antipodal-line seeds. Generation 31’s layered/random portfolio and tiered survivor refinement reaches `1.00`; Generation 33’s antiprism/pole plus cube/axis portfolio reaches `1.00`; and Generation 39 starts from seven-line antipodal geometry but explicitly uses symmetry-breaking full-space candidates, also reaching `1.00`. These results extend the prior conclusion that seed geometry improves basin coverage, but final freedom of all 14 points is essential.

- **Exact-monotone final polishing is repeatedly associated with reliable 0.24-level solutions.** Generation 22’s four 850-step high-beta polishing attempts retain `local_point` and `best_points` according to the exact evaluator. Generation 28 (`1.00`, `0.24`, **3.87 s**) similarly uses exact-monotonic multi-branch polishing and line-search scales; Generation 34 uses exact-score checkpointing plus monotonic polishing; and Generation 37 (`1.00`, `0.24`, **4.80 s**) uses multi-scale exact-monotone high-sharpness refinement. The common mechanism is protection against surrogate-driven motion across a nonsmooth contact transition that lowers the actual min/max ratio.

## Ineffective Approaches

- **Purely or overly constrained antipodal formulations remain risky, despite one successful hybrid.** Previous Generation 21 achieved only `0.23` / `0.94` in **4.56 s**, and Generation 26 only `0.22` / `0.94` in **1.54 s**, because their line-packing/icosahedral structure limited accessible final contact graphs. Generation 39 does not contradict this pattern: it succeeds because it begins with line structure but then applies full 14-point smooth-gradient optimization, perturbation restarts, and symmetry-breaking candidates. The effective ingredient is therefore not line packing alone, but escape from the line constraint.

- **Structured initialization without sufficiently strong continuation and polishing can stop below the top tier.** Generation 32 (*Layered Soft-Gradient Point Packing*) uses strong staggered-hexagon and cube-based initializations, exact checkpointing, and focused final refinement, yet obtains only `0.22` and combined score `0.93` in **2.24 s**. Relative to Generation 22 and the 1.00-scoring programs, its optimization budget and late-stage basin exploration appear less robust: it emphasizes the strongest candidate rather than maintaining several competitive survivor basins through high-sharpness polish.

- **More branches or longer runtime do not by themselves improve the evaluator result.** Generation 37 achieves the same `1.00` / `0.24` quality as Generation 22 but takes **4.80 s**, nearly 1.85× the current best’s runtime. Generation 28 (`3.87 s`), Generation 35 (`3.64 s`), and Generation 40 (`3.63 s`) also attain the quality plateau more slowly. This supports the earlier finding that exact-aligned state management and targeted survivor selection matter more than simply increasing multistart/polish volume.

## Implementation Insights

- **Generation 22’s surrogate is directly aligned with the evaluator’s ratio.** It computes all 91 squared pair distances once per gradient call, constructs numerically stable soft-min and soft-max weights using shifts by the observed `lo` and `hi`, and uses  
  `low_weights / soft_low - high_weights / soft_high`  
  as pair coefficients. The induced force simultaneously expands short pair distances and contracts excessively long pair distances. This is materially more evaluator-aligned than nearest-contact repulsion alone, and matches the successful soft min/max designs in Generations 31, 33, 34, and 40.

- **The current best removes both irrelevant state motion and irrelevant gradient motion.** Every update is recentered and RMS-normalized through `normalize`, enforcing translation and scale invariance. In addition, `smooth_gradient` subtracts mean gradient and removes its component parallel to `x`, producing a tangent-space update on the centered fixed-RMS manifold. This avoids spending adaptive-momentum capacity on translation or uniform rescaling and is consistent with the stable projected-gradient behavior reported in Generation 33 and Generation 36.

- **Generation 22’s optimizer-state rollback is more substantial than simple incumbent storage.** At exact checkpoints, a candidate must improve the exact ratio to replace `local_point` or `best_points`. After two regressions, the program restores `local_point`, clears first momentum, scales the second-moment accumulator by `0.15`, and contracts `step_scale` by `0.62`; successful checkpoints gradually restore scale up to `1.10`. This detailed response prevents accumulated momentum from continuing through a harmful active-contact change. The same general principle appears in Generation 28’s checkpoint rollback, Generation 33’s rollback on regressions, and Generation 40’s responsive beta retreat.

- **The current best separates broad discovery from local refinement in a computationally disciplined way.** Its broad phase uses 20 independent Gaussian starts × 1,500 iterations, but exact scoring occurs only every 25 steps. It then performs four 850-step polishing trajectories, with attempt 0 starting from the global exact incumbent and attempts 1–3 using deterministic tangent-space perturbations. Generation 31’s first-tier/second-tier selection and Generation 36’s top-five survivor refinement use the same efficient allocation principle: broad enough search to identify distinct basins, followed by concentrated high-beta work only on promising configurations.

## Performance Analysis

- **Generation 22 remains a strong quality/runtime reference, but newer geometry-assisted variants are faster at the same reported quality.** Its `1.00`, `0.24`, **2.60 s** result is faster than Generation 28 (**3.87 s**), Generation 34 (**3.21 s**), Generation 35 (**3.64 s**), Generation 37 (**4.80 s**), and Generation 40 (**3.63 s**), all of which also reach `1.00` / `0.24`. However, Generation 33 reaches the same maximum score in **2.02 s**, Generation 39 in **1.85 s**, and Generation 31 in **2.16 s**. The best observed quality remains tied, while runtime differentiates how efficiently each portfolio reaches that plateau.

- **The reported `0.24` ratio is necessary but not sufficient to distinguish all results.** Generation 36 reports `0.24` but receives `0.99` in **2.72 s**, whereas Generations 22, 31, 33, 34, 35, 37, 39, and 40 receive `1.00` at the same rounded ratio. As noted in the prior insights, this likely reflects unrounded ratio differences and/or the runtime component of combined scoring. Exact incumbent tracking and final contact polishing appear to matter for crossing this narrow unrounded threshold.

- **The new results strengthen the evidence that seed diversity affects efficiency more than final attainable quality.** Random-only broad continuation in Generation 22 obtains the top score in **2.60 s**. Adding effective structured basins reduces runtime in Generation 31 (**2.16 s**) and Generation 33 (**2.02 s**) without changing the reported `0.24` optimum. Conversely, broader but less selectively managed portfolios, such as Generation 37 (**4.80 s**), produce no visible quality gain over the current best.

- **There is a clear lower tier around `0.22–0.23`, separated from the exact-polished full-space methods.** Generation 32 reaches `0.22` / `0.93`, while the prior constrained antipodal Generations 21 and 26 reach `0.23` / `0.94` and `0.22` / `0.94`. In contrast, the current best and most continuation-plus-exact-polish programs consistently reach `0.24` and approximately `0.99–1.00`. The observed dividing line is not merely initialization quality: it is unrestricted full-space refinement coupled with evaluator-based retention through late nonsmooth contact-graph changes.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Replace a subset of the 20 Gaussian starts with a compact structured-seed portfolio, then keep the same unrestricted optimizer.** Add normalized cube-plus-axis, staggered antiprism/pole, layered-ring, and slightly symmetry-broken antipodal-line seeds, each with tangent-space noise of about `0.02–0.05`; retain perhaps 8–12 random starts for diversity. Generations 31, 33, and 39 reached the same `0.24` tier faster by using geometry to enter strong basins, while their success depended on releasing all 14 points into full-space optimization exactly as Generation 22 does.

2. **Use a two-tier survivor allocation instead of running every start for all 1,500 broad iterations.** Run all random and structured seeds for roughly `450–600` steps under the existing low-to-medium beta continuation, score them exactly, retain the best 5–8 distinct candidates, then spend the remaining budget on only those survivors with beta continued toward `250–350`. This preserves the current program’s exact-objective selection and continuation behavior, but follows the efficient survivor-refinement pattern of Generations 31 and 36 and may reduce time or provide more late-stage basin coverage.

3. **Add a late-polish active-contact correction to the existing soft min/max gradient.** At high beta, identify pairs within a narrow relative band of the exact minimum and maximum squared distances—for example, `dsq <= lo * 1.015` and `dsq >= hi * 0.985`—and form an equal-weight or distance-weighted exact active-set ratio gradient. Blend it conservatively with the current surrogate, such as `grad = 0.85 * soft_grad + 0.15 * active_grad`, then apply the same centering and radial tangent projection; this targets the nonsmooth contacts that actually determine the retained objective without abandoning the successful smooth continuation.

4. **Turn the four final polishing attempts into schedule-diverse exact-monotone branches.** Keep one current `260→1110` branch, but add a moderate branch such as `180→650` with slightly larger learning rates and an aggressive branch such as `500→1500` with smaller learning rates and checkpoints every `10–15` steps; initialize branches from the incumbent plus deterministic tangent perturbations. Multiple continuation schedules have repeatedly reached the top tier, and this is a low-risk extension because exact-ratio checkpointing remains the sole criterion for retaining candidates.

5. **Strengthen rollback around harmful contact-graph transitions with immediate reset and temporary beta retreat.** When an exact checkpoint falls materially below `local_best`—for example by more than `2e-4` relative—or after the existing second regression, restore `local_point`, clear both `first` and `second`, reduce `step_scale`, and use `beta *= 0.75` for the next `50–75` iterations before resuming the nominal schedule. Generation 22 already benefits from rollback, while related successful methods show that reducing sharpness after a failed transition can prevent high-beta momentum from repeatedly crossing into the same inferior contact arrangement.
