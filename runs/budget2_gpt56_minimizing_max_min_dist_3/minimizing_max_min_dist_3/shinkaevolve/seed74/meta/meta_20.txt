# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- **Projected annealed optimization of the full, evaluator-aligned ratio is the dominant successful approach.** The current best program, **Generation 19: Annealed Projected Distance-Ratio Optimization**, reaches the maximum combined score of **1.00** and `min_max_ratio = 0.24`. Like the other top-scoring projected optimizers—Generation 11 (`1.00`, `0.24`, 2.54 s), Generation 17 (`1.00`, `0.24`, 2.59 s), and Generation 18 (`1.00`, `0.24`, 1.55 s)—it optimizes a soft approximation of `log(min squared distance / max squared distance)` rather than only repelling close pairs. This confirms the earlier trend that directly controlling both shortest and longest distances is more effective than maximin-only or line-packing objectives.

- **Exact nonsmooth objective checkpointing is consistently associated with the 0.24 plateau.** Generation 19 periodically evaluates `exact_ratio_squared(x)` and retains `best_points` independently of the smooth objective. Its final 750-step phase is also checkpointed against the exact ratio. Generation 11, 15, 17, and 18 all explicitly retained the best exact min/max ratio during smooth refinement and each reached `0.24` with scores from `0.99` to `1.00`. This protects against surrogate drift: at large soft-extrema sharpness, a configuration may improve the finite-temperature objective without improving the actual minimum/maximum pair ratio.

- **Continuation plus multistart basin coverage reliably improves on rigid and single-mode searches.** Generation 19 uses **18 independent Gaussian starts**, 1,450 updates each, then a sharp final continuation; Generations 11 and 17 use the same broad pattern and also reach `0.24`. Generation 18 reaches the same quality more efficiently by augmenting random starts with staggered-hexagon and cube-plus-axis seeds. Across these results, the shared successful element is not a single fixed geometry but exploration of multiple contact-graph basins followed by sharp local refinement.

- **Adaptive late-stage polishing is a validated refinement mechanism.** The current best differs from simpler continuation variants through its regression logic: after repeated exact-score declines, it reduces `step_scale` by `0.65`, clears the first moment, dampens the second moment, and resumes from the current configuration while preserving the global exact best. This explicitly addresses the late-stage mismatch between momentum following smooth contacts and the changing identities of true closest/farthest pairs. Generation 19’s `1.00` result validates this safeguard, although its final ratio remains tied with the other leading 0.24 methods.

## Ineffective Approaches

- **Rigid analytic symmetry is fast but leaves measurable packing quality unrealized.** **Generation 12: Twisted Hexagon 14-Point Sphere Configuration** is effectively instantaneous and valid, but achieves only `min_max_ratio = 0.22` and score `0.93`. Its two twisted hexagons plus poles equalize selected distance classes, but its fixed parameterization cannot adapt all 91 pairwise distances. This is consistent with the earlier cube-and-axis closed-form construction, which reached only `0.18`.

- **Purely stochastic local search is substantially slower without surpassing smooth projected optimization.** **Generation 13: Guided Stochastic 3D Point Packing** reaches `0.22` / `0.93` but takes **7.48 s**, despite ten deterministic restarts, close-neighbor repulsion, two-point moves, cooling, and batch selection. **Generation 14: Greedy Search with Gradient Packing Polish** improves to `0.24` / `0.98`, but still needs **6.72 s**. Neither beats Generation 19’s perfect score in 2.87 s or Generation 18’s perfect score in 1.55 s. The feedback supports the conclusion that guided discrete moves can find viable configurations, but smooth full-configuration gradients allocate search effort more efficiently.

- **More restarts and iterations alone do not guarantee the best outcome.** **Generation 10** uses 24 deterministic restarts with incumbent-guided perturbation and reaches `0.23` / `0.97` in 3.07 s; **Generation 16** likewise uses 24 starts and 1,500 iterations, yet obtains `0.23` / `0.96` in 2.89 s. In contrast, the 18-start Adam-style methods in Generations 11, 17, and 19 reach `0.24`. The performance gap indicates that optimizer dynamics, exact-state retention, and late polishing matter more than simply expanding restart count.

- **Constrained antipodal line-packing formulations remain below unconstrained point optimization.** Previous antipodal methods remained around `0.21`, and even later symmetry-breaking variants reached only `0.22`. The evaluated Generation 12 analytic symmetric arrangement and Generation 13 force-biased search similarly stop at `0.22`. The repeated `0.24` results from unrestricted 14-point optimizers reinforce that fixed antipodal or strongly symmetric contact structures constrain the attainable ratio.

## Implementation Insights

- **Generation 19’s smooth gradient is tightly matched to its stated objective.** For all 91 point pairs, it computes squared distances once, builds stable soft-min weights
  `exp(-beta * (dsq - mn))` and soft-max weights `exp(beta * (dsq - mx))`, then uses the coefficient
  `low / soft_min - high / soft_max`.
  The resulting gradient simultaneously expands short-distance contacts and contracts diameter contacts. Numerical shifts by `mn` and `mx` prevent exponential overflow as the final sharpness rises to `beta = 950`.

- **Projection removes irrelevant degrees of freedom at every update.** The current best recenters configurations, normalizes RMS radius, removes mean gradient, and subtracts the radial gradient component before applying Adam-style ascent. The normalization means the optimizer cannot obtain artificial objective changes through translation or uniform rescaling. This recurring design is shared by Generations 11, 17, and 18, all of which achieved `0.24`, and is materially stronger than stochastic searches that only intermittently repair geometry.

- **The current best combines two time scales of annealing.** The main search increases beta from `10` to `220` using `u ** 1.65`, with learning rate falling from `0.0495` to `0.0045`. It then runs a separate final phase from beta `300` to `950`, using smaller steps. This delayed concentration on true extremal pairs reflects the successful continuation behavior seen in Generation 15’s delayed sharpening and Generation 18’s sharper final polish. Broad early soft weights improve a general distance distribution; high-beta late updates focus on the active contact graph.

- **Adam-style per-coordinate scaling improves robustness across starts.** Generation 19 uses first/second moments with `(0.88, 0.96)` during basin search and `(0.90, 0.975)` in polishing. Dividing by `sqrt(second_moment) + 1e-8` avoids a few large pair-contact gradient components dominating all point coordinates. Relative to normalized-gradient momentum approaches in Generations 10 and 16, the Adam-style projected variants are the ones repeatedly reported at `0.24` and score `1.00`.

## Performance Analysis

- **Generation 19 is quality-optimal in the observed evaluation set, but not runtime-optimal among tied-quality methods.** It achieves the maximum **combined score 1.00** and `min_max_ratio = 0.24` in **2.87 s**. It matches Generation 11 (`1.00`, `0.24`, 2.54 s) and Generation 17 (`1.00`, `0.24`, 2.59 s), while Generation 18 reaches the same maximum score and ratio in **1.55 s**. Thus, adaptive late polishing preserves top quality, but the available data do not show it exceeding the established 0.24 ratio ceiling or improving runtime over the structured-initializer variant.

- **The major observed quality tiers remain clear.** Closed-form/rigid symmetric configurations reach roughly `0.18–0.22`; antipodal optimizers sit near `0.21`; guided stochastic or simpler annealed methods frequently reach `0.22–0.23`; and full projected soft-extrema methods with exact checkpointing consistently reach `0.24`. Score follows the same progression: Generation 12 and 13 score `0.93` at `0.22`, Generation 10 scores `0.97` at `0.23`, Generation 15 scores `0.99` at `0.24`, and Generations 11, 17, 18, and 19 score `1.00` at `0.24`.

- **A 0.01 ratio improvement is significant in the combined metric.** Generation 10 and Generation 16 obtain `0.23`, with scores `0.97` and `0.96`; the projected Adam-style methods improve that measured ratio to `0.24` and receive `1.00`. Similarly, Generation 14 reports `0.24` but only `0.98`, likely reflecting other aspects of the combined evaluation such as substantially higher 6.72-second runtime. The data therefore show both geometry and runtime affecting combined score, even when reported ratios round to the same two decimal places.

- **The strongest methods improve efficiency through objective alignment rather than brute-force search.** Generation 13 requires 7.48 s for `0.22`, Generation 14 requires 6.72 s for `0.24`, and Generation 10 uses 3.07 s for `0.23`. In comparison, Generation 19’s 18-start, 1,450-step search plus 750-step adaptive polish reaches `0.24` in 2.87 s, while Generation 18 reaches the same result in 1.55 s through geometry-informed initializers. The evaluations consistently favor stable differentiable ratio optimization, projected normalization, and exact-best tracking over longer stochastic move schedules or purely analytic constructions.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Replace a subset of Gaussian starts with geometry-informed and perturbed structured seeds.** Port Generation 18’s staggered-hexagon and cube-plus-axis initializers, then add 2–4 RMS-normalized Gaussian perturbations (`σ≈0.02–0.06`) around each before running the existing projected annealing. This preserves Generation 19’s strong multistart/exact-checkpoint pipeline while improving basin coverage more efficiently than adding random restarts, with potential runtime gains toward Generation 18’s 1.55 s result.

2. **Use a two-tier restart allocation instead of fully refining every start for 1,450 steps.** Run all random and structured seeds through a cheaper shared first stage, such as 400–650 iterations with beta `10→80`, rank them by exact ratio, then continue only the best 4–8 candidates through the current `80→220` annealing and sharp polish. The current method establishes that multiple basins matter, but most starts are unlikely to merit the full schedule; survivor selection can free compute for better polishing or richer seed diversity without increasing evaluation time.

3. **Branch the final continuation into a small portfolio of sharpness/step schedules from the best checkpoint.** From `best_points`, run several short exact-checkpointed polish trajectories—for example the current `300→950` schedule, a slower `220→600` schedule, and an aggressive `500→1400` schedule with lower learning rates—and retain the global exact best across branches. The observed 0.24 plateau is consistently reached through continuation, but different basins can have different active-contact transitions; a small late-stage portfolio directly targets that remaining surrogate/contact-graph sensitivity.

4. **Add explicit active-pair stabilization during the last high-beta updates.** At each late checkpoint, identify a small band of near-minimum and near-maximum squared-distance pairs, such as distances within 1–3% of the current extrema, and optionally blend their exact-class gradient with the existing soft-extrema gradient. This extends the successful evaluator-aligned objective rather than replacing it: soft weights remain useful when contacts switch, while a bounded active-set term can reduce late drift caused by finite-beta averaging over nonextremal pairs.

5. **Make adaptive polishing revert to the last exact-best state after repeated deterioration, not only reduce momentum and step scale.** When two or three consecutive checkpoints are below the best value by a meaningful tolerance, restore `x = best_points.copy()`, reset first moments, heavily damp second moments, reduce the step scale, and resume with a slightly lower beta or learning rate. Generation 19 already validates regression-aware damping; explicit rollback prevents the sharp optimizer from spending the remaining iterations in a poorer contact basin while retaining exact-best checkpointing as the authoritative selection rule.
