# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns
- **Joint optimization of centers and unequal radii is the decisive improvement.** The initial *Concentric Ring Circle Packing* used fixed centers followed by greedy pairwise radius reductions and achieved only `sum_radii=0.96` / score `0.36`. Programs that optimized all 52 coordinates plus 26 radii with boundary and pairwise constraints consistently reached `2.50–2.63`; the current best *Multi-Start SLSQP Circle Packing* reaches `2.63`, score `1.00`.
- **Multi-start asymmetric staggered seeds improved over single-layout local optimization.** Generation 3’s direct SLSQP with a lattice seed and one jittered restart reached `2.62` in `0.68 s`, while the current best evaluates four row layouts—`(5,6,5,5,5)`, `(5,6,5,6,4)`, `(6,5,5,5,5)`, and `(5,5,6,5,5)`—with two deterministic perturbations each and reaches `2.63`. This supports the evaluation feedback that asymmetric seeding avoids inferior symmetric local optima.
- **Strict feasibility selection plus a tiny final global shrink preserves perfect validity.** The current best retains an SLSQP result only when `min(inequalities(proposal)) >= -1e-7`, then applies a `1 - 1e-10` uniform shrink. It achieved `validity=1.00` while losing negligible objective value. Generations 8 and 11 used the same broad pattern and also obtained `2.63`, `1.00` combined score.
- **Variable radii matter near boundaries.** The best-performing methods all allow independently optimized radii. This outperformed fixed-radius or post-processed constructions: Generation 4’s staggered joint optimization achieved `2.50`, while the better multi-start unequal-radius searches achieved `2.58–2.63`.

## Ineffective Approaches
- **Clipped concentric rings with sequential greedy scaling were strongly suboptimal.** The initial program clipped outer centers into the square, collapsing several outer-ring positions near borders, then reduced radii pair-by-pair. Although valid, this produced only `0.96` total radius, far below the `2.63` current best. Sequential scaling is conservative because a reduction made for one collision is not jointly reconsidered across the packing.
- **Single or weakly diversified initializations commonly stalled below the best basin.** *SLSQP Staggered Circle Packing* (Generation 4) reached `2.50` / `0.95`, and the initial hexagonal SLSQP program (Generation 1) reached `2.58` / `0.98`. Both used strong local constrained optimization, but their more limited seeding was associated with lower local optima than the current best’s 12 starts across four layouts.
- **More starts alone did not guarantee improvement unless the row topology was useful.** Generations 5–7 used multi-start SLSQP and analytic constraints but remained at `2.58–2.59`, below the `2.63` achieved by Generations 8, 11, and the current program. The performance evidence indicates that the specific asymmetric row-layout portfolio, not merely perturbation count, is important.
- **Post-hoc radius construction is not competitive with direct radius variables.** Generation 2 explicitly retained `compute_max_radii`, but its main successful path optimized radii directly and reached `2.62`. The helper’s pairwise proportional scaling resembles the weak initial program’s approach and is not part of the high-scoring construction.

## Implementation Insights
- **The current best uses a compact vectorized nonlinear formulation.** It packs the optimization vector as 52 center coordinates followed by 26 radii, constructs all `26 choose 2 = 325` pair constraints through `np.triu_indices`, and combines those with four wall constraints per circle. This directly matches the geometric objective rather than deriving radii after center placement.
- **Its candidate-selection logic separates optimization quality from numerical safety.** Rather than accepting every SLSQP return, the program checks finite values and explicit constraint feasibility before comparing objective values. The later scaling is deliberately only a rounding safeguard. This matches the feedback for Generations 8 and 11: strict feasibility handling reached high objective values without relying on invalid solutions.
- **The current initialization is intentionally topology-diverse.** `make_seed` provides equally distributed points within each row, while the layouts vary which row contains the sixth circle. Deterministic jitter from `np.random.default_rng(26031991)` breaks exact lattice symmetry reproducibly. This is more targeted than random restarts and aligns with the `2.63` result.
- **There is an efficiency tradeoff in the current implementation.** Unlike several earlier SLSQP variants, the current best does not supply analytic Jacobians; nevertheless it reaches the top observed score. Its `2.46 s` evaluation time is materially slower than Generation 3’s `0.68 s` at `2.62`, reflecting the cost of 12 SLSQP starts with `maxiter=2200` and numerical differentiation.

## Performance Analysis
- **The progression shows a large gain from formulation, then a narrow gain from globalized initialization.** Moving from the initial greedy construction (`0.96`) to joint SLSQP optimization produced `2.50–2.62`, a gain of roughly `1.54–1.66` in summed radius. The final improvement from solid single/multi-start local solutions (`2.58–2.62`) to the best asymmetric multi-start result (`2.63`) is smaller but sufficient for the reported perfect `target_ratio=1.00`.
- **The current best is tied for the strongest measured quality.** It matches Generation 8 and Generation 11 at `sum_radii=2.63`, `validity=1.00`, and combined score `1.00`. It exceeds Generation 3 and Generation 2 (`2.62`, score `0.99`), Generation 5 (`2.59`), and Generations 1, 6, and 7 (`2.58`).
- **Runtime does not monotonically predict packing quality.** Generation 3 achieved `2.62` in only `0.68 s`, whereas the current best needed `2.46 s` for `2.63`. Likewise, Generation 6 took `1.63 s` but reached only `2.58`. The extra runtime is beneficial here primarily because it explores more meaningful seed geometries, not because SLSQP iterations alone guarantee a better solution.
- **Validity was consistently protected across optimizer-based methods.** Every SLSQP-based program reported `validity=1.00`, including lower-scoring `2.50` and top-scoring `2.63` variants. Therefore, score differences among these programs are attributable chiefly to packing quality and local-optimum selection, rather than failures of feasibility enforcement.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Expand the asymmetric topology portfolio around the winning five-row patterns.** Add layouts such as `(4,6,6,5,5)`, `(5,5,6,6,4)`, `(6,4,6,5,5)`, and reversed/reflected row assignments, while retaining the current four proven layouts. The evidence suggests that which rows contain the extra circles matters more than merely increasing random restarts, so prioritize structurally distinct row-count patterns rather than many perturbations of the same lattice.

2. **Use continuation from the best feasible candidate with targeted contact-breaking perturbations.** After the first multi-start pass selects its best SLSQP solution, launch several short second-stage SLSQP optimizations from that solution after perturbing only selected interior centers by roughly `1e-3` to `5e-3`, keeping radii slightly shrunk first (for example `r *= 0.995`). This preserves the high-quality unequal-radius structure already found while giving SLSQP a chance to cross a nearby active-contact basin and recover a slightly better `2.635+` arrangement.

3. **Provide analytic gradients/Jacobians for the objective and geometric inequalities.** The current vectorized formulation is correct but uses numerical differentiation across 78 variables and hundreds of constraints; supply a constant objective Jacobian and derivatives for wall and pair-separation constraints. This should reduce the cost of each SLSQP start, allowing either more topology-diverse starts within the same runtime budget or tighter/more reliable convergence from the existing successful starts.

4. **Introduce a staged feasibility-and-objective solve instead of optimizing full radii immediately.** Begin each seed with a low-radius feasibility relaxation or center-spreading phase, then progressively raise a common radius floor or scale the radii toward their optimized values before the final unequal-radius SLSQP pass. Such homotopy can prevent early convergence to a poor contact graph, while the final direct optimization still retains the demonstrated advantage of independently variable radii.

5. **Add deterministic transformed variants of every successful seed and retain strict candidate filtering.** For each asymmetric layout, include horizontal reflection, vertical reflection, and optionally a small row-dependent horizontal offset pattern before applying the existing jitter; use separate deterministic RNG streams so these variants are reproducible. Although the square itself is symmetric, finite-precision optimization and asymmetric contact formation can make transformed initializations converge to different local optima; continue accepting only explicitly feasible proposals and applying the final negligible global shrink.
