# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- **The current best program’s direct joint optimization remains a highly effective baseline.** Its 78-variable formulation jointly optimizes 52 center coordinates and 26 independent radii, reaches `sum_radii=2.63`, `validity=1.00`, and `combined_score=1.00` in `1.57 s`. Its four asymmetric five-row layouts—each with an unperturbed seed and two deterministic jitters—give a compact 12-start portfolio that reliably reaches a target-quality basin without staging or excessive polishing.

- **Asymmetric staggered topology portfolios consistently produce full-score solutions.** Generation 32’s staged layered/reflected starts, Generation 33’s ten staggered seed topologies, Generation 35’s jittered row layouts, Generation 37’s triangular/transposed variants, and Generation 39’s deterministic five-row variants all reached `2.63` and `combined_score=1.00`. This reinforces the prior observation that controlled topology diversity and symmetry breaking are more important than generic random restarting.

- **Analytic Jacobians plus strict feasibility repair are shared by every strong correct program.** The current best, Generations 30, 32–39, and the previously identified high performers all use exact wall and pairwise-contact derivatives with SLSQP, followed by conservative inward scaling. This combination repeatedly yielded `validity=1.00`, including the current best’s `1.57 s` result and Generation 36’s slightly faster `1.55 s`, `2.63`, full-score result.

- **Fixed-center LP initialization can replace random perturbation without sacrificing quality.** Generation 36 achieved `sum_radii=2.63`, `validity=1.00`, and `combined_score=1.00` in the best reported runtime among the new summaries, `1.55 s`. Its layered asymmetric seeds and LP-derived feasible unequal radii gave SLSQP a strong starting point while avoiding the broader random-jitter search used by several other successful variants.

## Ineffective Approaches

- **A sophisticated multi-stage strategy can fail completely if output or feasibility handling is incompatible.** Generation 31 used common-radius continuation, asymmetric perturbations, analytic Jacobians, multi-start search, and scaling, yet received zeros for radius sum, target ratio, validity, time, and combined score. Since comparable methods were valid elsewhere, this result indicates an implementation/runtime/output-validation failure rather than evidence that unequal-radius continuation is intrinsically weak.

- **More search phases and memetic refinements did not improve the measured objective beyond the compact direct solve.** Generation 33 added elite re-optimization and deterministic interior mutations (`2.42 s`); Generation 39 added contact-escape refinements (`2.99 s`); Generation 35 used broader jittered multistart and selective polishing (`4.83 s`). ## Implementation Insights

- **The current best’s constraint representation is compact and directly aligned with SLSQP.** It creates `4 × 26 = 104` wall constraints and `26 choose 2 = 325` pairwise non-overlap constraints, for 429 inequalities total. Pair constraints use  
  `sqrt(dx² + dy²) - r_i - r_j >= 0`,  
  with exact normalized center-gradient terms and `-1` radius derivatives. This avoids finite-difference cost and helps explain its strong `2.63` result in `1.57 s`.

- **Candidate selection is based on independently checked geometric feasibility, not solver status alone.** The current best accepts a candidate only if all values are finite and `min(inequalities(proposal)) >= -1e-7`, then compares actual radius sums. This is important because SLSQP may return marginal or unfinished iterates; Generation 30 and the other correct programs similarly benefited from final repair, while Generation 31 demonstrates that sophisticated optimization without evaluator-compatible handling can receive no credit.

- **Deterministic diversity is implemented economically in the current best.** A fixed RNG seed, four explicit row layouts, and only two small `±0.012` jitters per layout produce 12 reproducible starts. This is much narrower than Generation 33’s ten-topology memetic search or Generation 35’s broader multistart procedure, yet reaches the same full-score objective faster.

- **The final scale-down is objective-neutral relative to its validity benefit.** After clipping centers and checking walls/pairs, the current best computes the minimum allowable border and pair scaling factor, caps it at one, and multiplies by `1 - 1e-10`. This certification pattern is corroborated by Generations 30 and 32–39: nearly all correct implementations report `validity=1.00` despite optimizer tolerances. Generation 36’s global normalization and Generation 39’s uniform shrink follow the same effective principle.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Replace a few redundant jitter starts with structurally distinct asymmetric five-row layouts while keeping exactly 12 solves.** Retain the current four layouts but test alternatives such as `(4,6,6,5,5)`, `(5,5,6,6,4)`, `(6,4,6,5,5)`, and vertical reversals; use one unperturbed seed plus selected jitters so runtime remains near the current `1.57 s`. The evidence favors topology/contact-graph diversity over simply adding more random restarts, and weaker `2.60–2.62` outcomes show that some otherwise valid layouts enter inferior basins.

2. **Add deterministic transformed variants of the strongest layouts rather than additional IID jitter.** Before optimization, apply a small alternating row shift, e.g. `±0.006–0.010` by row parity, a mild horizontal shear proportional to row index, or reflection followed by a non-symmetric shift; clip only if necessary and preserve feasible small initial radii. Reflected/staggered programs repeatedly reached the `2.63` basin, while these transforms alter the initial active-contact trajectory even when the underlying layout is geometrically similar.

3. **Use feasible unequal-radius initialization for a small subset of starts.** Keep the uniform `0.025` initialization for baseline comparability, but for 3–4 promising layouts assign larger tentative radii to sparse boundary/corner positions (`0.027–0.030`) and smaller radii to dense interior positions (`0.021–0.024`), then globally scale the whole radius vector against wall and pair limits before SLSQP. Generation 36 demonstrated that LP-derived or otherwise feasible unequal radii can match full quality slightly faster, and radius asymmetry can help avoid the symmetric local solutions that end near `2.62`.

4. **Introduce an elite-only second optimization pass with tighter termination settings.** Perform the existing direct analytic-Jacobian runs with a somewhat lower first-pass budget, retain the best two feasible proposals by actual radius sum, then re-run only those from their own solution vectors with `ftol=1e-12` or `1e-13` and `maxiter=3500–5000`. This preserves the current compact direct formulation while allocating expensive convergence effort only to candidates already near the `2.63` threshold, where small numerical improvements can determine whether a better certified packing is found.

5. **Add a narrowly targeted deterministic contact-escape refinement after choosing the best candidate.** Shrink the winning radii by roughly `0.2–0.4%`, perturb only centers not close to a wall by fixed vectors of magnitude about `1e-3–3e-3`, and run a small number of short analytic-Jacobian SLSQP refinements; accept only independently feasible improvements. This extends the successful direct solve without broad memetic search, and may move a near-optimal contact network into a neighboring unequal-radius basin while retaining the proven final conservative scaling certificate.
