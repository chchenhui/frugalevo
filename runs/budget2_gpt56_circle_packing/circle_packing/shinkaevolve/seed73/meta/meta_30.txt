# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- **The current best, Generation 18 `analytic_constraint_jacobian`, remains the strongest quality/runtime balance.** It achieved `sum_radii=2.63`, `target_ratio=1.00`, `validity=1.00`, and `combined_score=1.00` in `1.57 s`. Its key pattern is a direct 78-variable solve—52 center coordinates plus 26 independent radii—over four asymmetric five-row topologies, with a base seed and two deterministic jitters per topology. This targeted 12-start portfolio reached target quality faster than most later successful variants.

- **Joint unequal-radius optimization consistently outperformed more constrained geometric constructions.** Generations 21 and 25 both reached `sum_radii=2.63`, perfect validity, and score `1.00` using the same essential pattern: optimize all centers and radii simultaneously, use analytic constraints, break symmetry with multiple starts, then apply a minimal feasibility scale-down. Generation 21 reached this in `1.80 s`; Generation 25 in `1.94 s`.

- **Asymmetric row-layout diversity is repeatedly associated with target-level solutions.** The current best explicitly tries `(5,6,5,5,5)`, `(5,6,5,6,4)`, `(6,5,5,5,5)`, and `(5,5,6,5,5)`. Later variants confirm that staggered and asymmetric layouts remain useful: Generation 27’s topology-diverse starts reached score `1.00`, and Generation 28’s reflected/shifted seed variants also reached `2.63`. The evidence supports topology selection as more important than merely using generic random restarts.

- **Explicit feasibility certification is a robust common success factor.** The current best independently checks SLSQP output before retaining it, then computes both boundary and pair-contact radius scale limits and applies a `1 - 1e-10` shrink. This matches the reliable validity behavior of Generations 21–29: all correct scored programs reported `validity=1.00`, including the `2.62–2.63` solutions. The repair is nearly objective-neutral while preventing tolerance-level overlaps.

## Ineffective Approaches

- **Extra optimization phases did not exceed the current best’s direct multistart solve.** Generation 26 used shared-radius optimization followed by unequal-radius refinement and achieved `2.63`, but took `2.05 s`, slower than Generation 18’s `1.57 s`. Similarly, Generation 25 added elite tighter re-optimization yet reached the same `2.63` in `1.94 s`. These additions preserved quality but did not produce a measured improvement over the current best.

- **Some seed portfolios were valid but settled in slightly weaker basins.** Generations 22, 23, and 24 used unequal radii, analytic Jacobians, jittered/transposed starts, and safety scaling, but each recorded `sum_radii=2.62`, `target_ratio=0.99`, and `combined_score=0.99`. Since all had `validity=1.00`, their shortfall versus the current best is attributable to attained packing quality rather than constraint correctness.

- **Broader search complexity can substantially increase runtime without raising the score.** Generation 28 reached the same top-level result as Generation 18—`2.63`, score `1.00`, validity `1.00`—but required `8.28 s`, over five times the current best’s `1.57 s`. Its extra reflected/shifted geometry variants and polishing improved search breadth but did not improve the recorded objective.

- **Correct geometry can still fail to receive objective credit if output/evaluator handling is incompatible.** Generation 19 passed validation (`validity=1.00`) in `2.29 s`, and comments indicated a packing near `2.635`, yet its recorded `sum_radii`, target ratio, and score were all zero. This mirrors the earlier Generation 16 interface/scoring anomaly: feasibility alone did not ensure that the evaluator recognized the objective result.

## Implementation Insights

- **Generation 18’s constraint model is compact, exact, and well aligned with SLSQP.** It constructs `104` wall inequalities (`4 × 26`) plus `325` pairwise separation inequalities, for `429` constraints total. Each pair uses  
  `||c_i - c_j|| - r_i - r_j >= 0`,  
  and its Jacobian includes normalized center-direction derivatives plus `-1` derivatives for both radii. This exact derivative structure supports its `1.57 s` runtime despite the 78-variable, 429-constraint nonlinear program.

- **The current best separates optimization candidate selection from solver status.** Rather than accepting `result.success` unconditionally, it verifies that the returned vector is finite and that the minimum explicit constraint value is at least `-1e-7`, then compares true radius sums across candidates. This protects the selected result from incomplete or numerically marginal SLSQP iterations and is consistent with the repeated perfect-validity outcomes.

- **Deterministic seed construction makes search diversity reproducible rather than uncontrolled.** The fixed RNG seed `26031991`, small `±0.012` coordinate perturbations, and four specific row topologies create a reproducible 12-start search. Generations 21, 25, and 29 retained similar deterministic asymmetric restart patterns and also attained `2.63`, indicating that reproducible symmetry breaking is sufficient for target-level quality.

- **Independent radii provide useful local flexibility near boundaries and irregular gaps.** Generations 23 and 24 specifically emphasize that nonuniform radii help circles adapt near corners, edges, and narrow contacts; both achieved valid `2.62` packings. The current best and top-scoring later programs retain this same full independent-radius representation, rather than restricting all circles to a shared radius.

## Performance Analysis

- **Generation 18 remains the best observed efficiency among the target-quality programs.** It achieved `2.63`, score `1.00`, and validity `1.00` in `1.57 s`. Generation 21 matched the objective in `1.80 s`; Generation 25 matched it in `1.94 s`; Generation 26 matched it in `2.05 s`; and Generation 28 matched it in `8.28 s`. Thus, later search refinements matched but did not surpass the current best’s measured quality, while generally increasing runtime.

- **The `2.62` versus `2.63` difference remains meaningful in several evaluations.** Generations 22–24 received `combined_score=0.99` and `target_ratio=0.99` at `sum_radii=2.62`, whereas Generation 18’s reported `2.63` earned the full score. Generation 27 is an exception: it reported `2.62` but still received a perfect `1.00` score, showing that evaluator rounding or thresholding may create small discontinuities near the target.

- **Validity is now stable across nearly all evaluated optimization variants.** Generations 18 and 21–29 all reported `validity=1.00` except that Generation 19’s issue was scoring/reporting rather than geometric validity. Consequently, the observed score differences among correct programs are primarily explained by local-optimum quality, reported radius sum precision, and evaluation handling—not by overlap or boundary violations.

- **Runtime does not scale monotonically with solution quality.** Generation 24 was faster than the current best at `1.47 s` but attained only `2.62` and score `0.99`; Generation 18 took `1.57 s` and reached `2.63`; Generation 28 took `8.28 s` yet did not exceed `2.63`. The measured results show that the current best’s compact topology portfolio, analytic Jacobian, and direct candidate filtering produce an especially favorable quality/time tradeoff.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Expand the topology portfolio selectively with new asymmetric five-row layouts, while preserving the current 12-start budget.** Add layouts such as `(4,6,6,5,5)`, `(5,5,6,6,4)`, `(6,4,6,5,5)`, and vertical reversals, but replace rather than merely append some redundant jitter-only starts to protect the current `1.57 s` runtime. The strongest solutions consistently arise from asymmetric placement of 6-circle rows, so structurally distinct contact graphs are more promising than many perturbations of the same geometry.

2. **Seed a small subset of starts with deterministic unequal radii instead of only uniform `0.025` radii.** For example, initialize corner/edge circles at `0.027–0.030`, dense interior-row circles at `0.021–0.024`, then compute one global feasibility scale before passing the vector to SLSQP. The final optimizer already benefits from fully independent radii, and controlled radius asymmetry may avoid symmetric local basins responsible for the repeated `2.62` outcomes.

3. **Use a two-stage solver schedule that spends additional iterations only on elite first-pass candidates.** Run the present 12 direct analytic-Jacobian solves with slightly lower first-pass limits if needed, retain the best two or three explicitly feasible candidates, then re-solve those with `ftol=1e-12` or `1e-13` and a higher `maxiter` such as `3500–5000`. This extends the proven direct joint optimization approach without paying the tighter-convergence cost for every topology, and could recover small radius-sum gains near the `2.62–2.63` threshold.

4. **Add deterministic seed transforms that alter solver trajectories without introducing uncontrolled random search.** For selected promising layouts, include horizontal reflection, alternating row shifts of roughly `±0.006–0.012`, and a slight shear/stagger based on row parity before applying the existing fixed jitters. Generations using reflected and shifted variants reached target quality, while the current solver’s local optimum is sensitive to the initial active-contact structure even when transformed configurations are geometrically equivalent.

5. **Implement a low-cost contact-escape refinement around the best feasible candidate.** After the main portfolio, shrink the winning radii by approximately `0.2–0.4%`, perturb only non-boundary or interior centers by deterministic vectors of magnitude `1e-3–3e-3`, and perform a few short SLSQP refinements with the same exact constraints and Jacobian. Unlike broad multi-phase optimization, this focuses extra search on the already successful basin and may discover a nearby unequal-radius contact network with a slightly larger certified sum.
