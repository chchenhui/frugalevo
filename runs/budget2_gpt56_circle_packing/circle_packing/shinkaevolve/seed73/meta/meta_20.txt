# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- **The current best, Generation 18 “analytic_constraint_jacobian,” combines topology-diverse seeding with exact first derivatives and achieved `sum_radii=2.63`, `validity=1.00`, `combined_score=1.00` in `1.57 s`.** It evaluates four five-row layouts—`(5,6,5,5,5)`, `(5,6,5,6,4)`, `(6,5,5,5,5)`, and `(5,5,6,5,5)`—and creates three starts per topology: the base lattice plus two deterministic jitters. This is a targeted 12-start search rather than untuned random restarting, and it consistently finds a high-quality asymmetric basin.

- **Joint optimization of all 52 center coordinates and 26 independent radii remains the central quality driver.** The current best optimizes the full 78-variable vector directly against wall-clearance and pairwise non-overlap constraints, rather than fixing centers and deriving radii afterward. This matches the strongest results: Generation 10 reached `2.63` in `1.99 s`, Generation 17 reached `2.63` in `1.97 s`, and Generation 18 reached `2.63` in `1.57 s`. In contrast, the earlier fixed-center/greedy-radius baseline reported in the global history reached only `0.96`.

- **Asymmetric staggered row topologies are more valuable than merely increasing restart count.** Generation 15’s topology-guided five-row candidates reached `2.62`, score `1.00`, in `1.56 s`; the current best’s additional deterministic perturbations and broader selection logic raised the reported radius sum to `2.63`. This supports the earlier evidence that layouts such as rows containing a sixth circle in different positions avoid the weaker symmetric local optima seen in less diverse searches.

- **Strict feasibility filtering plus an almost-lossless final radius certification reliably preserves validity.** Generation 18 rejects an SLSQP proposal unless all explicit inequalities are at least `-1e-7`, then computes wall- and pair-based scale limits and applies only a `1 - 1e-10` final shrink. The result retained `validity=1.00` without materially reducing its `2.63` objective. The same broad safeguard was successful in Generations 10, 15, and 17, all of which obtained perfect validity and target-level scores.

## Ineffective Approaches

- **Sequential post-hoc radius reduction is substantially weaker than optimizing radii inside the nonlinear program.** The retained `compute_max_radii` helper in the current code limits border radii and then proportionally shrinks colliding pairs one pair at a time. That is the same conservative pattern identified in the low-performing concentric-ring construction (`sum_radii=0.96`, score `0.36`): reductions made for one pair are not globally re-optimized across all contacts. The helper is not used on the successful optimization path.

- **Limited seed diversity can leave SLSQP in inferior packing basins even when the mathematical formulation is sound.** Generation 12, using a 5×5-plus-corner seed and a layered seed, achieved only `2.61`, `combined_score=0.99`, albeit in a fast `1.14 s`. Generation 13 similarly reached `2.60`, `0.99`; Generation 14 used a grid plus several layered layouts but remained at `2.61`, `0.99`. These results are fully valid, so the gap from Generation 18’s `2.63` is primarily local-optimum quality rather than constraint failure.

- **Continuation or staged logic did not reliably exceed the direct multistart joint solve.** Generation 20’s interior perturbation/deflation continuation reached `2.62`, `0.99`, in `1.16 s`, below the current best. Generation 17’s shared-radius-then-unequal-radius staging did reach `2.63`, but at `1.97 s`, so its added phase did not improve measured quality beyond the direct analytic-Jacobian multistart approach.

- **More elaborate optimization can fail at the evaluation/interface level despite valid geometry.** Generation 16 reported `validity=1.00` and `1.62 s` runtime but received zero sum-radii, target ratio, and combined score. Since validation confirmed a legitimate packing and comments expected approximately `2.635`, the failure appears to be in evaluation/scoring outcome rather than the geometric approach. It demonstrates that correctness and feasibility alone did not guarantee objective credit in that evaluated version.

## Implementation Insights

- **Generation 18’s constraint construction is compact and directly aligned with the geometry.** It uses `np.triu_indices(26, 1)` to create all `325` pair constraints, alongside `4 × 26 = 104` wall constraints. Each pair inequality is exactly  
  `distance(center_i, center_j) - r_i - r_j >= 0`,  
  while each wall inequality enforces the radius-clearance condition. This explicit formulation is more effective than indirect radius repair because SLSQP can trade center movement and unequal-radius changes jointly.

- **The current best supplies an analytic Jacobian for both wall and pair constraints.** For each pair, it differentiates Euclidean separation with respect to both centers using normalized displacement vectors and adds `-1` derivatives for both radii. This avoids finite-difference work in a problem with 429 inequality constraints and 78 variables. Its `1.57 s` runtime is notably better than Generation 10’s similar-quality `2.63` solution at `1.99 s` and Generation 17’s `1.97 s`, while retaining the same top-level score.

- **Candidate selection explicitly separates solver output quality from numerical safety.** The code does not trust `result.success` alone or blindly retain the last iterate. It checks that each proposal is finite, evaluates all constraints itself, retains only feasible candidates, and compares their true summed radii. This is a robust implementation detail behind the repeated `validity=1.00` results, and it prevents a poor or unfinished restart from replacing a better valid packing.

- **The initialization is deliberately feasible and reproducible.** Every start begins with radii of `0.025`, small enough for the row lattices and their `±0.012` coordinate jitter. The fixed RNG seed `26031991` makes the perturbation portfolio deterministic, while the fallback returns the unperturbed feasible lattice if SciPy is unavailable. This gives predictable validity without sacrificing the high-quality SLSQP path in normal evaluation.

## Performance Analysis

- **Generation 18 is tied for the best observed measured packing quality and is among the faster top-quality implementations.** Its `sum_radii=2.63`, `target_ratio=1.00`, `validity=1.00`, and `combined_score=1.00` match Generation 10 and Generation 17. At `1.57 s`, it is faster than Generation 10 (`1.99 s`) and Generation 17 (`1.97 s`), and essentially matches Generation 15’s `1.56 s` while improving its reported radius sum from `2.62` to `2.63`.

- **The difference between `2.62` and `2.63` is small numerically but meaningful at the evaluator threshold.** Generation 9 reached `2.62` in `2.89 s` yet received a perfect score, and Generation 15 also reached `2.62` with a perfect score. However, Generations 12, 14, and 20 reported approximately `2.61–2.62` with `target_ratio=0.99` and `combined_score=0.99`. Thus, evaluation quality is sensitive both to the attained local optimum and to small reported objective differences around the target boundary.

- **Runtime is driven more by search portfolio and derivative quality than by score alone.** The current best performs 12 SLSQP starts with `maxiter=2200` but completes in `1.57 s`, aided by its supplied Jacobian. Generation 9 also used multistart analytic optimization but took `2.89 s` for `2.62`; Generation 13 took `1.58 s` for `2.60`; and Generation 12 produced `2.61` in `1.14 s`. Therefore, runtime does not monotonically correlate with packing quality—specific seed topology and the local basin reached are decisive.

- **Across the evaluated correct programs, validity is no longer the differentiator; objective quality is.** Generations 9, 10, 12–15, 17, 18, and 20 all reported `validity=1.00`. The main separation is between the current best/top tier at `2.63` and near-target variants at `2.60–2.62`. The consistent final scaling/certification mechanisms mean that score differences are attributable primarily to seed selection, asymmetric topology, and the optimization trajectory rather than accidental overlaps or boundary violations.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Broaden the five-row topology portfolio with structurally distinct 26-circle layouts, not just more jitters.** Retain the four winning layouts and add candidates such as `(4,6,6,5,5)`, `(5,5,6,6,4)`, `(6,4,6,5,5)`, `(5,6,6,4,5)`, plus vertical reversals of each. The best results came from asymmetric placement of the six-circle rows, so allocate one or two starts to each new topology while reducing redundant perturbations of less promising seeds if runtime becomes limiting.

2. **Add deterministic geometric variants of each seed before SLSQP: reflection, row offsets, and alternating stagger.** For every row-layout lattice, include horizontal reflection and a row-dependent shift such as alternating `±0.01` horizontal displacement for interior rows, clipped only enough to keep the initial small-radius packing feasible. Although reflected layouts are geometrically equivalent in principle, they can produce different active-contact graphs and SLSQP trajectories, which is valuable near the narrow `2.62–2.63` score boundary.

3. **Run a short second-stage “contact escape” optimization from the best first-pass feasible solution.** Take the selected candidate, shrink radii modestly (for example `r *= 0.997`), apply several deterministic `1e-3` to `4e-3` perturbations to interior centers only, and launch shorter analytic-Jacobian SLSQP refinements. This builds on the proven direct joint optimization while testing nearby contact configurations; prior continuation was weaker when used as the primary strategy, but it is promising as a low-cost local refinement after the strong topology-guided pass.

4. **Use a two-tolerance solve schedule for the strongest starts.** Keep the current broad 12-start pass, then re-solve only the top two or three feasible candidates with tighter settings such as `ftol=1e-12` or `1e-13`, increased `maxiter`, and the same exact constraint Jacobian. Since the current method already finds high-quality feasible basins quickly, spending additional effort only on the best basins may recover small objective improvements without paying the cost for every topology.

5. **Replace uniform initial radii on selected starts with feasible, topology-aware radius perturbations.** In addition to `0.025` uniform radii, seed a few runs with deterministic unequal radii—for example, slightly larger radii for circles near corners and slightly smaller radii for dense middle-row circles—while scaling the full vector to strict feasibility before optimization. The final solutions benefit from unequal radii, and introducing controlled asymmetry in the radius variables may help SLSQP reach a better unequal-radius contact network rather than first passing through a symmetric local optimum.
