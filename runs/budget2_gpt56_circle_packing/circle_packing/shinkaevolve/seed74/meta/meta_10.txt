# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- **Multi-seed joint center-and-radius SLSQP optimization is the strongest recurring pattern.** The current best, **Generation 5: Multi-Start SLSQP Circle Packing**, reaches `sum_radii=2.63`, `validity=1.00`, and combined score `1.00` in `0.66 s`. It combines a feasible 5×5 lattice-plus-interstitial seed, two deterministic perturbed lattice seeds, and a staggered five-row seed. This seed diversity helps SLSQP enter different contact-graph basins rather than being limited by the original square-lattice structure.

- **The current best’s continuation restart provides a concrete late-stage improvement mechanism.** After evaluating its initial seeds, it perturbs the strongest feasible solution by only `±0.009` in center coordinates, reduces radii to `72%`, and reoptimizes. The evaluation feedback specifically credits “multi-seed optimization plus a small continuation restart” for reaching `2.63`, above earlier one-pass variants such as Generation 4 (`2.59`) and Generation 7 (`2.59`).

- **Unequal individual radii matter.** Programs jointly optimizing all radii consistently outperform uniform-radius or greedy-radius methods: Generation 5 reaches `2.63`; Generation 6 reaches `2.62`; Generation 8 reaches `2.62`; and Generation 9 reaches `2.62`. This is substantially better than the initial ring constructor’s `0.96` and better than the weaker Generation 3 hybrid result of `2.39`.

- **Explicit post-solve feasibility certification reliably preserves perfect validity.** The current best’s `safe_scale` computes the limiting common scale from both wall clearance and every pairwise distance, then applies `0.9999995 * scale`. This matches the successful final safety scaling used by Generations 4, 6, 7, and 9, all of which retained `validity=1.00` despite SLSQP-level residuals.

## Ineffective Approaches

- **Clipped ring placement and sequential pairwise radius shrinking perform very poorly.** Generation 0’s ring-based approach is valid but has only `sum_radii=0.96` and score `0.36`. Its outer-ring coordinate clipping creates crowded boundary positions, and sequentially shrinking overlapping pairs is conservative and order-dependent rather than globally maximizing total radius.

- **A single restricted geometric basin can leave substantial quality on the table.** Generation 3’s staggered SLSQP-plus-LP approach is valid but reaches only `2.39` in `6.22 s`, far below the current best’s `2.63` in `0.66 s`. Despite using nonlinear center optimization and fixed-center LP radii, its specific five-row initialization and optimization trajectory did not find the high-quality contact structure reached by more varied multi-start methods.

- **LP polishing alone does not guarantee the best layout.** Generation 1’s exact fixed-center HiGHS LP achieves `2.61`, and Generation 8’s multistart LP-assisted version achieves `2.62`; both are strong but remain below Generation 5’s `2.63`. The fixed-center LP optimally sizes circles only after centers are chosen, so its final quality remains constrained by the center configuration found by the nonlinear search.

- **More restarts do not automatically improve the result or runtime.** Generation 6 uses six perturbed restarts but obtains `2.62` in `1.87 s`, while the current best obtains `2.63` in `0.66 s` from four structured seeds plus one focused continuation. The results indicate that seed quality and targeted refinement were more important than merely increasing restart count.

## Implementation Insights

- **The current best uses a compact, fully vectorized constraint representation.** Its `clearance(z)` concatenates four wall constraints per circle with all `26 choose 2 = 325` pair constraints. Pair distances are computed by indexed vector operations (`np.triu_indices`, coordinate differences, `einsum`), allowing SLSQP to optimize all 78 decision variables—52 center coordinates and 26 radii—against the complete geometry.

- **Its seeds are deliberately feasible or made feasible before optimization.** The base seed is a 5×5 lattice at coordinates `0.1` through `0.9`, with a 26th circle at `[0.2, 0.2]` and radius `sqrt(0.02)-0.1`. Perturbed seeds are assigned radii by `uniform_feasible_radii`, which limits the common radius by minimum wall clearance and half the minimum pair distance. This avoids starting SLSQP from severely infeasible configurations.

- **The best program’s search strategy balances exploration and cost.** It uses only four initial deterministic layouts: one lattice/interstitial seed, two lattice perturbation scales (`0.018`, `0.036`), and one staggered five-row layout. This is enough structural diversity to outperform single-family approaches while staying much faster than Generation 3 (`6.22 s`) and Generation 6 (`1.87 s`).

- **Safety scaling is better than the retained fallback radius routine for final certification.** `safe_scale` derives one global feasible factor against all borders and pairs simultaneously. In contrast, the included `compute_max_radii` fallback repeatedly rescales only the two radii of an overlapping pair; that resembles the weaker order-dependent behavior criticized in Generation 0. The evaluated best path correctly relies on SLSQP plus global certification instead.

## Performance Analysis

- **The main progression is from validity-only construction to high-quality nonlinear search.** Generation 0 establishes that feasibility alone is insufficient: score `0.36` at total radius `0.96`. Joint constrained optimization raises totals to `2.39–2.63`, with every nonlinear method maintaining `validity=1.00`.

- **Generation 5 is the highest measured result and is also among the fastest high-quality methods.** Its `2.63` exceeds Generation 1 and 2 (`2.61` each), Generations 6, 8, and 9 (`2.62` each), and Generations 4 and 7 (`2.59` each). Its `0.66 s` evaluation is close to Generation 2’s `0.60 s` and Generation 7’s `0.64 s`, but it delivers an additional `0.02–0.04` in total radius.

- **The score threshold is sensitive near the target.** Totals of `2.59`, `2.61`, and `2.62` correspond to reported target ratios of `0.98`, `0.99`, and `0.99–1.00`, respectively, whereas Generation 5’s `2.63` reaches both target ratio and combined score `1.00`. Thus, the relatively small improvement over `2.61–2.62` is decisive under the benchmark’s capped scoring.

- **Reported headroom is small but real.** The current program comments that AlphaEvolve improved the construction to `2.635`; the evaluation summaries likewise cite approximately `2.635` as a later evolved result. Generation 5’s `2.63` is therefore close to the known improved structure, whereas the `2.39–2.61` methods are further from that packing quality despite being valid.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Add several targeted continuation restarts from the current best candidate, not just one.** Keep the successful pattern of perturbing centers by about `±0.009` and shrinking radii to roughly `0.72`, but try 2–4 deterministic perturbation scales/directions (for example `0.004`, `0.009`, and `0.015`) from the best certified layout. This extends the mechanism credited for Generation 5’s improvement while remaining much cheaper than broad random multi-start search.

2. **Use a fixed-center LP radius polish after every successful SLSQP center-and-radius solve.** Given certified SLSQP centers, solve the linear program maximizing `sum(r)` subject to `r_i <= wall_clearance_i` and `r_i + r_j <= distance_ij`; then apply the existing `safe_scale`. Generations 1 and 8 show LP sizing is strong (`2.61–2.62`), and using it as a polish rather than a replacement may recover radius lost to SLSQP’s joint-variable termination without sacrificing the superior center placement of Generation 5.

3. **Expand seed diversity with contact-graph-aware asymmetric seeds rather than adding many generic random restarts.** Add a few hand-structured variants such as a 6-5-5-5-5 row arrangement, a mirrored/shifted version of the current 5-5-6-5-5 staggered seed, and lattice seeds where the extra circle is inserted into different interior cells rather than always near `[0.2, 0.2]`. The best result already demonstrates that distinct structured basins outperform merely increasing the count of perturbed copies, and alternative interstitial locations may expose the reported `~2.635` structure.

4. **Perform alternating local optimization: joint SLSQP, LP radius sizing, then center-only SLSQP with radii slightly deflated.** After a good joint solve, LP-polish the radii, scale them down modestly (such as `0.97–0.99`), and optimize only centers against constraints using those radii before returning to full joint SLSQP. This can release near-degenerate contacts and improve center geometry while preserving the key advantage of unequal radii, addressing the limitation that fixed-center LP alone cannot improve a suboptimal center arrangement.

5. **Add analytic or semi-analytic constraint Jacobians to SLSQP for the vectorized clearance function.** Supply derivatives for wall constraints and pair-distance constraints, including derivatives with respect to both involved centers and radii, while retaining the present compact pair-indexed representation. The current method is already fast and high quality, so more accurate local steps can be spent on tighter final refinement or a few extra continuation attempts rather than increasing the expensive `maxiter` budget indiscriminately.
