# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- **The current best’s compact, topology-diverse seed portfolio is the strongest measured search strategy.** It achieves `sum_radii=2.63`, `validity=1.00`, `combined_score=1.00` in only `0.66 s`. Its four initial layouts are deliberately distinct but few: a 5×5 lattice with an interstitial 26th circle, two deterministic asymmetric lattice openings (`±0.018`, `±0.036`), and a five-row staggered 5/5/6/5/5 layout. This outperforms the broader multistart approaches of Generation 20 (`2.62`, `1.87 s`), Generation 23 (`2.62`, `3.25 s`), and Generation 28 (`2.62`, `1.91 s`).

- **Joint nonlinear optimization of all 52 center coordinates and 26 unequal radii remains the key quality mechanism.** The current best directly maximizes total radius with all wall and pairwise non-overlap constraints active. Generation 22, *Deterministic Multistart Unequal Circle Packing*, and Generation 24, *Analytic-Jacobian Multi-Seed Circle Packing*, confirm this pattern: both also reach `2.63` with perfect validity and score. Their substantially longer times—`3.60 s` and `1.57 s`, respectively—show that the current best reaches the same quality more efficiently.

- **A single targeted continuation restart is more productive than many generic restarts.** After selecting the strongest seed result, the current best perturbs centers only within `±0.009`, contracts radii to `72%`, and reoptimizes. This preserves a promising contact graph while allowing contacts to rearrange. The result supports the prior observation that focused basin escape is more valuable than larger restart counts: Generation 22 uses deterministic multistarts and takes `3.60 s` for the same `2.63`, while the current best obtains it in `0.66 s`.

- **Conservative common-radius certification reliably maintains validity without materially damaging score.** The current best’s `safe_scale` explicitly checks every wall radius limit and pair-distance radius sum, then applies a `0.9999995` factor. Its full validity at `2.63` supports the same conclusion seen in Generations 20, 21, 23, 24, and 26: final contraction/scaling is an effective defense against SLSQP tolerance residuals. The approximately documented `2.635` evolved result indicates that only limited quality headroom remains beyond this robust `2.63` solution.

## Ineffective Approaches

- **Fixed-center LP polishing alone did not improve the best measured packing quality.** Generations 20, 21, 23, 25, 26, 27, and 28 all use an LP to maximize radii after optimizing or fixing center positions, but most finish at `2.62` and `0.99` combined score. For example, Generation 21’s LP-certified contact-graph method is relatively fast at `1.37 s`, but remains below the current best by `0.01` radius. The LP solves radius allocation well for a given placement, but does not by itself escape a weaker center/contact-graph basin.

- **More elaborate refinement pipelines increased runtime without surpassing the compact current-best search.** Generation 23 combines seed diversification, joint SLSQP, fixed-center LP, clearance-maximizing center relocation, final polishing, and conservative scaling, yet reaches only `2.62` in `3.25 s`. Generation 26 similarly adds deflated center relaxation, sinusoidal continuation, LP certification, and final polishing but achieves `2.62` in `2.99 s`. These pipelines are robust, but their additional stages did not translate into a measured score gain over the current best.

- **Broad multistart search has weak measured return once meaningful seed topologies are covered.** Generation 22 reaches `2.63`, but requires `3.60 s`; the current best ties its quality at about one-sixth of the runtime using only four initial seed families and one continuation attempt. Earlier global evidence is consistent: high restart counts often reach `2.60–2.62` rather than reliably exceeding the compact portfolio.

- **Overly regular or insufficiently asymmetric initial geometries tend to settle at the `2.62` plateau.** Several later programs explicitly use square, layered, staggered, or near-hexagonal seeds and pass validation, but remain at `2.62`: Generation 20’s square/hex portfolio, Generation 25’s perturbed 5×5 grid plus interstitial seed, and Generation 28’s square/hex/layered family. The current best’s controlled asymmetric perturbations appear important because they create alternate contact-graph basins without discarding the strong lattice/interstitial structure.

## Implementation Insights

- **The current best’s geometry representation is computationally compact and complete.** It creates all `325` unordered circle pairs with `np.triu_indices(26, 1)` and computes pair clearances in a vectorized form using indexed center differences and `np.einsum`. It then concatenates those pair constraints with the four wall constraints per circle. This avoids Python-level pair loops in the optimization path and is a plausible contributor to its exceptional `0.66 s` runtime.

- **Feasible initialization is handled explicitly rather than left to SLSQP recovery.** The base 25-circle lattice uses radius `0.1`; the interstitial radius is initialized as `sqrt(0.02) - 0.1`, matching its nearby lattice geometry. Perturbed and staggered seeds use `uniform_feasible_radii`, bounded by minimum wall distance and half the closest center spacing with a `0.995` safety factor. This keeps every seed feasible or conservatively feasible and lets optimization spend work improving the contact graph rather than resolving severe initial overlaps.

- **The current best obtains its speed despite not supplying an explicit Jacobian to SLSQP.** In contrast, Generations 20–28 repeatedly emphasize analytic constraint Jacobians, yet their runtimes range from `1.37 s` to `3.60 s` and most finish at `2.62`. The current program instead benefits from a low number of solves, vectorized constraint evaluation, compact seed selection, and one focused continuation run. Thus, in these measured programs, search economy and initialization quality matter at least as much as derivative sophistication.

- **Post-solve certification is correctly separated from optimization.** `safe_scale` derives the largest single scale allowed by both boundary clearances and pairwise distances, rather than iteratively shrinking individual circles. This is preferable to the retained `compute_max_radii` fallback-style function, whose sequential pair scaling is order-dependent and conservative. The evaluated high-quality path uses global certification after SLSQP, matching the robust validity behavior reported by LP-polished and analytic-Jacobian variants.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Add a very small set of topology-directed lattice/interstitial seeds rather than broad random multistart.** Retain the existing 5×5 lattice construction, but place the 26th circle in 2–4 alternate interior cells and use mirrored versions of the current asymmetric perturbations, e.g. fixed perturbation signs rather than additional random layouts. This directly targets alternate local contact graphs while preserving the compact seed portfolio and low runtime that distinguish the current best.

2. **Replace the single continuation attempt with two staged, deterministic basin-escape continuations from only the best first-pass result.** For example, run one mild restart with center jitter `±0.004` and radii scaled by `0.84`, then one stronger restart with jitter `±0.011` and radii scaled by `0.65`; only keep and continue from an improving certified solution. The existing `±0.009`/`0.72` restart has demonstrated value, so a tightly limited schedule is more evidence-supported than adding many independent SLSQP seeds.

3. **Add a fixed-center LP radius polish for candidates near the incumbent, then use that polished result as an optional joint-SLSQP restart.** Given optimized centers, solve the linear program maximizing `sum(r_i)` under wall bounds and `r_i + r_j <= distance(i,j)`, and only invoke it when a candidate is within a small margin, such as `0.01`, of `best_value`. LP alone has not escaped weaker center basins, but it can cheaply recover unequal-radius allocation slack and provide a stronger starting point for one final joint nonlinear refinement.

4. **Implement analytic Jacobians only for the final refinement solves, while retaining the current vectorized constraint values and small solve count.** Supply derivatives for the four wall constraints and pair constraints, using normalized center differences for pair-distance gradients and `-1` entries for each involved radius. Prior Jacobian-heavy pipelines were slower mainly because of broader search/refinement stages, but applying exact derivatives selectively to the best candidate and continuation restarts may improve convergence from the documented `2.63` plateau toward the reported `~2.635` solution.

5. **Introduce a short center-only contact-graph relaxation between certification and the strongest continuation restart.** Deflate the best radii by roughly `1–2%`, optimize just the 52 center coordinates under wall and fixed-radius pair-separation constraints, then restore the full center-and-radius SLSQP solve. This uses the small amount of slack created by deflation to rearrange near-locked contacts, addressing the known limitation of fixed-center radius polishing without replacing the successful joint optimization and global `safe_scale` certification path.
