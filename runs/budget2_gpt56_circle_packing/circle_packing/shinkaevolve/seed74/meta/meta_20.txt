# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- **The current best’s small, topology-diverse seed portfolio remains the strongest measured strategy.** The current best program reaches `sum_radii=2.63`, `combined_score=1.00`, `validity=1.00` in only `0.66 s`. Its four initial seed families—a 5×5 lattice plus interstitial circle, two asymmetric lattice perturbations (`±0.018` and `±0.036`), and a staggered 5/5/6/5/5-row layout—cover meaningfully different contact-graph basins without paying the cost of many generic restarts. This is more effective than the Generation 10–19 variants, most of which used 10–18 starts yet measured only `2.60–2.62` at `1.12–3.95 s`.

- **Joint optimization of centers and unequal radii with full nonlinear pairwise constraints consistently produces the best packings.** The current best jointly optimizes all 78 variables and reaches `2.63`. Later correct SLSQP variants using the same essential formulation were close but lower: Generation 13 also reached `2.63` with perfect score, but needed `3.95 s`; Generations 15 and 19 reached `2.62` in `2.04 s` and `2.17 s`. This reinforces the prior evidence that individual-radius optimization is central to crossing the benchmark threshold.

- **A focused continuation restart is more valuable than broad restart counts.** After selecting the best initial candidate, the current best perturbs centers only by `±0.009`, contracts radii to `72%`, and reoptimizes. Evaluation feedback explicitly identifies this continuation mechanism as part of the `2.63` result. In contrast, Generation 10’s 18 perturbed starts achieved only `2.60` in `2.71 s`, while Generation 14’s ten jittered starts reached `2.62` in `1.80 s`. The measured results favor targeted late-stage escape from a promising local basin over simply adding many initial starts.

- **Strict post-solve certification reliably preserves full validity.** The current best’s `safe_scale` derives the maximum common radius factor allowed by every wall and pair constraint, then multiplies by `0.9999995`. This produced validity `1.00` despite SLSQP tolerance limitations. Generations 11, 13, 17, and 19 likewise combined nonlinear optimization with safety repairs or global scaling and all reported full validity; the common successful pattern is optimizer-driven quality followed by deterministic geometric certification.

## Ineffective Approaches

- **Increasing restart count and optimizer work did not improve beyond the compact current-best search.** Generation 10 used 18 deterministic starts and took `2.71 s` for only `2.60`; Generation 13 used 16 restarts and took `3.95 s` to tie the current best at `2.63`; Generation 18 used diverse seeds plus LP polishing and took `2.06 s` for `2.62`. Compared with the current best’s `0.66 s`, these results show that extra starts often increase runtime substantially without a measured quality gain.

- **Fixed-center LP polishing is helpful but does not overcome an inferior center arrangement.** Generation 11’s nonlinear-plus-LP method is efficient (`2.62` in `1.12 s`) and Generation 17’s continuation-LP method is valid at `2.62` in `2.18 s`, but both remain below `2.63`. Similarly, Generations 12, 14, 16, and 18 use LP polishing and report `2.60–2.62`. This matches the earlier conclusion: an LP optimally reallocates radii for fixed centers, but cannot alter the center contact graph that limits the total.

- **Single-family or overly regular layouts remain prone to weaker local optima.** Generation 10’s staggered hexagonal seed family reached `2.60`, and Generation 16’s staggered six-row approach also reached `2.60`. Their validity was perfect, but their totals were lower than the current best’s mixture of square-lattice/interstitial and staggered layouts. The evaluation feedback repeatedly attributes improvements to asymmetric starts, indicating that symmetric or narrowly structured layouts constrain SLSQP’s reachable packing topology.

- **The retained pairwise shrinking fallback is not a high-quality optimization mechanism.** The current best includes `compute_max_radii`, which sequentially scales two overlapping radii at a time. As identified previously, this is order-dependent and conservative, resembling the poor Generation 0-style repair behavior rather than the evaluated high-quality path. The successful execution instead uses joint SLSQP and one global post-solve scale.

## Implementation Insights

- **The current best efficiently represents all geometry in a vectorized clearance function.** It constructs `325` pair constraints from `np.triu_indices(26, 1)` and combines them with four wall-clearance constraints per circle. Pair distances use indexed coordinate differences and `np.einsum`, giving SLSQP a complete 429-constraint feasibility representation while avoiding Python-level pair loops. This compact formulation supports the best measured combination of quality (`2.63`) and speed (`0.66 s`).

- **Feasible or nearly feasible initialization is a practical performance feature.** The base lattice has radius `0.1` for 25 circles and an exact interstitial radius of `sqrt(0.02) - 0.1` for the 26th. Perturbed seeds receive a uniform radius bounded by wall clearance and half the closest pair distance, with a `0.995` margin. This avoids wasting iterations recovering from severe overlap and differs from approaches that depend on later repair alone.

- **The current best makes exploration inexpensive by using deterministic perturbations rather than large random ensembles.** A fixed RNG seed (`26031991`) yields reproducible lattice openings, while just two perturbation magnitudes create distinct asymmetric alternatives. This is operationally more efficient than the later multistart variants: Generation 10’s 18 starts cost `2.71 s`, Generation 15’s 14 starts cost `2.04 s`, and Generation 13’s 16 starts cost `3.95 s`.

- **LP polishing is an effective robustness/quality supplement but not necessary for the best measured runtime-quality point.** Generations 11 and 14 show that HiGHS fixed-center LP polishing can deliver `2.62` with robust feasibility. However, the current best obtains `2.63` without an LP dependency by directly optimizing radii and centers, then using `safe_scale`. This suggests its nonlinear search reaches a stronger geometry directly, whereas LP-assisted programs primarily recover radius quality after a less favorable placement optimization.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Add 2–3 deterministic continuation restarts around the best certified solution, while keeping the seed portfolio unchanged.** Reuse the successful center perturbation/contract-and-reoptimize mechanism, but try distinct small perturbation schedules such as center jitters of `0.004`, `0.009`, and `0.014` with radius contractions of `0.85`, `0.72`, and `0.60`. Run these only from the best first-pass candidate and stop early if no continuation improves it, preserving the current program’s advantage over broad multistart search.

2. **LP-polish radii at each promising SLSQP result before `safe_scale`, then optionally re-run joint SLSQP from the polished point.** For fixed optimized centers, solve `max sum(r_i)` subject to wall limits `r_i <= min(x_i, y_i, 1-x_i, 1-y_i)` and pair limits `r_i + r_j <= d_ij`; this can recover radius allocation lost when SLSQP terminates on a center/radius compromise. Use the LP result as a low-cost polish rather than replacing joint optimization, because the best program’s key advantage is its stronger center topology.

3. **Create a few additional structured asymmetric seeds that alter the extra circle’s local contact graph.** Keep the successful 5×5 lattice base, but insert the 26th circle into several different interior cells—e.g. around `(0.2, 0.4)`, `(0.4, 0.2)`, `(0.4, 0.4)`—and include mirrored versions of the existing asymmetric perturbations. These are cheap, topology-directed alternatives to generic random restarts and directly target the documented possibility of a slightly stronger `~2.635` packing basin.

4. **Introduce a center-relaxation phase after a good solve: deflate radii slightly, optimize centers, then restore joint optimization.** Starting from a certified candidate, reduce radii by about `1–3%`, optimize only the 52 center variables while enforcing pair separations based on the fixed deflated radii, then perform the existing full center-and-radius SLSQP solve. This specifically addresses the limitation of fixed-center radius polishing: it can create slack, but a dedicated center step can use that slack to alter a nearly locked contact graph.

5. **Supply analytic Jacobians for the clearance constraints and objective to make final local refinement more reliable.** The wall derivatives are trivial, while each pair constraint has derivatives `(c_i-c_j)/distance` and its negative for the two involved centers, plus `-1` for both corresponding radii. The current vectorized constraint implementation is already fast; accurate derivatives could improve convergence quality within the same small restart budget rather than spending time on many more seeds or a much larger `maxiter`.
