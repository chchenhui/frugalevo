<DESCRIPTION>
Replace several nearly identical random restarts of the initial `(5,6,5,6,4)` lattice with a controlled portfolio of asymmetric five-row contact graphs.  The principal improvement opportunity is at the boundary: moving the six-circle row or a four-circle defect changes which circles can expand against square sides and corners.  Independently varying the left and right row margins additionally breaks reflection symmetry without relying solely on random noise.

The portfolio retains the proven hexagonal layering geometry, uses conservative initial radii, and reserves later attempts for perturbations around the best discovered contact graph.  This increases the chance that SLSQP enters a basin associated with the approximately `2.635` asymmetric construction while preserving the existing execution budget and public interface.
