# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- **The current best evaluated program, Generation 25 (`layered_seed_portfolio`), demonstrates that asymmetric layered seed diversity can break the recurring 2.62 plateau without requiring LP radius reallocation.** It achieved **sum_radii 2.63**, **validity 1.00**, and **combined score 1.00** in **2.28 s**. Its eight explicit row-pattern/phase seeds—including single-six-row patterns such as `(5,6,5,5,5)` and mixed-defect patterns such as `(5,6,5,6,4)`—change boundary contact structures while retaining a dense staggered triangular character. This supports the earlier Generation 16 finding that the remaining quality gain comes mainly from contact-graph and seed quality rather than from basic solver formulation changes.

- **Joint optimization of all centers and individual radii, with analytic derivatives, remains the consistently successful core formulation.** Generation 25 optimizes **78 variables**—52 center coordinates and 26 radii—using SLSQP with exact objective and inequality Jacobians. This matches the successful structure of Generations 20, 21, 23, 24, 26, 27, 28, and 31, which all produced valid packings near **2.61–2.62**. The current best’s **2.63** shows that the same formulation becomes more effective when initialized from a stronger layered topology portfolio.

- **Adaptive restarts around the incumbent appear to be a useful complement to fixed template starts.** In Generation 25, the first starts come from deterministic layered templates, while later starts perturb the current `best_centers` and shrink incumbent radii to roughly `0.82 * best_radii`. This preserves a strong discovered geometry while allowing SLSQP to alter active contacts. That is consistent with the previous Generation 16 insight that targeted refinement of promising contact graphs is more productive than treating all starts equally.

- **Strict common-radius feasibility scaling is strongly associated with perfect validation.** Generation 25 computes the limiting wall and pairwise geometric ratios, then scales all radii by the minimum ratio times `1 - 1e-10`. It achieved **validity 1.00**, as did the other successful safety-scaled variants: Generation 20 (**2.62**), Generation 28 (**2.62**), and Generation 31 (**2.62**). The high score therefore reflects a combination of aggressive nonlinear optimization and conservative final certification rather than trusting SLSQP feasibility tolerances directly.

## Ineffective Approaches

- **Incomplete integration can erase all potential value from an otherwise plausible seed strategy.** Generation 22 (`analytic_multistart_packing`) reported **0.00** for every metric because it was incorrect and failed validation. Its shown code relied on undefined surrounding objects, including `base`, `rng`, `layered_seed`, and `construct_packing()`. This is a direct contrast with Generation 25, whose constructor, fallback, optimizer path, and return interface are self-contained and evaluable.

- **More expensive optimization and LP machinery did not reliably improve the result beyond the simpler current-best approach.** Generation 23 added fixed-center LP radius optimization but reached **2.62** in **3.79 s**; Generation 24 also used LP recovery and achieved **2.62** in **3.01 s**. Both were valid, but neither surpassed Generation 25’s **2.63** in **2.28 s**. The evaluated evidence indicates that LP postprocessing is robust for feasibility and radius allocation, but it is not by itself sufficient to produce the higher-quality center/contact graph.

- **Restricted or weak seed portfolios can produce much lower-quality local optima despite using the same broad SLSQP-plus-certification family.** The previously evaluated Generation 17 achieved only **2.40**, **target ratio 0.91**, and **combined score 0.91** with perfect validity. Its limited near-hexagonal starts did not explore enough distinct layered contact graphs. This reinforces the contrast with Generation 25’s eight asymmetric templates plus incumbent-based restarts.

- **Complexity increases correctness risk when the full execution path is not robust.** The previous Generation 15 was classified incorrect with all-zero results despite using multiple SLSQP stages, dense Jacobians, LP certification, and many perturbed seeds. Generation 22 provides another recent example of this failure mode. Successful programs such as Generation 25 avoid this by retaining a dependency-free fallback and enclosing optional SciPy optimization in a safe exception path.

## Implementation Insights

- **Generation 25’s constraint representation is direct and analytically differentiated.** It creates all **325** pair constraints for 26 circles and enforces
  `||c_i - c_j|| - r_i - r_j >= 0`,
  alongside four wall-clearance constraints per circle. The Jacobian explicitly supplies normalized center-separation directions and `-1` radius derivatives. This supports reliable SLSQP progress and is consistent with the strong results from the analytic-Jacobian programs, including Generation 21 (**2.61**, **1.50 s**) and Generation 31 (**2.62**, **1.78 s**).

- **The current best’s seed generator intentionally breaks horizontal symmetry.** For each row, `layered_seed` uses independently varied left and right margins and adds deterministic coordinate perturbations based on row, column, and phase. This is more specific than merely randomizing a lattice: it preserves useful dense-row structure while producing different boundary contacts and unequal-radius opportunities. Generation 25’s improvement from the common **2.62** level to **2.63** is the clearest evaluated evidence that these asymmetric seeded structures matter.

- **The optimization loop has a staged search character without a separate formal second optimization pass.** Generation 25 runs 18 total attempts: template-based starts, additional perturbed template starts, and incumbent-centered restarts. Because later attempts use the best result found so far, search effort is conditionally concentrated around stronger arrangements. This resembles the successful selective-polishing principle identified earlier for Generation 16, but Generation 25 attained the same reported **2.63** level in less time (**2.28 s** versus Generation 16’s previously reported **3.65 s**).

- **Certification is redundant by design, which explains stable validity.** The program uses variable bounds, explicit wall constraints, pairwise constraints, a scale based on the exact limiting wall or pair ratio, and an additional `1e-10` safety factor. Even though `compute_max_radii` is included as a utility and is not used in the main optimization path, the actual returned radii are protected by the more reliable global limiting-ratio procedure. This aligns with the consistent **validity 1.00** of Generation 25 and the other successful scaled solutions.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Expand the eight layered templates through deterministic symmetry and defect variants.** Add horizontal mirrors, vertical row-order reversals, and phase-shifted copies of the strongest `(5,6,5,5,5)` family, plus nearby five-row partitions such as `(6,5,5,6,4)`, `(5,6,4,6,5)`, and `(4,5,6,6,5)`. Preserve independently asymmetric margins after reversal/mirroring so these are genuinely different boundary-contact graphs rather than equivalent starts.

2. **Add coherent row-level deformations to `layered_seed`, not just per-circle noise.** Sample deterministic variants with a small horizontal translation per row, alternating row shear, and slight vertical-gap changes while retaining row ordering; for example, apply row shifts in approximately `[-0.012, 0.012]` and adjust intermediate row heights by `[-0.008, 0.008]`. The current program’s success comes from structured asymmetric layered seeds, and coherent changes are more likely than independent noise to cross into a new active-contact topology.

3. **Reserve a targeted high-precision polish phase for only the strongest certified candidates.** Store several top candidates rather than just one incumbent, ranked both by certified radius sum and by distinct near-active pair/wall contact signatures, then re-run SLSQP from each with radii reduced to about `0.97–0.99` of certified values, `ftol=1e-12`, and a larger iteration budget. This extends the current incumbent-centered restart mechanism while focusing expensive refinement on layouts most likely to recover the small improvement between 2.63 and the noted 2.635 level.

4. **Use active-contact-guided local escape mutations around incumbent layouts.** After certification, identify circles participating in the smallest pair or wall slacks, then generate short-restart seeds that perturb only a small subset of weakly constrained interior circles and perhaps an entire adjacent row by offsets around `1e-3–5e-3`. Keep radii shrunk before reoptimization, as in the successful `0.82 * best_radii` restart strategy, but make the center perturbations deliberately targeted at contact-graph changes rather than uniformly perturbing every circle.

5. **Introduce a lightweight fixed-center LP radius diagnostic and use it for candidate selection, not as the primary solver.** For converged SLSQP center sets, solve the linear radius-allocation problem with wall and pairwise sum constraints, certify its result by common scaling, and retain candidates whose LP allocation improves the nonlinear allocation or has a distinct set of binding constraints. Prior results show LP recovery alone did not beat the best direct joint optimization, but it can expose promising center geometries and diversify which candidates receive the costly final joint SLSQP polish.
