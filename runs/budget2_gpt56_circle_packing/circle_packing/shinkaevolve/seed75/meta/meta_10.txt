# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- **Joint optimization of centers and independent radii is the strongest recurring pattern.** The current best program, *Multi-Start SLSQP Circle Packing* (Generation 6), jointly optimizes all 52 center coordinates and 26 radii, reaching **sum_radii 2.62, validity 1.00, combined score 1.00**. This avoids the weakness of assigning radii greedily after choosing centers, as in the Generation 0 concentric construction, which reached only **0.96** total radius and **0.36** combined score.

- **Multi-start symmetry breaking consistently produces near-target dense arrangements.** The current best uses ten deterministic perturbations of a 5×5-plus-interstitial seed and reaches the top recorded score. Comparable multi-start SLSQP designs also perform strongly: Generation 2’s deterministic triangular-lattice starts achieved **2.62 / 1.00** in **0.87 s**, while Generations 1, 4, and 5 achieved **2.61–2.62** with **0.99** scores. Multiple starts allow different contact graphs to emerge rather than trapping optimization in a single symmetric lattice arrangement.

- **Explicit analytic geometric constraints are highly effective.** High-performing programs formulate wall containment as `x-r`, `1-x-r`, `y-r`, and `1-y-r`, and pair non-overlap as squared center distance minus squared radius sum. The current best follows this exact formulation. Generation 2 and Generation 5 additionally supplied analytic Jacobians and also reached **2.62**, reinforcing that direct constrained optimization is much stronger than post-hoc radius adjustment.

- **Final conservative global radius scaling reliably preserves perfect validity.** The current best computes the tightest wall/pair radius factor and applies a `0.999999` safety multiplier. This mirrors the successful feasibility safeguards in Generations 1–8 and 11, all of which reported **validity 1.00**. The shared pattern is to permit SLSQP to search aggressively, then remove tiny tolerance-related violations without moving centers.

## Ineffective Approaches

- **The regular concentric-ring construction was structurally poor for the objective.** Generation 0’s center circle plus 8/16 ring pattern obtained only **sum_radii 0.96** and **combined score 0.36**. Its outer centers were clipped against the square boundary, compressing spacing and wasting usable area. Its regular ring geometry also prevented the unequal boundary/interior radius allocation seen in the 2.6-range solutions.

- **Greedy pairwise radius scaling is too conservative as the main allocation mechanism.** Generation 0 starts radii from boundary clearances and repeatedly reduces overlapping pairs. This local procedure can shrink circles because of early pair decisions without compensating enlargement elsewhere. In contrast, the current best makes radii decision variables during global optimization, allowing a small constrained circle to coexist with larger nearby circles.

- **A single or weakly varied layered initialization can lose packing quality despite fast runtime.** Generation 7’s *Optimized Layered 26-Circle Packing* used a fixed nonuniform 5/6/5/6/4 row structure and reached only **2.59**, **0.98**, although it was fast at **0.58 s**. The result supports the evaluation feedback that its initialization/contact structure remained a limiter compared with richer multistart exploration.

- **More certification or restart machinery did not automatically improve the evaluated objective.** Generation 8 used ten starts, fixed-center LP certification, safety scaling, and a second polish, but achieved **2.61** in **2.72 s**. Generation 11 added diverse seed topologies and took **3.71 s**, yet remained at **2.62 / 0.99**. These mechanisms were robust, but their added complexity and runtime did not surpass the current best’s recorded **1.00** score.

## Implementation Insights

- **The current best’s constraint representation is compact and appropriate for SLSQP.** It precomputes upper-triangular pair indices with `np.triu_indices(26, 1)`, so all **325** pairwise clearances are evaluated vectorially. The squared-distance constraint avoids square roots in the optimizer’s dominant pairwise calculation:
  `||c_i-c_j||² - (r_i+r_j)² >= 0`.
  This is the same core nonlinear geometry used by the other successful SLSQP variants.

- **Starting with very small radii makes perturbed starts feasible and lets optimization determine the allocation.** The current best initializes every radius at `0.003`, while centers begin from the 5×5 grid plus an extra interstitial circle. This prevents initial overlap infeasibility even after Gaussian center perturbations. It is consistent with the successful Generation 6 evaluation feedback: joint optimization, rather than a fixed-radius lattice, was able to develop a dense final arrangement.

- **The polish routine is a particularly robust postprocessing design.** Instead of changing centers or greedily modifying selected radii, it derives all allowable common scale factors from current wall distances and all pair distances, then applies the minimum with a strict margin. This preserves the optimized relative radius pattern and every pairwise relation. That matches the validation success of the current program and the repeated feedback for Generations 1, 2, 4, and 5 that conservative scaling protects against finite SLSQP termination tolerances.

- **The current best trades derivative sophistication for broader multistart search.** Unlike several alternatives that provide analytic Jacobians—Generation 2 at **0.87 s**, Generation 5 at **0.97 s**, and Generation 1 at **1.12 s**—the current program passes only the constraint function to SLSQP, but uses ten starts and a strict `ftol=1e-10`, `maxiter=900`. Its **2.07 s** runtime is higher than the analytic-Jacobian variants, but it still achieved the top evaluated quality tier.

## Performance Analysis

- **The evaluation separates low-quality geometric constructions from a tight SLSQP performance cluster.** Generation 0 is a large outlier at **0.96** radius sum and **0.36** score. All constrained, multistart SLSQP methods occupy a narrow **2.59–2.62** range with **0.98–1.00** combined scores, showing that global nonlinear optimization—not merely the exact seed pattern—is the dominant quality transition.

- **The current best is tied for the best measured objective/score, not uniquely highest in raw radius.** Its **2.62**, **target ratio 1.00**, and **combined score 1.00** match Generation 2’s *Constrained Hexagonal Circle Packing* and Generation 6’s own stated evaluation. Generations 1, 5, and 11 also reached **2.62**, but were scored **0.99** under their corresponding evaluations. The current best’s perfect validity and target ratio are therefore central to its top combined score.

- **Runtime varies substantially without a monotonic objective benefit.** Generation 2 reached the same **2.62 / 1.00** as the current best in **0.87 s**, versus the current best’s **2.07 s**. Generation 11 took **3.71 s** for **2.62 / 0.99**, and Generation 8 took **2.72 s** for **2.61 / 0.99**. Thus, more starts, LP certification, or diverse seed families improved robustness/exploration but did not consistently improve the measured radius sum.

- **Reported comments about approximately 2.635 are not reflected in evaluated output.** Generations 2, 3, 4, 7, and the current code mention a prior or claimed value near **2.635**, yet actual evaluated sums range from **2.59 to 2.62**. The concrete evaluation evidence supports treating **2.62 with strict feasibility** as the demonstrated high-water mark, with validity-preserving postprocessing appearing more reliable than the unverified higher commented value.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Add analytic Jacobians for both the objective and geometric constraints while retaining the current 10-start search.** Implement derivatives for the 325 squared pair-clearance constraints and the linear wall constraints, then pass `jac` and constraint Jacobians to SLSQP. Generations 1, 2, and 5 reached the same 2.62 quality tier faster with analytic derivatives, so this is a low-risk way to improve convergence accuracy and reduce the current 2.07 s runtime.

2. **Broaden initialization topology rather than only perturbing the 5×5-plus-one scaffold.** Keep several of the current deterministic Gaussian perturbations, but add starts from triangular/hexagonal lattices, staggered alternating-row layouts, and reflected/rotated versions with an interstitial circle placed in different cells. The evidence indicates multistart symmetry breaking is highly effective, while Generation 11 suggests that diversity must be selective rather than simply adding many expensive restarts; use a compact portfolio of 6–10 meaningfully distinct feasible seeds.

3. **Use a two-stage optimization schedule: exploratory solve followed by high-precision polish on the best few candidates.** First run all starts with somewhat lower iteration limits or looser `ftol`, rank feasible outputs by unscaled radius sum, then rerun the top 2–3 candidates with `maxiter≈1200–2000`, `ftol≈1e-12`, and analytic Jacobians. This preserves the current program’s contact-graph exploration while concentrating expensive high-precision work only on layouts already near the demonstrated 2.62 ceiling.

4. **Seed radii from local geometric clearance instead of assigning every circle the same 0.003 radius.** For each perturbed center set, initialize radii to a small safe fraction, such as 10–20%, of `min(distance_to_wall, half nearest-center distance)`, with a common scaling chosen to guarantee pair feasibility. The current tiny-radius approach is robust, but a differentiated feasible radius vector can give SLSQP better scale information about boundary versus interior circles without reverting to greedy final radius allocation.

5. **Add a fixed-center radius LP refinement before the final common-scale feasibility safeguard.** After each successful joint SLSQP solution, hold centers fixed and maximize `sum(r_i)` subject to linear wall bounds and pair constraints `r_i + r_j <= distance(i,j)`, then apply the existing `0.999999` global scale. This directly exploits the successful principle of independent radius optimization, may recover radius lost to nonlinear solver tolerances, and retains the current best program’s highly reliable validity-preserving final polish.
