# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- **The current best, Generation 13 “Diversity-Aware Beam Search Puzzle Solver,” establishes diversity-preserving beam management as the strongest evaluated pattern.** It achieved **441,723.90**, the highest score among all listed programs, with **50/50 accepted cases**. Its selection pipeline deliberately reserves up to one quarter of the beam for the highest-ranked candidate at each blank position, fills to 75% with global elites, then shuffles candidates from the top `4 × width` fringe. This is materially stronger than purely elite-focused or simpler randomized designs.

- **DSU evaluation centered on the largest acyclic component remains the common high-performing structural signal.** Generation 13 uses `bestTree` as the dominant term (`1,000,000,000 × bestTree`), supplemented by compatible-edge rewards, extra-component penalties, and cyclic-vertex penalties. This matches the successful graph-aware approaches in Generation 9 (**431,761.82**), Generation 14 (**429,527.90**), and Generation 15 (**438,515.46**). The score differences indicate that the DSU objective is necessary but beam management determines much of the remaining quality.

- **Hash-based duplicate control and compact move histories consistently support strong throughput.** Generation 13 incrementally updates its Zobrist hash during each blank/tile swap, caches evaluations by hash, and keeps only a parent index plus move character per history entry. It reached **441,723.90**, surpassing Generation 15’s related A*-initialized diversified beam implementation by **3,208.44 points** and Generation 9 by **9,962.08 points**.

- **Randomness works best when bounded by structural quality rather than used as the primary optimizer.** Generation 13 samples only from a high-ranking fringe after retaining blank-position representatives and elites. This supports earlier evidence that elite-plus-near-elite diversification outperforms less controlled selection, such as the earlier tournament-selection variant (**412,173.44**), while retaining strong exploitation of the DSU ranking.

## Ineffective Approaches

- **Tabu-guided simulated annealing was correct but substantially weaker than beam-based structural search.** Generation 10, “Tabu-Guided Simulated Annealing Tile Tree Search,” passed all 50 cases but scored only **182,073.52**—less than half of Generation 13’s **441,723.90**. Its union-find objective, tabu memory, reheating, and anti-reversal logic provided robustness, but temperature-weighted local exploration did not match the broad parallel coverage of a diversified beam.

- **Tournament-style diversification underperformed ranked elite/fringe sampling.** The prior tournament-selection beam variant scored **412,173.44**, which is **29,550.46 points below** Generation 13. The evaluation history supports that merely adding stochasticity is insufficient: the current best’s specific ordering—blank-position coverage, strong elites, then randomized high-quality fringe—better preserves both quality and search-space coverage.

- **Expensive or unnecessary initialization does not appear to be required for top performance.** Generation 12’s Hybrid A*-Initialized Stochastic Beam Search scored **427,143.84**, and Generation 15’s A*-initialized diversified beam scored **438,515.46**. Both were accepted and strong, but Generation 13’s actual implementation begins directly from the initial board and instead spends its budget on beam iterations and diversity, outperforming Generation 15 by **3,208.44**. This suggests that the search-quality benefit of explicit blank positioning did not outweigh its cost in this evaluated configuration.

- **Unbounded or insufficiently guarded resource use can invalidate an otherwise viable search design.** Generation 16 passed **43/50** cases but received an overall **0.00** because of `RUNTIME_ERROR`. Its DSU beam-search ingredients were broadly similar to successful programs, so the failure is evidence that cache/history growth and edge-case execution reliability are critical. In contrast, Generation 13 caps evaluation-cache insertion at **1.8 million**, reached-state insertion at **1.8 million**, reserves history storage, and halts if history exceeds **2.4 million** entries.

## Implementation Insights

- **Generation 13’s ranking function is objective-aligned but still provides local gradient.** It gives overwhelming priority to `bestTree`, then uses:
  - `+18,000 × compatibleEdges`
  - `−26,000 × (components − 1)`
  - `−9,000 × cyclicVertices`
  
  The resulting score prioritizes the official largest-tree goal while distinguishing structurally promising partial boards. This is a more detailed gradient than tracking only tree size, and it likely helps the beam choose useful intermediate states before a large tree is completed.

- **The current best avoids full path-string work in the hot loop.** Every successor stores only:
  ```cpp
  history.push_back({cur.hist, mch[d]});
  ```
  and `reconstruct()` is called once for `bestHist` at the end. This extends the earlier successful insight from Generation 11’s reduced path-reconstruction overhead: compact parent-linked histories avoid repeated string allocation and copying while allowing many candidate expansions within the fixed time budget.

- **Its duplicate pruning is depth-aware, not merely visited/unvisited.** `reachedDepth` maps Zobrist hashes to the smallest known depth. A successor is discarded only when the same state was already reached at an equal or lower depth:
  ```cpp
  if (it->second <= nd) continue;
  ```
  This permits a shorter rediscovery to replace a worse route while preventing the beam from repeatedly spending work on equivalent longer paths. Combined with reverse-move pruning, it reduces immediate cycles without overconstraining exploration.

- **Generation 13 controls computational scale at several levels.** Beam width decreases from **850 for `N <= 6`** to **230 for `N = 10`**, matching the increased cost of board evaluation at larger sizes. The program checks elapsed time every eight depth iterations and stops after roughly **2.78 seconds**, limits cache growth, and caps history at **2.4 million** nodes. These safeguards distinguish it from Generation 16’s failed resource behavior and preserve its 50/50 validation reliability.

Generation 13 improves further to **441.7K** with its explicit blank-position representation phase before elite/fringe selection. This is the most concrete architectural distinction associated with the best observed result.

Generation 13 does not contain that preprocessing step yet obtains the best score. The data therefore favors efficient direct beam search with stronger selection diversity over spending part of the fixed budget on an initial routing phase.

- **Correctness alone is not enough; resource discipline separates valid and invalid beam implementations.** Generation 16’s **0.00** despite solving **43/50** cases contrasts sharply with the accepted scores of Generations 9, 12–15. Generation 13’s explicit cache/history limits and time guard preserve complete validation success while still allowing sufficient search volume to produce the best mean score.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Replace the full candidate sort with bounded partial selection while preserving the exact diversity pipeline.** Use `nth_element` to retain only the top `max(width * 4, eliteTarget + cells)` candidates by `betterState`, then sort that retained prefix before computing `bestAtBlank`, elites, and the shuffled fringe. Generation 13’s quality comes from blank-position representatives plus elite/fringe sampling, not from needing a total ordering of every generated child; reducing sort cost can buy more beam iterations or safely wider fringes.

2. **Make blank-position diversity score-aware rather than selecting representatives by board-position scan order.** Continue reserving roughly `width / 4` places for distinct blank squares, but gather all available `bestAtBlank` representatives and sort those representatives by `betterState` before admission instead of iterating positions `0..cells-1`. This retains the proven empty-position coverage while preventing a weaker representative at an early board index from consuming diversity capacity ahead of a substantially stronger representative elsewhere.

3. **Add a structural diversity key in the fringe in addition to blank-square coverage.** During the shuffled `top 4 × width` fringe fill, bucket candidates by a compact signature such as `(empty position, previousDir, bestTree, components, cyclicVertices)` or `(empty position, largest-component root region)` and cap each signature at a small number of retained states. Generation 13 already demonstrates that controlled diversity beats purely elite selection; extending it to prevent many nearly identical DSU states from filling the randomized 25% is a conservative way to broaden trajectories without reverting to weak tournament-style randomness.

4. **Use adaptive beam width based on observed expansion throughput and remaining time, with strict memory caps unchanged.** Start from the successful size-based widths, then after several layers estimate milliseconds per generated/evaluated successor and increase width modestly when cache hits and remaining time allow it, or shrink it early enough to preserve several final expansion layers. This builds on Generation 13’s disciplined 2.78-second limit and bounded storage, potentially converting saved work on easier instances into deeper or broader structurally guided search rather than risking Generation 16-style resource failures.

5. **Introduce a late-stage “full-tree completion” beam mode when `bestTree` approaches `cells - 1`.** Once a candidate reaches, for example, `bestTree >= cells - 3`, reserve an additional portion of the beam for states with the same maximal tree size but lower depth, fewer components, fewer cyclic vertices, and blank positions adjacent to tiles in or near that tree. The existing rank correctly makes `bestTree` dominant, but this targeted mode can improve the chance of preserving or shortening complete-tree solutions, aligning selection more tightly with the current program’s `bestTree == full && nd < bestK` incumbent rule.
