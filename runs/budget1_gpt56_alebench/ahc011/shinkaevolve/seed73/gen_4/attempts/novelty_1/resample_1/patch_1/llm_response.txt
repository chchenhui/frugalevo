<DESCRIPTION>
The beam search spends most of its runtime evaluating states. `calculate_scores` constructs a DSU for nearly every newly visited board, and the current DSU performs three heap allocations and vector initialization per evaluation. Since the board has at most 100 cells, fixed-size arrays eliminate this allocator overhead and permit evaluating substantially more beam candidates within the same time budget.

Additionally, the evaluation currently penalizes every extra connected component almost as strongly as it rewards increasing the largest valid tree by one tile. The actual score only depends on the largest tree, so this can incorrectly prefer states with many small connected fragments over states that have already grown the promising tree but leave unrelated tiles disconnected. Reducing this penalty makes the beam more directly optimize the contest objective while retaining a weak preference for globally connected states.

The saved evaluation time is invested primarily in the difficult larger boards, where the original beam widths are comparatively narrow and additional diversity is valuable.
</DESCRIPTION>
