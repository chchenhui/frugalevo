# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- **The current best program’s no-replacement chance sampling is the strongest measured optimization.** It scored **725,015.74** with perfect **50/50 acceptance**, exceeding all newly listed variants. At each chance node it partially shuffles ranks `1..num_empty` and samples the first `min(sample_budget, num_empty)` ranks, guaranteeing distinct empty-cell placements. This avoids spending rollout budget on duplicate insertion states. The prior comparison between the earlier stochastic version (**707,430.50**) and this distinct-sampling version supports a **17,585.24-point** improvement from this change within the same general two-ply framework.

- **A fixed, runtime-safe two-ply sampled expectimax budget remains consistently effective.** The best program uses `{18, 7}` chance samples, then maximizes over all four tilt responses after each sampled placement. This balances future uncertainty coverage against the interactive CPU limit. Generation 12’s **“Sampled Lookahead Candy Tilt Solver”** also used `{18, 7}` distinct samples and achieved **682,423.96**, while Generation 18’s `{18, 7}` close-resampling variant reached **702,118.96**. The budget is clearly viable, but the roughly 42.6k score spread between these variants shows that evaluator and sampling policy matter as much as count.

- **Connectivity-first scoring with early spatial shaping is validated across high-performing programs.** The current best heavily rewards squared connected-component sizes with a connectivity multiplier that rises from `16.1` on turn 1 to `125.0` on turn 100. It simultaneously reduces the center-of-mass dispersion penalty over time and retains flavor-specific strip and edge terms. This mirrors the successful family of programs: Generation 9’s **Heuristic Sampled Expectimax Tilt Solver** scored **712,598.48**, and Generation 16’s antithetic variant scored **709,707.52**.

- **Frequency-derived flavor regions are a useful structural prior.** The current best assigns vertical strips proportionally to total flavor counts, giving larger overall flavor populations wider target areas. This is shared by Generation 15 (**687,594.10**), Generation 17 (**694,066.44**), and Generation 13 (**695,898.44**). The approach helps the planner keep flavors separable while tilts create opportunities for same-flavor components to merge.

## Ineffective Approaches

- **Common chance samples across root directions did not outperform independent distinct sampling.** Generation 14’s **“Two-Step Sampled Expectimax Candy Tilting”** used shared random ranks specifically to reduce comparison noise, but scored only **699,701.72**, which is **25,314.02 below** the current best. Reduced root-action variance was not enough to compensate for the potential loss of independently broader stochastic exploration.

- **Antithetic placement sampling was competitive but weaker than uniformly distinct sampling.** Generation 16 paired sampled empty cells with near-opposite board positions and obtained **709,707.52**. That is strong and fully accepted, but still **15,308.22 below** the best. The result suggests that spatially paired samples can constrain chance-node coverage in ways that do not match the true distribution of rank-based future placements as effectively as the best program’s uniform no-replacement ranks.

- **Extra root-action resampling did not justify its additional complexity.** Generation 18 resampled statistically close root actions and scored **702,118.96**, **22,896.78 below** the best. Although the feedback indicates this was timeout-safe, the measured score does not show that targeted re-evaluation improved decisions relative to spending the available rollout budget on the standard distinct chance samples.

- **Higher or more elaborate rollout configurations have not shown a score advantage under the real limit.** Previous evaluations found the heavier `{23, 9}` setting could fail to emit all actions within the time limit, and Generation 7’s `22/9` configuration scored **694,548.68**, **30,467.06 below** the best. The evidence favors completing all 100 decisions with efficient, nonduplicating samples rather than increasing nominal rollout volume.

## Implementation Insights

- **The current best’s partial Fisher–Yates implementation directly eliminates duplicate chance states.** It initializes `empty_ranks[i] = i + 1`, then for sample `s` swaps with a random index in `[s, num_empty - 1]`. Because `find_pth_empty_cell` maps those ranks to legal current-board positions, every sampled placement at that node is distinct. This is a concrete implementation detail tied to the best observed score, **725,015.74**.

- **Exact stable compaction keeps simulation and live state consistent.** `apply_tilt` packs rows or columns in directional traversal order, preserving candy order along the movement axis. It then rebuilds `candies_list` from `board`, ensuring that later empty-cell counting, rank insertion, center-of-mass evaluation, region penalties, and component calculations all use synchronized coordinates. The universal 50/50 acceptance of the current best and all listed planners supports the reliability of this board-update pattern.

- **The evaluator is deliberately phase-dependent rather than a static proxy.** In the current best, the connection coefficient rises linearly with turn, while the CoM penalty falls to zero late in the game. Region penalties decline but remain active, and edge bonuses increase. Thus, early turns favor compact and geographically organized flavor structures, while late turns increasingly value realized connected-component growth. This is consistent with Generation 10’s feedback that stronger late-game connectivity weighting improved clustering, though its rollout-guided greedy approach reached only **565,129.20**.

- **Fixed-size board-side structures are appropriate for the search workload.** The current best uses `std::array` for the 10×10 board, a fixed 100-cell BFS queue for exact component scoring, and a fixed 100-rank sampling buffer. These choices reduce allocation overhead in a search that repeatedly copies and tilts states across four root actions, up to 18 first-level placements, four replies, and seven second-level samples. The retained `candies_list` vector is reserved to 100 entries, further limiting allocation churn.

## Performance Analysis

- **The current best is materially ahead of every reported evaluated alternative.** Its **725,015.74** mean is **10,424.92** above the prior next-best cited result, Generation 8/11 at **714,590.82**. Among the newly supplied individual summaries, the closest is Generation 9 at **712,598.48**, still **12,417.26 lower**. Its lead is therefore not explained by correctness differences: all these compared programs passed all 50 cases.

- **The current best’s distinct random sampling appears more valuable than deterministic or variance-reduction sampling schemes.** Deterministic stratification previously reached **675,292.62**, deterministic rotated sampling reached **678,592.72**, common-rank sampling reached **699,701.72**, close-root resampling reached **702,118.96**, and antithetic sampling reached **709,707.52**. All are below the best’s independently drawn, no-replacement chance sampling.

- **The score range among similar two-ply expectimax programs is large.** Programs sharing board simulation, four tilt actions, component-square scoring, strip planning, and approximately `{18–20, 7–8}` samples range from Generation 12’s **682,423.96** to the current best’s **725,015.74**—a difference of **42,591.78**. This confirms that the remaining performance variation comes from the interaction of evaluator weights, chance-sampling mechanics, and decision dynamics, not merely from adopting two-ply expectimax.

- **Greedy rollout guidance was substantially weaker than direct sampled expectimax in these results.** Generation 10’s rollout-guided greedy planner was robust and accepted but scored **565,129.20**, trailing the best by **159,886.54**. In contrast, the strongest results cluster around sampled expectimax with exact tilt simulation and connectivity-oriented evaluation, indicating that explicitly optimizing the post-placement response tilt at chance nodes is strongly correlated with higher score.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Keep independent partial-Fisher–Yates no-replacement sampling, but add a small independent tie-break batch only for close root actions.** Run the existing `{18,7}` search for all four directions, then if the top two estimates differ by a narrow relative threshold, re-evaluate only those two directions with an additional small unique batch such as `{4,3}` using fresh ranks. This preserves the best program’s strongest property—independent, distinct chance coverage—while focusing extra computation where sampling noise is most likely to change the chosen tilt.

2. **Use phase-adaptive redistribution of the proven rollout budget without exceeding the current worst-case search cost.** Early in the game, where many empty cells make the first chance placement especially uncertain, try allocations such as `{20,5}` or `{21,4}`; retain `{18,7}` during the middle game; and shift toward `{14,9}` or exact enumeration whenever late-game empty counts are small. This extends the successful two-ply sampled-expectimax structure while respecting the evidence that larger total workloads can threaten the interactive time limit.

3. **Add exact chance-node enumeration selectively at late shallow nodes.** When `num_empty` is at most a modest threshold, such as 10–14, enumerate every possible next insertion rank rather than sampling; compensate by reducing second-level samples or limiting exact enumeration to the first lookahead chance layer. The current implementation already enumerates automatically when empties are below its configured sample count, and extending this selectively should improve high-value late-game decisions where connectivity dominates.

4. **Test a hybrid unique sample set that is mostly uniform random but reserves a few board-extreme placements.** For example, retain 14–16 ranks drawn through the current partial shuffle, then add up to four distinct empty cells chosen from meaningful geometric positions—topmost, bottommost, leftmost, and rightmost legal empties—before evaluating their best replies. Unlike the weaker fully antithetic or deterministic samplers, this keeps the dominant distributional coverage while ensuring that directional tilt consequences at board boundaries are represented in every chance estimate.

5. **Introduce asymmetric second-ply sampling based on the first-ply placement’s immediate response ambiguity.** After placing a sampled next candy, perform a cheap evaluation of its four possible reply tilts; if one reply is clearly superior, use fewer depth-2 samples for that placement, but allocate the saved samples to placements whose top reply directions are close. This retains direct expectimax maximization after every chance placement, while spending more of the fixed runtime budget on uncertain branches rather than on branches whose response action is already obvious.
