<DESCRIPTION>
The current annealing phase spends 70% of proposals on direct deletion. After the initial erosion, most uniformly chosen cells are not adjacent to the outside zero component, so these proposals are rejected immediately and do not help reshape difficult bottlenecks. Use the broader random-cell / neighboring-color kernel instead: it naturally supplies deletion, zero restoration, and ward-to-ward recoloring moves. This preserves enough restoration and recoloring moves for topology changes, while still allowing deletion whenever it is locally feasible.

Also reserve the last part of the time budget for monotone erosion. Annealing is useful for rearranging the map early, but once it has made useful rearrangements, deterministic deletion passes exploit newly exposed removable cells more efficiently and cannot degrade the incumbent best solution.
</DESCRIPTION>
