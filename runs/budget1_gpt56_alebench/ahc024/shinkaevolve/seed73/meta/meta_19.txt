# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- **The current best, Generation 18 `adaptive_greedy_erosion`, establishes the strongest observed greedy-plus-SA recipe at 1935.48.** It narrowly exceeds the previous best Generation 7 `fast_erosion_sa` (**1934.44**) by **1.04 points**, and beats Generation 16 `balanced_sa_polish` (**1917.76**) by **17.72 points**. Its effective schedule is: randomized greedy deletion, then non-monotone annealing with local recoloring, while retaining the best board independently of the current SA state.

- **Broad random-cell proposals with neighbor-derived target colors outperform strong zero-frontier targeting.** Generation 18 samples any of the 2500 cells, then selects among zero and the four neighboring colors. This preserves useful deletion pressure while allowing transfers and temporary restoration. It outperformed frontier-oriented methods such as Generation 17 `frontier_peeler` (**1884.30**), Generation 12 `zero_frontier_sampling` (**1877.02**), and Generation 10 `frontier_peeling` (**659.68**).

- **Repeated greedy testing is useful when it is adaptive rather than a rigid long phase.** Generation 18 revisits cells in shuffled orders, but stops greedy search after three 256-attempt blocks with no successful deletion. This avoids spending the whole nominal 500 ms allocation on saturated erosion states and leaves time for annealing. The resulting score surpassed other staged schedules, including Generation 16’s greedy/SA/final-greedy design (**1917.76**) and Generation 14’s restart/reheat SA design (**1861.84**).

- **Reversible score-decreasing moves remain a high-value pattern.** Like the prior Generation 7 leader, Generation 18 permits zero-to-nonzero moves according to an annealing probability, but snapshots `best_grid` only on objective improvement. This enables structural changes that unlock later deletion moves without risking a worse final output. Purely erosion-centered randomized approaches were substantially weaker, including Generation 13 (**735.98**) and Generation 10 (**659.68**).

## Ineffective Approaches

- **Strong frontier-only or near-frontier deletion bias consistently underperformed the best broad local search.** Generation 12’s zero-frontier sampling scored **1877.02**, Generation 17’s frontier peeling scored **1884.30**, and Generation 9’s deletion-biased annealing scored **1886.94**. All were correct, but remained **48.54–58.46 points** below Generation 18. Their proposals emphasize immediately removable outside-adjacent cells, reducing access to topology-changing nonzero transfers and restoration moves.

- **Simpler randomized erosion passes without a comparably effective annealing phase produced much lower results.** Generation 13 `frontier_erosion` reached **735.98** and Generation 10 `frontier_peeling` reached **659.68**, despite full correctness and incremental legality maintenance. Their local reshaping was only occasional, whereas Generation 18 continuously includes neighboring ward-transfer and restoration possibilities in its main SA proposal distribution.

- **Move-class adaptation did not by itself compensate for reduced proposal simplicity or throughput.** Generation 15 `adaptive_move_classes` scored **1372.40**, far below the 1935.48 leader. Its deletion/restoration/ward-transfer classes were valid and theoretically diverse, but the measured result suggests that explicit class weighting was less productive than Generation 18’s inexpensive uniform cell sampling plus randomly selected local target color.

- **Additional deterministic polishing did not reliably beat the simpler best-state annealing approach.** Generation 16’s final greedy polish was strong at **1917.76**, but still fell short by **17.72**. This supports the earlier evidence that more erosion stages are not automatically beneficial once the search has reached structural bottlenecks; the quality and volume of non-monotone local exploration matter more.

## Implementation Insights

- **Generation 18 performs exact adjacency validation with only local edge deltas.** For a recolored cell, it processes at most four grid neighbors plus implicit exterior-zero contacts at borders, aggregates changes into at most eight `Delta` records, updates `edge_count`, and checks only the affected color pairs against `required_adj`. This avoids global adjacency recomputation while enforcing both requirements: every original color-pair contact remains present, and no unrequired contact is introduced.

- **Its connectivity checks are intentionally minimized without sacrificing correctness.** After a recolor, it runs `connected_color(old_color)` only for the departing nonzero color. The incoming nonzero color does not require BFS because it is selected from an orthogonal neighbor’s color, so the new cell is attached immediately to that color’s existing connected component. Zero is separately validated by `connected_zero_to_outside()`, which verifies that every zero cell is reachable from a boundary zero cell. This same localized-validation principle was identified in the former Generation 7 leader and is strongly correlated with top-end scores.

- **O(1) cell-list maintenance supports frequent BFS and exact rollback.** `cells[color]` and `pos_in_list[p]` use swap-with-last deletion, making insertion/removal constant-time. BFS starts from `cells[color][0]` and compares its visited count to `cells[color].size()`, rather than rescanning the full grid to find color members. On rejection, `revert_move` restores the grid, cell lists, and each altered edge count exactly.

- **The current best combines efficient search control with best-state preservation.** Greedy erosion uses shuffled orders and early stopping after unproductive blocks. SA checks time only every 256 iterations, reducing clock overhead; it uses a cooling schedule from temperature 2.0 toward 0.01; and it permits accepted negative score moves while keeping `best_grid` separate. These low-level choices are consistent with Generation 18’s slight but meaningful lead over Generation 7 and its larger advantage over the 1860–1918 score range of other SA variants.

## Performance Analysis

- **Generation 18 is now the measured leader, but the gain over the prior best is narrow.** Its **1935.48** score is **1.04** above Generation 7’s **1934.44**, so both support the same central conclusion: high-throughput greedy initialization plus broad, reversible local SA is the strongest observed family. Generation 18’s result is also **17.72** above Generation 16 (**1917.76**) and **29.58** above Generation 9 (**1905.90**).

- **The high-performing cluster is dominated by incremental greedy-plus-SA implementations, but proposal mechanics create meaningful separation.** Generation 18 (**1935.48**), Generation 7 (**1934.44**), Generation 16 (**1917.76**), Generation 9 (**1905.90**), and Generation 17 (**1884.30**) all use incremental adjacency accounting and connectivity checks. Since all passed **50/50** cases, the roughly **51-point** spread between Generation 18 and Generation 17 reflects search effectiveness and move throughput rather than correctness differences.

- **Frontier-heavy approaches form a lower-score band even when fully correct.** Generation 12 (**1877.02**), Generation 10 (**659.68**), and Generation 13 (**735.98**) all emphasize frontier erosion. Generation 17 is the strongest frontier-oriented entry at **1884.30**, but still trails Generation 18 by **51.18**. The results reinforce that immediate zero-frontier growth is useful as a greedy mechanism but insufficient as the dominant long-run proposal strategy.

- **Correctness was uniformly robust, making score comparisons especially informative.** Every listed program was accepted on all 50 cases, including the low-score randomized erosion variants. Therefore, Generation 18’s lead is not explained by relaxed constraints: its exact required-adjacency checks, source-color connectivity BFS, zero-to-boundary connectivity verification, and reversible rollback achieved full validity while also producing the best mean zero-area objective.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Add an articulation-style precheck before `connected_color(old_color)` to avoid unnecessary BFS calls.** Count orthogonal neighbors still having `old_color` before the move; if this count is 0 or 1, removing the cell cannot disconnect the departing color, so skip its connectivity BFS. This directly extends the current localized-validation design and should raise SA proposal throughput, especially after erosion has made most regions thin.

2. **Keep the uniform random-cell proposal kernel, but adapt only the target-choice distribution based on recent valid/accepted move statistics.** Continue sampling `p` uniformly from all 2500 cells, then choose among zero and neighboring colors, but track attempted-valid-accepted rates for deletion (`nonzero→0`), restoration (`0→nonzero`), and transfers (`A→B`). Every few thousand attempts, mildly increase the probability of productive target categories while enforcing a floor for restoration/transfer moves so the search does not collapse into frontier-heavy erosion behavior that historically underperformed.

3. **Use brief cyclic reheats when the current SA score remains below `best_score` without producing improvements.** Preserve the current best-board snapshot mechanism, but when, for example, 20–50k accepted or attempted moves yield no best-score improvement, raise temperature temporarily from the cooling-schedule value to roughly `0.3–0.8` for a short window, then resume cooling. Generation 18 already benefits from non-monotone moves; controlled reheats may help it escape late structural bottlenecks without requiring expensive full restarts.

4. **Introduce rare transactional two-cell local slides built from existing legal single-cell moves.** At low frequency—such as 1–3% of SA proposals—try patterns like restoring a zero cell to adjacent color `A`, then deleting an adjacent `B` cell to zero, or a two-step `A→B` / `B→0` shift; retain the sequence only if every intermediate state is legal and the net score is accepted under SA. This preserves the proven exact delta accounting and rollback infrastructure while allowing small topology changes that ordinary one-cell moves may not traverse efficiently.

5. **Make greedy stopping yield-sensitive rather than stopping only after completely empty blocks.** Retain shuffled deletion passes, but monitor deletion success rate over 128–256 attempted nonzero cells and switch to SA once the rate stays below a small threshold, such as 1–2 successful deletions per block, rather than requiring three zero-success blocks. The current adaptive greedy phase is already a key improvement; this variation can reserve additional time for the SA phase when greedy erosion is technically still succeeding but no longer efficiently improving the board.
