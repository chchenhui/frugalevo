<DESCRIPTION>
The edge-count strategy currently chooses adjacent codewords with a separation of only about 2.5 standard deviations of the noisy edge count. This produces a substantial adjacent-class confusion probability, and because 100 independent predictions are scored exponentially through \(0.9^E\), reducing errors is much more valuable than the moderate increase in N.

For an interior codeword, a normal approximation gives error probability approximately \(2\Phi(-K/2)\), where K is the adjacent mean separation in standard deviations. Maximizing the expected score proxy \((1-0.1p)^{100}/N\), while noting that N grows approximately linearly with K, gives an optimum near K=4.8 rather than 2.5. This change increases graph size only when the edge-count strategy is selected, but greatly improves robustness against noise.
</DESCRIPTION>
