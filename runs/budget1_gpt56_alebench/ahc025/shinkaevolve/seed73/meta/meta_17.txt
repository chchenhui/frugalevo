# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- **The current best program, Generation 11 “Pivot Estimation with Annealed Group Balancing,” establishes a markedly stronger hybrid pipeline:** it scored **630.29 combined** with a **1,586,568.56 raw mean**, passing **50/50** cases. Its key distinction is not merely pivot-based estimation or annealing—both were already known to work—but reserving approximately **20% of the query budget** for **exact assignment-level group comparisons after estimated optimization**. This substantially outperformed the prior best reported in the global insights, Generation 6 at **309.88 combined** and **3,227,051.56 raw mean**.

- **Best-so-far surrogate optimization remains strongly validated.** Generation 11 keeps `best_assignment_array` throughout SA and restores it before exact refinement. This preserves the previously successful best-state pattern from Generations 6 (**309.88**) and 15 (**277.41**) while avoiding the known weakness of outputting a potentially worse terminal annealing state, as seen in Generation 12 from the earlier results (**269.31**).

- **Combining approximate item-level information with true group-level feedback is the dominant successful pattern.** Generation 11 uses cached 1v1 pivot sorting, 1v2 pivot-gap queries, binary-search placement of non-pivots, LPT initialization, and SA for broad optimization. It then uses exact set comparisons to identify a heavier and lighter group and validates whether moving an item reduces true imbalance. This is materially more effective than relying on estimated weights alone: Generation 13’s pivot-estimation-plus-SA approach reached only **222.23**, and Generation 15’s closely related pivot-guided annealing reached **277.41**.

- **Targeted exact move validation is more valuable than proxy-only repair.** Generation 14’s rank-based proxy balancer with query-driven transfer searches performed reasonably well at **427.22**, but Generation 11’s exact post-SA refinement achieved a further **203.07 combined-score-point improvement**. The evidence indicates that assignment-level comparisons become especially useful after a good estimated partition already exists.

## Ineffective Approaches

- **Purely ordinal/exponential proxy reconstruction underperformed richer pivot-gap estimation.** Generation 9’s “Pivot Rank Estimation with Query-Based Repair” used rank buckets and exponential-like proxy weights, but achieved only **119.96 combined** and **8,336,186.22 raw mean**. This is dramatically below Generation 11’s **630.29** / **1.587M**, supporting the prior observation that rank-only quantile models lose too much magnitude information for high-quality balancing.

- **Annealing without meaningful exact final correction remained substantially weaker.** Generation 13 used pivot estimates, greedy assignment, and simulated annealing but scored **222.23**. Generation 15 similarly used pivot-guided interpolation and best-state annealing, scoring **277.41**. Both passed all cases, so the large gap to Generation 11 is partition-quality evidence rather than a correctness artifact. Their common limitation is that their final partitions are governed primarily by approximate inferred weights.

- **Simple pairwise group balancing was less effective than the current program’s post-SA exact move test.** Generation 10’s proxy LPT construction followed by pairwise group comparisons and cautious transfers scored **301.28**. This was robust and fully accepted, but its direct balancing mechanism was less successful than Generation 11’s procedure of first finding a strong estimated solution, then testing the true condition for a beneficial move: whether `heavy_without_item > light`.

- **Protocol mistakes can erase otherwise competitive heuristic quality.** Generation 16 had a raw mean of **3,994,545.70** and passed **48/50** cases, but received **0.00 combined score** due to `WRONG_ANSWER: Item 0 appears multiple times`. Its final padding queries could compare `{0}` against `{0}` or otherwise violate distinct/disjoint-item requirements. This confirms that cache/query-budget machinery must preserve interactive validity, not just heuristic behavior.

## Implementation Insights

- **Generation 11’s exact refinement is implemented around a mathematically meaningful local condition.** After restoring `best_assignment_array`, it compares two complete groups. If one is truly heavier, it samples an item from that group and queries:
  - left: the heavy group excluding that item;
  - right: the lighter group.
  
  A result of `heavy_without_item > light` proves that moving the sampled item reduces the true squared-sum imbalance for that pair. This is a stronger and more directly objective-aligned use of queries than merely using them to refine individual weight estimates.

- **The query budget is explicitly split between estimation and correction.** The current program reserves `max(2, Q/5)` queries for refinement and selects its pivot count using an estimated cost model based on pivot sorting, 1v2 gap inference, and non-pivot binary searches. This avoids spending the entire budget on imperfect inference. The evaluation result—**630.29 combined**, more than double the prior reported leader’s **309.88**—shows that this controlled allocation is associated with a major gain.

- **The surrogate optimization remains computationally efficient enough to leave time for true-query polishing.** Like the previous strong Generation 6 implementation, Generation 11 maintains group sums and sum-of-squares incrementally for relocate and swap moves. Its group-item vectors plus per-item positions allow accepted relocations and swaps to update membership in constant-time-style operations. This lets SA run within the **1.85-second** limit without repeatedly recomputing the full partition objective.

- **The estimator is richer than rank bucketing while retaining ordinal consistency.** Pivots are merge-sorted with cached singleton comparisons. For later pivots, 1v2 queries compare the current pivot to `previous_pivot + auxiliary_pivot`, bracketing an estimated incremental gap. Non-pivots are placed by binary search among pivots and assigned geometric-mean interpolated estimates. Monotonicity safeguards ensure estimates do not contradict observed singleton ordering. This preserves the earlier successful Generation 6-style magnitude-aware reconstruction, now combined with substantially better exact refinement.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Make exact refinement choose candidate group pairs from the estimated imbalance rather than uniformly at random.** After restoring `best_assignment_array`, reconstruct estimated group sums and spend most refinement iterations on one of the estimated-heaviest groups versus one of the estimated-lightest groups, with a smaller random fraction for diversification. Generation 11 demonstrates that exact group feedback is highly valuable; directing it toward likely imbalanced pairs should yield more productive two-query move tests than random pair selection.

2. **Test several items per confirmed-heavy group comparison, prioritizing items near the estimated transfer amount.** Once an exact comparison identifies heavy group `A` and light group `B`, estimate the gap as `estimated_sum[A] - estimated_sum[B]` and test items from `A` ordered by closeness to half that gap, rather than selecting one random item. The existing condition `A \ {x} > B` is an exact proof of improvement, so improving only the choice of `x` preserves the strongest feature of Generation 11 while raising the probability that each expensive validation query causes a beneficial transfer.

3. **Add exact swap refinement after move attempts fail, using a two-stage query test on heavy/light groups.** When no single item from the exact-heavy group can be moved beneficially, choose a candidate heavy item `x` and light item `y` whose estimated weights suggest `x > y`, then query `(heavy \ {x}) ∪ {y}` versus `(light \ {y}) ∪ {x}`. Accept the swap only if the comparison shows the modified heavy side is lighter than the modified light side, or use a second orientation/validation comparison to ensure the swap reduces pairwise squared imbalance; this extends true-feedback correction to cases where all improving changes require exchanges rather than relocations.

4. **Replace the fixed 80/20 query split with a small adaptive reserve policy based on inferred-estimator confidence.** Retain a substantial exact-refinement reserve, since this is the major distinguishing success of Generation 11, but choose it dynamically: reserve more queries when pivot-gap brackets are wide, many non-pivots lie outside pivot extremes, or estimated group sums remain poorly separated; reserve fewer when pivot coverage and brackets are strong. Evaluate conservative variants such as `Q/6`, `Q/5`, `Q/4`, and a confidence-driven value clamped to that range, because the best allocation likely depends on instance size and the reliability of reconstructed magnitudes.

5. **Introduce a short targeted surrogate intensification phase before restoring the best state and beginning exact refinement.** During the final portion of SA, repeatedly inspect estimated heaviest/lightest group pairs and greedily apply only estimated-improving relocations or swaps, particularly items whose weights are close to the estimated balancing gap; keep the global `best_assignment_array` safeguard unchanged. This builds on the validated best-state SA design while focusing local search where random relocate/swap proposals are least efficient, potentially supplying exact refinement with a stronger starting partition.
