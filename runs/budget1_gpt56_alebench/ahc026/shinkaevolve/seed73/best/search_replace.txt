<DESCRIPTION>
Replace the purely local “largest minimum label” destination rule with a bounded deterministic rollout. For each possible destination, the solver applies the candidate move on a trial state, removes the current target, and greedily simulates the next several removals. The primary comparison is predicted relocation energy, followed by total relocated boxes, maximum stack height, and the urgency of boxes still buried after the rollout.

The existing rule protects an urgent destination stack, but cannot distinguish two destinations that look equally safe now and create very different blocking patterns shortly afterward. Since there are only 10 stacks and 200 boxes, evaluating 9 candidates over a short horizon is cheap while providing a materially better placement signal.
</DESCRIPTION>
