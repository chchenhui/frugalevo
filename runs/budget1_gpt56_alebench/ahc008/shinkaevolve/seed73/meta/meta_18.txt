# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- **The current best, Cooperative Strip Separator Builder (Generation 16, `strip_planner`), establishes the strongest measured result at 3,727,463.30 with 50/50 accepted cases.** Its core structural choice is horizontal strip partitioning, with humans sorted by initial row and assigned one strip each. Every internal separator is split into non-overlapping halves: the upper strip builds columns 1–15 of its lower boundary, while the lower strip builds columns 16–30 of its upper boundary. This gives complete separators without duplicate ownership or same-target contention.

- **Rank-ordered construction planning improved coordinated execution.** Generation 16 determines action-planning priority by strip rank rather than original human input order. Its feedback specifically identifies this as preventing arbitrary input order from delaying top or bottom separator completion. This is a concrete coordination refinement over otherwise similar strip approaches, and it outperformed Generation 15’s Coordinated Strip-Based Pet Containment (3,643,761.58) by **83,701.72** and Generation 14’s Cooperative Row-Partition Wall Builder (3,355,022.06) by **372,441.24**.

- **BFS over both existing walls and same-turn reserved walls remains the essential correctness-and-score foundation.** Generation 16’s `bfs` treats `wall` cells and `plan.reserved_wall` cells as impassable, and `choose_build_job` only considers adjacent standing squares with known BFS distance. This matches the successful pattern previously seen in Generation 14 and Generation 15: reachable standing-position selection prevents humans from choosing nominally nearby but inaccessible construction jobs.

- **Safe-side construction preferences and separator-connectivity scoring are productive when attached to a focused strip plan.** Generation 16 adds a very large penalty (`+10000`) for standing outside the human’s assigned strip interior, plus a small preference (`-2` per neighboring wall/boundary) for extending connected separator portions. The strong safe-side preference preserves a human’s ability to keep working on its own side of a closing cut, while the connectivity term encourages progressive barrier completion. This is more effective than earlier adjacency-focused targeting alone, such as Generation 1’s 3,202,376.04 result.

## Ineffective Approaches

- **Incorrect local simulation of same-turn legality caused catastrophic invalidity in several otherwise promising BFS strip builders.** Generation 9 (`stripplanner_bfs`) passed 38/50 cases but failed on turn 94 with an attempted move into an impassable square; Generation 10 (`dual_line_partitioner`) passed 49/50 but failed similarly on turn 78; Generation 13 (`stripwall_fast`) passed 46/50 but failed on turn 22; and Generation 17 (`hybrid_reachable_strips`) passed 42/50 but failed on turn 108. All consequently received combined score 0.00. The common failure mode was not the strip concept itself, but divergence between local movement/wall assumptions and the judge’s actual simultaneous-action state.

- **The failed variants demonstrate that “BFS exists” is insufficient unless its obstacle model matches planned construction.** The invalid solutions tracked some combination of walls, tentative builds, pets, and move destinations, but still emitted moves into squares that the judge considered impassable. In contrast, Generation 16’s planning consistently makes earlier same-turn planned walls unavailable to later BFS searches, and its `apply_and_read` mirrors the required construction-before-movement ordering.

- **More elaborate local scoring did not reliably beat economical separator ownership.** Previous evaluations showed pet-aware or heavily weighted local wall-target heuristics underperforming the simpler internal-cut structure: Generation 5 reached 3,384,198.56 and Generation 1 reached 3,202,376.04, both well below the prior best strip designs. Generation 16 confirms that the main score driver is still efficient, conflict-free separator completion rather than rich pet-distance or wall-adjacency scoring; its pet treatment is primarily a legality filter (`pet_near`) rather than a large optimization term.

- **Implementation integrity is a hard prerequisite.** Generation 12 (`allow_human_overlap`) scored 0.00 and passed 0/50 because unresolved merge artifacts such as `>>>>>>> REPLACE` and `</DIFF>` broke compilation. This contrasts with the accepted programs, whose relatively compact turn-planning flow supports both correctness and performance.

## Implementation Insights

- **Generation 16’s wall assignment is precise and balanced.** In `build_strip_plan`, each human receives at most two 15-cell wall segments: the right half of its top separator and the left half of its bottom separator. Thus each shared divider has exactly 30 assigned wall cells, split deterministically between adjacent strips. The design avoids duplicate target lists and distributes construction responsibility across humans without requiring runtime negotiation.

- **Its legality layers are separated cleanly.** `can_build` rejects out-of-board cells, existing walls, earlier reserved walls, cells occupied by another human, and targets within Manhattan distance one of a pet. Then `choose_build_job` separately verifies that an adjacent standing cell is in bounds, unblocked by existing or reserved walls, and BFS-reachable. This separation of target legality from standing-position reachability is a major reason Generation 16 remained valid across all 50 cases.

- **The current best accurately reserves direct builds before later humans plan.** `make_turn` processes humans in strip-rank order. Once an earlier human chooses a lowercase build action, that target is recorded in `reserved_wall`; subsequent humans’ BFS and target selection treat it as blocked immediately. This specifically protects later movement routing from a square that will become a wall in the same turn, addressing the impassable-square failures that invalidated Generations 9, 10, 13, and 17.

- **Fallback behavior favors legal inactivity over risky actions.** When all assigned wall targets are temporarily unavailable—typically because pets are adjacent—the solver waits, increments `stalled`, and after 12 turns routes toward the strip-safe interior point. Generation 16 feedback notes that temporary pet blockage is handled by waiting and eventual retreat. This avoids converting short-lived pet interference into unsafe construction or movement attempts.

## Performance Analysis

- **Generation 16 is the new measured leader among all cited accepted variants.** Its **3,727,463.30** exceeds the previous reported best, Generation 2’s internal-cut configuration at **3,689,312.64**, by **38,150.66**. It also exceeds Generation 7’s **3,672,557.92** by **54,905.38**, Generation 0’s **3,669,080.60** by **58,382.70**, and Generation 15’s **3,643,761.58** by **83,701.72**. The margin is modest against the close strip-builder cluster, but clear and consistent.

- **The successful score cluster strongly supports deterministic horizontal partitioning.** Accepted strip-oriented programs range from Generation 11’s 3,605,180.02 through Generations 15 and 16 at 3.64M–3.73M. The current best combines the shared strengths of that cluster—strip assignment, BFS reachability, build reservations, interior-side positioning, and blocked-target fallback—with rank-based planning and explicit half-divider ownership.

- **Correctness failures erase any potential strategic value.** Generations 9, 10, 13, and 17 had substantial validation pass counts—38, 49, 46, and 42 cases respectively—but every one scored 0.00 because a single illegal movement invalidated the submission. Relative to Generation 16’s accepted 50/50 result, this shows that legality-preserving action simulation is more important than small strategic variations in divider layout or target scoring.

- **The measured improvements favor coordination discipline over increasingly complex heuristics.** Generation 14’s BFS-aware row partitioning achieved 3,355,022.06, Generation 15’s coordinated split containment rose to 3,643,761.58, and Generation 16’s rank-ordered, half-separator planner reached 3,727,463.30. The progression aligns with better work partitioning and same-turn conflict handling, whereas earlier pet-aware and adjacency-heavy scoring variants remained roughly 0.3M–0.5M below the leading structural designs.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Test deterministic asymmetric separator split points while preserving exclusive ownership.** Replace the fixed `y=1..15` / `16..30` split with a small set of safe variants such as 12/18, 18/12, or an alternating 12/18 then 18/12 pattern by separator rank; the two neighboring strips must still own disjoint halves covering every column exactly once. This retains Generation 16’s key no-contention property, while potentially reducing initial travel for row-sorted humans that begin disproportionately on one side of the board.

2. **Use BFS distance, rather than Manhattan-like target ordering, to break ties among a limited set of build candidates.** `choose_build_job` already computes a full BFS map, so rank candidate `(target, stand)` pairs primarily by `dist[stand]`, then apply safe-side and connectivity terms only as bounded tie-breakers or among the closest few candidates. This should preserve the proven reachability correctness while reducing the chance that the `-joined*2` bonus selects a locally connected but materially farther wall segment.

3. **Make separator completion explicitly directional from the central ownership endpoint.** For each owned half-boundary, store its intended endpoint order and preferentially build the next unbuilt cell adjacent to the already completed contiguous portion, provided its inside-strip standing square is reachable. This is a narrower, lower-risk version of wall-connectivity scoring: it should reduce fragmented half-walls and help close full separators sooner without reverting to the broadly adjacency-heavy heuristics that underperformed earlier.

4. **Add same-turn human-position and planned-destination reservations to movement routing, without changing wall reservation semantics.** As humans are processed in rank order, reserve each earlier human’s current square when they wait/build and reserve their chosen next square when they move; later BFS calls should treat these squares as temporarily unavailable except for the planning human’s own start. The current solver’s sequential wall reservations are a major correctness advantage, and extending the same disciplined scheduling to human movement can reduce avoidable collisions, swaps, and wasted routing near narrow unfinished gaps.

5. **Introduce a bounded “return-to-interior” trigger based on separator progress rather than only 12 stalled turns.** When a human is on the unsafe side of its assigned strip and its nearest available target would require approaching an increasingly connected boundary from outside, immediately route toward `safe` instead of continuing to pursue that target; retain the existing 12-turn pet-blocked fallback for normal cases. This directly strengthens the successful `+10000` safe-side preference and protects against the high-cost failure mode where a partially completed divider isolates its assigned builder from the remaining half-wall cells.
