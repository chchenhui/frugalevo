<DESCRIPTION>
The current search spends a substantial fraction of its limited time sorting all cells whenever it chooses a dirt target, despite only needing a random element from the highest-contribution prefix. Replacing the full sort with `nth_element` preserves the intended target distribution while reducing this operation from O(N^2 log N) to expected O(N^2).

Additionally, the shortest-path-through-target operation is currently restricted to replacing a segment of at most 20 moves. This makes it unable to repair the long revisit intervals that dominate the dirt objective, especially after the route has grown through detours. Increasing its replacement window allows the operator to replace a large poor section with two shortest paths through an important dirty cell; it can both improve visit frequency and remove inefficient route sections. The length constraint and simulated annealing acceptance still protect against invalid or consistently harmful replacements.

Finally, operation 1 currently allocates and scans the entire route to find an immediate backtrack pair. Sampling several positions finds removable backtracks cheaply, allowing materially more score evaluations during the same time budget.
</DESCRIPTION>
