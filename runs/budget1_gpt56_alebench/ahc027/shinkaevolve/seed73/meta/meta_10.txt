# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- **The current best, Generation 12 (`copy_moves_only`), combines strong dirt-aware simulated annealing with lower copying overhead**, reaching the top combined score of **111.70** and lowest raw mean score of **8,952,856.62**. Its key successful structure is: DFS closed coverage initialization, APSP/BFS routing, exact cyclic revisit scoring, and route-preserving mutations. This outperformed the prior best Generation 7 (`fast_route_sa`) at **111.66 / 8,955,481.66**, albeit narrowly.

- **Targeting cells by their actual current weighted contribution is consistently beneficial.** Generation 12 computes per-cell cyclic dirt terms and selects targets using `D_SUSC[r][c] * cell_dirt_term_sum[r][c]`, then samples from a top-ranked subset. This pattern is also present in strong performers Generation 7 (**111.66**) and Generation 0 (**110.41**), supporting the conclusion that mutations should focus on presently expensive revisit intervals rather than susceptibility alone.

- **APSP-backed route edits are a common ingredient in the higher-scoring programs.** The current program uses BFS-precomputed parent routes for both shortening and dirt-targeted rerouting. Its operations include shortest-path compression, detour insertion, and replacement through expensive cells while preserving endpoints. Generation 7 used the same broad strategy and nearly matched the best score; Generation 0 and Generation 4 (**109.63**) also benefited from precomputed shortest paths.

- **A diverse neighborhood is more effective than a single detour mechanism.** Generation 12 mixes backtrack insertion/removal, shortest-path replacement, reversal/inversion, target detours, and target-cell rerouting. This is stronger than the more specialized generations: targeted-detour-focused Generation 2 scored **107.02**, nearest-detour Generation 3 scored **106.95**, and exact-target-visit insertion Generation 8 scored **106.55**.

## Ineffective Approaches

- **More narrowly specialized target-detour variants underperformed the balanced mutation portfolio.** Generation 8 (`exact_target_visit_insertion`) scored **106.55**, Generation 3 (`nearest_detour_insertion`) scored **106.95**, and Generation 2 (`targeted_detours`) scored **107.02**. Although all retained correctness and dirt-aware routing, their lower scores indicate that emphasizing a particular insertion/rerouting rule did not match the broader search behavior of Generations 7 and 12.

- **Full candidate-state copying is a likely throughput bottleneck.** Several summaries explicitly identify full route scoring and copying as limiting iteration count: Generation 1 scored **109.37**, Generation 6 **109.20**, and Generation 8 **106.55**. Generation 12’s patch name, `copy_moves_only`, directly addresses this by copying only `moves` into candidates and reconstructing coordinates, coverage flags, and scoring fields during evaluation. Its improvement to **111.70** is concrete evidence that reducing candidate-copy cost matters.

- **Exact full rescoring is robust but expensive when not paired with other overhead reductions.** Generations 1, 2, 6, and 8 all use full cyclic scoring and were accepted on every case, but ranged from **106.55** to **109.37**. The current best still uses exact full rescoring, so the issue is not correctness of the scorer; the weaker results point to insufficient iteration throughput when rescoring is combined with repeated path copying or less efficient proposal construction.

- **Detour targeting alone does not guarantee improvement.** Generations 2, 3, 5 (**109.34**), and 6 used weighted dirt targeting but remained below Generation 12 and Generation 7. The score evidence indicates that targeted insertion needs to coexist with effective compression/removal moves and enough search iterations to offset route-length growth.

## Implementation Insights

- **Generation 12 avoids copying expensive derived state before each mutation.** `PathData` contains a move vector, coordinate vector, visited grid, score, total numerator, and per-cell dirt-term table. Instead of `candidate_pd_obj = current_pd_obj`, the current code performs:
  ```cpp
  candidate_pd_obj.moves = current_pd_obj.moves;
  ```
  and lets `calculate_score_full()` rebuild the remaining state. This directly matches the `copy_moves_only` patch intent and is the clearest implementation-level distinction associated with the best measured result.

- **The current best uses reusable global buffers to reduce allocation churn.** It reuses per-cell visit-time vectors, APSP move buffers, and the dirt-target list. In particular, `CELL_VISIT_TIMES_GLOBAL_BUFFER`, `GET_APSP_MOVES_BUFFER1/2`, and `TMP_CELL_DIRT_INFOS_LIST_GLOBAL_BUFFER` are allocated once and reused through the annealing loop. This follows the same performance-oriented direction noted in Generation 4, which used `nth_element`, sampled shortening checks, APSP, and reusable buffers to reach **109.63**.

- **Top-K dirt targeting uses `nth_element` rather than sorting all cells.** The current program calculates weighted terms for all grid cells, partitions only enough to isolate the best candidate subset, and randomly selects one target from that subset. This preserves stochastic diversity while avoiding a full sort. Generation 4’s feedback explicitly identified this optimization as beneficial, and Generation 12 incorporates the same efficient targeting pattern while scoring higher.

- **Route feasibility is preserved structurally rather than repaired after invalid proposals.** The initial DFS creates a legal closed coverage walk. Most mutations either insert closed excursions, remove immediate backtracks, replace a segment with a shortest path between its original endpoints, or reverse/invert a subpath. The code still runs complete validation/scoring, but the mutation design keeps invalid candidates rare and maintains the all-50-case acceptance record.

## Performance Analysis

- **Generation 12 is the best measured program, but its lead over Generation 7 is very small.** Generation 12 scored **111.70** versus Generation 7’s **111.66**, a combined-score advantage of **0.04**. Its raw mean score is also slightly better: **8,952,856.62** versus **8,955,481.66**, an improvement of about **2,625**. Both programs share APSP, DFS initialization, simulated annealing, dirt-weighted targeting, and route-preserving mutations; the incremental advantage is consistent with Generation 12’s reduced candidate copying and buffer-conscious implementation.

- **The best two programs are clearly separated from the main cluster.** Generations 0, 1, 4, 5, and 6 occupy approximately **109.20–110.41**, while Generation 12 and Generation 7 reach **111.70** and **111.66**. This roughly 1.3–2.5-point combined-score gap corresponds to raw mean improvements of roughly **100,000–200,000** relative to the 109-point group, showing that implementation throughput and balanced operators materially affect final quality.

- **The weaker specialized variants form a lower cluster around 106.5–107.0.** Generation 8 scored **106.55**, Generation 3 **106.95**, and Generation 2 **107.02**, with raw mean scores from approximately **9.34M to 9.39M**. Compared with Generation 12’s **8.95M** raw mean, this is about **390,000–432,000** worse. All were correct, so the loss is heuristic quality and search efficiency rather than feasibility failures.

- **Correctness was stable across all versions; optimization quality drove the differences.** Every listed generation was accepted on all 50 cases. Therefore, the observed range—from **106.55** to **111.70** combined score—primarily reflects differences in annealing iteration throughput, mutation balance, targeting quality, and route-edit efficiency. Generation 12 best integrates these factors while retaining exact objective evaluation and robust validation.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Add an exact delta scorer for local route edits, while retaining periodic full rescoring for verification.** For operations 0, 1, 2, 4, and 5, only visit times near the edited interval and route-length-dependent cyclic gaps change; maintain per-cell visit-position lists and update the weighted numerator incrementally instead of rebuilding all coordinates and all cell terms every proposal. The current best already proved that reducing copy overhead improves final quality, so reducing full-score overhead should translate directly into more annealing iterations and better solutions.

2. **Bias mutation selection dynamically using current route length and recent operator success statistics.** Track, per operation type, attempted count, accepted count, and average score improvement, then increase the probability of shortening/compression moves when the route is long and targeted insertion/rerouting when expensive cells have unusually large weighted dirt contributions. Generation 12 succeeds through a balanced portfolio, so preserve all six neighborhoods but make their mixture adaptive rather than fixed at `15/15/30/10/15/15`.

3. **Strengthen expensive-cell targeting by sampling both high contribution cells and high marginal-gap cells.** Continue using `D_SUSC[r][c] * cell_dirt_term_sum[r][c]`, but also compute each cell’s largest cyclic revisit interval and prioritize targets whose largest gap contributes a large fraction of their total dirt cost. For a selected target, choose insertion or rerouting anchors near the midpoint of that longest gap rather than uniformly sampling route coordinates; this directly attacks the interval responsible for most of the target’s current penalty.

4. **Replace random sampled detour anchors in operation 4 with a small best-of-candidates search over route positions.** Instead of evaluating only 20 uniformly random coordinates, sample positions concentrated inside the target cell’s longest absence interval, plus a few global random positions, then choose the anchor minimizing a proxy such as detour length divided by expected dirt reduction. APSP-backed edits are already a proven strength, and this preserves their feasibility while making expensive target visits more deliberate and less likely to add low-value route length.

5. **Introduce a targeted segment replacement operator that routes through two expensive cells when their combined detour is cheap.** Select two distinct cells from the top weighted-contribution subset, choose a short existing segment with endpoints `A,B`, and replace it by `A -> target1 -> target2 -> B` using APSP only when the added length is bounded. This extends the successful single-target rerouting of operation 5, but can reduce multiple dominant revisit gaps in one accepted candidate and offers a more meaningful large-scale neighborhood than specialized single-detour variants.
