# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- **Generation 13 (`midpoint_sa_crossover`) is the new measured leader at 114.32 combined score and 8,747,084.30 raw mean**, exceeding the prior global best Generation 12 (`copy_moves_only`, 111.70 / 8,952,856.62) by **2.62 combined-score points** and improving raw mean by about **205,772**. Its strongest pattern is a balanced simulated-annealing neighborhood: 15% backtrack insertion, 15% backtrack removal, 30% shortest-path compression, 10% route reversal, 15% targeted closed detours, and 15% target-cell reroutes.

- **Exact current-cost targeting remains highly effective, especially when paired with cyclic-gap placement.** Generation 13 ranks cells by `p.term[x] * dirt[x]`, where `term[x]` is the exact sum of cyclic revisit-gap penalties. Its targeted-detour operator identifies the target’s largest cyclic gap, derives a midpoint, and then samples insertion positions while favoring positions with cheap APSP access to that target. This improves on merely selecting high-dirt cells: it targets cells that are currently expensive because of both dirt weight and poor revisit timing.

- **APSP-backed replacement and compression are central to the strongest programs.** In Generation 13, shortest-path segment replacement receives the largest mutation share, 30%, and only applies when the BFS path is strictly shorter than the replaced route segment. This gives the search a reliable way to reduce route length and overall revisit periods after detours are added. The same broad APSP-plus-SA pattern was strong in Generations 9, 11, 14, 15, and 16, but Generation 13’s 114.32 is the clearest evidence that the particular operator mix and targeted placement were especially successful.

- **Diversified route-preserving edits outperform detour-only refinement.** Generation 13 combines compression, reversals, removable backtracks, inserted revisits, and endpoint-preserving reroutes. This is substantially stronger than Generation 10’s more specialized DFS-plus-gap-detour refinement, which scored only **62.40** despite passing all 50 cases. The result reinforces the earlier finding that detours must be offset by shortening/removal moves and broader structural search.

## Ineffective Approaches

- **Detour-centric local improvement without a comparably broad mutation portfolio was much weaker.** Generation 10 (`gap_split_patrol`) inserted shortest-path round trips near high-value revisit gaps and achieved correctness on all cases, but its **62.40 combined score / 16,026,337.76 raw mean** was dramatically below Generation 13’s **114.32 / 8,747,084.30**. Its insertion-focused mechanism likely accumulated route length without enough route-compression or structural alternatives to consistently improve periodic revisit timing.

- **Narrow target-insertion variants remained below balanced SA approaches.** The prior evaluated specialized variants—Generation 8 at **106.55**, Generation 3 at **106.95**, and Generation 2 at **107.02**—all used dirt-aware targeting but underperformed the earlier balanced leaders and are now even further behind Generation 13. The evidence indicates that selecting expensive cells alone is insufficient; changes must coexist with operations that remove redundant movement and reshape visit schedules.

- **The earlier “copy only moves” optimization is not a universal predictor of final score.** Generation 12’s `copy_moves_only` implementation was previously the leader at **111.70**, supporting the conclusion that avoiding derived-state copying improved throughput. However, Generation 13 reaches **114.32** even though its proposal loop uses `cand = current`, copying `moves`, `pos`, score, and the fixed-size `term` array before `evaluate()` reconstructs much of that derived state. This does not invalidate the overhead concern, but it shows that Generation 13’s stronger mutation behavior outweighed this extra copying cost in measured end-to-end quality.

## Implementation Insights

- **Generation 13 preserves feasibility structurally and validates exactly.** Its initial DFS produces a closed tour from cell 0 that covers all cells. Subsequent operators either insert a closed out-and-back excursion, remove a detected immediate backtrack, replace a segment with an APSP path between identical endpoints, or reverse-and-invert a valid move subsequence. `evaluate()` then confirms legal moves, full coverage, return to origin, maximum length, and exact cyclic score. This robust design contributed to its **50/50 accepted** outcome.

- **The current best directly computes the objective signals used for targeting.** `evaluate()` records visit times per cell and calculates each cell’s cyclic contribution as:
  \[
  \sum_{\text{gaps}} \frac{gap(gap-1)}{2}.
  \]
  Target selection ranks by this term multiplied by `dirt[x]`, rather than by dirt alone. This continues the successful Generation 12/Generation 7 pattern of targeting actual present penalty contributors, and Generation 13 demonstrates that it scales to the best observed score.

- **Generation 13 uses efficient partial ranking rather than fully sorting targets.** `select_target()` builds `(weighted_term, cell)` pairs and uses `nth_element` to isolate a top subset: approximately the top 10% for broad targeting and top `N` cells for narrow rerouting. It then samples randomly within that elite set. This retains search diversity while avoiding a full sort at every targeted mutation.

- **Its time-limited SA schedule supports both exploration and late refinement.** Generation 13 uses a temperature schedule from `5000 * sqrt(N)` to `0.1` over a roughly **1.93-second** budget, accepting worse routes early according to the score difference and progressively becoming more selective. The best solution is retained separately from the current annealing state, so exploratory deteriorations do not discard the best-known route.

## Performance Analysis

- **Generation 13 establishes a clear new top tier.** Its **114.32** combined score is higher than Generation 15’s **112.15**, Generation 16’s **111.54**, Generation 9’s **110.69**, Generation 14’s **110.52**, and Generation 11’s **110.48**. Relative to the closest program in the newly supplied set, Generation 15, it improves raw mean from **8,916,630.88** to **8,747,084.30**, a reduction of about **169,547**.

- **Generation 13 also decisively surpasses the prior global leader.** Compared with Generation 12’s **111.70 / 8,952,856.62**, Generation 13 gains **2.62 combined-score points** and lowers raw mean by roughly **205,772**. Since both programs use DFS initialization, APSP routing, exact cyclic scoring, high-contribution targeting, and simulated annealing, the measured gain is most consistent with Generation 13’s effective operator distribution and its large-gap-aware targeted-detour logic.

- **The cluster around 110–112 confirms that the overall SA/APSP strategy is robust, but not all variants optimize equally well.** Generations 9, 11, 14, 15, and 16 all pass every case and score from **110.48 to 112.15**, with raw means from about **8.92M to 9.05M**. Generation 13’s **8.75M** raw mean is materially better, showing that the difference is not validity but route-quality optimization.

- **Correctness remained stable while optimization mechanics determined score.** Every listed program was accepted on all 50 cases, including the low-scoring Generation 10. Therefore, the very large spread from Generation 10’s **62.40** to Generation 13’s **114.32** is attributable to optimization quality: neighborhood diversity, APSP compression, current-cost-based targeting, and the ability to trade additional high-priority revisits against globally shorter route structure.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Make the operation mixture adaptive while preserving Generation 13’s six-neighborhood portfolio.** Track attempted/accepted counts and score deltas for each operator in rolling windows, then shift probability toward compression/removal when route length is inflated and toward gap-targeted insertion/rerouting when a few cells dominate `term[x] * dirt[x]`. Keep nonzero exploration probability for every operator, since the measured advantage of the current best comes from structural diversity rather than any single move type.

2. **Improve operation 4’s anchor selection by searching within the selected target’s actual longest cyclic absence interval.** The current code computes that interval’s midpoint but then mostly competes against 20 globally random anchors, which can choose a cheap access point outside the costly gap; instead sample several positions around the midpoint and throughout the gap, score them by APSP detour cost versus expected gap-splitting reduction, and retain a few random global anchors for diversification. This directly extends the current best’s successful largest-gap logic while avoiding low-value visits that do not repair the expensive revisit interval.

3. **Add a two-target APSP reroute mutation for jointly expensive cells.** Select two cells from the top weighted-term subset, choose a short segment `[a,b]`, and replace it with `pos[a] -> target1 -> target2 -> pos[b]` or the reversed target order when its added length is bounded relative to the removed segment. Prefer pairs whose largest gaps overlap the selected segment’s time region and whose APSP distance is small, allowing one structural edit to split two dominant cyclic gaps rather than servicing only one target.

4. **Make shortest-path compression more deliberate by sampling redundant long segments instead of uniformly random endpoints.** Build a lightweight candidate set of route intervals where the traveled subpath is substantially longer than `dista[pos[a]][pos[b]]`, prioritizing intervals with large excess length and avoiding intervals containing the sole useful visit to a high-contribution cell. Generation 13 already gives compression the largest mutation share and proves APSP replacement is powerful; targeting known-wasteful segments should produce stronger shortening proposals than random `(a,b)` selection.

5. **Introduce a large-neighborhood “remove detour then reconnect” mutation around low-value revisit excursions.** Identify a cell or short subroute whose visits have low `term[x] * dirt[x]` contribution, select two route positions surrounding one of its excursions, and replace the entire interval by the APSP path between its endpoints if this removes substantial length while preserving later high-value visits. This generalizes immediate two-step backtrack deletion into removal of longer redundant loops, complementing targeted insertions with stronger route-budget recovery.
