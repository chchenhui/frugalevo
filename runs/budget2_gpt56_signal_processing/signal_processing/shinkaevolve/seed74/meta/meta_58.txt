# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- **Hard direction-constrained reported output remains the strongest validated pattern.** The current best, **Generation 49 Causal Persistent Trend Adaptive Filter**, achieved the highest combined score (**0.59**) and composite score (**0.66**) by separating its adaptive latent tracker from its displayed signal. Its `reported_level = max(reported_level, level)` in an uptrend and `min(...)` in a downtrend reduced activity to **11.60 slope changes** and **9.00 false reversals**, producing the best smoothness score (**0.63**). None of Generations 50–57 exceeded this stability/score balance.

- **Persistent reversal evidence plus output locking is more effective than adaptive reversal gates alone.** Generation 50, also named `evidence_locked_reversals`, used a related alpha-beta tracker with reversal evidence and achieved **14.80 false reversals**, far below the highly reactive Kalman/consensus variants. It scored **0.54**, despite weak correlation (**0.41**) and lag (**1.04**). This supports the prior result that strict visible-direction control—not merely robust observation fitting—is what most improves the combined metric.

- **Causal endpoint local-linear regression is consistently effective as an internal observation model.** The current best uses a compact exponentially weighted endpoint regression capped at 12 samples. The later reactive filters confirm its raw-tracking value: Generation 55 Robust Multi-Scale Causal Trend Tracker reached **0.90 correlation**, **0.37 lag error**, and **0.73 responsiveness**, while Generation 53 reached **0.88 correlation**, **0.38 lag**, and **0.73 responsiveness**. Endpoint alignment preserves responsiveness without the group delay of trailing averaging, but its benefit is greatest when decoupled from the visible output.

- **Moderate internal adaptation with conservative trend persistence can outperform high-fidelity tracking.** Generation 49’s relatively low-to-moderate gains (`alpha` from 0.17 to 0.47 and `beta` from 0.018 to 0.088), innovation clipping, slope deadband, and momentum retention during an unconfirmed reversal produced **0.55 responsiveness** while preserving stability. This is slower than the **0.72–0.73** responsiveness of Generations 51–55 and 57, but it yields a materially better total score because those variants expose excessive directional activity.

## Ineffective Approaches

- **Reactive Kalman, consensus, and multi-scale state updates remained poor at visible reversal suppression.** Generation 51 Robust Adaptive Kalman Trend Filter had excellent raw behavior—**0.90 correlation**, **0.37 lag**, **0.73 responsiveness**, and **0.38 noise reduction**—but only **0.15 smoothness**, with **114.80 slope changes** and **95.60 false reversals**, for a **0.53** combined score. Generation 55 showed nearly the same outcome: **117.40** slope changes, **97.60** false reversals, and **0.53** combined despite equally strong correlation and lag.

- **Robust regression and MAD/Huber noise handling alone did not stabilize reported trends.** Generations 52, 53, and 54 all used robust endpoint regression, MAD-derived noise estimates, innovation clipping, and some form of reversal confirmation. They retained strong correlation (**0.86–0.89**) and low lag (**0.38–0.40**), yet their smoothness stayed at only **0.12–0.15**, with **90.80–115.60 false reversals**. Robust measurement construction reduces outlier sensitivity, but it does not by itself prevent the output from following noisy local slope changes.

- **Short confirmation hysteresis without a strict presentation constraint is insufficient.** Generation 57 used a three-point median, weighted regression, adaptive Kalman noise scaling, and innovation-supported hysteresis. It reduced false reversals versus the most reactive family to **61.80**, but still had **74.40 slope changes**, only **0.21 smoothness**, and a **0.53** combined score. Generation 56’s deadbands, directional confirmation, and catch-up limits performed better on stability—**51.80 false reversals**, **65.20 slope changes**, and **0.23 smoothness**—but still did not approach Generation 49’s **9.00** reversals and **0.63** smoothness.

- **Directly exposing high-responsiveness adaptive state movement consistently limited overall score.** Generation 54’s alpha-beta tracker reached **0.72 responsiveness**, **0.39 lag**, and **0.86 correlation**, but its exposed behavior yielded **141.80 slope changes** and **115.60 false reversals**, the worst among Generations 50–57. Its combined score fell to **0.51**, demonstrating that raw tracking improvements were outweighed by directional chatter.

## Implementation Insights

- **Generation 49’s three-layer architecture is the key implementation distinction.** `_EndpointRegressor.observe()` provides a causal measurement; `_PersistentTrendTracker.step()` updates the latent `level` and `slope`; and `reported_level` is controlled independently. The important detail is that state adaptation is not frozen while the output is locked: the internal level can move against the current visible direction, but the reported value remains monotone until a reversal is confirmed. This directly aligns with its **11.60 slope changes**, **9.00 false reversals**, and **0.63 smoothness**.

- **The current best uses reversal confirmation as a state-transition gate, not only as a smoothing condition.** Its `_update_direction()` requires a counter-trend proposed slope, innovation sign agreement, a noise-scaled slope deadband, and either two pending observations or a decisive immediate slope threshold. During an unconfirmed crossing, the tracker applies `self.slope *= 0.985` rather than assimilating the opposite `proposed_slope`. This conservative momentum retention distinguishes it from the later adaptive filters, whose slope updates more readily reflect every local counter-move.

- **The output monotonicity rule explains both the strengths and the intentional fidelity cost of Generation 49.** The `max`/`min` reported-level rule prevents small pullbacks from appearing in the output. It is therefore consistent with the program’s best-in-set stability metrics, but also with its relatively weak **0.50 correlation**, **0.83 lag error**, **0.90 average error**, and **0.00 measured noise reduction**. By comparison, Generation 51’s less constrained Kalman output had **0.90 correlation** and **0.37 lag**, but its internal adaptation was much more directly exposed and created **95.60** false reversals.

- **The compact endpoint observer is efficient and sufficient for the stability-oriented design.** Generation 49 limits regression span to `max(4, min(window_size, 12))`, precomputes the full-window weighted projection, and uses a simple least-squares startup path. It completed in **0.08 seconds** with **1.00 efficiency**. The newer multi-scale and Kalman implementations were also efficient (**0.05–0.17 seconds**), so computational complexity was not the differentiator; displayed trend control was.

## Performance Analysis

- **Generation 49 remains clearly ahead of all newly evaluated programs on the target score.** The strongest new result was Generation 56 at **0.55**, followed by Generations 50, 51, 52, 53, 55, and 57 at **0.53–0.54**; Generation 54 reached only **0.51**. Generation 49’s **0.59** combined score is therefore **0.04 above Generation 56** and **0.05–0.08 above** the remaining Generation 50–57 variants.

- **Combined score tracks directional stability much more strongly than raw correlation or lag.** Generation 49 has only **0.50 correlation** and **0.55 responsiveness**, substantially below the **0.83–0.90 correlation** and **0.67–0.73 responsiveness** of most newer filters. Nevertheless, its **9.00 false reversals** and **0.63 smoothness** produced the best score. Conversely, Generation 51’s **0.90 correlation**, **0.37 lag**, and **0.73 responsiveness** did not lift its combined score above **0.53** because it had **95.60 false reversals** and **0.15 smoothness**.

- **Generation 56 is the closest new stability-oriented comparison, but still exposes much more trend chatter.** Its robust local regression, Huber/MAD logic, directional confirmation, deadbands, and catch-up limits reduced activity to **65.20 slope changes** and **51.80 false reversals**, substantially better than Generations 51–55. This produced a higher **0.55** combined score and **0.23 smoothness**, but Generation 49 still reduced slope changes by roughly **82%** and false reversals by roughly **83%**, with nearly three times the smoothness score.

- **The evaluated programs show a persistent fidelity-versus-stability split.** The reactive endpoint/Kalman/consensus family clusters around **0.88–0.90 correlation**, **0.37–0.40 lag**, and **0.72–0.73 responsiveness**, but also around **74.40–141.80 slope changes** and **61.80–115.60 false reversals**. Generation 49 instead accepts **0.83 lag**, **0.50 correlation**, and **0.55 responsiveness** to obtain dramatically lower directional activity and the strongest composite result (**0.66**).

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Replace the two-sample `pending_count` reversal gate with signed cumulative, noise-normalized evidence while preserving the hard `max`/`min` presentation lock.** Accumulate evidence only when `candidate_direction`, clipped innovation sign, and short-term slope sign agree, for example `pending_evidence += max(0, pending_direction * clipped_innovation / noise_scale)`, resetting it on contradiction; confirm near 2.5–3.5 cumulative units or after 3 qualifying samples. This directly extends Generation 49’s strongest feature—persistent reversal validation—and should reject alternating weak pullbacks better than a simple two-observation count.

2. **Add a direction-safe catch-up mode immediately after a confirmed reversal to improve the current 0.83 lag error without reopening chatter.** For the first 2–4 samples after changing `self.direction`, move `reported_level` toward latent `level` by a bounded amount such as `min(level - reported_level, c * noise_scale)` in an uptrend and the symmetric negative form in a downtrend, with `c` around 1.0–2.0. The output must remain monotone in the newly confirmed direction, retaining the architecture responsible for only 9 false reversals while reducing stale output after legitimate turns.

3. **Use a second short causal endpoint regression solely to modulate tracker gains and reversal confidence, not as a directly reported signal.** Add a 5–7 sample weighted endpoint fit returning both endpoint level and slope, then increase `alpha` and `beta` modestly when short-fit slope, 12-sample-fit slope, and tracker slope all agree; reduce `beta` or strengthen slope retention when they disagree. Generations 51–55 demonstrated that short-horizon endpoint information substantially improves correlation and lag, while Generation 49 demonstrates that it should remain behind the direction-locked output layer.

4. **Make reversal thresholds trend-maturity-aware and asymmetric, especially for established visible trends.** Track `confirmed_run_length` since the last direction change, and raise the counter-trend confirmation requirement after a mature run—for example, use a slope deadband of `0.16–0.24 * noise_scale`, higher evidence threshold, or require an extra qualifying point after 8–12 same-direction samples. Preserve lower thresholds shortly after initialization or after a recent reversal so genuine new trends can still establish quickly; this targets the remaining false reversals without broadly sacrificing responsiveness.

5. **Harden the endpoint observer against newest-sample contradiction outliers with one lightweight robust reweighting pass.** After the existing weighted local-linear fit, compute residuals, estimate residual MAD, and refit once with Huber-style weights; apply strongest downweighting when the newest one or two samples sharply oppose both fitted slope and the current confirmed direction. Robust regression alone was not enough in reactive variants, but within Generation 49’s separated observer/tracker/output architecture it can prevent isolated spikes from creating unnecessary innovations, reversal evidence, and visible lag.
