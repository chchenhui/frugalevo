# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- **The current best program’s confirmed-direction presentation layer is the strongest validated pattern.** The Causal Adaptive Trend-Preserving Filter reached **0.59 combined score / 0.66 composite**, with only **11.60 slope changes** and **9.00 false reversals**, versus roughly 53–158 false reversals in the newly evaluated robust alpha-beta variants. Its key distinction is that `level` and `slope` update internally, while `reported_level` is forced monotonic in the currently confirmed direction. This transformed smoothness to **0.63**, far above the **0.09–0.23** range of Generations 28–40.

- **Causal endpoint-aligned regression remains an effective observation model, but only when coupled with strict output-direction control.** The current best’s exponentially weighted local linear fit estimates the newest point via an intercept at time zero, avoiding moving-average delay. The same endpoint principle gave strong raw tracking in Generation 31’s Robust Multi-Scale Alpha-Beta Trend Filter (**0.91 correlation, 0.34 lag error, 0.75 responsiveness**) and Generation 33’s quadratic version (**0.92 correlation, 0.37 lag error**). The current best shows that endpoint regression can also support the highest overall score when its reactive estimate is not directly exposed during unconfirmed turns.

- **Persistence gating is effective when it controls the visible trend rather than merely filtering velocity updates.** The current best requires counter-trend evidence through `pending_direction` and `pending_count`, with reversal accepted only after two consistent events or a large noise-scaled proposed slope. This is substantially more successful than the two-sample confirmation in Generation 39, which still had **155 slope changes**, **126.6 false reversals**, and smoothness of **0.11**, because Generation 39’s reported output remained responsive to local state fluctuations.

- **Moderate internal adaptation with conservative momentum retention supports stability.** The current best uses clipped innovations, adaptive gains (`alpha` from 0.17 to 0.47 and `beta` from 0.018 to 0.088), then preserves old momentum during an unconfirmed opposite-direction event with `self.slope *= 0.985`. This preserves enough state adaptation for **0.55 responsiveness** while preventing the high directional activity seen in more reactive multi-scale designs such as Generation 34 (**133.20 slope changes**) and Generation 31 (**198.8 slope changes**).

## Ineffective Approaches

- **Reactive multi-scale blending consistently improved correlation and lag but damaged the composite score through reversal chatter.** Generation 31 achieved **0.91 correlation**, **0.34 lag error**, and **0.75 responsiveness**, but only **0.51 combined score** because it produced **158 false reversals** and **198.8 slope changes**. Generation 34 similarly reached **0.91 correlation** and **0.35 lag error**, but had **108.60 false reversals** and **133.20 slope changes**, limiting its score to **0.53**. These results reinforce that short/long-horizon agreement and blending alone do not stabilize output direction.

- **Robust fitting, MAD scaling, and Huber residual weighting do not by themselves solve visible trend instability.** Generations 28, 33, 37, and 39 all applied robust endpoint regression and innovation handling. Their raw metrics remained good—Generation 33 had **0.92 correlation** and **0.43 noise reduction**, while Generation 39 had **0.92 correlation** and **0.42 noise reduction**—yet smoothness remained only **0.11–0.17** and false reversals stayed at **78.8–129.4**. Robust observation estimation reduced isolated sample effects but did not impose stable directional behavior on the final output.

- **Quadratic endpoint models added measurement fidelity without improving directional stability.** Generation 33’s recency-weighted regularized quadratic fit and Generation 39’s quadratic endpoint regression both achieved strong accuracy and responsiveness, but had very high slope-change counts (**158.60** and **155**, respectively) and low smoothness (**0.11** each). The added curvature sensitivity appears to retain more local variation in the output, which is unfavorable under an evaluator strongly rewarding low directional activity.

- **Velocity deadbands and reversal gates were insufficient when state velocity remained free to cross direction frequently.** Generation 35’s Robust Adaptive Trend-Preserving Filter was the most stable of the new reactive family, with **65.60 slope changes**, **53 false reversals**, and smoothness of **0.23**, but it still trailed the current best by **0.04 combined-score points** (**0.55 vs. 0.59**). Its gated velocity logic reduced chatter relative to Generations 31–40, but lacked the current best’s hard monotonic reported-output constraint.

## Implementation Insights

- **The current best separates estimation, state tracking, and presentation constraints.** `_EndpointRegressor.observe` produces a causal endpoint measurement; `_PersistentTrendTracker.step` updates an internal alpha-beta state; then the presentation layer emits either `max(reported_level, level)` for an established upward trend or `min(reported_level, level)` for a downward trend. This separation is directly consistent with the best measured smoothness (**0.63**) and lowest reversal counts (**11.60 slope changes, 9 false reversals**) across the listed programs.

- **The endpoint regressor is intentionally small and computationally bounded.** It caps fitting span at `max(4, min(window_size, 12))`, precomputes a weighted pseudoinverse for full windows, and only uses a least-squares startup path for short histories. This retains full **1.00 efficiency**, with **0.08 execution time**, despite applying causal local regression at every sample. The newly evaluated robust models were also efficient (**0.05–0.16 seconds**), so runtime was not the source of their weaker scores; their differences are primarily behavioral.

- **The noise-scaled turn logic has multiple aligned conditions rather than a single sign test.** The current program derives noise from both recent innovation MAD and raw-difference MAD, applies a slope deadband of `0.10 * noise_scale`, and requires innovation sign agreement with the proposed counter-trend direction. This makes a reversal depend on both state evidence and new measurement evidence. In contrast, Generation 36’s multi-scale slope agreement and small-innovation gate still yielded **124.60 slope changes** and **101.80 false reversals**, indicating that agreement checks without output locking did not sufficiently constrain presentation behavior.

- **The current best deliberately accepts poorer instantaneous reconstruction to prevent visible chatter.** Its reported output can remain flat during an unconfirmed pullback even while internal `level` moves downward or upward. This explains its relatively weak **0.50 correlation**, **0.83 lag error**, **0.90 average error**, and **0.00 measured noise reduction**, alongside its leading smoothness and combined score. The code is optimized around the evaluator’s directional-stability behavior rather than minimizing pointwise tracking error.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Replace the binary two-count reversal gate with normalized cumulative counter-trend evidence while retaining the hard monotonic output lock.** During a pending turn, accumulate `pending_evidence += max(0, pending_direction * clipped_innovation / noise_scale)` only when the proposed slope, innovation, and candidate direction agree; confirm at roughly `2.5–3.5` evidence units or after three consecutive qualifying samples. This extends the current best’s persistence mechanism and should better reject alternating weak pullbacks than `pending_count >= 2` without exposing reactive internal movement.

2. **Add a monotone, bounded catch-up mode immediately after a confirmed reversal.** Rather than allowing `reported_level` to jump directly to the potentially distant internal `level`, introduce `release_remaining` for 2–4 samples and update with `reported_level += clip(level - reported_level, -k*noise_scale, k*noise_scale)`, followed by the existing `min`/`max` monotonic constraint in the new direction. The presentation layer remains direction-safe—the primary source of the current 0.63 smoothness—but this can reduce the weak lag error of 0.83 after genuine turns.

3. **Use a second short causal endpoint slope solely to modulate internal alpha-beta gains, never as a direct output blend.** Compute a cheap 5–7 sample endpoint-linear slope alongside the current up-to-12-sample estimate; when its sign agrees with the long slope and current state slope, modestly increase `alpha` and `beta`, and when it disagrees, reduce `beta` or strengthen the existing `0.985` momentum retention. Earlier multi-scale variants demonstrated excellent correlation and lag, but their failure came from exposing that reactivity; restricting it to state adaptation preserves the validated output-direction controller.

4. **Make counter-trend entry hysteretic and dependent on the established trend’s maturity.** Keep the existing low `0.10 * noise_scale` deadband for neutral initialization and in-direction motion, but require a larger opposing slope such as `0.16–0.22 * noise_scale` before creating a pending reversal; reduce this threshold only after sustained counter-trend evidence has accumulated. This directly targets the remaining 9 false reversals and 11.6 slope changes while preserving responsiveness for already-confirmed turns.

5. **Add one lightweight robust reweighting pass to the endpoint regressor, emphasizing protection from newest-sample outliers.** Fit the existing weighted line, calculate residuals, then refit with Huber-style weights; apply extra downweighting to the newest 1–2 points only when their residual direction opposes both the fitted slope and confirmed presentation direction. Robust endpoint designs alone did not stabilize visible output in prior generations, but here the robustification would reduce unnecessary innovations and pending reversals inside the already successful locked-output architecture.
