# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- **The current best, Causal Persistent Trend Adaptive Filter (Generation 49), confirms that hard output-direction locking is the most valuable pattern.** It achieved the highest combined score (**0.59**) and composite score (**0.66**) despite only moderate raw fidelity (**0.50 correlation**, **0.84 lag error**, **0.90 average error**). Its defining behavior is that the internal alpha-beta `level` and `slope` continue adapting, while `reported_level` is constrained with `max()` during confirmed upward movement and `min()` during confirmed downward movement. This reduced directional activity to **11.80 slope changes** and **9 false reversals**, while raising smoothness to **0.63**.

- **Causal endpoint regression remains useful as an observation model, especially when it does not directly control the exposed output.** Generation 49 uses a compact exponentially weighted local-linear endpoint fit over at most 12 samples, estimating the current endpoint through the fitted intercept. This preserves causal alignment and avoids moving-average delay. The same endpoint-regression principle produced strong raw tracking in the more reactive filters—for example, Robust Multi-Scale Hysteretic Endpoint Filter (Generation 38) achieved **0.90 accuracy/correlation**, **0.42 lag**, and **0.70 responsiveness**—but Generation 49 is the version that converts the observation quality into the best total score by adding strict presentation control.

- **Multiple aligned reversal conditions are more effective than slope sign changes alone.** Generation 49 requires a counter-trend proposed slope, innovation sign agreement, a noise-scaled slope deadband, and either two consecutive pending counter-trend observations or a sufficiently large immediate turn. This persistence logic is reflected in its low **9 false reversals**. Evidence-Locked Adaptive Causal Filter (Generation 44) applied a related evidence-lock concept and improved stability relative to reactive trackers, reaching **65.40 false reversals** and **81.80 slope changes**, but did not match Generation 49 because its visible output remained substantially more variable.

- **Conservative momentum retention during an unconfirmed turn is associated with the best smoothness tradeoff.** In Generation 49, an unconfirmed opposing `proposed_slope` does not immediately replace the current slope; instead, the tracker retains momentum using `self.slope *= 0.985`. This differs from the more adaptive alpha-beta and Kalman designs, which rapidly assimilate opposing motion. The result is lower responsiveness (**0.54–0.55**) than the reactive family, but vastly lower reversal counts and the highest evaluated overall score.

## Ineffective Approaches

- **Reactive multi-scale consensus and blending improved correlation but consistently exposed excessive directional chatter.** Robust Causal Consensus Tracker (Generation 41) scored **0.53** with **0.89 accuracy**, **0.40 lag**, and **0.72 responsiveness**, but had **116.60 slope changes**, **92.60 false reversals**, and only **0.15 smoothness**. Robust Multi-Scale Hysteretic Endpoint Filter (Generation 38) showed the same pattern: strong **0.90 accuracy**, **0.42 lag**, and **0.70 responsiveness**, but **115.60 slope changes** and **91.20 false reversals**, limiting it to **0.52** combined score.

- **Robustification alone did not produce stable visible trends.** MAD scales, Huber reweighting, clipped innovations, and robust endpoint regressions were present in Generations 38, 41, 45, 46, and 48. These methods limited spike sensitivity and preserved accuracy, but smoothness generally remained only **0.14–0.20**. For example, Robust Adaptive Kalman Trend Filter (Generation 45) reached **0.90 accuracy/correlation**, **0.37 lag**, and **0.38 noise reduction**, yet still produced **112.40 slope changes** and **93.20 false reversals**, scoring **0.53**.

- **High-gain adaptive state updates and predictive extrapolation worsened reversal behavior in volatile regions.** Robust Adaptive Kalman Trend Filter (Generation 42) had the strongest responsiveness among the listed filters at **0.77**, with excellent **0.31 lag** and **0.28 average error**, but its activity-dependent process noise and predictive output behavior yielded **176.40 slope changes** and **141.80 false reversals**. Its smoothness was only **0.10**, and combined score was **0.50**, demonstrating that fast internal state adaptation is harmful when directly reflected in output direction.

- **The most aggressively adaptive alpha-beta variant had the worst directional stability.** Adaptive Robust Alpha-Beta Trend Tracker (Generation 43) achieved strong raw tracking—**0.91 accuracy/correlation**, **0.33 lag**, **0.75 responsiveness**, and **0.41 noise reduction**—but had **254.20 slope changes** and **197.80 false reversals**, with smoothness of only **0.07**. Its combined score of **0.51** shows that velocity hysteresis without a hard reported-output constraint is insufficient.

## Implementation Insights

- **Generation 49’s effectiveness comes from separating three responsibilities: measurement, internal tracking, and displayed output.** `_EndpointRegressor.observe()` creates a causal current-time measurement; `_PersistentTrendTracker.step()` updates the latent level/slope state; and the final `reported_level` is independently constrained according to confirmed direction. This architecture explains why the filter can retain internally adaptive behavior while achieving **0.63 smoothness** and only **9 false reversals**.

- **The current best uses a deliberately bounded endpoint model that supports full efficiency without overfitting local variation.** Its span is capped with `max(4, min(window_size, 12))`, and the weighted regression projection is precomputed for full windows. The resulting implementation reached **1.00 efficiency** in **0.08 seconds**. This is notable because efficiency was strong across nearly all programs, so Generation 49’s advantage is behavioral rather than computational; its compact observer avoids the instability associated with more elaborate multi-scale or quadratic observation schemes.

- **Noise-scaled thresholds are used as a turn-validation mechanism rather than merely as clipping parameters.** Generation 49 computes noise from both innovation MAD and raw-difference MAD, then uses that scale for innovation clipping, adaptive alpha-beta gains, slope deadbanding, and reversal confirmation. The turn is accepted only when candidate direction, innovation sign, and persistence agree. In contrast, Generation 46’s persistence- and amplitude-based gate reduced but did not eliminate instability, leaving **72 false reversals** and **91.20 slope changes**, because its output still appears to track short-term state motion more directly.

- **The monotonic presentation rule intentionally sacrifices pointwise reconstruction during unconfirmed pullbacks.** When direction is positive, Generation 49 emits `max(reported_level, level)`; when negative, it emits `min(reported_level, level)`. This means the output may remain flat even though the latent state has begun moving in the opposite direction. That mechanism is consistent with its weak measured **noise reduction of 0.00**, moderate **0.50 correlation**, and higher **0.84 lag**, but it is also directly consistent with the best smoothness and reversal metrics in the evaluated set.

- **Generation 49 materially outperforms the newly evaluated high-fidelity filters on the combined metric.** The next-best listed programs generally scored **0.53**: Robust Causal Consensus Tracker (Generation 41), Evidence-Locked Adaptive Causal Filter (Generation 44), Robust Adaptive Kalman Trend Filter (Generation 45), and Evidence-Locked Robust Adaptive Trend Filter (Generation 47). Generation 49 had just **0.50 correlation** and **0.54 responsiveness**, yet scored **0.59** because it reduced false reversals to **9** and achieved **0.63 smoothness**.

- **The evaluated filters form a clear stability-versus-fidelity split.** Reactive Kalman, consensus, and alpha-beta filters cluster around **0.69–0.77 responsiveness**, **0.31–0.44 lag error**, and **0.84–0.91 accuracy/correlation**, but usually exhibit **81.80–254.20 slope changes** and **65.40–197.80 false reversals**. Generation 49 instead accepts slower visible reaction—**0.54 responsiveness**, **0.84 lag**—to lower slope changes to **11.80** and false reversals to **9**.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Replace `pending_count` with signed, normalized cumulative reversal evidence while retaining the hard `max`/`min` output lock.** Accumulate evidence only when `proposed_slope`, clipped innovation, and pending direction agree, e.g. `pending_evidence += max(0, pending_direction * clipped_innovation / noise_scale)`, and confirm around 2.5–3.5 units or after 3 qualifying samples. This extends Generation 49’s successful persistence gate and should reject alternating weak pullbacks more reliably than the current two-sample rule, reducing the remaining 9 false reversals without exposing internal jitter.

2. **Introduce a direction-safe post-confirmation catch-up limiter to reduce the current 0.83 lag error.** On a confirmed reversal, do not immediately let `reported_level` move arbitrarily far toward the latent `level`; instead, for the next 2–4 samples, move it by a bounded amount such as `clip(level - reported_level, -c * noise_scale, c * noise_scale)` while enforcing monotonicity in the new direction. This preserves the winning presentation-layer constraint while allowing faster visible recovery after genuine turns, where the current lock can remain stale.

3. **Use a short endpoint-regression slope as an internal gain modulator, not as a direct output component.** Add a second causal 5–7 sample weighted endpoint fit returning both intercept and slope; modestly increase `alpha`/`beta` when short slope, 12-sample slope, and tracker slope agree, but reduce `beta` and strengthen momentum retention when they disagree. Reactive multi-scale designs showed that short-horizon endpoint information improves correlation and lag, while Generation 49 showed that it must remain behind the direction-locked presentation layer to avoid chatter.

4. **Make opposing-turn thresholds asymmetric and trend-maturity aware.** Track the number of consecutive samples in the confirmed direction, then require a larger counter-trend slope threshold—such as `0.18–0.24 * noise_scale`—during a mature trend, while allowing a lower threshold near initialization or shortly after a prior reversal. The existing `0.10 * noise_scale` deadband is effective for suppressing tiny movement, but a maturity-aware entry barrier should further protect stable runs while preserving legitimate early reversals.

5. **Add a single robust residual-reweighting pass to the endpoint observer, focused on newest-sample contradiction outliers.** After the weighted linear fit, compute residuals and refit once with Huber weights; apply additional downweighting only when one of the newest 1–2 points has a large residual opposing both fitted slope and the confirmed presentation direction. Robust regression alone did not solve visible instability in earlier generations, but inside Generation 49’s separated observation/state/output architecture it can prevent isolated endpoint spikes from generating unnecessary innovations, pending reversals, and avoidable lag.
