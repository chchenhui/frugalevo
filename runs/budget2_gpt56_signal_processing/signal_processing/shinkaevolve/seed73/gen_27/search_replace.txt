<DESCRIPTION>
Introduce a persistent two-state trend/flat controller driven by the local regression slope relative to its estimated slope noise. Entering trend mode requires stronger evidence over consecutive windows, while returning to flat mode uses a lower threshold and longer confirmation. This prevents gain and reversal logic from reacting to noisy threshold crossings.

In flat mode, velocity adaptation is deliberately conservative and output reversals require three confirmations with a wider deadband. In trend mode, velocity adapts faster and genuine turns retain the existing two-sample confirmation. This targets the high false-reversal and slope-change counts without applying a uniform slower filter that would worsen lag during sustained signal motion.
</DESCRIPTION>
