## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- **The current best Robust Adaptive Local-Trend Filter remains the strongest overall stability/quality trade-off at 0.55 combined score.** Its median/MAD window clipping, exponentially weighted endpoint regression, and alpha-beta tracker produced **102.6 slope changes**, **81.2 false reversals**, and **0.16 smoothness**, materially better than every newly evaluated program. Its raw tracking metrics remain strong: **0.89 correlation/accuracy**, **0.39 lag error**, **0.72 responsiveness**, **0.39 noise reduction**, and **0.01 s** runtime.

- **Robust local preprocessing before temporal tracking continues to be the most effective directional-stability pattern.** The best program clips each trailing window around `median ± 3.5 × 1.4826 × MAD` before estimating a local level and slope. The newer tracker-heavy designs, despite innovation clipping, were less stable: Generation 38’s Kalman/Huber/regression fusion reached **0.93 correlation**, **0.80 responsiveness**, and **0.55 noise reduction**, but had **235 slope changes**, **184.4 false reversals**, and only **0.08 smoothness**.

- **Endpoint-aligned recency-weighted regression reliably supports low lag and high accuracy.** Generations 26 and 32, both based on Kalman plus endpoint/multiscale consensus estimates, achieved **0.93 correlation**, approximately **0.30–0.31 lag error**, and **0.76–0.77 responsiveness**. Generation 33’s pure multiscale endpoint regression likewise achieved **0.89 correlation**, **0.36 lag**, and **0.74 responsiveness**. This confirms that evaluating the fitted regression at the newest sample is effective for causal low-delay tracking.

- **Direct output-step suppression remains more effective than direction-label hysteresis alone.** The current best freezes a candidate when its step opposes velocity and is smaller than a joint noise/momentum band. This yields **81.2 false reversals**, outperforming Generation 39’s two-window confirmed-reversal alpha-beta design (**79.2 false reversals**) only narrowly, but with better overall stability through fewer total slope changes (**102.6 vs. 98.4**) and a higher combined score (**0.55 vs. 0.54**). ## Implementation Insights

- **The current best efficiently separates batch local estimation from sequential state stabilization.** `sliding_window_view` creates trailing windows; median, MAD, clipping, and weighted linear projections are applied vectorially; only the alpha-beta update is sequential. This implementation reached **0.01 s** execution time and **1.00 efficiency**, matching or exceeding the speed of the newer leading filters while avoiding the complexity of repeated multiscale fits or iterative robust regression.

- **The endpoint-regression coordinate system is central to the best program’s low-lag behavior.** It uses `t = arange(window_size) - (window_size - 1)`, so the regression intercept estimates the newest sample rather than the window center. Combined with weights increasing from `exp(-3)` to `1`, this preserves recent directional information while retaining enough older context for local stabilization. That structure supports its **0.39 lag error** and **0.72 responsiveness** without the severe jitter of predictive Kalman variants.

- **The best program adapts measurement gain from slope evidence rather than residual magnitude alone.** Its alpha varies from **0.42 to 0.72** according to `abs(local_slope) / (abs(local_slope) + local_noise)`. Thus, a noisy flat window receives a lower effective measurement gain, while a window with strong slope evidence receives faster tracking. This is structurally different from Generations 26, 32, 34, 36, and 38, where adaptive Kalman noise/process updates and fusion weights maintained stronger responsiveness but passed more endpoint fluctuations through.

- **The placement of hysteresis inside the output-state update matters.** In the best program, the reversal test operates on the actual proposed output step before `level` and `velocity` are committed: `step * velocity < 0` and `abs(step) < 0.16 * local_noise + 0.08 * abs(velocity)`. This state-dependent gate is stronger than merely labeling direction after output generation. Generation 34 also used output deadband/hysteresis and bounded acceleration, but retained **184.6 slope changes** and **146.8 false reversals**, indicating its level-and-slope Kalman update remained too reactive before suppression could stabilize it.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Replace raw-window `local_noise` in gain and reversal logic with a post-fit residual MAD, while retaining raw MAD for clipping.** After the clipped weighted regression, compute `residuals = clipped - (measurements[:, None] + local_slopes[:, None] * t)` and derive a robust residual scale per window. Coherent ramps then retain strong slope evidence and faster alpha response even when their raw window spread is large, while genuinely irregular windows receive lower gain and a wider reversal band.

2. **Add a pending-reversal confirmation state inside the sequential alpha-beta loop.** When a counter-velocity step is suppressed, store its sign and magnitude; permit a reversal only if the following measurement’s local slope and proposed step agree with that sign and exceed a stronger residual-noise-scaled threshold. This extends the current best program’s successful direct output-step suppression, borrowing the false-reversal benefit seen in confirmed-reversal designs without making confirmation the sole stabilization mechanism.

3. **Use hysteretic discrete gain modes rather than allowing alpha to vary continuously at every sample.** Compute slope confidence from `abs(local_slope) / (abs(local_slope) + residual_noise)` and switch into a trend mode only after two high-confidence windows, then return to flat/noisy mode only after several low-confidence windows. Keep the existing alpha range but use stable mode-specific values—for example, lower alpha and wider countertrend suppression in flat mode, higher alpha and slightly faster velocity adaptation in trend mode—to avoid gain jitter transmitting into output-direction changes.

4. **Fuse accepted output motion with robust local slope when updating velocity, with a residual-scaled acceleration cap.** Instead of `velocity = 0.72 * velocity + 0.28 * step`, form `target_velocity = w * step + (1 - w) * local_slopes[i]`, where `w` is high in noisy/flat conditions and lower during confident trends; clip `target_velocity - velocity` before smoothing. This preserves the best filter’s local robust slope information for sustained-trend propagation while preventing the highly reactive velocity behavior that hurt Kalman-style variants.

5. **Introduce a conservative two-profile endpoint regression blend conditioned on confidence, not a broad multiscale consensus.** Precompute the current `exp(-3, 0)` fit and a slightly more recent endpoint-weighted fit such as `exp(-4.5, 0)`, then blend toward the recent fit only when residual noise is low and trend confidence has remained high for multiple windows. Endpoint alignment is already validated for low lag, while limiting the blend to persistent clean trends avoids the slope disagreement and directional instability that made full multiscale fusion underperform.
