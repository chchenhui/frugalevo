<DESCRIPTION>
Separate the robust raw-window scale from the post-fit residual noise estimate. The raw MAD is retained only as a small numerical floor for Huber clipping, preventing an almost perfectly linear window from producing a near-zero clipping threshold. The residual MAD remains the quantity returned to the pipeline and therefore continues to drive trend confidence, fusion, strong-turn evidence, and reversal-band sizing. This lets coherent ramps retain a low noise estimate rather than being mistaken for volatility.

Additionally, weak opposing steps now require three persistent observations before reversing, while strong locally and Kalman-supported turns still bypass confirmation. This specifically targets the dominant remaining penalties—slope changes and false reversals—without imposing the extra delay on decisive genuine turns.
</DESCRIPTION>
