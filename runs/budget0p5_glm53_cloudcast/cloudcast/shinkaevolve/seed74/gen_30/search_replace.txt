<DESCRIPTION>
The current sequential greedy k-path assignment suffers from lock-in: the first destinations pick cheap paths, and later ones may fail to find better shared routes. Add a local-search improvement pass after the initial assignment. For each destination, recompute a shortest path on a graph where edges already used by *other* destinations are discounted (since shared edges are effectively amortized across the broadcast). If routing all of this destination's partitions along the new set of edges yields a lower total unique-edge cost than its current assignment, keep the swap; otherwise revert. This exploits multi-network redundancy (discounting shared edges steers destinations toward common trunks, then parallel branches), reduces redundant transfers, and avoids the exception-prone tree grafting seen in earlier generations. Edges with unknown cost (None) are treated as expensive to avoid free rides on unpriced links.
