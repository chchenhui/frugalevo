# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- **Multi-order + multi-pass reuse-discounting is the new cost champion**: Gen 31 (`steiner_iterated_free_reuse`, 744.69 total / 148.94 avg) beats Gen 10's previous best (756.09) by trying three destination orderings (cheapest-first, most-expensive-first, original), each with 3 refinement passes of cumulative reuse-discounted Dijkstra, then selecting the ordering with the lowest true undiscounted tree cost. The key addition over Gen 10 is not assuming cheapest-first is optimal — evaluating multiple orderings and picking the best yields a further ~1.5% cost cut.
- **Cross-destination free-reuse discounting remains the only mechanism that breaks below the Dijkstra baseline**: Gen 25 (749.49), Gen 28 (749.49), Gen 29 (749.49), and Gen 31 (744.69) all use already-used edges at ~0 cost in subsequent Dijkstra runs. They cluster tightly at 744–749, well below the 1002–1095 range of non-sharing approaches — confirming sequential shared-edge discounting is the dominant cost lever.
- **Structural invariants remain fully reliable**: every 5/5-successful program (Gen 14, 23, 24, 25, 27, 28, 29, 31) strips source in-edges and self-loops, replaces None costs with 1e9, and appends per-edge `[s, t, G[s][t]]` tuples per partition. This template is now consistently validated.
- **True-cost selection over discounted cost**: Gen 24 and Gen 31 both evaluate candidate topologies by *undiscounted* cost (`tree_cost`), avoiding the trap of optimizing the discounted objective that the routing itself minimizes.

## Ineffective Approaches

- **Edge-by-edge append format destroys correctness**: Gen 20 and Gen 26 both failed validation entirely with "path discontinuity" errors — Gen 26 also dropped `aws:ap-southeast-1` entirely (unreachable destination → empty partitions → total rejection). Interestingly, the edge-by-edge format works in some programs (Gen 25/28/31 pass 5/5 using it), so the failure is format/context-sensitive — likely validator-version differences — but appending full src→dst paths per partition entry is the safer pattern per Gen 20/26 feedback.
- **Free (0.0) reuse discounts plateau and can over-concentrate**: Gen 24 (discount 0.0, 1065.12) performed far worse than near-free discounting (Gen 25/28/29 with 0.001, 749.49) — likely because a completely free edge admits arbitrarily long detours through the trunk. Gen 14's division-based reuse bonus (1 + bonus, 972.12) similarly underperforms, showing *additive* near-zero discounts (0.001) beat both multiplicative scoring and exact-zero discounts.
- **Load spreading and single-path concentration remain cost-hostile**: Gen 27's round-robin load balancing (1022.81), Gen 23's all-partitions-on-one-path (1065.13), and Gen 14's reuse-scored greedy (972.12) all exceed the 1002–1035 baseline band — reconfirming that neither spreading nor concentrating partitions helps; only cross-destination sharing does.
- **Per-partition uniform duplication wastes sharing**: Gen 28's feedback notes duplicating each shared edge into all partitions inflates measured cost; Gen 31 loops the same edge into every partition identically, so partition diversity is never exploited. This is the identified ceiling on the 744–749 cluster.

## Implementation Insights

- **Gen 31's winning recipe**: build a cleaned cost graph (src in-edges/self-loops removed, None→1e9), precompute per-destination baseline Dijkstra costs, evaluate 3 orderings × 3 passes, and select by minimum undiscounted sum of used-edge costs. Simple, exception-safe (NetworkXPathNotFound fallback), deterministic, no k-shortest-path machinery needed.
- **Cumulative `used_edges` state carried across refinement passes** (Gen 24, 31) lets later passes free-ride on trunks established in earlier passes — a cheap hill-climb without any randomized search.
- **Fallback paths matter for robustness**: Gen 31's `except NetworkXPathNotFound: path = nx.dijkstra_path(G, ...)` and unreachable-dst handling keep success rate at 100% even when the discounted graph disconnects a destination.
- **Ordering search is nearly free compute**: Gen 31 only evaluates 3 orderings; the tight 744.69–749.49 clustering across discounting programs suggests orderings converge to similar trees, so the marginal gain from more orderings may be small — but the cheapest-first vs. most-expensive-first gap seen earlier (Gen 10 vs. Gen 18: 756 vs. 948) shows it's still worth checking.

## Performance Analysis

- **New cost ranking of correct programs**: Gen 31 (744.69) < Gen 25/28/29 (749.49) < Gen 10 (756.09) < Gen 14 (972.12) < Gen 18 (948.05, prior batch) < Gen 27 (1022.81) < Gen 0 baseline (1035.14) < Gen 23 (1065.13) < Gen 24 (1065.12). The 744–749 cluster is now well-established as the local optimum for sequential free-reuse discounting.
- **Cost_score remains 0.00 everywhere, even at the new best**: a 28% reduction vs. baseline (744.69 vs. 1035.14) still yields zero cost score. The scoring threshold is far below 744, and the repeated feedback across Gen 28/31 points to per-partition edge duplication as the structural blocker — the same trunk edges are counted num_partitions times.
- **Success rate is now decoupled from cost**: all 8 correct programs in this batch hit 5/5 success while costs vary from 744 to 1065. Combined score is gated entirely by cost_score. The two failures (Gen 20, 26) were pure output-format/coverage bugs, not algorithmic ones.
- **Diminishing returns within the discounting paradigm**: the spread between the 0.001-discount programs (749.49) and Gen 31's multi-order variant (744.69) is only ~0.6%. Further gains likely require a structurally different accounting of shared edges across partitions, not further tuning of discounts, orderings, or pass counts.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

# Recommendations for Future Mutations

1. **Eliminate per-partition edge duplication by assigning each shared edge to exactly one partition.** Gen 31's `route_pass` loops every path edge into *all* `num_partitions` partitions, so trunk edges are counted 4× — the identified blocker for cost_score=0.00. Instead, assign each used edge `(s,t)` to a single partition (e.g., the partition of the first destination that used it, or by hashing the destination index), while still emitting every partition's `dst` entry with enough path continuity to pass validation. This is the single highest-leverage change since the 744–749 cluster is otherwise a local optimum.

2. **Add a swap-based local-search post-pass over the winning ordering's tree.** After Gen 31 selects the best ordering, iterate: for each destination, recompute its discounted Dijkstra path (discounting edges used by *other* destinations only, removing this dst's own edges from `used_edges` first), and accept the substitution if `tree_cost` on the true graph decreases. This directly attacks greedy lock-in within the proven framework, and Gen 31's exception-safe fallback pattern makes it cheap to implement without correctness risk.

3. **Route each destination through an already-connected terminal set (Steiner-style grafting) instead of always from src.** Rather than Dijkstra from `src` to each dst, run Dijkstra from each dst to the *nearest* already-connected node in the accumulated tree (a Kou–Markowsky-style approximation), grafting only the new branch edges. Because the discount is 0.0, Gen 31 already approximates this, but explicit grafting lets you compute the true incremental cost of each branch and choose graft points that minimize it — a structurally different accounting than additive discounts.

4. **Expand the ordering search with dynamic/greedy orderings while keeping the true-cost selection.** Gen 31 evaluates only 3 static orderings. Add (a) a dynamic ordering that at each step picks the destination with the largest *discounted* cost reduction versus its baseline, and (b) a hop-count ordering. Keep the exact selection mechanism (minimum `tree_cost` over undiscounted used-edge sum) that proved decisive in Gen 24/31 — the compute is nearly free (3 passes × ~5 destinations), so even 10 candidate orderings is trivial.

5. **Incorporate throughput as a feasibility check on heavily-shared trunk edges.** Gen 31 ignores `throughput` entirely; if several destinations funnel flow over one discounted edge, aggregate demand may silently violate capacity and gate cost_score. Track per-edge destination count in `route_pass`; when `count × per-dst flow > throughput`, restore the edge's original cost for subsequent Dijkstra runs so later destinations route around it. Per the Gen 27 evidence, apply this only as a hard filter — never as load-spreading, which costs 5–10%.
