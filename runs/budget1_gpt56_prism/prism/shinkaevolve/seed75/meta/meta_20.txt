# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

- **The current best program, Multi-Start KV-Pressure Placement (Generation 19), establishes the strongest evaluated result at 26.11 combined score / 25.11 max_kvpr with 0.01 execution time and 1.00 success rate.** Its five deterministic construction orderings—pressure/size, size/pressure, pressure density, large-model residual-memory risk, and models larger than 40 GB—make greedy construction less sensitive to ordering-induced memory fragmentation. This extends the previously successful multi-start pattern while preserving negligible runtime.

- **Global prospective KVPR evaluation during greedy assignment is a consistently successful construction rule.** Generation 19 chooses the GPU yielding the lowest *resulting global maximum* `load / remaining_memory`, rather than simply the lowest target-GPU pressure. Generation 11 used the same broad strategy and matched the top result exactly at **26.11 / 25.11**, confirming that multi-start global-minimax greedy placement is the leading evaluated family.

- **Strictly improving local search remains valuable after greedy construction.** The current program searches capacity-safe single relocations and pairwise swaps, then conditionally uses three-GPU evacuation chains for instances of at most 160 models. Generation 10’s comparable chain-relocation approach reached **25.85 / 24.85**, while the current five-start variant improved the combined score by **0.26** to **26.11**, indicating that stronger diversified initialization combined effectively with bounded repair moves.

- **Beam construction is reliable but not superior on the measured objective.** Generations 12, 16, and 18 all passed validation with beam search plus exchange refinement, scoring **25.84**, **25.90**, and **25.90** respectively. Their correctness and low runtimes show that beam search is viable, but the simpler multi-start greedy-plus-local-search design in Generations 11 and 19 produced the highest measured score.

## Ineffective Approaches

- **Validation-breaking implementation defects eliminate all algorithmic value.** Generation 13’s greedy/local-exchange design received **0.00** because of the parse-time error, `Generator expression must be parenthesized`. Although its intended move, swap, and one/two-model exchange strategy resembled successful local-search methods, the syntax error in `pressure_vector` prevented any placement evaluation.

- **Unsound bounded branch-and-bound harmed correctness.** Generation 15 scored **0.0** despite attempting threshold optimization through binary search and DFS. Its 50,000-node DFS limit could declare a feasible threshold infeasible, while state rounding and zero-remaining-memory handling could create invalid or inconsistent results. This contrasts with Generation 19’s direct constructive feasibility checks, which achieved a **1.00 success rate**.

- **Heuristic construction must remain reliably feasible, not merely pressure-aware.** Generation 17’s multi-start greedy approach also scored **0.0** and failed validation. The feedback specifically identifies strict capacity handling and heuristic-only construction as potential causes of rejecting valid boundary cases or missing required feasible assignments. By comparison, the current program also enforces positive residual memory (`size >= remaining[gpu]` is rejected), but combines five orderings, a fallback across starts, and local repairs sufficiently robustly to pass every validation case.

- **Narrower local-search neighborhoods leave quality below the top tier.** Generation 14’s pressure-aware greedy placement with relocations and reverse two-for-one exchanges was correct but scored only **23.26 / 22.26**, substantially below Generation 19’s **26.11 / 25.11**. Targeted exchanges alone did not compensate for weaker initial diversification and less comprehensive multi-start construction.

## Implementation Insights

- **Generation 19’s state representation is simple and directly aligned with feasibility.** It maintains per-GPU `remaining` memory and demand `loads`, calculates pressure as `load / remaining`, and treats positive-load, zero-memory states as infinite pressure. Every construction, move, swap, and chain candidate recomputes affected residual memory before acceptance. This defensive handling corresponds with its perfect validation result.

- **The current program uses deterministic diversity rather than expensive stochastic search.** Its five sorted orderings explicitly target different packing risks: high pressure, large sizes, pressure density, size relative to the 80-GB capacity, and models exceeding half a GPU. It evaluates each completed candidate and retains the placement with the lowest final objective. This likely explains why it matches Generation 11’s best **26.11** result while remaining at only **0.01** execution time.

- **Local improvement is strictly objective-decreasing, preventing cycling.** Generation 19 accepts only candidates satisfying `value < best_max - EPS`; it then applies the best move found in an iteration. The bounded iteration count, `max(1, 2 * len(model_list))`, limits search cost. This is consistent with prior successful programs: Generation 10’s moves/swaps/chains achieved **25.85**, and beam variants with bounded strict-improvement exchange phases achieved roughly **25.84–25.90**.

- **The current program optimizes scalar maximum KVPR in its refinement loop, whereas several beam programs optimize a lexicographically sorted full pressure profile.** The beam implementations of Generations 12, 16, and 18 explicitly compare descending pressure vectors, yet their measured scores remained below the current multi-start result. In these evaluations, the current program’s stronger five-start construction and effective bottleneck reductions outweighed the apparent advantage of a more detailed lexicographic objective.

- **Generation 19 and Generation 11 form the top measured tier.** Both Multi-Start KV-Pressure Placement implementations achieved **26.11 combined score**, **25.11 max_kvpr**, approximately **0.01** execution time, and **1.00** success. This ties the best observed performance to multi-start global-minimax greedy construction followed by bounded relocation/swap refinement.

- **The current best exceeds the beam-search family by a modest but consistent margin.** Generation 19’s **26.11** is **0.21** above Generations 16 and 18 at **25.90**, and **0.27** above Generation 12 at **25.84**. Since all of these programs had 100% success and runtimes from **0.03–0.05** for beam search versus **0.01** for the current program, the multi-start greedy method was both stronger on the reported combined score and less costly in measured runtime.

- **Three-GPU chains help but are not alone responsible for top performance.** Generation 10 included three-GPU evacuation chains and reached **25.85**, while Generation 19 also includes chains for smaller instances and reaches **26.11**. The improvement of **0.26** is therefore better explained by the current program’s expanded five-ordering initialization and cross-start candidate selection than by chains alone.

- **Correctness is a hard performance boundary in these results.** Generations 13, 15, and 17 all received **0.0** because they failed parsing or validation, despite containing sophisticated local search, branch-and-bound, or multi-start logic. In contrast, all top-scoring methods—including Generations 10, 11, 12, 16, 18, and 19—paired pressure optimization with explicit capacity checks and achieved **1.00 success**, making reliable feasible placement a prerequisite for any objective-score gain.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Run the existing strict-improvement refinement on every feasible greedy start, not only the ultimately selected construction.** Keep the five deterministic orderings, but let `solve(order)` fully perform moves, swaps, and chains for each ordering, then select the best refined result; if runtime becomes relevant, refine only starts whose initial objective is within a small tolerance of the best initial objective. The strongest evidence is that diversified starts are responsible for much of Generation 19’s gain, and a slightly worse greedy placement may enter a better local-search basin.

2. **Add capacity-safe two-for-one and one-for-two exchange neighborhoods around bottleneck GPUs.** In addition to swaps, evaluate replacing one model on a high-pressure GPU with a pair from another GPU, and symmetrically replacing a pair on a high-pressure GPU with one model elsewhere; update both loads and residual memories exactly before scoring the resulting global maximum pressure. Generation 14 showed that exchange-style repairs can improve feasible placements, while the current best already demonstrates that richer coordinated neighborhoods such as chains remain safe and useful when strict improvement is enforced.

3. **Use a lexicographic pressure-profile tie-breaker while retaining maximum KVPR as the primary acceptance criterion.** Compare candidates first by `max(ratios)` and, when maxima are equal within `EPS`, by the descending sorted vector of all GPU pressures; use this both for greedy GPU ties and local-search candidate ties. Beam-search generations consistently used full pressure profiles successfully, and this can reduce secondary bottlenecks without sacrificing the current program’s proven global-minimax objective.

4. **Expand construction diversity with residual-memory-aware ordering and tie-breaking for hard-to-pack models.** Add one or two deterministic orders prioritizing models by combinations such as descending `(model_size / (80 - model_size), demand / model_size, model_size)` and descending `(model_size > 40, demand / max(80 - model_size, eps), demand)`. The present five starts already validate deterministic diversification, and additional emphasis on models that sharply reduce residual KV memory directly targets fragmentation cases that greedy pressure balancing can miss.

5. **Add a bounded two-step “unlock” search after direct moves, swaps, and chains fail.** Enumerate a small deterministic candidate set—e.g., move a low-demand model away from a target GPU to create room, then relocate or swap a model from one of the top two pressure GPUs—and accept only if the final global maximum strictly decreases; the intermediate state must remain capacity-safe and should not exceed the current maximum by more than a tiny tolerance. This extends the successful evacuation-chain principle while avoiding the unsoundness and runtime risk of broad branch-and-bound search.
